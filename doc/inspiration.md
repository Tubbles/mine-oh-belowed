# Inspiration and lessons from the genre

What the genre research of 2026-09-26 changes in our design, with the sources that carry it. The full report is `work/genre-research.md` (untracked).

## Lessons we build on

- Techtonica is the game we are closest to (user, 2026-10-03): first person, controller first, mining and factories built on 3D terrain with hard surface machine models. We are that "on the surface and in space with multiple planets", in a slightly more low-fi look ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), Art direction).
- Progression is "you did this by hand, now a machine does it". Wube orders Factorio as manual mining, automated mining, logistics, then production and science, "so you appreciate the upgrade", and warns that assemblers in the first five minutes break it ([FFF-327](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-327)). Our phases follow that order, and quests ask only for what the player has just done by hand.
- A mechanic that costs explanation without adding a decision goes. Factorio 0.17 cut pickaxes ("just bloat"), ore hardness and mining power ("hardly ever utilized"), burner efficiency and the assembler ingredient limit ([FFF-266](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-266)). We put every Minecraft habit to that test: tool tiers pass as a gate on hand mining, since which pickaxe to craft first is a decision (0051). Durability and hunger stay out.
- Visible, throughput limited logistics are the fun. Wube argues belts beat bots, which "teleport" items and reduce expansion to "plopping more cells without any thought" ([FFF-224](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-224), [FFF-225](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-225)). Modded Minecraft's autocrafting storage networks (Applied Energistics 2, Refined Storage) flatten the game the same way ([FTB forum thread](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/is-automation-ruining-modded-minecraft-whos-to-blame.73775/)). We have no bots and no storage network: belts, ramps, lifts, inserters and pipes carry everything, rails join later, and the recycler and rocket shipments are the sinks.
- Every 3D factory game avoids running out of ore. Satisfactory's nodes are infinite with purity multipliers ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Resource_node)). FOUNDRY makes veins endless through fracking with a mining fluid, "endless, but not without cost" ([roadmap](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/foundry/roadmap)). Dyson Sphere Program has an infinite setting and vein utilisation research. Techtonica's veins take "thousands of hours" to exhaust. Only Captain of Industry keeps ore finite, because reshaping terrain is its identity. Our world setting offers finite and infinite, and the finite default has a costly late game revival.
- Reservoirs, not ore blocks, at Factorio scale. Manufactio, the pack that set the expectation for this game, uses Factory0-Resources: ore per chunk "as an abstract number rather then adding them as blocks", from simplex noise, extracted by a Deep Core Drill ([README](https://github.com/V0idWa1k3r/Factory0-Resources)). Its author targeted concentrations of 100k to 200k, deposits of 30k and scatterings of 2k ([FTB forum](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/manufactio-minecraft-factorio-hybrid.283615/page-4)). Immersive Engineering's veins default to 38,400 ore as a weighted mix plus spoils such as gravel ([IEServerConfig.java](https://github.com/BluSunrize/ImmersiveEngineering/blob/1.20.1/src/main/java/blusunrize/immersiveengineering/common/config/IEServerConfig.java)). Our veins are reservoirs with an outcrop on that scale, mixed ores and spoils included.
- Recipe lookup is the first information need. FNEI and Recipe Book were the most used information mods, and Wube built Factoriopedia into 2.0 because "what do I make with this" was nearly impossible through search ([FFF-397](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-397)). Ratio math (Helmod, Factory Planner, Max Rate Calculator), bottleneck spotting (Bottleneck Lite) and spreading items over machines (Even Distribution, over half a million downloads) follow. We build all of it in: the graph browser, a rate on every machine, a bottleneck overlay, a distribute gesture.
- Exploration can pay in recipes. Satisfactory's hard drives turn crash sites into recipe choices, two alternates per drive with one reroll ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Hard_Drive)). Our cave schematics do the same for the optional underground.
- Overflow needs a home. Satisfactory's AWESOME Sink destroys items for coupons ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/AWESOME_Sink)). Our recycler and rocket shipments keep overflow in the material loop instead of a point shop.
- Power overload is the common first crisis: over half of Satisfactory's Steam players have the blown fuse achievement ([Steam stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/526870/achievements)). We lean into it with visible proportional brownouts and a power overview, not a fuse hunt.
- Verticality pays when it is cheap. Satisfactory players build logistics floors over machine floors and see a lift as "using a lift instead of moving products further" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/526870/discussions/0/4843148592919098595/)). Placed blocks are our foundations. Floors, ramps and lifts are the vertical toolkit.

## Pitfalls we avoid

- Terrain tools draw complaints. Captain of Industry's excavate and dump designations are called confusing and "my least favorite part of this game" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1594320/discussions/0/3721691844089795619/)). Techtonica's 1.0 level gating replaced exploration with grinding for veterans. FortressCraft's remote mines may unload because the game follows the power network ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/254200/discussions/0/558755530389482152)). Digging stays the Minecraft act (look, hold, block gone) and is never required for ore.
- The drop offs are steep. Of all Steam owners, 39% of Factorio players build an assembler and 24% launch a rocket ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/427520/achievements)). 44% of Satisfactory players reach Phase 1 and 6% finish ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/526870/achievements)). 11% finish Dyson Sphere Program ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1366540/achievements)). The quest chain exists to carry players past the first assembler and their first mess.
- Spaghetti and the rebuild spiral: players get bored by their own mess and start over, and a teardown stops the factory ([forum](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?t=32190), [forum](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?t=103321)). Blueprints are post alpha. Foundations and floors leave room, and the quests teach building the new line beside the old one.
- Controller weak spots. Factorio's Switch port made every widget stick navigable and added a radial quick panel, and picking one entity among dense neighbours stays the weak point ([FFF-370](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-370), [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2023/06/factorio-adds-controller-support-in-experimental-update/)). A Satisfactory console review praised the radial menus but found first person placement "harder than it needs to be" ([review](https://lootlevelchill.com/reviews/satisfactory-console-edition-review/)). FOUNDRY and Dyson Sphere Program have no real gamepad support. Techtonica was developed controller only at first "to make sure we did the controls well" ([developer post](https://techtonicagame.com/techtonica-has-an-early-access-release-date-controller-support-and-more/)). We do the same, add selection assist for dense clusters, and keep third person for placement.
- Pace. Wube cut their first Space Age playthrough from 370 hours with "lower the cost of stuff, reduce unnecessary recipe steps" ([FFF-417](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-417)). Our depth comes from byproducts and alternative routes, not step count.
