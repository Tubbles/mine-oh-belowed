# Inspiration and lessons from the genre

Distilled from the genre research of 2026-09-26. The full report with every source lives in `work/genre-research.md` (untracked, like all reports). This document keeps what changes our design, with the sources that carry it.

## Lessons we build on

- Progression is a chain of "you did this by hand, now a machine does it". Wube orders Factorio as manual mining, automated mining, automated logistics, automated production and science, "so you appreciate the upgrade", and warns that forcing assemblers in the first five minutes breaks it ([FFF-327](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-327)). Our gameplay phases follow that order and the quests only ask for what the player has just done by hand.
- Mechanics that cost explanation without adding a decision get cut. In 0.17 Wube removed pickaxes ("just bloat"), ore hardness and mining power ("hardly ever utilized"), burner efficiency and the assembler ingredient limit ([FFF-266](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-266)). We apply the same test to every Minecraft habit we are tempted to keep: tool tiers, durability, hunger.
- Visible, throughput limited logistics are the fun. Wube's designers argue belts beat bots because bots "teleport" items and turn expansion into "plopping more cells without any thought" ([FFF-224](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-224), [FFF-225](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-225)). Modded Minecraft's storage networks with autocrafting (Applied Energistics 2, Refined Storage) flatten the game the same way, and players have argued about it for years ([FTB forum thread](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/is-automation-ruining-modded-minecraft-whos-to-blame.73775/)). We have no logistic bots and no storage network. Belts, ramps, lifts, inserters and pipes carry everything, rails join later, and the recycler and rocket shipments are the sinks.
- Every 3D factory game avoids running out of ore. Satisfactory nodes are infinite with purity multipliers ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Resource_node)), FOUNDRY makes veins endless through fracking paid for with a mining fluid, "endless, but not without cost" ([roadmap](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/foundry/roadmap)), Dyson Sphere Program has an infinite setting plus vein utilisation research, Techtonica's veins take "thousands of hours" to exhaust. Only Captain of Industry keeps ore finite, because reshaping terrain is its identity. Our world setting offers finite and infinite, and the finite default gets a late game revival route at a cost.
- Reservoirs, not ore blocks, at Factorio scale. Manufactio, the pack that set the expectation for this game, uses Factory0-Resources: ore is stored per chunk "as an abstract number rather then adding them as blocks", generated from simplex noise and extracted by a Deep Core Drill with drill heads ([README](https://github.com/V0idWa1k3r/Factory0-Resources)). The pack author targeted rare concentrations of 100k to 200k, deposits of 30k and common scatterings of 2k ([FTB forum](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/manufactio-minecraft-factorio-hybrid.283615/page-4)). Immersive Engineering's veins default to 38,400 ore as a weighted ore mix plus spoils such as gravel ([IEServerConfig.java](https://github.com/BluSunrize/ImmersiveEngineering/blob/1.20.1/src/main/java/blusunrize/immersiveengineering/common/config/IEServerConfig.java)). Our veins are reservoirs with an outcrop, sized on that scale, mixed ores and spoils included.
- Recipe lookup is the number one information need. FNEI and Recipe Book were the most used information mods, and Wube built Factoriopedia into 2.0 because "what do I make with this" was nearly impossible through search ([FFF-397](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-397)). Ratio math (Helmod, Factory Planner, Max Rate Calculator), bottleneck spotting (Bottleneck Lite) and spreading items over many machines (Even Distribution, over half a million downloads) follow. All of it is built in from the start: the graph browser, a rate readout on every machine, a bottleneck overlay, and a distribute gesture for hand feeding.
- Exploration can pay in recipes. Satisfactory's hard drives from crash sites turn exploration into recipe choice, two alternates per drive with one reroll ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Hard_Drive)). Our cave schematics do the same for the optional underground.
- Overflow needs a home. Satisfactory's AWESOME Sink destroys items for shop coupons ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/AWESOME_Sink)). We use the recycler and rocket shipments instead of a point shop, so overflow stays in the material loop.
- Power overload is the common first crisis: over half of Satisfactory's Steam players have unlocked the blown fuse achievement ([Steam stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/526870/achievements)). Our power puzzle leans into it with visible proportional brownouts and a power overview screen, not a fuse hunt.
- Verticality pays when it is cheap. Satisfactory players build logistics floors above machine floors and see a lift as "using a lift instead of moving products further" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/526870/discussions/0/4843148592919098595/)). Placed blocks are our foundations, so floors, ramps and lifts are the vertical toolkit.

## Pitfalls we avoid

- Terrain digging tools are a recurring complaint. Captain of Industry's excavate and dump designations are called confusing and "my least favorite part of this game" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1594320/discussions/0/3721691844089795619/)), Techtonica's 1.0 level gating replaced exploration with grinding for veterans, and FortressCraft's remote mines may not stay loaded because the game follows the power network ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/254200/discussions/0/558755530389482152)). We keep digging to the Minecraft act (look, hold, block gone) and never require it for ore.
- The drop offs are steep. Of all Steam owners, 39% of Factorio players build an assembler and 24% launch a rocket ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/427520/achievements)), 44% of Satisfactory players reach Phase 1 and 6% finish ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/526870/achievements)), 11% finish Dyson Sphere Program ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1366540/achievements)). The quest chain exists to carry players past the first assembler and past their first mess.
- Spaghetti and the rebuild spiral. Players describe getting bored by their own mess and starting over, and a teardown stops the factory ([forum](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?t=32190), [forum](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?t=103321)). Blueprints are post alpha, but foundations and floors leave room and the quests teach building the new line beside the old one.
- Controller weak spots. Factorio's Switch port made every widget stick navigable and added a radial quick panel, and picking one entity among dense neighbours is still the weak point ([FFF-370](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-370), [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2023/06/factorio-adds-controller-support-in-experimental-update/)). A Satisfactory console review praised the radial menus but found first person placement "harder than it needs to be" ([review](https://lootlevelchill.com/reviews/satisfactory-console-edition-review/)). FOUNDRY and Dyson Sphere Program have no real gamepad support. Techtonica developed controller only at first "to make sure we did the controls well" ([developer post](https://techtonicagame.com/techtonica-has-an-early-access-release-date-controller-support-and-more/)). We do the same, add selection assist for dense clusters, and keep the third person toggle for placement.
- Pace. Wube cut their first Space Age playthrough from 370 hours with the rule "lower the cost of stuff, reduce unnecessary recipe steps" ([FFF-417](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-417)). Our depth comes from byproducts and alternative routes, not from step count for its own sake.
