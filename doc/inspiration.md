# Inspiration and lessons from the genre

What the genre research of 2026-09-26 changes in our design, with the sources that carry it. The full report is `work/genre-research.md` (untracked).

## Lessons we build on

- Techtonica is the game we are closest to (user, 2026-10-03): first person, controller first, mining and factories built on 3D terrain with hard surface machine models. We are that "on the surface and in space with multiple planets": Techtonica in space, the leading star of the look and feel (user, 2026-10-05, [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), Art direction).
- The Last Caretaker is a major influence and inspiration (user, 2026-11-11): Channel37's first person survival and crafting game, a seafaring journey to rebuild civilization ([announcement](https://gamespress.com/Veteran-Led-Studio-Channel37-Unveils-The-Last-Caretaker-a-Seafaring-Su)), in early access on PC since 2025-11-06 ([Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2025/10/the-last-caretaker-for-pc-launches-in-early-access-on-november-6)). We take all four of the parts the user named: its look, the lone machine's tone, the vessel as a base, and its power and cable play. The user's decisions from them (2026-11-11, [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md)): the player is a machine and Mission Control stays for now; the pod stays and a land vehicle is built from scratch and improved like a ship of Theseus, a new one on every planet; all power is run by hand; its look takes precedence over Techtonica's. What each part is in that game: The Last Caretaker (0353), below.
- Progression is "you did this by hand, now a machine does it". Wube orders Factorio as manual mining, automated mining, logistics, then production and science, "so you appreciate the upgrade", and warns that assemblers in the first five minutes break it ([FFF-327](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-327)). Our phases follow that order, and quests ask only for what the player has just done by hand.
- A mechanic that costs explanation without adding a decision goes. Factorio 0.17 cut pickaxes ("just bloat"), ore hardness and mining power ("hardly ever utilized"), burner efficiency and the assembler ingredient limit ([FFF-266](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-266)). We put every Minecraft habit to that test: tool tiers pass as a gate on hand mining, since which pickaxe to craft first is a decision (0051). Durability and hunger stay out.
- Visible, throughput limited logistics are the fun. Wube argues belts beat bots, which "teleport" items and reduce expansion to "plopping more cells without any thought" ([FFF-224](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-224), [FFF-225](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-225)). Modded Minecraft's autocrafting storage networks (Applied Energistics 2, Refined Storage) flatten the game the same way ([FTB forum thread](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/is-automation-ruining-modded-minecraft-whos-to-blame.73775/)). We have no bots and no storage network: belts, ramps, lifts, inserters and pipes carry everything, rails join later, and the recycler and rocket shipments are the sinks.
- Every 3D factory game avoids running out of ore. Satisfactory's nodes are infinite with purity multipliers ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Resource_node)). FOUNDRY makes veins endless through fracking with a mining fluid, "endless, but not without cost" ([roadmap](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/foundry/roadmap)). Dyson Sphere Program has an infinite setting and vein utilisation research. Techtonica's veins take "thousands of hours" to exhaust. Only Captain of Industry keeps ore finite, because reshaping terrain is its identity. Our world setting offers finite and infinite, and the finite default has a costly late game revival.
- Reservoirs, not ore blocks, at Factorio scale. Manufactio, the pack that set the expectation for this game, uses Factory0-Resources: ore per chunk "as an abstract number rather then adding them as blocks", from simplex noise, extracted by a Deep Core Drill ([README](https://github.com/V0idWa1k3r/Factory0-Resources)). Its author targeted concentrations of 100k to 200k, deposits of 30k and scatterings of 2k ([FTB forum](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/manufactio-minecraft-factorio-hybrid.283615/page-4)). Immersive Engineering's veins default to 38,400 ore as a weighted mix plus spoils such as gravel ([IEServerConfig.java](https://github.com/BluSunrize/ImmersiveEngineering/blob/1.20.1/src/main/java/blusunrize/immersiveengineering/common/config/IEServerConfig.java)). Our veins are reservoirs with an outcrop on that scale, mixed ores and spoils included.
- Recipe lookup is the first information need. FNEI and Recipe Book were the most used information mods, and Wube built Factoriopedia into 2.0 because "what do I make with this" was nearly impossible through search ([FFF-397](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-397)). Ratio math (Helmod, Factory Planner, Max Rate Calculator), bottleneck spotting (Bottleneck Lite) and spreading items over machines (Even Distribution, over half a million downloads) follow. We build all of it in: the graph browser, a rate on every machine, a bottleneck overlay, a distribute gesture.
- Exploration can pay in recipes. Satisfactory's hard drives turn crash sites into recipe choices, two alternates per drive with one reroll ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Hard_Drive)). Our cave schematics do the same for the optional underground.
- Overflow needs a home. Satisfactory's AWESOME Sink destroys items for coupons ([wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/AWESOME_Sink)). Our recycler and rocket shipments keep overflow in the material loop instead of a point shop.
- Power overload is the common first crisis: over half of Satisfactory's Steam players have the blown fuse achievement ([Steam stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/526870/achievements)). We lean into it with visible proportional brownouts and a power overview, not a fuse hunt.
- Verticality pays when it is cheap. Satisfactory players build logistics floors over machine floors and see a lift as "using a lift instead of moving products further" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/526870/discussions/0/4843148592919098595/)). Placed blocks are our foundations. Floors, ramps and lifts are the vertical toolkit.

## Pitfalls we avoid

- Terrain tools draw complaints. Captain of Industry's excavate and dump designations are called confusing and "my least favorite part of this game" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1594320/discussions/0/3721691844089795619/)). Techtonica's 1.0 level gating replaced exploration with grinding for veterans. FortressCraft's remote mines may unload because the game follows the power network ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/254200/discussions/0/558755530389482152)). Digging stays the Minecraft act (look, hold, block gone) and is never required for ore.
- The drop offs are steep. Of all Steam owners, 39% of Factorio players build an assembler and 24% launch a rocket ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/427520/achievements)). 44% of Satisfactory players reach Phase 1 and 6% finish ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/526870/achievements)). 11% finish Dyson Sphere Program ([stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1366540/achievements)). The quest chain exists to carry players past the first assembler and their first mess.
- Spaghetti and the rebuild spiral: players get bored by their own mess and start over, and a teardown stops the factory ([forum](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?t=32190), [forum](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?t=103321)). Blueprints are post alpha. Foundations and floors leave room, and the quests teach building the new line beside the old one.
- Controller weak spots. Factorio's Switch port made every widget stick navigable and added a radial quick panel, and picking one entity among dense neighbours stays the weak point ([FFF-370](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-370), [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2023/06/factorio-adds-controller-support-in-experimental-update/)). A Satisfactory console review praised the radial menus but found first person placement "harder than it needs to be" ([review](https://lootlevelchill.com/reviews/satisfactory-console-edition-review/)). FOUNDRY and Dyson Sphere Program have no real gamepad support. Techtonica was developed controller only at first "to make sure we did the controls well" ([developer post](https://techtonicagame.com/techtonica-has-an-early-access-release-date-controller-support-and-more/)). We do the same, add selection assist for dense clusters, and keep third person for placement.
- Pace. Wube cut their first Space Age playthrough from 370 hours with "lower the cost of stuff, reduce unnecessary recipe steps" ([FFF-417](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-417)). Our depth comes from byproducts and alternative routes, not step count.

## How machines look in the genre (0211)

Source research for the art direction booklet (0211), judged against the direction the session found: astro-industrial punk, clean space technology and rough dirty industry interleaved all over every machine, the ratio sliding from 80 rough at the wood and coal stage to 80 clean late, everything dirty and used, automated machines nobody enters, Satisfactory's sense of size and the readable shapes of Techtonica and Astroneer ([log](log/2026-10-03.md), The art direction). The look notes come from the official screenshots on each game's store page or press kit, the numbers and quotes from the wikis and developer posts linked.

### Satisfactory

- Look: tall boxy machines in the default FICSIT swatch, orange (#FA9549) panels on blue grey (#5F668C), over dark steel frames with grilles and hazard stripes ([Customizer](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Customizer), [store screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/526870/)). Smelting buildings stand in long rows with black smoke from their stacks.
- Scale: the Pioneer is 1.8 m tall ([Pioneer](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Pioneer)). The first machines already tower over the player: a smelter is 8.5 m high ([Smelter](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Smelter)), a biomass burner 10 m ([Biomass Burner](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Biomass_Burner)), a miner 18 m ([Miner](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Miner)). The late ones are landmarks: a coal generator 36 m with its chimney ([Coal-Powered Generator](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Coal-Powered_Generator)), a nuclear plant 39 m ([Nuclear Power Plant](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Nuclear_Power_Plant)), the space elevator 118 m and visible from all parts of the map ([Space Elevator](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Space_Elevator)).
- State: one indicator light per building with four meanings, solid green working, blinking yellow standby, blinking red no power, white or blue overclocked ([Indicator Light](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Indicator_Light)). Smoke and animation run only while a machine processes. The nuclear plant's tower steams when it has fuel and a blue glow lies on the floor under it.
- Tiers: Miner Mk.1 to Mk.3 keep one 6 by 14 by 18 m footprint while the cost moves from iron plates and concrete to supercomputers and fused modular frames. Progress shows mostly as new and bigger buildings, all in the same paint.
- What we take: the size. The furnace stands well over the astronaut from the start and the late machines read as landmarks from far away. One status light per machine with a few fixed colours.
- What we leave: one painted swatch on every machine and plating that stays clean. Our machines are dirty and used, and their mix of rough and clean changes with the tier.

### Techtonica

- Look: squat, rounded machines with one big readable feature each ([press kit](https://techtonicagame.com/press-kit/)). Smelters are slate blue drums with copper orange lids and a glowing grille window. Assemblers carry worn red paint with chipped edges, chrome pistons, hazard stripes and yellow welding arms. Accumulators show their copper coils. Small screens, cyan holograms and fluorescent tubes sit on and around the machines. The world is a set of caverns lit by "bioluminescent flora", with "Eco-Brutalist" building parts ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1457320/Techtonica/)).
- Scale: machines stand about chest to head height of the player. The smelter takes 2 by 2 cells with 4 by 4 blocked, and Mk.II and Mk.III keep that footprint ([Smelter](https://techtonica-calculator.com/seiten/Items_page.php?id=Smelter), [Smelter MKIII](https://www.techtonica-calculator.com/seiten/Items_page.php?id=Smelter_MKIII)).
- State: the furnace window and lid glow orange with heat rising over the top, welding arms throw sparks, and a hall of smelters reads by its rows of lit windows.
- Tiers: the same footprint with an "enhanced" description and electricity from Mk.III on. Tiers differ in detail more than in shape or size.
- What we take: shapes that read at a glance, the glowing mouth as the face of a furnace, chipped paint over metal, coils, pistons and small screens as the clean and rough parts of a mid tier machine.
- What we leave: machines at human scale, since ours grow big, and the cavern glow as the main light, since our factories stand on the surface and in space.

### Factorio

- Look: seen from above, in muted brown, grey and rust ([store screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/427520/)). Boilers and steam engines trail white plumes, furnaces glow orange in the dark. For the Space Age foundry Wube wanted "heavy, industrial, powerful", with "massive moving parts" blocked out in 3D before the paint, and the big mining drill exaggerates "aggressive ground penetration" with rails pressed into the ground ([FFF-387](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-387)). The rule Wube states for Vulcanus: "when we diverge from reality we do it intentionally", and usability wins over realism ([FFF-386](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-386)).
- Scale: the character is small beside a 3 by 3 assembler ([Assembling machine 3](https://wiki.factorio.com/Assembling_machine_3)) and tiny beside the 9 by 9 rocket silo ([Rocket silo](https://wiki.factorio.com/Rocket_silo)). Size comes from the top down view of the whole base more than from one machine.
- State: glow, smoke, steam and looping animation while working. The silo shows its progress as a sequence: doors open, the rocket rises, launches, the doors close.
- Tiers: the Space Age machines take the theme of their planet (the foundry is the metallurgy of Vulcanus), so a late machine looks like where it was made.
- What we take: machines that show their work with big moving parts and heat, drills that grip the ground, a rocket launch told as a sequence, and the rule that every departure from realism is chosen for readability.
- What we leave: the uniform dieselpunk brown without a clean counterpart, and the top down reading. Our machines are seen up close by a player beside them.

### Dyson Sphere Program

- Look: clean science fiction, dark grey and white plating with cyan and blue light, glowing towers and beams, the whole factory lit at night ([store screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1366540/)). A review calls it "vibrant and colourful" next to "Factorio's brownish aesthetic" and the night lighting "fantastic" ([bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/dyson-sphere-program-review/1/)).
- Scale: the factory wraps a small planet seen from the player's mecha, other planets hang in the sky, and the end game is a sphere around a star. Size comes from the planet and the star rather than from one machine.
- State: light. Machines, belts and power lines glow, blue beams rise from buildings to the sky.
- Tiers: the screenshots go from a few machines on a bare globe to a planet covered in lit structures and the sphere itself.
- What we take: light as the main state cue at the high end, panel lines and light strips as the clean world at its 80 percent, and beams to the sky for the launch machines.
- What we leave: the spotless finish. Our high tier is smudged and keeps a vent, a pipe or a cog.

### Astroneer

- Look: low poly with "bold geometric shapes and vibrant color palettes" and no textures at all, the made objects with "less of a faceted look" than the terrain ([The Art of Astroneer](https://blog.astroneer.space/p/the-art-of-astroneer-low-poly/)), "curved geometric, sort of broad vibrant colors" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astroneer)). The modules are white rounded shells with blue stripes, orange trim, round hatches and big dials ([store screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/361420/)).
- Scale: every object has a size class, small, medium, large or extra large, and plugs into a slot of that size ([Items](https://astroneer.wiki.gg/wiki/Items)). The smelting furnace is a large module ([Smelting Furnace](https://astroneer.wiki.gg/wiki/Smelting_Furnace)), the biggest modules stand two to three astronauts high.
- State: the pages read do not describe state cues beyond the furnace stopping when its storage is full.
- Tiers: progression is the size class, each printer making the next size up. The material and finish stay the same.
- What we take: silhouettes made of a few large shapes, round hatches and oversized handles and dials, and white panels with one coloured stripe as the clean world of a machine.
- What we leave: the untextured toy finish and the playful palette. Our clean parts carry grime, and our rough parts carry rivets, iron and stone.

### FOUNDRY

- Look: "soft, vibrant, and cleanly illustrated", metal with "weathered, chipped edges" that stays clean rather than grimy, each building with "unique shapes and colour palettes" so categories stay apart when "placed in multiple rows of hundreds", and soft biomes behind "heavy, bulky, industrial" machines ([80.lv interview](https://80.lv/articles/channel-3-entertainment-on-creating-the-robot-factory-builder-game-foundry)). The screenshots show navy and yellow robot arms on arches welding robots with sparks, cyan neon strips, smokestacks with dark smoke and cooling towers ([store screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/983870/)).
- Scale: a voxel world where the player robot stands beside machines several times its height and towers far above.
- State: sparks, smoke and moving arms on the assembly lines, neon on the floors and walls.
- Tiers: the research tree unlocks "more advanced, complex and faster technology" after manual crafting ([Paradox](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/foundry/about)).
- What we take: the assembly line of robot arms throwing sparks, close to the assembler the user asked for, and the habit of checking a machine both alone and in a row of many, each family with its own shape.
- What we leave: chipped but clean metal and neon as decoration. Our metal is dirty and our lights mean something.

### Across the six

- In the material read, none of the six mixes clean and rough on one machine as a rule. Satisfactory, Techtonica and FOUNDRY sit on painted industry with a clean edge, Dyson Sphere Program and Astroneer on clean technology. The interleave and its sliding ratio are ours.
- Size is Satisfactory's alone among them at the scale of one machine. Readability comes from a few large shapes (Astroneer, Techtonica) and from one shape and palette per family (FOUNDRY).
- Every game shows a working machine by motion, heat or light. Satisfactory adds one status light with fixed colours on every building.

## The Last Caretaker (0353)

Channel37's first person survival and crafting game, built in Unreal Engine 5 by a Helsinki studio of fewer than ten ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Caretaker)), single player, in early access on PC since 2025-11-06 with eight updates shipped up to Update 6.0 of 2026-10-18 and 1.0 announced for spring 2027 ([roadmap](https://thelastcaretaker.com/roadmap), [FAQ](https://thelastcaretaker.com/faq)), 85 percent positive of 9342 English Steam reviews on 2026-11-11 ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1783560/The_Last_Caretaker/)). The player is "the last active Caretaker", "a machine built for one purpose: save humanity" ([site](https://thelastcaretaker.com)): on a drowned Earth it recovers human seeds, grows them in the Lazarus Complex and launches the new humans to orbit ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1783560/The_Last_Caretaker/)). Studied on 2026-11-11 from the store page, the developer's patch notes (read through Steam's news API), the official site, the community wiki ([thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/), most pages stubs), reviews, previews and guides. No picture was looked at, so the look below is what writers describe. The pictures are the [store's screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1783560/The_Last_Caretaker/) and the [press kit](https://f.io/ZEFluXEZ) (a Frame.io share whose contents did not load here). Numbers are early access values that move between updates. Where sources disagree, each is given. The proposals each part leads to are in [SUGGESTIONS.md](../SUGGESTIONS.md), What The Last Caretaker's decisions leave open.

### The look

- World: a sea over Earth's ruins "dotted with rusted towers", "rusted silhouettes" on the horizon ([3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)), "oil rig-style platforms" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-last-caretaker/reveal-new-survival-game)), "Weathered megastructures, decayed platforms, and forgotten stations" ([announcement](https://gamespress.com/Veteran-Led-Studio-Channel37-Unveils-The-Last-Caretaker-a-Seafaring-Su)), underwater cities and seed vaults ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1783560/The_Last_Caretaker/)).
- Palette: "dominated by blues, browns, and grays", the Caretaker's orange scarf "a hint of color" ([Punished Backlog](https://punishedbacklog.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)). A caption shows the robot "with blue triangular eyes and an orange scarf" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-last-caretaker/reveal-new-survival-game)). These two are the only written sources on colour found.
- Light and weather carry the mood: "strong lighting, varied weather, and a dynamic day-night cycle", sunsets that "cast the ocean in warm reds", storms that "tilt the ship against choppy water", nightfall "a sudden and dramatic shift in tone" ([3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)). Lighting and weather "add a sense of danger" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/previews/the-last-caretaker/)), dark interiors need a torch that "needs power" ([Gert Lush](https://gertlushgaming.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-early-access-preview/)), and enemies rise sharply after sunset ([Roundtable Co-op](https://roundtablecoop.com/?p=11892)). Later updates raised the waves ([Update 5.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1836506165545642)) and added snow and cold ([Update 6.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1846651706689776)).
- Rendering: realistic and physical. Reviewers range from "sumptuous visuals, authentic physics" ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/reviews/the-last-caretaker-review/)) to "impressive but fairly standard", not standing out "from other games built in Unreal Engine" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/previews/the-last-caretaker/)) and "decent looking ... if unspectacular" ([Loot Level Chill](https://lootlevelchill.com/?p=10948)). The boat floats, cables and mooring lines are simulated ropes, and the patch notes keep fixing both ([Update 02](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1824459501603403), [Update 5.5](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1842212951309907)).
- Machines and interiors: "Interiors have a 1980s and '90s tech vibe", terminals "resemble what you find in Fallout" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-last-caretaker/reveal-new-survival-game)), "retro-futuristic furniture" ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/reviews/the-last-caretaker-review/)). Devices carry invented product names: the DONK-LIFT 3200 forklift ([Update 01](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1818752592135205)), the PECO Grid Scan 2000 and TideRipper hand jets ([Update 5.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1836506165545642)), the LumiLite solar array and the Whisper wind generator ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/LumiLite)). State reads by light: the Fabricator's "orange lights across the top" are its progress bar ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Fabricator)). Colour tells what a line carries: electric cables blue, fuel cables orange ([Neon Lights guide](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/the-last-caretaker-beginner-guide-tips-tricks)), the boat's diesel engine yellow and its electric engine blue ([Patch Crazy](https://patchcrazy.co.uk/how-to-use-engines-for-boats-in-the-last-caretaker/)). No source describes the machines' silhouettes: unverified.
- Scale: "a grand sense of scale" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/previews/the-last-caretaker/)). The boat is 30 m long ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Boat)), and "every inch of the world" is meant to be reachable, rafters and ladders included ([Punished Backlog](https://punishedbacklog.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)).
- Interface and camera: "simplistic" ([Thumb Culture](https://www.thumbculture.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-pc-early-access-preview)), "remarkably simplistic and outdated" ([3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)). A blue bar for the internal battery and a green one for vitality ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Caretaker)), a temperature shown only when the cold turns dangerous ([Update 6.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1846651706689776)). First person on foot ([Sidequesting](https://www.sidequesting.com/2025/05/the-last-caretaker-hands-on-preview)). One preview reports a third person view once the ship leaves port ([Punished Backlog](https://punishedbacklog.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)), not confirmed elsewhere.
- Recognisable, from these descriptions: a grey blue sea under weather, a rusted tower or platform on the horizon, cables lying across a deck, a small robot with an orange scarf.
- Against ours. Agreeing: decay and use everywhere, muted blue, brown and grey with one warm accent, light as the state cue, retro electronics as the clean world (our pod's kept interior is already a bank of amber and green screens, [booklet](art/booklet.md)). Differing: its rust is a human world's ruin the player finds, ours is what the factory does to an untouched planet. Its rendering is physical, ours is flat shaded with the realism in the lore and the numbers ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), Art direction). Its interface is what its reviewers fault, ours follows Techtonica's screens.

### The lone machine's tone

- Who: "a lone machine" ([Gert Lush](https://gertlushgaming.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-early-access-preview/)), "a sweet little robot with a big purpose" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-last-caretaker/reveal-new-survival-game)), one of many Caretakers "designed to restore and protect human life", most of them malfunctioned ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Caretaker)).
- What it needs: no food and no sleep ([Neon Lights guide](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/the-last-caretaker-beginner-guide-tips-tricks)). An internal battery of 500 Wh, up to 2500 Wh with upgrades, "constantly draining" and drawn by every action (a melee hit costs 5 Wh), charged "from any source of electricity" by a cable plugged into its chest. At zero its vitality falls, only a Heal Station restores vitality, and when none is left the game reloads a save ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Caretaker), [Neon Lights guide](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/the-last-caretaker-beginner-guide-tips-tricks)). "Everything you do requires power, flashlight, jumps, etc." ([Gert Lush](https://gertlushgaming.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-early-access-preview/)). A carried weight over 100 kg slows it ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Caretaker)). Since Update 6.0, "When temperatures fall far enough, the Caretaker slows down and begins taking damage", and heaters answer it ([Update 6.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1846651706689776)). Update 01 added a device that turns electricity into health ([Update 01](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1818752592135205)).

### The vessel as a base

- What: "your ship, which serves as your home base while at sea" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/previews/gamescom-the-last-caretaker)), "a large, upgradable freighter that doubles as your base of operations" ([3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)), 30 m and "intentionally left unnamed" ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Boat)). The game opens on a rundown boat in a dock: repair the diesel engine, fit engine cylinders, fuel and charge it by cable from the dock's tank and battery, sail away ([Neon Lights guide](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/the-last-caretaker-beginner-guide-tips-tricks), [Punished Backlog](https://punishedbacklog.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/), [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~games/1r4c/the_last_caretaker_early_access_launch_trailer)).
- What it holds: two engines, diesel and electric, each taking up to five cylinders whose grade sets the speed, both needed for full speed. A 2000 L diesel tank and a 6.0 kWh battery are built in, with 5 diesel outlets and 14 power outlets. The Recycler and the Fabricator sit in the hold, a Heal Station and a Backup Station midships, a fishing net, a water pump and a CO2 outlet at the stern, and the cab holds the wheel, throttle, anchor, map, paint table, the red phone and a terminal ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Boat), [Patch Crazy](https://patchcrazy.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-how-to-use-ship/), [Patch Crazy](https://patchcrazy.co.uk/how-to-use-engines-for-boats-in-the-last-caretaker/)). The Fabricator crafts from the storage around it, the Caretaker's inventory and items on the floor ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Fabricator)).
- How it grows: modules go "nearly anywhere on its surface, including the keel" and add batteries and tanks that can feed the engines without docking ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Boat)), generators, crafting stations and storage crates, of which "many of them may be required" ([Patch Crazy](https://patchcrazy.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-how-to-use-ship/), [3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)). Then better engine cylinders in three grades per fuel ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Vehicles)), a stabiliser and a mooring socket ([Update 03](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1827626365750132)), a sonar and paint ([Update 02](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1824459501603403)), and the first hull extension, the Gyro Deck, a landing deck for the gyrocopter ([Update 5.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1836506165545642)). The hull stays the same: what grows is what is bolted on and swapped. Space on board is the limit: a big battery "can require moving equipment or walkways" ([Whisper of the House](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets)). Whether placement snaps to a grid is not described: unverified.
- How it moves and docks: steered from the cab, "The ship moves quite slow" and docking takes "brief bursts of forward and reverse thrust" ([Roundtable Co-op](https://roundtablecoop.com/?p=11892)). Its top speed falls with its hull's damage ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Boat)). It anchors on a button and moors by cables to sockets, which players distrust: the lines "don't lock into place", and several only anchor ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1783560/discussions/0/841740442836975921/)). Travel is crafting time: "slow but intentionally so" ([3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)).
- Off the boat: crafting stations can stand on the boat or at a site ([Patch Crazy](https://patchcrazy.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-how-to-use-ship/)), and the Lazarus Complex is the fixed site for heavy projects such as powering the Transfer Gate ([Whisper of the House](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets)). Smaller vehicles join it, a forklift, a jet ski and a gyrocopter ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Vehicles)), and since Update 6.0 "You can even take a vehicle through" the gate to a new region ([Update 6.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1846651706689776)). What becomes of modules left at a site is not described: unverified.
- Against ours. Agreeing: a base that travels and is improved part by part, travel as working time, power and fuel brought to a site by the vessel. Differing, by decision: ours is a land vehicle built from scratch on every planet, beside a pod that stays ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), Building).

### Power and cables

- Sources: solar arrays in three tiers (LumiLite, LumiMid, LumiMax), working only in direct sunlight ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/LumiLite)) and "realistically affected by the sun and weather" ([Roundtable Co-op](https://roundtablecoop.com/?p=11892)). Wind generators in three (Whisper, Cyclone, Typhoon, the last two 1 kW and 2 kW by a community tools site, [tools](https://the-last-caretaker-tools.pages.dev/guides/power-grid-setup)). Petrol generators, the large one burning 6000 L an hour for 30 kW plus CO2 and idling once its batteries are full ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Large_Electric_Generator)). Found batteries. Turbines need upkeep, panels do not ([tools](https://the-last-caretaker-tools.pages.dev/guides/power-grid-setup)). One preview found its first solar and wind "generate almost nothing" ([Thumb Culture](https://www.thumbculture.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-pc-early-access-preview)).
- Stores: consumable batteries, a Small Battery (500 Wh or 2 kWh, sources disagree), a Medium Battery of 6 kWh, a Bulk Battery of 18, 20 or 30 kWh by source and update, a Battery Barrel of about 1 kWh ([tools](https://the-last-caretaker-tools.pages.dev/guides/power-grid-setup), [wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Bulk_Battery), [Whisper of the House](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets)).
- Consumers: doors, lights, terminals, the Fabricator (100 W idle), the boat's electric engine, heaters, the Transfer Gate, guns charged from the player, and the Caretaker itself ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/previews/the-last-caretaker/), [wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Fabricator), [Update 6.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1846651706689776)). "Power never appears out of thin air", and "your main limitation will be power" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-last-caretaker/reveal-new-survival-game)).
- A cable: an item in four kinds, electric, fuel, gas and water ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Cables)), a one metre item of copper and plastic by the tools site. The player takes an end and plugs it into a port, winds it back into the inventory by holding a button, and "Each section of cable is limited to a maximum of 20m before it has to be connected" ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Electric_Cable)). A mod raises all four kinds from 20 m to 2 km ([VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/games/the-last-caretaker/files/89330-dalji-kabli-ice-cevi-itd..html)). Cables "can wrap around the environment" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-last-caretaker/reveal-new-survival-game)): they are ropes. Each cable has a direction, switched on it or set to equalize between its ends ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Electric_Cable)). One set the wrong way leaves what is behind it unpowered ([tools](https://the-last-caretaker-tools.pages.dev/guides/power-grid-setup)), and the Fill and Equalize modes decide whether a big battery fills or levels down to the small ones ([Whisper of the House](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets)).
- Ports and junctions: a generator has one output port ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/LumiLite)). The Electric Cable Extender has four ports and a switch, mounts on any surface, joins generators into one line, splits a line to several consumers and holds a run on fixed points ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Electric_Cable_Extender)). Wall sockets pass a network through a wall, two to a circuit ([wiki](https://thelastcaretaker.wiki.gg/wiki/Electric_Wall_Socket)), and the PECO Grid Scan 2000 "shows how systems are connected" ([Update 5.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1783560/view/1836506165545642)). The player is a node too: a cable into the chest charges it ([Neon Lights guide](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/the-last-caretaker-beginner-guide-tips-tricks)), and it can "use yourself as a walking power station" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-last-caretaker/reveal-new-survival-game)).
- Capacity, shorts and overloads: no source describes any. A machine without power stops ([Whisper of the House](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets)). Unverified, possibly absent.
- Scale: an energy system "could incorporate dozens, if not hundreds, of parts" ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/reviews/the-last-caretaker-review/)). A base grows by more generators and batteries joined at extenders, and the guides advise building from the machine back to the source and changing one outlet at a time ([Whisper of the House](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets)).
- Fun and tedium: praised as depth, "I found a kind of bliss in the systems" ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/reviews/the-last-caretaker-review/)), "very hands-on and sometimes messy" but "solving an environmental puzzle" that gives the game "a distinct identity" ([3rd-Strike](https://3rd-strike.com/the-last-caretaker-preview/)). Faulted for handling: "a somewhat confusing control scheme (esp when it comes to managing power cables)", cables knocked under a bed frame ([Tildes](https://tildes.net/~games/1r4c/the_last_caretaker_early_access_launch_trailer)), "most difficult to functionally understand" ([Thumb Culture](https://www.thumbculture.co.uk/the-last-caretaker-pc-early-access-preview)), "It's a slow grind at first" ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/reviews/the-last-caretaker-review/)). The guides spend their length on battery banks that stall and networks broken by a rearrangement ([Whisper of the House](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets)), and the 2 km mod says what some players made of the 20 m reach.
- Against ours. Agreeing: power as the base's main limit, generators that answer to sun, wind and fuel, a view of the networks (our power overview). Differing: its scale is dozens to hundreds of parts, ours hundreds of machines. Its cables are simulated ropes, our simulation is lockstep. Its per cable direction is a chore the guides document, our networks balance themselves with a proportional brownout ([fluids.md](fluids.md), Power).

## Techtonica's controls and screens (2026-10-05)

The user's notes from playing Techtonica (2026-10-05): its early game is polished enough to copy, the screens, the ground and the placing above all. The items 0276 to 0283 carry what we take, each with its control design.

- Screens: one button opens a view with the inventory on the left half and the schematics on the right, since with a chest or a machine open one shuffles items and with the own inventory open one crafts; the schematics are a grid (not a list) walked by the d-pad or the left stick, the right stick cycles the tabs (production, logistics, base building, intermediates, equipment). On a schematic A crafts one, RB with A five, LB with A as many as can be afforded; B always leaves to the game, no modifier; Y assigns to the toolbar and held selects the toolbar (the edit mode); X clears the crafting queue. LT and RT step the window: schematics, journal, audio log, databank, tech tree (terraforming, synthesis, logistics, transportation, energy, science, construction), map, power network viewer with its usage history, and round. Our statistics fit that carousel.
- Toolbars: two at the start, shortcuts to what is in the inventory, not inventories. In the edit mode the d-pad moves between the rows and along them, and moving down past the lowest row shifts the rows like a carousel; A picks a shortcut up and drops it (on another, they swap and the new one is held), X clears the slot, X held clears the row, backing out with one held drops it.
- In the world: the d-pad alone walks the toolbars, not the bumpers; B toggles the toolbars and sheathes the held item (no use, no ghost); LT enters the deconstruct mode where RT picks machines up and LT leaves; X jumps and A interacts or gathers; L3 toggles sprint.
- The ground: the world is blocks, hidden by doodads and a blending of the textures at every boundary (moss next to an iron vein grows a little onto the iron), so the blocks are hard to see even when sculpted.
- Placing: machines go anywhere, no foundation is required, foundations make the grid neat.
