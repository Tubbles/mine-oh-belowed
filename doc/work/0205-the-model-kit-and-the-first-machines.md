### Answers to what the item left open

1. **The palette as named constants.** 0204's `palette.MATERIALS` already is DESIGN.md's palette table, keyed by name; a misspelt name is a `KeyError` at build time, so no second set of Python constants. Added (approved by the main agent, 2026-10-03): `timber` (122, 88, 56), `timber_dark` (82, 58, 38), `stone` (128, 126, 120) for the hand built pieces (wooden chest, crate, small pole, cutting table), which DESIGN.md's steel palette has no colour for, and `lamp_glow` (255, 236, 190, emissive) for a lamp's head, which is neither heat nor an electric indicator. DESIGN.md's Art direction palette clause gains them in the same commit.
2. **The seed rule.** `kit.model_random(machine, salt)` returns `random.Random(zlib.crc32(f"{machine.model}:{salt}".encode("ascii")))`. Never `hash()`, which Python salts per process. Each detail row draws from its own salt (`"left_vents"`), so editing one row leaves every other row's draws unchanged, and a script gives the same numbers in every run of one Blender version. The only draw is `random.uniform`.
3. **Openings keep the target's material.** 0204's furnace shows it: its mouth walls are exported in `smelting_brick` (60 brick faces, 16 more than the bevelled box), not in the cutter's `soot`. So `opening()` takes no material, and a dark inside is a separate box or disc (the furnace's soot plate, the drill's bore floor).
4. **Elbows.** The pipe is a POLY spline whose corners the kit replaces by three arc points it computes (start of bend, midpoint, end of bend). A Bézier spline with automatic handles would bend the straight runs too.
5. **Accents by role.** Chests, crate, splitter: logistics grey with yellow edges (the wooden chest and crate in timber with steel straps). Poles, lamp, switch, boiler, steam engine: power yellow with hazard black. Electric drill: mining ochre. Offshore pump, pump, storage tank, wood gasifier (its product leaves by pipe): fluids teal. Lab: science white with cyan. Assembler and every crafting machine (the production role, approved by the main agent, 2026-10-03): logistics grey with yellow edges and a cyan state strip; DESIGN.md's palette clause names it so 0206's assemblers follow it. The wood gasifier and the stone cutter are crafting machines by kind but keep the accent of what they visibly are (a fluids maker, a stone bench with a fire). Cutting table and stone cutter: stone and timber or steel, no accent.
6. **A scale cue on every machine larger than one cell**, read as more than one footprint cell, so the poles get one too (a meter box, climbing rungs). The 1 by 1 by 1 machines get none.
7. **Wires.** `wire_anchor` (render_power.odin) meets a pole on its axis `WIRE_DROP` (0.15) below the footprint's top and a switch at its middle, so each pole carries its insulator on the axis at that height and the switch body contains its centre. No side insulators: no wire would reach them.
8. **Smoke** leaves at the footprint's centre at the model's top (render_particles.odin:85), so the boiler's chimney stands near its centre and is its highest point.
9. **The tests the deleted .vox files break** (found by grep in 0207's tree): `test_an_obj_model_wins_over_a_voxel_model` and `test_the_shipped_chest_model_loads` embed `wooden_chest.vox`, `test_a_missing_model_or_part_file_is_refused` loads it, and `test_shipped_spinning_parts_turn_about_their_middle` reads `steam_engine_part.vox` and `assembler_1_part.vox`. Each is changed below. `data_watch_test.odin` names `models/wooden_chest.vox` as a path string only and stays.

### The kit (`tools/models/kit.py`)

Every primitive builds in kit.py's Blender frame (front +X, y = game -z, Z up, one unit per cell, footprint x ±width/2, y ±depth/2, z 0 to height), makes mesh objects with one material each through the existing `mesh_object` (or bmesh plus `mesh_object`), and returns the object, or a list for `rib_row` and `hatch`. A script extends its `volumes` list with them and ends with `kit.join(volumes, "body")` and, with a moving part, `kit.join_part(part_volumes, machine, pivot)` (pivot from `records.pivot(machine)` for a spin; omitted for a pump or bob). `join` already turns any evaluated object into a mesh and deletes boolean cutters. Faces: `face` is one of `"+X"`, `"-X"`, `"+Y"`, `"-Y"`, `"+Z"`; `centre` is a point on that face's plane; `width` runs along the face's first in-plane axis and `height` along the second: Y then Z for ±X, X then Z for ±Y, X then Y for +Z. Triangle costs below are after the exporter's triangulation; they match 0204's furnace (340 counted, 324 from these formulas plus 16 for its mouth).

| Primitive | Signature | Builds | Triangles |

| seed | `model_random(machine, salt)` | answer 2 | 0 |

| wedge | `wedge(minimum, maximum, material_name, rise="+X")` | a right triangular prism in the box: full height on the `rise` side (`+X`, `-X`, `+Y`, `-Y`), zero on the opposite one. bmesh: 6 vertices, faces bottom, high side, slope and two triangles, `recalc_face_normals` | 8 |
| ring | `ring(centre, radius, thickness, material_name, axis="Z", sides=8, minor_sides=4)` | a torus in bmesh: `sides` around `axis` through the 3-tuple `centre`, a tube of radius `thickness` with `minor_sides`, the major vertices at angles `i * 2π / sides` from 0 and the tube's at `(j + 0.5) * 2π / minor_sides` (a flat faces outward); quads. Flanges and valve wheels; bolt heads stay boxes | 2 × sides × minor_sides (8 by 3: 48, 12 by 3: 72, 8 by 4: 64) |
| pipe | `pipe(points, radius, material_name, sides=8, bend=None)` | `bend` defaults to `2 * radius`. Every interior corner is replaced by three arc points (answer 4): with unit directions `a` in and `b` out and angle θ between them, the tangent length is `t = bend * tan(θ / 2)`, the points are `P - a t`, `C + normalize(P - C) * bend` with `C = P + normalize(b - a) * bend / cos(θ / 2)`, and `P + b t`; a straight run shorter than the tangents at its ends is a `SystemExit` naming the run. A `bpy.data.curves` curve (`'CURVE'`, `dimensions='3D'`, one `'POLY'` spline), `bevel_depth = radius`, `bevel_resolution = sides // 4 - 1` (sides 8 or 12, else `SystemExit`), `use_fill_caps = True`, linked to the scene; then `bpy.data.meshes.new_from_object(curve_object.evaluated_get(depsgraph))`, its materials replaced by `material(material_name)`, the kit asserts the mesh has `sides * len(arc points)` vertices (so a different Blender bevel count fails loudly, and the implementer reports it), and the curve object and data are removed. Returns a mesh object | 2 × sides × segments + 2 × (sides - 2), with segments = points - 1 + 2 × corners (an L of three points at 8 sides: 76) |
| hatch | `hatch(face, centre, width, height, material_name, handle_material="galvanised", thickness=0.03)` | a plate box `width` by `height` from the face plane outward by `thickness`, bevel 0.01, and a handle bar box along the width axis, half the width long, 0.03 by 0.03, standing 0.02 off the plate, centred a quarter height above the plate's centre | 44 + 12 = 56 |
| rib row | `rib_row(axis, start, end, count, thickness, minimum, maximum, material_name, random, jitter=0.35)` | `count` boxes along `axis` (`"X"`, `"Y"`, `"Z"`): spacing `(end - start) / count`, rib i centred at `start + (i + 0.5) * spacing + random.uniform(-jitter, jitter) * spacing / 2`, `thickness` along the axis; `minimum` and `maximum` give the other two axes (their `axis` component is ignored). `SystemExit` when `jitter` is outside 0 to 1 or `thickness > spacing * (1 - jitter)` (ribs could touch) | 12 × count |
| strip | `strip(face, centre, width, height, material_name, depth=0.02)` | a box `width` by `height` from `depth` inside the face plane to 0.005 outside it, so it reads as an inset plate; emissive material for indicators | 12 |
| opening | `opening(target, minimum, maximum)` | `cut(target, box(minimum, maximum, "soot"))`; the walls take the target's material (answer 3). Round bores use `cut` with a `cylinder` as 0204's drill does | about +16 for a box through one face, about +50 for a 12-sided blind bore |

### The machines

The script starts with `kit.expect_footprint(machine, ...)`. Port centres come from `records.port_face(machine, records.port(machine, name))` and pivots from `records.pivot(machine)`, never typed; the values quoted below are what those return for the shipped records, for checking. Counts are estimates from the table; the workbench's check is the bound. If a coordinate below fails the fit or the sweep check, the implementer moves that volume inward or apart, keeps the silhouette, and names the change. Each script's docstring states the footprint, the silhouette line and the part, as 0204's do.
