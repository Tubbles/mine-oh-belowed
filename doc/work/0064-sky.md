# 0064 Sky and atmosphere

Status: todo
Milestone: M11

## Goal

A flat sky colour changes with the day. Sun and moon, stars, horizon haze and distance fog tied to the load radius so the world edge never shows.

## Deliverables

- Sun and moon discs on the day path, stars at night, a horizon gradient, dusk and dawn colours, distance fog that hides the load boundary.
- Sun direction feeds the day light tint already computed by the day cycle.
- Tests: the sun position per day tick, fog distance from the load radius.

## Verify

- Builds and tests pass.
- User: Screenshots at dawn, noon, dusk, night.

## Notes

Order decision (main agent, 2026-09-28): this item lands before 0063 weather, since weather modulates the fog, the sky colours and the light tint this item defines.

Implementation pointers, decisions taken so the item is unambiguous:

- Time of day stays the one input: `daylight_blend` and the day fraction in `src/render_day.odin`. New pure procedures there: `sun_direction(fraction) -> [3]f32` (east is +x, the sun rises at fraction 0, stands highest at 0.25 towards +y, sets at 0.5 towards -x; the path tilts 20 degrees towards +z so the sun never passes straight overhead), `moon_direction` (opposite the sun), and a `Sky_Colors {zenith, horizon, fog, sun_tint}` per fraction: day zenith the current `DAY_SKY_COLOR`, day horizon a paler blue, night zenith the current night colour with a slightly lighter horizon, and a dawn and dusk band (warm orange horizon, the zenith still blue) over the twilight window; `fog` is the horizon colour, so chunks fade into the sky at the horizon; `sun_tint` is white by day, warm at dawn and dusk, blue grey at night, and multiplies the sky light term (below).
- Sky pass, in a new `src/render_sky.odin`, drawn inside `BeginMode3D` before the chunks (`draw_session_world` in `src/loop.odin`), centred on the camera and with the depth test and depth writes off (`rlgl.DisableDepthTest`, `rlgl.DisableDepthMask`, the batch flushed around it like the item billboards), so it sits behind everything: a sky dome (a hemisphere mesh of 24 by 8 segments, built once, vertex colours from `Sky_Colors` blended zenith to horizon and rewritten each frame with `rl.UpdateMeshBuffer`, no texture), then stars (a fixed set of 500 points from a hash, on the dome, turning with the day about the sun path's axis, drawn as small quads with alpha from the night share of the blend), then the sun and the moon as camera facing discs (`rl.DrawBillboard` of a small generated disc texture, radius about 0.04 of the dome) in their directions; the moon shows a phase from the day count (a second disc in the sky colour overlapping it). The clear colour becomes the zenith colour. Nothing here reads the simulation except the tick and the day length it already gets.
- Fog from the load radius: `FOG_START` and `FOG_END` (`src/render_chunks.odin`) become derived from `LOAD_RADIUS_HORIZONTAL` (`src/world_streaming.odin`): the end one chunk inside the load radius in blocks, the start at 60 percent of the end, so the world edge never shows whatever the radius; a pure `fog_distances(load_radius_chunks) -> (start, end)` with a test. The fog colour uniform takes `Sky_Colors.fog`.
- Light tint: `data/shaders/chunk.fs` gains `uniform vec3 sky_tint` multiplying the sky light term only (block light stays as it is, torches glow the same at night), set per frame from `sun_tint` in `apply_daylight`. Models: `Model_Frame` gains `sky_tint` and `draw_model_layers` tints the lit layer by it (a colour, not a grey), with the split between sky and block light already in `model_light_tint` (`src/model_motion.odin`): tint the sky share, not the block share.
- Tests (`src/render_sky_test.odin`, `src/render_day_test.odin` if none exists): the sun direction at fractions 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 (east, up, west, down) and the moon opposite; the tilt keeps the zenith pass off vertical; sky colours at noon, midnight and the middle of dusk match the constants and the dusk horizon is warmer than the zenith; fog distances from a load radius; the star set is the same on every call and lies on the dome; the mesh builder gives the expected vertex count and a closed hemisphere. No test calls raylib: keep the geometry and colour code apart from the upload and draw calls.
