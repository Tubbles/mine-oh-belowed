# 0220: The third person camera pulled in by the field

Status: verified (2026-10-05, implementer agent a46f7444c33c45a1b, verified the same day with no fix round (one low finding kept as is: a tall trunk rooted beyond the tree query's reach is not cast, the tick's query has the same limit), in `.claude/worktrees/0220` on `item/0220` from `main` at 957a01d, the specification approved the same day with the decisions below; 2026-10-04, found by the 0218 design: the field's third person camera sits behind the eye at the settings' distance and is never pulled in by the terrain, so in a tunnel it looks from inside the rock; the block world's camera is pulled in by the blocks (`third_person_position`); after 0218)

## Goal

The third person camera on the field never sits inside the ground, a frame or a tree: it is pulled in along its line from the eye to the first surface it would cross, with a margin for the near plane, as the block world's camera is pulled in by the blocks ([doc/architecture.md](../architecture.md), The player on the field: "the third person camera sits behind the eye and is not yet pulled in by the field").

## Change

- `field_third_person_camera` (or the procedure `render_field_camera.odin` names for it) casts from the eye back along the camera's offset with `raycast_field` and the frames' cast, and stops the camera `THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN` short of the first hit; a hit nearer than the margin puts the camera at the eye (first person for that frame).
- Presentation only: the cast reads the field and the frames, never writes, and runs per viewport per frame.
- A crouched player (0218) is cast from the crouched eye.

## Verify

- Tests: a camera behind a player standing with a wall at 2 m behind them sits 2 m minus the margin away; in a 1 m tunnel (the 0218 tunnel terrain) the camera stays inside the tunnel; on open ground it sits at the settings' distance.
- The couch: third person in the dug tunnel, the camera never shows the inside of the rock.

### The procedures

All in `src/render_field_camera.odin` (presentation cluster; it may reference world and simulation, so the new edges into `world_frame_collision.odin` and `field_trees.odin` are allowed). Presentation only: nothing writes the field, the frames, the trees or the players.

1. `field_third_person_offset :: proc(view: Field_Camera_View, distance, shoulder: f32) -> [3]f32`: the offset from the eye in metres, `-look * distance + up * THIRD_PERSON_HEIGHT + right * shoulder`, the three lines `field_camera` has today moved out (up, look and right computed as there). `field_camera` keeps its signature and calls it in its `.Third_Person` branch (`position += field_third_person_offset(view, distance, shoulder)`), so its result is unchanged bit for bit and the existing tests and callers stay.
2. `field_camera_obstacle :: proc(world: ^Field_World, frames: ^Frame_Table, trunks: []Field_Capsule, spacing_millimetres: int, origin: World_Position, direction: [3]i64, reach: i64) -> (distance: i64, normal: [3]i64, hit: bool)`: the nearest surface along the unit direction within reach: `field_solid_raycast` (the field, the solid frame cells and the bodies, nearer wins), then each trunk with `field_trunk_ray_distance(trunk, origin, direction, reach)`, a nearer trunk taking the hit with `field_capsule_normal_at(trunk, field_ray_point(origin, direction, along))`. Called only by `field_third_person_position`.
3. `field_capsule_normal_at :: proc(capsule: Field_Capsule, point: World_Position) -> [3]i64`: the unit vector from the nearest point of the capsule's axis to the point (`along` clamped to `0 ..= length` as `field_distance_to_capsule_axis` does, `normalize_fixed` of `point - bottom - fixed_scale(up, along)`, zero when the point is on the axis). Called by `field_camera_obstacle`.
4. `field_camera_pulled_distance :: proc(distance, cosine: f32) -> f32`: metres along the ray where the camera stops: `distance - THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN / cosine`, or 0 (the eye) when `cosine * distance <= THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN` (the margin does not fit, which also covers a ray that starts in the ground or a solid cell, distance 0, and a normal facing away, cosine at or below 0). `cosine` is the cosine between the ray and the surface (`-dot(direction, normal)`).
5. `field_third_person_position :: proc(world: ^Field_World, frames: ^Frame_Table, trunks: []Field_Capsule, spacing_millimetres: int, eye: World_Position, offset: [3]f32) -> [3]f32`: the pure procedure, the field's `third_person_position`. The offset goes to position units (`i64(offset.x * POSITION_UNITS_PER_METRE)` per axis), `direction, _ := normalize_fixed(units)`, `reach := vector_length(units)`, then `field_camera_obstacle`. No hit: `world_position_to_metres(eye) + offset` (exactly `field_camera`'s third person position). A hit: `world_position_to_metres(eye) + unit_vector_to_f32(direction) * field_camera_pulled_distance(f32(distance) / POSITION_UNITS_PER_METRE, -f32(fixed_dot(direction, normal)) / UNIT_VECTOR_ONE)`. It takes `spacing_millimetres` rather than the tuning because that is the one value of the tuning it reads and the ray procedures take it the same way.
6. `pulled_in_field_camera :: proc(simulation: ^Simulation_State, content: Field_Content, view: Field_Camera_View, mode: Camera_Mode, distance, shoulder, field_of_view: f32) -> rl.Camera3D`: `field_camera(view, mode, distance, shoulder, field_of_view)`; for `.First_Person` returned as is. For `.Third_Person`: the trunks round the eye, `field_tree_trunks(field_trees_near(&simulation.field, view.eye, reach), content)` with `reach := i64((distance + THIRD_PERSON_HEIGHT + abs(shoulder)) * POSITION_UNITS_PER_METRE) + millimetres_to_position_units(FIELD_TREE_QUERY_MARGIN_MILLIMETRES)` (the offset's length is at most the sum, the margin is the tick's own for the widest trunk; both lists are temp allocated, freed by the frame's `free_all`); `position := field_third_person_position(&simulation.field.world, &simulation.world.entities.frames, trunks, simulation.field.spacing_millimetres, view.eye, field_third_person_offset(view, distance, shoulder))`; then `camera.target += position - camera.position` and `camera.position = position`, so the look is `field_camera`'s.

Each procedure gets a comment in the file's style. The file's header comment loses "The third person camera is not yet pulled in by the field (the block camera's third_person_position)." and gains: "The third person camera is pulled in along its line from the eye by the field, the solid frame cells and volumes and the trunks, kept THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN off the surface it meets (pulled_in_field_camera, 0220)." The comment on `THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN` (`src/render_player.odin`) becomes: "Kept between the pulled in camera and the block that blocked it, or on the field the surface (measured along the surface's normal, 0220), so the near plane does not clip into it." Its value stays 0.2.

### The call sites

- `field_viewport_camera` (`src/loop_field_session.odin`): `field_camera(view, ...)` becomes `pulled_in_field_camera(&state.session.simulation, state.session.field_content, view, player.field.camera_mode, state.settings.third_person_distance, state.settings.third_person_shoulder, state.settings.field_of_view)`. It runs per viewport per frame (`draw_field_viewport_world` calls it for every viewport, split screen included), before `arrival_viewport_camera`, which replaces the camera during the descent and shakes the pulled in one after the hit. `viewport.presentation.camera` stores the pulled in camera, so the HUD's projections and the touch aim read the camera that drew.
- `planet_preview_raylib_camera` (`src/loop_planet_preview.odin`): the same replacement with `&preview.session.simulation`, `preview.session.field_content`, `THIRD_PERSON_DISTANCE`, `0` and `PLANET_PREVIEW_FIELD_OF_VIEW`, so the preview's walk (V) shows what the game plays.

### Decisions

- The cast: the field's ray, the frames' cells and bodies, and the trunks. The frames are cast through `field_solid_raycast` (cells with `Solid`, the bodies of 0230), the filter the walk collides with, not the aim's (`excluded = {.Open}`): belts and splitters occupy whole cells without `Solid` (`machine_occupant_flags`) and would pull the camera in at the air over a thin belt; the pod's open cells pass as the player walks through them. The cost: the top row of an open hatch (no `Solid`, no `Open`) does not stop the camera, so it can sit in the open hatch's panel; accepted. A ray against trunks exists (`field_trunk_ray_distance`, the aim's, 50 mm steps, so its hit is up to 50 mm inside the trunk, which the 200 mm margin covers). The canopies are left out: the simulation has no solid canopy (the player walks through it), so the camera may sit among leaves as the player's head does.
- The margin, `THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN` (0.2 m), measured along the hit surface's normal, not along the ray. Along the ray (the item's literal text and the block world's rule) leaves the camera 4 to 6 cm under the roof of the 0218 tunnel at every spacing, measured with the prototype (a crouched player 7.8 m into `TEST_ONE_METRE_TUNNEL`, settings distance 4, shoulder 0.6: the ray meets the roof at a grazing angle, cosine about 0.18). The field's near plane is `FIELD_NEAR_METRES` (0.1 m, `loop_field_session.odin`), whose top edge reaches 0.07 m above the camera at the default 70 degree field of view (0.1 times tan 35 degrees), so a camera 4 cm under the roof would show the inside of the rock along the top of the screen. Along the normal the camera stays at least 0.2 m from the surface it met: in the same tunnel it sits 0.54 to 0.56 m from the eye and 0.200 to 0.209 m under the roof. A wall faced square on gives the same result as along the ray (2 m minus 0.2 m). 0.2 m covers the near plane's half height (0.07 m at 70 degrees, 0.143 m at 110 degrees, the setting's top) and its half width at 16:9 up to the default (0.124 m); at 110 degrees the half width (0.25 m) can still reach a side wall the ray did not meet (below).
- A hit nearer than the margin allows (`cosine * distance <= margin`) puts the camera at the eye: first person for that frame, as the block world's `max(hit.distance - margin, 0)`. No easing in or out: the camera follows the obstacle frame by frame, as the block world's does.
- The crouched eye: the cast starts from `view.eye`, the interpolated eye at the eased crouch progress the frame draws (`field_player_view` with `field_crouch_progress_of`), so a crouched player is cast from the crouched eye with no extra code. The pull in runs after the interpolation, on the eye the frame draws; the field and the frames are the simulation's, the state the renderer draws; the eye is the predicted player's (`lockstep_view_player`).
- Floats and fixed point: the existing camera turns the integer view into f32 metres at the end (`world_position_to_metres`, `unit_vector_to_f32`); the pull in follows: the casts are the integer ray procedures, the margin and the final position f32 metres. Presentation only, never read by the tick.
- A ray, not a sphere: a surface beside the line (a tube's side wall when the shoulder points along it) can still come within the near plane's half width at a high field of view; not handled, named in the log.
- The body: in third person the viewer's body is still drawn (`draw_field_players`) when the camera reaches the eye, as in the block world; not changed (question 2 below).

### Tests

In `src/render_field_camera_test.odin` (it gains `import "core:math"` for a `[3]f32` distance helper, `f32_distance :: proc(first, second: [3]f32) -> f32`, test only). Tolerance `PULL_IN_TEST_TOLERANCE_METRES :: 0.02` (the prototype's results were well inside it).

1. `test_the_field_third_person_camera_stops_short_of_a_wall`: per spacing of `TEST_FIELD_SPACINGS` (333, 500, 1000), `Test_Terrain{kind = .Ledge, ledge_height = metres_to_position_units(4)}` (a 4 m face at `TEST_LEDGE_FACE_METRES`, 2 m along +x); eye `test_site_point(0, 1.6 m, 0)`, offset `{4, 0, 0}`, nil frames and trunks: the camera is `2 - THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN` metres from the eye within the tolerance. Same spacing: an eye 0.1 m short of the face (x = 1.9 m) gives exactly `world_position_to_metres(eye)` (the hit nearer than the margin).
2. `test_the_field_third_person_camera_stays_inside_a_one_metre_tunnel`: per spacing, `TEST_ONE_METRE_TUNNEL`; `start_crouch_test_player`, then `run_field_player(..., FIELD_SNEAK_FORWARD, 360)`; asserts the player is crouching and past x = 5 m (so the ray meets the roof, not the open mouth at 2 m); `view := field_player_view(player, tuning, 1, 1)`, offset `field_third_person_offset(view, THIRD_PERSON_DISTANCE, 0.6)`. Asserts: the camera is closer than the full offset's length less the margin (pulled in); `raycast_field` from the eye along the offset's direction to the camera's distance does not hit (no rock between); the camera's point is not ground (`field_position_is_ground`); its x is past the face and its height under the roof's surface (`ledge_height + sample_axis_to_position(1, spacing) / 2`, the midpoint of the last air sample and the first ground sample) by at least `THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN - PULL_IN_TEST_TOLERANCE_METRES` (the normal margin).
3. `test_the_field_third_person_camera_keeps_its_distance_on_open_ground`: per spacing, `.Flat`, a player settled 30 ticks at the site facing +x, `view := field_player_view(player, tuning, 1, 0)`; for the distances `THIRD_PERSON_DISTANCE_RANGE.minimum`, `THIRD_PERSON_DISTANCE` and `THIRD_PERSON_DISTANCE_RANGE.maximum` (2, 4, 8) with shoulder 0.6: `field_third_person_position` equals `field_camera(view, .Third_Person, distance, 0.6, 70).position` exactly (`expect_value`).
4. `test_the_field_third_person_camera_stops_short_of_a_foundation`: `make_test_frame_table(0)` (solid cells (0,0,0) and (2,0,0), 500 mm pitch) plus cell (-2,0,0) occupied as a belt is (`flags = {.Blocks_Water}`, no `Solid`); an empty `Field_World` (reads air); eye `frame_cell_centre(frame, {-4, 0, 0})`, offset `unit_vector_to_f32(frame.axes[FRAME_RIGHT]) * 4`: the camera passes the belt's cell and is `1.75 - THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN` metres from the eye (cell 0's near face) within the tolerance.
5. `test_the_field_third_person_camera_stops_short_of_a_trunk`: an empty `Field_World`, nil frames, one `Field_Capsule{bottom = test_site_point(2 m, 0, 0), up = +y, length = 6 m, radius = 0.3 m}`; eye at 1.6 m over the site, offset `{4, 0, 0}`: the camera is `2 - 0.3 - THIRD_PERSON_WALL_MARGIN` metres from the eye within `FIELD_TREE_AIM_STEP_MILLIMETRES` (the trunk ray's step) plus 2 mm.

No test of `pulled_in_field_camera` itself: a `Simulation_State` with a tree generation is not built in tests, and the procedure is the composition of the tested parts; the couch check covers it.
