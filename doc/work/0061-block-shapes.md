# 0061 Blocks that are not cubes

Status: implemented
Milestone: M11

## Goal

Torches, slabs, stairs, pipes and poles are full cubes. Shapes for what should be thin or partial, with their own collision and light.

## Deliverables

- A shape per block in data: cube, slab, stairs, post (torches, poles), pipe; meshes from the model pipeline (0055) or built in.
- Torches with a flame quad, flicker, and their light; slabs and stairs for factory floors placed with rotation; pipes and poles drawn thin with connections to neighbours.
- Collision and raycasting respect shapes; outcrops get an ore texture and spent rock a cracked look.
- Tests: shape collision boxes, connection resolution for pipes and poles.
- Moved here from 0058: ground cover decoration per biome (grass tufts, flowers, dead wood), which needs blocks that are not cubes.

## Verify

- Builds and tests pass.
- User: Screenshots of a floor with stairs and a pipe run.

## Notes

Scope decisions (main agent, 2026-09-28): the ore texture on outcrops and the cracked spent rock came with 0060's texture files, so they are done. Ground cover decoration (moved here from 0058) becomes its own item, 0082, built on this item's cross shape once it exists. Pipes and poles are entities, not blocks: the pole already has a model (0056) and the pipe keeps its box drawing with thinner constants here. This item delivers the block shapes.

Implementation pointers, decisions taken so the item is unambiguous:

- Shape in data: `Block_Definition` (`src/world_block.odin`) gains `shape` (`cube` the default, `slab`, `stairs`, `post`, `cross`) and the loader expands oriented shapes into variants: a `slab` block `<id>` (bottom half) gets `<id>_upper` (upper half), a `stairs` block gets `<id>_r1`, `<id>_r2`, `<id>_r3` for the three other quarter turns (rotation 0 rises towards +x like every rotation in the game, `belt_direction_offset`). Variants copy the definition with the orientation set, are not `discoverable` on their own, share the `name_key`, and drop the base block's item (`block_drop`); the save's block remap by name handles them like any block. Document the expansion in `data/blocks.sjson`'s header. `Block_Definition` also gets `orientation` after resolution (`Block_Orientation {rotation: u8, upper: bool}`). New blocks in data: `stone_slab` and `stone_stairs` (stone texture), `concrete_slab` and `concrete_stairs` (concrete), `plank_slab` and `plank_stairs` (a plank texture file, wood coloured), each with items and recipes (a slab: 1 block gives 2 slabs, stairs: 3 blocks give 4, hand and assembler, `materials` category, `start` channel like `plank`) and strings; `torch` becomes `shape = "post"`. The `cross` shape has no block yet (0082 adds them) but is drawn and tested with a test only block.
- Geometry, in a new `src/block_shape.odin` (pure, no raylib): per shape and orientation the collision boxes (`block_collision_boxes(registry, block) -> []Box` in a fixed small array: cube one box, slab a half box, stairs a half slab plus a quarter box at the back, post and cross none), the union bounds for the target outline, and the mesh quads (`shape_quads`: local corners, face direction and the tile group per quad; a post is a 2 by 2 texel wide column from the floor to 10 texels up, a cross is two diagonal full quads drawn both sides). `block_is_solid` stays the movement rule for cubes; a slab and stairs are solid for water and light purposes but collide with their boxes; post and cross are not solid.
- Mesher (`src/world_mesh.odin`): a shaped block never joins the greedy pass as a cube. `face_is_visible` treats a shaped neighbour as not solid (the cube face behind a slab shows, the slab covers half of it), and a shaped cell's own faces come from `shape_quads` in a second pass per chunk (`mesh_shaped_cells`) that appends each quad with the block's tile origin and the vertex light of the cell itself (`vertex_light` at the cell's corners), so the greedy rectangles and their tests are untouched. Texcoords of a partial quad cover the part of the tile it shows (a slab side shows the lower half).
- Collision (`src/player_collision.odin`): `layer_has_solid` and the sweep become box based: the cells a layer overlaps contribute their collision boxes, and `sweep_box_axis` moves up to the nearest box face along the axis (a slab stops the player half way, stairs are walked up by the existing step logic if there is one, else the player jumps them, which is fine for now). `box_has_ground` uses the boxes too, so a player stands on a slab at half height. Entities keep the whole cell rule (`cell_blocks_movement`).
- Raycast (`src/world_raycast.odin`): a targetable shaped cell is hit only where the ray meets one of its collision boxes (a slab-and-box intersection inside the cell, the entered face from that box), else the ray continues; post and cross use their bounds box for targeting so a torch can still be mined. `draw_target_outline` (`src/render_player.odin`) outlines the shape's bounds.
- Placement (`src/player_interaction.odin`, `place_block_with_player`): an item that places a slab picks the upper variant when the hit face is the underside of a block or the hit point lies in the upper half of a side face, else the bottom one; a stairs item picks the rotation facing away from the player (`yaw_direction`), and Rotate_Building turns the held stairs' rotation like a machine ghost; the HUD's block target outline previews nothing new. Placement validity stays as it is.
- Light (`src/world_light.odin`, `block_is_opaque`): only cubes are opaque; slabs, stairs, posts and crosses let light through, so a torch on a slab floor lights the room. Water (`src/world_water.odin`) already leaves non air blocks alone.
- Torch flame: the mesher collects the cells of `post` shaped blocks with a light level into `Chunk_Mesh_Data.flames` (positions), the chunk renderer keeps them per chunk, and `draw_torch_flames` (`src/render_chunks.odin` or a new `src/render_flames.odin`) draws a small camera facing quad of a flame colour above each post, its size and brightness flickering from a hash of the cell and the render time (no simulation state), after the chunks with depth writes off like item billboards.
- Pipes (`src/render_fluids.odin`): `PIPE_CORE_SIZE` and `PIPE_STUB_SIZE` become thinner (about 0.3 and 0.22) so a pipe run reads as a pipe; nothing else changes there.
- Tests (`src/block_shape_test.odin`, plus the existing collision, raycast, mesh and placement tests): collision boxes per shape and orientation, bounds, quads face outward with the right tile groups, variant expansion (ids, orientation, drop, the shared name key), a sweep that stops on a slab at half height and stands on it, a ray through the empty upper half of a bottom slab hits the block behind, a ray hits a torch's post bounds, the mesher emits the slab's quads and the cube face behind it, slab placement picks upper and lower from the hit, stairs placement picks the rotation, light passes a slab, and the UI audit keeps passing.

Follow ups (2026-09-28): the upper slab variant is `<id>_upper`, not `<id>_top`, since `<id>_top.png` is the listed block's top face texture and variant ids never take a face group suffix. A post's target box (and outline) is 8 texels wide, half a block, centred and the drawn height, while the post is drawn 2 texels wide and has no collision.

Known limits (outside this item): a machine placed on a bottom slab floats half a block, because entity placement reads `block_is_solid` and treats the slab's cell as a full block. The ±x faces of the greedy mesher ran texcoord x along world y (pre-existing), so side textures with a direction (the grass fringe) showed rotated on those faces; shaped quads used the same mapping so they matched their neighbours. Resolved by work item 0083: every side face, cube or shaped, now has its tile upright.
