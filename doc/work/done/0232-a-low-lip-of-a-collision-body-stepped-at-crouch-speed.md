# 0232: A low lip of a collision body stepped onto at crouch speed

Status: landed (2026-10-04, "Step a low lip of a collision body at crouch speed (0232)", 808c94f, installed the same day; implementer agent ac9fde58309606566, verified the same day with no fix round (two measured settle changes on frames noted in the log and 0259), in `.claude/worktrees/0232` on `item/0232` from `main` at 87dfb1e, the specification approved the same day with the decisions below; from the 0230 verifier's re-check of the fix round: the steep contact step rule (`field_landing_held_by_steep_contact`, `player_field.odin`) is reached only for a lip lower than a quarter of the terrain's sample spacing, such as the pod's 0.175 m heat shield rim or a stair, and at crouch speed the edge contact there gets steeper than walkable, so the rule may refuse a step the walk should take; no test covers that case, the fix round's lip test used a lip of the full step height, which the ledge path climbs before the rule runs; after 0230)

## Goal

A player crouching onto a low lip of a machine's collision body (a rim, a stair tread, a threshold under a quarter of the sample spacing) steps onto it as a walking player does, and the steep contact rule of 0230 refuses only what it was made for: a landing on a slope steeper than walkable, such as the pod's hull cone.

## Controls

No binding changes.

## Change

- A test in `player_field_volume_test.odin`: a box of a quarter of the step height (under spacing/4 at 333, 500 and 1000 mm) walked onto at crouch speed and at walking speed, the feet landing on its top within `FIELD_GROUND_TOLERANCE` and the walk continuing; and the same box at the pod's rim height (0.175 m) at 1000 mm.
- If the rule refuses the crouched step: the contact read at the bottom sphere's centre is the nearest volume, which on an edge is the lip's top face only once the sphere is far enough over it; the design stage chooses between reading the contact a little ahead of the centre along the walk, or accepting a contact steeper than walkable when the ray under the centre finds the landing within the land tolerance (the lip's own top), and says what each does to the hull cone, which must stay refused (test 8 of 0230).
- The verifier's other note goes with it: the probe returns the nearest volume, which may be a wall beside the capsule rather than the surface under it, so a step beside a wall may be refused that was hanging anyway; the design stage says whether the contact should be the nearest volume below the centre instead.

## Specification (design, 2026-10-04)

### What the current rule does, worked by hand

Units: the capsule radius r is 300 mm (1228 position units), `FIELD_GROUND_TOLERANCE` 15.6 mm (64 units), the land tolerance spacing/16 (20.8, 31.25 and 62.5 mm at 333, 500 and 1000 mm), the leave tolerance spacing/4 (83.3, 125 and 250 mm), one tick's sneak 21.7 mm (88.75 units) and walk 71.7 mm (293.5 units), the walkable cosine 0.5. The numbers below are the continuous geometry with `drop_field_capsule`'s stop modelled step by step (it drops by the gap, at most half a spacing, until the gap is at most 15.6 mm); the implementer's runs are the fixed point truth.

`field_landing_held_by_steep_contact` refuses only when three things hold at once: the bottom sphere touches a volume whose contact normal is steeper than walkable, the ground under its centre is walkable and on (within the leave tolerance), and the feet hang more than the land tolerance over that ground. A landing whose centre is over the lip's top is never refused: the ray under the centre finds the top, so `under.below` is within the drop's tolerance. That is option (b) of the item, and it is the current rule already. The refusal happens only while the centre is still short of the edge, the sphere resting on the edge with its centre a horizontal d before it and s above the top: the contact's cosine is s over r, steep when s < r/2, and the feet hang h + s - r over the floor. Both together need a lip higher than (r - tolerance)/2 + spacing/16: 205, 173 and 163 mm at 1000, 500 and 333 mm.

The walk's step against a lip of height h: the plain walk stops where the corner pushes back (for h < r/2 = 150 mm the corner's contact is walkable and the walk simply rides up, no step), at d0 = sqrt(r² - (r - h)²); the step sweeps the raised capsule one tick's walk ahead, to d1 = d0 less a tick, and drops it onto the edge.

| spacing | lip | speed | d1 | s at the drop's stop | contact cosine | feet over the floor | today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 333 | 83 mm (spacing/4) | both | no step | | 0.72 at the floor | | walked up plainly |
| 500 | 125 mm (spacing/4) | both | no step | | 0.58 at the floor | | walked up plainly |
| 1000 | 250 mm (spacing/4) | crouch | 274 mm | 150 mm | 0.48, steep | 100 mm, over 62.5, under 250 | refused by the rule |
| 1000 | 250 mm | walk | 224 mm | 216 mm | 0.69 | 166 mm | taken |
| 1000 | 175 mm (the rim) | crouch | 251 mm | 178 mm | 0.58 | 53 mm | taken |
| 1000 | 175 mm | walk | 201 mm | about 223 mm | 0.74 | 98 mm | taken |

So the item's suspicion holds for the spacing/4 lip at 1000 mm: the crouched step is refused (the walking one is taken), and the walk then reaches the lip only through the ledge (`find_field_ledge` on the blocked walk), whose stride of r plus a sample (1.3 m) lurches the player forward and overshoots a tread shorter than that, so a crouched player stops at a 250 mm stair tread at 1000 mm. The cosine margin there is thin (0.48 against 0.50), so the implementer runs the new tests against the unchanged rule first and reports which cases fail. The pod's rim height (175 mm) at 1000 mm is already taken: its edge contact is walkable and the hang is under the land tolerance anyway.

The lip of the step height of 0230's fix round (`test_a_lip_of_the_step_height_is_stepped_onto_at_crouch_speed`) never reaches the rule: the step's landing hangs 145, about 330 and about 830 mm over the floor at 333, 500 and 1000 mm, beyond the leave tolerance, so `!ground.on` refuses it and the ledge climbs it. Its comment says the steep rule keeps it walkable; that is corrected below.

The pod's real rim is narrower than the capsule: the ring's top runs from radius 5.0 to 5.45 cells (225 mm on the 500 mm frame) and the hull wall rises at 5.0, so the bottom sphere's centre stops at 5.6 cells, 75 mm outside the ring's edge, resting on that edge with s = 290 mm (cosine 0.97, walkable) and the feet 165 mm over the floor. The player stands on the rim's edge against the hull, never over its top. At 1000 mm that is within the leave tolerance (250 mm) and on the ground; at 500 and 333 mm it is beyond it (125, 83 mm), so the player rests there with `on_ground` false (question 3).

### The choice: (a), a walkable top read a radius ahead along the walk

The landing is held only when, besides today's three conditions, the point a capsule radius ahead of the bottom sphere's centre along the walk finds no walkable volume surface within a radius and the tolerance. On a lip the point lies past the edge, over the top at the centre's height, so the nearest surface is the top face (normal up) and the landing is taken: at 1000 mm and 250 mm crouched the point is 26 mm past the edge and 150 mm over the top. On a steep slope the point lies closer to the same slope and reads it steep: test 8's cone (63.4 degrees, normal horizontal part 0.894) puts the point 0.106 r = 32 mm from the slope, the pod's hull (60.64 degrees, 0.872) 38 mm, both touching and steep, so the cone stays refused. A walk along the slope rather than up it moves the point away from the surface: it may then touch nothing, which counts as no walkable surface, so the landing stays refused. The override can only take a landing the rule refuses today, never refuse one it takes.

(b) is the current rule (above) and adds nothing. (c), the nearest volume below the centre, is not adopted: beside a vertical wall that touches the bottom sphere the rule never decides, because `drop_field_capsule` takes the nearest surface's gap, the wall's is within the tolerance before the drop moves, so the drop lands at the raised height, which is a step height (at least a sample) over the ground under the centre, beyond the leave tolerance, and `!ground.on` refuses the step first. A wall that does not touch is farther than the touching support, so the probe never returns it. The change (c) would make therefore alters nothing beside a wall, and the refusal the verifier saw is the drop's (question 1). The ahead probe is the nearest volume too: where a wall is nearer the ahead point than the lip's top it reads the wall and refuses as today, the conservative side.

Fixed point and determinism: the ahead direction is `collision_unit` of the walk's motion projected onto the tangent plane (integers, scaled before normalising so a motion of 88 units comes out exact), the point is the centre plus `fixed_scale(direction, capsule_radius)`, the probe is `frame_body_probe` (integers, bodies in ascending occupant handle). No float, no new state, no save or record change; the prediction (`predict_field_player_motion`) runs the same `step_field_walk`, so it agrees with the tick, and two machines of a lockstep game agree because both run the same build.

### Decision in the implementation round (main agent, 2026-10-04)

The implementer ran tests 1 and 3 on the unchanged code and traced the failures: the 1000 mm, 250 mm crouched case is the steep contact refusal the specification predicted, but the 500 mm, 125 mm and the 1000 mm, 175 mm crouched cases and test 3 fail in `settle_field_player`, not in the step. After a step whose landing rests the bottom sphere on the lip's edge with its centre still short of the top, the next tick's walk is not impeded, `after.below` is the hang over the floor under the centre, and the settle lowers the feet by that hang straight into the lip. `resolve_field_penetration` then pushes along the edge's normal, which is walkable (cosine 0.868 at 500 mm, 0.66 at 1000 mm and 175 mm), so the sphere rolls back down over the corner: down and back every tick, a bob on the edge and a slide off. The settle was written for the field's blurred faces (0203), where a walkable contact lifts the capsule up the face; a sharp volume corner has a walkable normal and is not a face to climb by lowering into it.

The rule: the settle lowers only through contacts steeper than walkable. New pure `settle_meets_walkable_surface :: proc(world: ^Field_World, frames: ^Frame_Table, tuning: Field_Player_Tuning, feet: World_Position, up: [3]i64) -> bool`: for every sphere of the capsule at `feet`, the field's probe and the frames' probe as `resolve_field_penetration` reads them; true when any has a depth above `FIELD_PENETRATION_TOLERANCE` with `fixed_dot(normal, up) >= tuning.walkable_cosine`. `settle_field_player` computes `lowered` as today, returns at once when `settle_meets_walkable_surface(world, frames, tuning, lowered, player.up)`, else resolves and takes the lowered position as today. The walk's own collision limited drop (`drop_field_capsule` to a step height, just before the `after` probe) has already rested the capsule on the edge, so skipping the settle keeps that rest, and the next tick's walk pushes into the corner and the sweep lifts along the walkable normal, which is how the capsule climbs over the edge. On a contact steeper than walkable (test 8's cone, the pod's hull, a face's rounded foot) nothing changes: the settle lowers and pushes across the up as before. On the field's blurred walkable face the settle no longer lifts along the normal; the capsule stays where the drop rested it and the sweep climbs it, which the 0203 tests check. The ahead rule of the specification stays: it takes the 1000 mm, 250 mm step the steep contact rule refused; the settle rule keeps every taken step.

The comment over `settle_field_player` says this (the sharp corner, 0232). `doc/architecture.md`'s step rule sentence gains: "the settle that follows a tick on walkable ground lowers the feet only through contacts steeper than walkable (`settle_meets_walkable_surface`, 0232), so a capsule the walk's drop rested on a volume's edge is not rolled back over it". The log section gains a bullet with the trace above (the three failing cases, the cause in the settle, the rule). Tests 1, 2 and 3 as specified, test 8 and the 0203 tests unchanged and passing; if test 8 or a 0203 test fails under the new rule, the implementer reports the case with its numbers and stops rather than loosening an assertion.

### Decision in the implementation round, second pass (main agent, 2026-10-04)
