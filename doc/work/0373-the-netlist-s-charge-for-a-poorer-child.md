# 0373: The netlist's charge for a poorer child

Status: implementing (2026-11-13)

Found by 0355's review. In the solve's slow path (`src/netlist/solve.odin`, `add_clamped_response`, `region_levels`) the region that feeds a poorer one across a limiting edge is charged the edge's full capacity, while the poorer region at its whole per mille level takes less. The difference is withheld from the feeding region, and when its demand is small that is tens of per mille with its own generation idle: a box with one 13 kW machine and a 12 kW generator on a light cable that passes on to an overloaded box runs its machine at 92.5 percent where full power is reachable. The main agent had accepted the charge as "a few per mille" before the review measured it; that was wrong. Under the poles it cannot occur (every edge unlimited), so 0355 landed with it named.

## Goal

A region is never held under what its own supply and its cables can give it.

## Controls

None.

## Change

- Above a poorer child's level the child is charged what it takes at that level, not the edge's capacity (the review's proposal: `clamp(R_child(poor_level), low, high)`), with the repair pass and the feasibility argument checked again.
- The brute force reference charges the same, and its capacities include the corners the review used (0, 1, 999, 1000, 1001, 1999, 2000, 3000, 4500, unlimited).
- The definition test measures the feeding region's idle reachable supply, and its bound is derived or zero.
- Before 0368.

## Verify

- The checks and the suite, with the review's two hand cases as tests (the middle at 990 and at full power).
- The adapter's paired test unchanged: no behaviour change under the poles.

## Specification (design, 2026-11-11)

Revised 2026-11-13 after Step 0, then again that day for the main agent's decisions on Questions 4 and 5: every spare joule follows one rule, by levels on either side of the region holding it, through one mechanism, the pool, in place of the up return, the fed region's room going to its feeders first and the far feeders' leftover. Taken out of the first revision: decision 5 (feeders first), `region_walk` and `return_slack` with their run bottom-up then top-down, the plan bounds on the dispatch (old decision 7) and Question 5's leftover. Evidence: a Python model of this specification (the design's scratch, not in the repository; its own random sets shaped like `brute_force_netlist`, `definition_netlist` and a set with offers behind small cables, 2 to 40 nodes) found on 10,400 trees no infeasible tree and no component that could rise alone, on 5,300 no offender under Step 2's metric, the fast path equal to the slow path wherever it holds, on 6,000 trees of up to 6 nodes its levels equal to the reference's, and its sorted levels never below today's. The model first found one component that could rise (a fed region's own surplus that no greedy drew); decision 12 is the fix.

Units below: R and every charge are in millijoules (joules times `LEVEL_FULL`), as in `region_levels`; `low = 1000 * lower`, `high = 1000 * upper`; `rl`, `pl` are a node's `rich_level`, `poor_level`. A net, a slack, a room, a source, a need and a take are whole joules.

### The model

- A node's level is a whole per mille; a consumer receives `delivered_at(demand, level)`, rounded down. An edge carries whole joules within its bounds.
- Each edge is open or limiting. Regions are the components of the open edges, one level each, the highest their supply allows by the exact need (today's pooled rule).
- A feeder (a region feeding its parent region across a limiting edge) sends its export, which starts at its capacity and falls by what the pool draws back through it; it sits at the highest level its export leaves it. A fed region (fed by its parent region) sits at its poor level, strictly below its parent; its room is its own net at that level plus its capacity.
- The rule: every spare joule a region can hand on, a domain root's slack at its level or a fed region's room, goes a level at a time to the poorest region that can reach it, on either side; at equal levels the cheapest next level, then the first in pre-order. A joule reaches a region through open edges with room towards it, a fed cable's unused room and a feeder's export.
- A domain is a region and the regions fed from it, recursively; its candidates are the feeders of its regions. A domain root is the root's region or a feeder; a source is a domain root's slack or a fed region's surplus its parent cannot take.
- Where the rule is computed:
  1. A fed region's room against its parent, bottom-up: at parent level λ it takes `max(0, drawn - own net)`, `drawn` what its room spends raising its domain's candidates up to λ - 1 by the rule (the fill); the rest is its parent's. At a tie across the cable the parent goes first (decision 5).
  2. Top-down, each domain root once its level is final: its slack over its domain's candidates by the rule.
  3. The pool: what a source has left stays drawable by every later greedy below it in pre-order, the nearest source first, within the rooms. So the feeders behind a feeder are reached when their own domain root's turn comes (decision 7).
- Direction: across a limiting edge the fed side's level is at most the feeding side's, a fed region's strictly below. Power flows towards the shortage.
- Feasible: a flow exists with every offer between 0 and itself, every open edge within its bounds, every limiting edge between 0 and its capacity in its direction, every node balanced.
- Maximised: the levels of the consumers with a demand, sorted ascending, lexicographically, over the edges' states, each state's levels by the rule. The rule's split of the spare is a greedy, not a search (decision 4). What whole per mille levels and whole joules leave stays undelivered (Leftover below).
- Inside a region the pooled level counts the exact need (`demand * level / 1000` unrounded), today's rule and the fast path's, so nothing changes under the poles. With demands in whole thousands the exact need is whole joules: the brute force uses such demands, and the two review hand cases add rounding.

### Why the review's proposal falls short

- `clamp(R_child(pl), low, high)` charges the exact need. Hand case 1: `R_child(713) = -4,995,991`, the middle at 940; hand case 2: `R_child(578) = -4,993,920`, the middle at 954. The 5 J and 34 J missing are the poorer region's deliveries rounded down (7 × 0.713 J, 40 × 0.848 J). The review's reachable values count whole joules, so the charge is the whole joules taken.
- A clamp to `[low, high]` credits a positive `R_child(pl)` (a poorer child with a surplus at its level) as an export, which `bound_flows_by_levels` then closes: the feeder's level counts joules that cannot arrive. The charge is never above 0.
- The 19 edges of 0355's probe were most likely those credits: feeders set too high, a repair unable to meet the target within the bounds, an edge left with the wrong sign or room. The record does not say which metric and capacities gave 123 and 19, so the probe below sets its own baseline.

### Step 1: the solve, `src/netlist/solve.odin`

- `Level_Thresholds :: struct {rich_level, poor_level: i32}`: `rich_until` goes (decision 6).
- `Child_State :: enum u8 {Joined, Feeder, Fed}` and `child_state :: proc(thresholds: Level_Thresholds, region_level: u32) -> Child_State`, in place of `child_level`: Feeder when `i32(region_level) <= rich_level`, Fed when it is above `poor_level`, else Joined. A feeder starts at `rich_level`, a fed region sits at `max(poor_level, 0)`.
- `make_non_increasing :: proc(table: []i64)`: each entry from level 1 up becomes the least of itself and the one below. A whole-joule take can lie above the exact need at `pl` by under 1000 per consumer, so a parent's table can rise there; the envelope keeps every response non-increasing, so `largest_level_at_least`'s binary search and the fast path's three levels stand. It is progressive filling: a region stops at the first level it cannot afford.
- `add_child_response :: proc(target: ^Response, child: Response, low, high: i64, poor_level: i32, own_net: i64, fill: []i64, spare_tables: ^[dynamic][]i64)` replaces `add_clamped_response`. The line case is unchanged (a clamp that does not bite means `poor_level == LEVEL_FULL`). In a table each level up to `poor_level` adds `clamp(response_at(child, level), low, high)`, each level above adds `-LEVEL_FULL * max(0, drawn - own_net)`, `drawn` being `fill[level - 1]`, 0 when `fill` is nil: the fed region's take, never above 0.
- `Pools :: struct {state: []Child_State, level: []u32, export, net, passed, source: []i64, ends: []i32, done: []bool, domain, candidates: [dynamic]i32}` and `make_pools :: proc(phase: Phase) -> Pools` in the temp allocator, `ends[p]` one past `p`'s subtree in pre-order (subtree sizes summed in reverse pre-order). By position, whole joules: `net` what a region node's part sends up its edge (a fed top's: minus its take), `passed` what draws sent down that edge, `source` a domain root's or a fed top's spare not yet drawn; a feeder top's room is its `export`.
- `walk_domain :: proc(phase: Phase, thresholds: []Level_Thresholds, pools: ^Pools, top: i32, level: u32)`: clears `domain` and `candidates`, sets `level[top]`, appends `top`; then from `p := top + 1` while `p < ends[top]`, its parent already in the domain since feeders' subtrees are skipped: `state[p] = child_state(thresholds[p], level[parent[p]])`; a feeder gets `level[p] = rich_level`, `export[p] = upper[p]`, `source[p] = 0`, goes to `candidates`, and `p = ends[p]`; any other gets its parent's level (joined) or `max(poor_level, 0)` (fed), `passed[p] = 0`, `source[p] = 0`, goes to `domain`, and `p += 1`. Both lists come out in pre-order.
- `candidates_below :: proc(candidates: []i32, first, end: i32) -> []i32`: the run of `candidates` with positions in `[first, end)`, contiguous in pre-order, found by binary search.
- `settle_domain :: proc(phase: Phase, responses: []Response, pools: ^Pools)`: zeroes `net` over the domain, adds each candidate's `export` to its parent's `net`, then goes over the domain in reverse: `net[v] += offer[v] - delivered_at(demand[v], level[v])`; for the top nothing more; a fed `v` runs its own greedy: `own := net[v]`, `source[v] = own - lower[v]` (its room), `greedy(phase, responses, pools, candidates_below(candidates, v, ends[v]), i32(level[parent[v]]) - 1, v, nil)`, `drawn := own - lower[v] - source[v]`, then `source[v] = max(0, own - drawn)` (the surplus its parent cannot take, decision 12) and `net[v] = -max(0, drawn - own)` (its take), added to its parent's `net`; a joined `v` adds `min(net[v], upper[v])` to its parent's. Inner fed regions settle first, so every path a greedy walks is settled.
- `reachable :: proc(phase: Phase, pools: Pools, candidate, bound: i32) -> i64`: what the sources from the candidate's parent up to `bound` can bring it. Walking up from `parent[candidate]` while `v != bound`: `suffix += source[v]`, `best = min(best, room + suffix)`, `room` being `export[v]` for a feeder top, else `min(net[v], upper[v]) - lower[v] - passed[v]`. Returns `min(best, source[bound] + suffix)`: a source joins below its own edge, so the amount is the least, over the edges on the path, of the edge's room plus the sources below it.
- `draw :: proc(phase: Phase, pools: ^Pools, candidate, bound: i32, amount: i64) -> (from_bound: i64)`: walking up from `parent[candidate]` while `v != bound`: takes `min(source[v], amount)` from `v`'s source, and what is left crosses `v`'s edge (`export[v] -=` it for a feeder top, else `passed[v] +=` it); the rest comes out of `source[bound]` and is returned. Nearest first never fails when `amount <= reachable` (decision 9).
- `greedy :: proc(phase: Phase, responses: []Response, pools: ^Pools, candidates: []i32, cap_level, bound: i32, fill: []i64)`: clears `done` over `candidates`, then repeats. A candidate `g` not done with `level[g] + 1 <= cap_level` (`LEVEL_FULL` for no cap): `next := response_at(responses[g], level[g] + 1)`, `need := export[g] - next / LEVEL_FULL`; it is done when `level[g] == LEVEL_FULL`, `next < 0` or `need > reachable(g, bound)`. Of the rest the least `level[g]`, then `need`, then position, takes its next level: `export[g] -= need`, `level[g] = largest_level_at_least(responses[g], LEVEL_FULL * export[g])`, `from_bound := draw(g, bound, need)`, and with `fill` set `fill[old level + 1] += from_bound`. It stops when no candidate is left. A candidate sits at the highest level its export allows, so `need >= 1`; within a run sources and rooms only fall, so a candidate done stays done and the levels bought never fall: a run capped at μ is the uncapped run's steps up to μ.
- `fed_fill :: proc(phase: Phase, thresholds: []Level_Thresholds, responses: []Response, pools: ^Pools, position: i32, spare_tables: ^[dynamic][]i64) -> (own_net: i64, fill: []i64)`: `walk_domain` of `position` at `poor_level`, `settle_domain`, `own_net = net[position]`; `fill` is nil when the domain has no candidate, else `source[position] = own_net - lower[position]`, `fill` a zeroed table from `take_table`, `greedy(..., candidates, LEVEL_FULL, position, fill)`, then running sums, so `fill[μ]` is what the room spends with the candidates capped at μ.
- `region_levels :: proc(phase: ^Phase)`:
  - Bottom-up as today, per non-root position in reverse pre-order: `make_non_increasing` on its table if it has one, the thresholds, `fed_fill` when `poor_level < LEVEL_FULL`, `add_child_response`. The fill goes back to `spare_tables`, and the child's table when `rich_level < 0` (never a candidate, so no greedy reads it again).
  - The root's table gets `make_non_increasing`; its level as today.
  - `settle_levels :: proc(phase: Phase, thresholds: []Level_Thresholds, responses: []Response, pools: ^Pools, root_level: u32)`, top-down: in pre-order each position that is the root or whose `state` is Feeder (set by the walk of the domain above it, earlier in pre-order) is a domain root: `walk_domain` at its level (the root's, or what the greedies above it left), `settle_domain`, `source[top] = net[top] - (top > 0 ? export[top] : 0)`, `greedy(..., candidates, LEVEL_FULL, 0, nil)`, its sources reaching up to the root.
  - `phase.level` from `pools.level`, delivered as today. The comment: the charge with its fill, the domains, the pool and the envelope.
- Unchanged: `bound_flows_by_levels` (the direction rule, now the only bounds: decision 10), `flows_within_bounds`, `repair_dispatch`, `dispatch_by_order`, `tree_flows`, phase 2 (its bounds come from the capacities and phase 1's flows; a store's charge room rounds like a demand).
- `pooled_level_holds`: a fourth accumulator per position, `whole`, in the same pass: `whole[position] += LEVEL_FULL * (offer - delivered_at(demand, level))`. Each position, before its own check: `responses[position][2] = min(responses[position][1], responses[position][2])` (the envelope at the three levels). `child_keeps_level` becomes `response[1] >= low && (response[1] < high || level == LEVEL_FULL)`: a child reaching its cable outwards below full is a candidate the pool may raise, which only the slow path computes; under the poles no response reaches `high`. Into the parent: indices 0 and 1 clamped as today; index 2 adds `min(whole[position], 0)` when `response[2] < low` (poor at L + 1, so `pl == L`; no descendant reaches its cable outwards at L, or its check would have failed, so its domain at L has no candidate, its fill is nil and its take is `-min(whole, 0) / LEVEL_FULL`), else the clamp; `whole[parent] += clamp(whole[position], low, high)`. The slow path gives the same value, so the fast path still never gives what the slow path would not.
- The file's header comment: the slow path's charge and pool in two sentences; the "Known defect (0373)" comment goes.

Deterministic: integers only; positions in pre-order; candidates scanned in pre-order under a fixed key; a draw follows the one tree path; no maps. A fed region's domain depends only on its own poor level and the thresholds below it, and inside it inner fed regions run first, so its capped run top-down is the prefix of its uncapped run bottom-up and draws exactly `fill[λ - 1]`, the take its parent's response was charged.

Work: a node is walked once by each position above it whose domain reaches it at that position's poor level (no feeder between them) and once top-down: at most h + 1 times, h the most edges on a path from the root whose child's poor level is below 1000, n²/2 node visits in all for a chain. A greedy step scans the run's candidates not done, each with a walk to the bound (at most the tree's depth), and buys at least one level for at least 1 J, so a run takes at most the joules its sources hold and 1000 steps a candidate; a fed region's capped run is repeated in every walk that reaches it. The worst case, a chain of nested fed regions each with a cheap feeder, is cubic in n, bounded by the rooms' joules (each under one level of its region's need plus a joule a consumer). Tables: a 1,001-entry fill per position with `pl < 1000` whose domain has a candidate, and one envelope pass per table.

Feasible: a feeder's export starts at its capacity and only falls, never below 0 (its room in `reachable`); a fed region's take `max(0, drawn - own)` is at most its capacity since `drawn` is at most its room, and later draws through it stay within `capacity - take - passed`; an open edge carries `min(net, upper) - passed`, at least `lower` since every draw checked its room (a part's net above its edge's capacity, rounding only, stays idle in it). Each region balances: a domain root's slack is at least 0 (its level is the highest its export allows by the exact response, the whole-joule net is at least the exact one, and a fed child's take is the one its response was charged); a source never goes below 0; a draw moves joules along a tree path within each edge's room. Direction holds: a feeder starts at `rl`, at least its parent's level, and only rises; a fed region sits at `pl`, below its parent; a joined node at its parent's. So the plan is an integer flow within the direction bounds, and `repair_dispatch`'s `L <= H` holds at every node as in 0355: no edge over capacity, no node over its demand or offer, joules conserved.

Under the poles nothing runs: no response reaches `high` and every `pl` is 1000, so there is no fill, no table, no candidate and no greedy; the fast path holds as before, and the slow path, if reached, gives the root's level everywhere. The result is byte equal.

### Decisions in this design

1. The charge above `pl` is the whole joules the fed region then takes, after its fill, not the review's `clamp(R_child(pl), low, high)`: the Verify's 990 and full power are whole-joule values, and a positive credit breaks the direction rule.
2. Every response is made non-increasing (progressive filling) rather than scanned raw. A raw response would place a region by whole-joule leximin even in the envelope's case, but the fast path would then need the root's response at every level above the pooled one, not three. The envelope's case, measured by hand: hand case 1 with a 4976 J edge to the root gives the middle and the 7 consumers 712; whole-joule leximin gives 713 and the middle 750.
3. The reference keeps whole-thousand demands, where the exact need is whole joules. With other demands the solve's thresholds count a joined child's exact need (0355's rule, kept), so an open edge whose exact need crosses its capacity while its whole joules fit makes the child poorer where a whole-joule reference would keep it joined, by up to about `1000 / d` levels for consumers of `d` joules. Odd demands are covered by the hand cases and the feasibility test only.
4. One rule for every spare joule (the main agent's decision on Question 4): a level at a time to the poorest region that can reach it, on either side, the cheapest then pre-order at equal levels, computed in three places (the fill, the domain roots' greedies, the pool). A level at a time needs only each candidate's next level. It is a greedy: where candidates' levels cost different amounts a cleverer split can leave a sorted level one higher (a knapsack); the reference uses the same rule.
5. At a tie across a fed cable, a candidate of the fed region's domain and its parent's region both at λ - 1, the parent goes first (it is first in pre-order), so the fill runs to λ - 1, not λ. The cheapest first would need the parent's cost of level λ while its response, which that cost depends on, is being built. Within one run the cheapest goes first.
6. A child reaching its cable outwards at its parent's level (`R >= high`, not only `R > high`) feeds, so a joined child at exactly its capacity can be raised, an answer the reference's up edge at equal levels finds; `rich_until` goes, and the fast path leaves such children to the slow path. A child flat at `high` over several levels (no demand in its region there) now starts at `rl` rather than its parent's level; no consumer's level changes.
7. A greedy's candidates are the feeders of its domain, the region and the regions fed from it recursively: their levels and costs do not move while it runs, since a fed region's level is its poor level and its own domain sits below it. A feeder's own feeders depend on the feeder's level, so they are the next domain's candidates, and the pool reaches them in pre-order (the main agent's decision on Question 5): by levels within a domain, by pre-order between domains.
8. A draw passes only edges with room, counting every offer below them supplied: without that a draw through a joined child near its cable's capacity overloads it (`test_a_return_passes_only_cables_with_room`).
9. A draw takes from the sources nearest the candidate first, keeping the far ones, which reach more candidates and cross fewer rooms; nearest first never fails when the reachable amount covers the need.
10. The plan bounds go: the plan is a flow within the direction bounds, so `repair_dispatch` meets the levels within them, and the definition metric counts a neighbour's spare on both sides of an edge, so where the dispatch leaves the idle no longer matters. The phase keeps the direction bounds only, which 0356's top-up reads.
11. The definition metric counts a richer neighbour's spare (`spare_toward`) rather than its edge's room: the room counts joules a feeder has already spent on its own level (the model's first offender, a root whose 5 J went to its feeder's next level). The rise count is exact with whole-thousand demands; an edge of capacity 0 is exempt from the no-demand clause.
12. A fed region's own surplus beyond what its parent can take is a source: without it the model found a fed region behind a 1 J cable whose 2 J no greedy drew, while the feeder behind its feeder could rise (`test_the_pool_reaches_a_feeder_behind_a_feeder`).
13. `test_netlist_solve_cost` gets the generators case: its two cases have no offer behind a limited edge, so they would show the walks and never a greedy.
