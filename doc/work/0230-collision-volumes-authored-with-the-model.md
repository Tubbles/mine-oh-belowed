## Goal

A machine model collides as its shape, not as its footprint's box of cells: the pod's sloped hull is sloped to a player walking round it, the empty corners of its footprint are walkable, and inside the cabin the chair, the desk, the bed and the drum stop the player where they stand. The volumes are authored in the model script beside the geometry, in the model's own frame, so the modeller shapes both at once.

## Why volumes, not triangles and not cells

The simulation is fixed point and lockstep: a capsule against thousands of triangles in integer arithmetic, per player and per tick, hashed alike on two machines, is a lot of delicate code for one benefit. A handful of analytic primitives in the model's frame (box, cylinder, cone frustum, each optionally a shell with a wall thickness) is tested exactly in integer millimetres, is deterministic by construction, and matches how the lab's models are built from the kit's box, cylinder and cone. Cells at half a metre give a staircase, which the user declined.

## Change

- The model script lists collision volumes in a `collision(b)` section (the kit grows `b.collision_box`, `b.collision_cylinder`, `b.collision_cone`, each with a `shell` thickness option, in the Blender frame like the geometry); the exporter writes them to `data/models/<model>.collision.sjson` in the model's frame in cells (x and z centred, y from the bottom, +x the front), the same conversion the OBJ gets. The lab's check reports the volumes' bounds against the footprint and draws them in the previews as wireframes.

- The field player's collision against frames (`world_frame_collision.odin`, `frame_solid_probe`, `raycast_frames` with `.Solid`) takes a machine with volumes by its volumes alone: the capsule, transformed into the machine's frame by the inverse of its rotation and pitch (integer millimetres, the pitch a whole number of millimetres), is tested against each volume (the distance from the capsule's segment to the box, the cylinder, the frustum, and for a shell the band between its two surfaces), and pushed out along the volume's normal, with the walk, step, slide and mantle rules of `field_player` applied as they are for cells (the design stage says how the step height and the walkable angle apply to a sloped hull: a cone of the pod's slope is steeper than the walkable angle, so the player slides off it). The hatches stay cell collided (a 1 by 2 by 2 door panel is a box of cells already) and their open rows passable as today.
- Determinism: all of it in integer millimetres with the simulation's own integer square root and vector helpers (`world_field_vector.odin`, `world_field_distance.odin`), no float in the simulation, the lockstep hash unchanged by the volumes themselves (they are content, loaded alike on every machine) and changed by the movement they cause, as any collision is.

### The design in one paragraph

A machine whose model has `data/models/<model>.collision.sjson` keeps the volumes on its record (`Machine.collision`, integers in 1/4096 cell). When such a machine occupies its cells on a frame other than the block frame, its held cells get a new occupant flag `Shaped` and the frame table gets a `Frame_Body`: the volumes scaled to position units for the frame's pitch, with the machine's placement. The field player's collision already works per sphere of the capsule against a signed distance (the field's, and the nearest solid cell's); the frames' probe becomes the nearer of the solid cells that are not `Shaped` and the bodies' volumes, and the frames' ray becomes the nearer of those cells and a sphere traced ray against the bodies. Walk, step, slide, mantle, ground and the walkable angle then apply to a volume exactly as to a cell, because they all read the probe's distance and normal and the ray's hit and normal. The cells keep `Solid`, so placement, the sealed room, the water and the light are unchanged; the hatches and fixtures are entities of their own and stay cell collided.

### Decisions on the open points

- The capsule: spheres, not a segment. The field player's capsule is already spheres along its axis at most a radius apart (`field_capsule_sphere_count`), each probed against the field and the cells; a volume is one more signed distance per sphere centre, so no segment to primitive closest point code is needed, and the volumes get the field's and the cells' rules for free. Exact Euclidean distances (a box's, a round volume's) keep the sweep's half radius steps from passing through anything thicker than the existing tolerance.
- Units: position units (1/4096 m, `POSITION_UNITS_PER_METRE`), not millimetres. The item says integer millimetres; every position, distance and capsule size of the simulation is in position units, so the volumes are too. The file is in cells (as the OBJ); the record keeps `COLLISION_UNITS_PER_CELL` (4096) units per cell, rounded once at load; a body scales them by the frame's pitch (`pitch_units / 4096`, exact at 500, 1000 and 2000 mm, floor division otherwise).
- The primitives in the game: two kinds. A `Box`, axis aligned in the model's frame, solid (a box shell is expanded at load into four solid wall boxes). A `Round`, about the model's x, y or z axis, from one point on the axis to another, with an outer radius at each end (a cylinder when equal, a cone frustum otherwise), solid or a shell (the inner surface the outer one less the shell along the radius, open at both ends), over the full turn or over a sector. Rotated boxes are not supported: they need a rotation per volume in integers for one use (the bed on the lean wall), which a cone shell sector covers.
- Sectors are an addition to the item's set, and needed: the airlock's outer hatch stands in the hull's wall, and the item keeps the hatches cell collided and passable, so the hull's shell must leave the doorway open. A revolved shell has no door; a sector (the hull's lower wall from 12.6 to 347.4 degrees) does, exactly, and the same option gives the desk and the lower wall cabinets, which run round part of the wall. Subtracting boxes from a volume was considered and dropped: the distance to a volume less a box is not `max(d_volume, -d_box)` near the box's faces outside the volume, which pushed a test capsule in the doorway against a wall that is not there. Question 1 asks for the approval.
- The resolution over several volumes: per sphere and per pass, the deepest contact pushes. The frames' probe returns the smallest signed distance over the solid non `Shaped` cells within reach and every volume of every body within reach (largest penetration first); `resolve_field_penetration` pushes by it and by the field's probe, as today, for `FIELD_COLLISION_ITERATIONS` (4) passes over the spheres, stopping early when no sphere overlaps. Ties keep the first: the cells before the bodies, the bodies in ascending occupant handle, a body's volumes in file order, a round volume's section edges in order and then its sector faces. The bodies are kept sorted by occupant handle so a world loaded from a snapshot breaks ties as the host does.
- Step, walkable angle, slide, mantle: by the normal, unchanged code. The ground ray hits a volume's surface with its normal; `probe_field_ground` compares that normal's cosine with the walkable cosine, so a volume's top is walkable ground and a cone steeper than walkable is slid off (gravity's move takes the full normal), and the walk into it is flattened along the tangent plane (`field_push_direction`), exactly as for the field. The step height against a volume is the data's (`tuning.step_height`): `field_step_height` keeps reading the cells only (its pitch rule exists so a one cell pad is a step, which does not apply to a volume). The mantle and the ledge use the probe and the ray, so they see volumes. The pod's cone (run 0.5625 per rise) is 60.64 degrees steep against the shipped walkable 60: steeper, so the player slides off it, by a margin of 0.64 degrees (cosine 0.4903 against 0.5000).
- The aiming ray: `raycast_frames_and_bodies` casts the cells with `Shaped` excluded and the bodies by sphere tracing, and the nearer hit wins (the cells on a tie), so Interact and the tool reach the hull where it is and pass the empty corners. A body's hit is a `Frame_Raycast_Hit` with the body's occupant, the cell holding the hit point moved a quarter pitch into the volume, the face of the normal's largest component in the frame's axes and the cell in front of it, so placement against the hull (`adjacent`) and the pick up path work unchanged.
- The hash: unchanged. The volumes are content, loaded alike on every machine like the records (there is no content fingerprint between peers today; a peer with another collision file desyncs as with any other differing data file). The bodies are derived like the occupant index: rebuilt by `rebuild_entity_cells`, never saved, never hashed. The players' positions after collision are hashed as today.
- The reader: `src/machine_collision.odin`, not `model_collision.odin`. The `model` prefix puts a file in the presentation cluster (`tools/code_graph.py`), which the simulation may not reference; the volumes are simulation content read with the machine registry, so the reader is a `machine_` file of the simulation cluster, and the file name helper sits in the `model_obj` package beside `model_file_path`, which every cluster may reference.
- Reload: as content, not as the model. A change to a collision file changes the lockstep simulation, so it may not apply in place like a model; `data_file_category` puts `models/*.collision.sjson` under `.Content`, which reloads on request (the reload command, F8, the Developer screen, or `watch_data` all), and the content reload rebuilds the entities and with them the bodies.
- The bounds: the loader refuses a volume whose box (a round one's full turn) leaves the footprint plus `MODEL_FOOTPRINT_TOLERANCE_CELLS` (0.02) on any side, top included, so `--model-check` never sees an out of bounds file: the registry's load fails first with the loader's line. The model check adds the intrusion check: no volume inside an open cells box or a fixture box shrunk by the tolerance.
- The cap: `COLLISION_VOLUME_LIMIT` 64 after a box shell's expansion into four. The pod's table below uses 35.
- The game's preview draws the volumes as wireframes in five extra shots per machine with volumes, `<machine>_<camera>_collision.png` at rest, so the hero shots stay clean.

### Files and procedures

`src/world_frame.odin`:

- `Occupant_Flag` gains `Shaped` after `Open`, comment: "A held cell of a machine that collides by its collision volumes (work item 0230, world_frame_body.odin): the field player's cell probe and the frames' rays pass it and the frame's body stands in for it. It stays Solid for everything else (placement, the sealed room)."
- `Frame_Table` gains `bodies: [dynamic]Frame_Body` after `extents`, comment: "The machines that collide by their volumes (0230), sorted by occupant handle; derived from the entities like the occupant index, rebuilt on load, never saved."
- `destroy_frame_table` deletes `table.bodies`; `clear_frame_occupants` clears it. The header comment gains one sentence on the bodies.

New `src/world_frame_body.odin` (world cluster), header comment: the collision volumes of a machine's model (work item 0230, doc/architecture.md, Frames): the record keeps them in 1/`COLLISION_UNITS_PER_CELL` of a cell in the model's frame (x and z centred on the unrotated footprint, y from its bottom, +x the front); a body is one placed machine's volumes in position units of its frame with its quarter turn; signed distances in integers (exact outside, the depth to the nearest face inside), a sphere traced ray; integer only, so every machine of a lockstep game agrees. Imports none beyond the package.

- `COLLISION_VOLUME_LIMIT :: 64`: volumes per model, a box shell counting four.
- `COLLISION_UNITS_PER_CELL :: 4096`.
- `COLLISION_WIRE_SEGMENTS` is not here (presentation, below).
- `COLLISION_RAY_STEPS :: 64`: sphere tracing steps of a ray against a body; a grazing ray that needs more misses.
- `COLLISION_RAY_HIT_UNITS :: 2`: a traced ray within this many position units of a volume hits it.
- `Collision_Volume_Kind :: enum u8 { Box, Round }`.
- `Collision_Sector :: struct { partial: bool, start: [2]i64, end: [2]i64, wide: bool }`: `partial` false is the full turn; `start` and `end` are unit vectors (`UNIT_VECTOR_ONE`) in the plane across the axis, components along the axis's first and second perpendicular (`collision_perpendicular_axes`); the sector runs from `start` to `end` by the right hand rule about the axis; `wide` when it spans more than half a turn.
- `Collision_Volume :: struct { kind: Collision_Volume_Kind, axis: int, from: [3]i64, to: [3]i64, radius_from: i64, radius_to: i64, shell: i64, sector: Collision_Sector, bound_minimum: [3]i64, bound_maximum: [3]i64 }`: a box's `from` and `to` are its minimum and maximum corners (`axis`, `radius_*`, `shell` and `sector` unused, `shell` always 0 after the expansion); a round's are its axis's points at its two ends, `from[axis] < to[axis]`, equal on the other two axes, with the outer radius at each, the shell's thickness along the radius (0 solid) and its sector. `bound_*`: the box round the volume (a round one's full turn), for the cull. In model units on the record, in position units in a body.
- `Frame_Body :: struct { occupant: Occupant, frame: Frame, centre: [3]i64, rotation: u8, bound: i64, volumes: [COLLISION_VOLUME_LIMIT]Collision_Volume, volume_count: int }`: the machine's occupant as its held cells carry it; a copy of its frame (a frame never re-tangents); the model frame's origin in the frame's axes in position units; the quarter turns; a radius round `centre` holding every volume; the volumes in position units.

Placement (all pure):

- `collision_perpendicular_axes :: proc(axis: int) -> (first, second: int)`: `(axis + 1) % 3, (axis + 2) % 3` (x: y, z; y: z, x; z: x, y), the order the sector's angles run in.
- `scale_collision_volume :: proc(volume: Collision_Volume, pitch_units: i64) -> Collision_Volume`: `from`, `to`, the radii, the shell and the bounds times `pitch_units`, `floor_divide_i64` by `COLLISION_UNITS_PER_CELL`; the sector unchanged. Called by `make_frame_body`.
- `make_frame_body :: proc(frame: Frame, occupant: Occupant, origin: World_Coordinate, size: [3]i32, rotation: u8, volumes: []Collision_Volume) -> Frame_Body`: `pitch := frame_pitch_units(frame)`; `centre = {(2 * origin.x + size.x) * pitch / 2, origin.y * pitch, (2 * origin.z + size.z) * pitch / 2}` (as `model_transform` centres the model on the rotated footprint, `size` the entity's rotated size); the first `min(len(volumes), COLLISION_VOLUME_LIMIT)` volumes scaled; `bound` = `vector_length` of the per axis largest absolute coordinate over every scaled volume's bound corners. Called by `register_entity_body`.
- `quarter_turn :: proc(rotation: u8) -> (cosine, sine: i64)`: `{1, 0}, {0, 1}, {-1, 0}, {0, -1}` for rotation % 4, the tables of `model_transform`.
- `body_direction_to_frame :: proc(rotation: u8, direction: [3]i64) -> [3]i64`: `{c * x - s * z, y, s * x + c * z}`, `model_transform`'s rotation.
- `body_direction_from_frame :: proc(rotation: u8, direction: [3]i64) -> [3]i64`: the inverse, `{c * x + s * z, y, -s * x + c * z}`.
- `body_point_from_frame :: proc(body: Frame_Body, local: [3]i64) -> [3]i64`: `body_direction_from_frame(body.rotation, local - body.centre)`.

Distances (all pure; `point` in the volume's units and frame; `normal` a unit vector out of the volume, from the nearest surface point to the point, or out through the nearest face when inside; `distance` negative inside):

- `box_signed_distance :: proc(point, minimum, maximum: [3]i64) -> (distance: i64, normal: [3]i64)`: today's body of `cell_box_distance` with the corners given: outside, the length of the offset from the clamped point and its direction; inside, minus the depth to the nearest face and that face's axis (faces in the order -x, +x, -y, +y, -z, +z, strict less keeps the first). `cell_box_distance` becomes `return box_signed_distance(local, low, low + pitch)` with `low := [3]i64{i64(cell.x), i64(cell.y), i64(cell.z)} * pitch`, its comment kept.
- `Round_Section :: struct { corners: [4][2]i64, solid: bool }`: the volume's cross section in the half plane of (radius, height), counter clockwise.
- `round_section :: proc(volume: Collision_Volume) -> Round_Section`: with `h0 = from[axis]`, `h1 = to[axis]`, `r0 = radius_from`, `r1 = radius_to`, `k = shell`: solid `{(0, h0), (r0, h0), (r1, h1), (0, h1)}`; shell `{(r0 - k, h0), (r0, h0), (r1, h1), (r1 - k, h1)}`. Edge i runs from corner i to corner i + 1 (mod 4); its outward normal is `(d.y, -d.x)` for `d` = the edge's vector; edge 3 of a solid is the axis and is no surface.
- `segment_closest_point_2d :: proc(start, end, point: [2]i64) -> [2]i64`: `d = end - start`, `n = dot(point - start, d)`, `D = dot(d, d)`; `start` for `n <= 0`, `end` for `n >= D`, else `start + d * n / D`.
- `section_closest_point :: proc(section: Round_Section, point: [2]i64) -> (closest: [2]i64, inside: bool)`: inside when for every edge `cross(d, point - corner_i) = d.x * (point.y - corner_i.y) - d.y * (point.x - corner_i.x) >= 0` (then `closest = point`); else the nearest of the four edges' `segment_closest_point_2d` (squared lengths compared, the first on a tie). All four edges, the axis too, so a point on the far side of the axis (an end face's coordinate, below) is measured right.
- `section_depth :: proc(section: Round_Section, point: [2]i64) -> (depth: i64, normal: [2]i64)`: for a point inside: the least `cross(d, point - corner_i) / length(d)` over the edges (edge 3 skipped for a solid), with that edge's outward normal normalised (`normalize_fixed` of `{d.y, -d.x, 0}`, the first two components). `length(d)` is `vector_length({d.x, d.y, 0})`.
- `sector_contains :: proc(sector: Collision_Sector, across: [2]i64) -> bool`: true for a full turn and for `across == {0, 0}`; with `cross_2d(a, b) = a.x * b.y - a.y * b.x`: not wide, `cross_2d(start, across) >= 0 && cross_2d(across, end) >= 0`; wide, `!(cross_2d(end, across) > 0 && cross_2d(across, start) > 0)`.
- `lift_across :: proc(axis: int, across: [2]i64, along: i64) -> [3]i64`: the 3D vector with `across` on the axis's two perpendiculars and `along` on the axis.
- `sector_face_distance :: proc(section: Round_Section, axis: int, face: [2]i64, outward: [2]i64, across: [2]i64, height: i64) -> (distance: i64, normal: [3]i64)`: the distance from the point (`across`, `height`) to the section placed in the half plane of the unit `face` direction: `s = dot_2d(across, face) / UNIT_VECTOR_ONE`; `closest, _ := section_closest_point(section, {s, height})`; `offset := lift_across(axis, across - face * closest.x / UNIT_VECTOR_ONE, height - closest.y)`; `distance = vector_length(offset)`; `normal = normalize_fixed(offset)`, or `lift_across(axis, outward, 0)` when the offset is zero.
- `round_signed_distance :: proc(volume: Collision_Volume, point: [3]i64) -> (distance: i64, normal: [3]i64)`: `first, second := collision_perpendicular_axes(axis)`; `across := [2]i64{point[first] - from[first], point[second] - from[second]}`; `radius := i64(integer_square_root(u64(across.x * across.x + across.y * across.y)))`; `radial` = `across` normalised (`{UNIT_VECTOR_ONE, 0}` when `radius == 0`); `section := round_section(volume)`; the outward faces `start_outward = {start.y, -start.x}` and `end_outward = {-end.y, end.x}` (away from the sector).
  1. Not in the sector (`!sector_contains`): the smaller of `sector_face_distance` for the start and for the end face (the start on a tie). Positive.
  2. In the sector, `closest, inside := section_closest_point(section, {radius, height})`. Outside: `offset2 := {radius - closest.x, height - closest.y}`, `distance = vector_length({offset2.x, offset2.y, 0})`, `normal = normalize_fixed(lift_across(axis, radial * offset2.x / UNIT_VECTOR_ONE, offset2.y))`. The nearest point of the full turn lies at the point's own angle, inside the sector, so no face is nearer.
  3. Inside: `depth, normal2 := section_depth(section, {radius, height})`, `normal = normalize_fixed(lift_across(axis, radial * normal2.x / UNIT_VECTOR_ONE, normal2.y))`; for a partial sector, each face's `sector_face_distance` less than `depth` replaces it with that face's outward normal (`lift_across(axis, outward, 0)`); `distance = -depth`.
  On the axis (`radius == 0`) the radial is the first perpendicular, so the normal is defined and the same on every machine.
- `volume_signed_distance :: proc(volume: Collision_Volume, point: [3]i64) -> (distance: i64, normal: [3]i64)`: `box_signed_distance(point, from, to)` for a box, `round_signed_distance` for a round.
- `volume_within_reach :: proc(volume: Collision_Volume, point: [3]i64, reach: i64) -> bool`: every axis of `point` within `bound_minimum - reach` to `bound_maximum + reach`.
- `body_signed_distance :: proc(body: Frame_Body, point: [3]i64, reach: i64) -> (distance: i64, normal: [3]i64, found: bool)`: `distance` starts at `reach`; every volume within reach whose signed distance is strictly less replaces it (`found` true); `normal` in the model's frame. Called by the probe and the ray.

Registration (called by the simulation, `entity.odin`):

- `register_frame_body :: proc(table: ^Frame_Table, body: Frame_Body)`: replaces the body of the same occupant handle, else inserts it before the first body with a larger handle (`inject_at`).
- `unregister_frame_body :: proc(table: ^Frame_Table, handle: Occupant_Handle)`: `ordered_remove` of the body with that handle, nothing when none.

Probe and ray:

- `frame_body_probe :: proc(table: ^Frame_Table, position: World_Position, reach: i64) -> Field_Surface_Probe`: `distance = reach`; for each body: `local := frame_local_position(body.frame, position)`; skipped when `vector_length(local - body.centre) > body.bound + reach`; `body_signed_distance(body, body_point_from_frame(body, local), reach)`; a strictly nearer one becomes the probe with `normal = frame_world_direction(body.frame, body_direction_to_frame(body.rotation, normal))` and `found = true`. None for a nil table.
- `raycast_frame_body :: proc(body: Frame_Body, origin: World_Position, direction: [3]i64, reach: i64) -> (distance: i64, frame_normal: [3]i64, hit: bool)`: none when `vector_length(frame_local_position(body.frame, origin) - body.centre) > body.bound + reach`; else in the model's frame (`start := body_point_from_frame(body, frame_local_position(...))`, `step := body_direction_from_frame(body.rotation, frame_local_direction(body.frame, direction))`), `travelled` from 0, at most `COLLISION_RAY_STEPS` times: `gap, normal, found := body_signed_distance(body, start + step * travelled / UNIT_VECTOR_ONE, reach - travelled + COLLISION_RAY_HIT_UNITS)`; not found: no hit; `gap <= COLLISION_RAY_HIT_UNITS`: hit at `travelled` (0 for a ray starting inside), `frame_normal = body_direction_to_frame(body.rotation, normal)`; else `travelled += gap`, no hit once `travelled > reach`. The steps are the exact distances, so the ray never passes through a volume; a ray grazing a face for more than 64 steps misses.
- `face_of_frame_normal :: proc(normal: [3]i64) -> Direction`: the axis of the largest absolute component (x before y before z on a tie) and its sign (`.Positive_X` for +x and so on).

### The geometry, worked

All in integers; `UNIT_VECTOR_ONE` is 2^24. The largest values: a footprint of 12 cells at the largest pitch (`MAXIMUM_FOUNDATION_PITCH_MILLIMETRES`, 2000 mm) is 98304 position units (2^16.6); squared sums stay under 2^35, a segment's `d * n` under 2^52, a unit vector times a coordinate under 2^41, all inside an i64. The integer square root is `integer_square_root` (`generation_planet.odin`); lengths and normals are `vector_length` and `normalize_fixed` (`world_field_vector.odin`), as the cells use.

- A point to the frame: `frame_local_position(frame, p)` (the dot products with the axes); to the model: `body_direction_from_frame(rotation, local - centre)`; a normal back: `frame_world_direction(frame, body_direction_to_frame(rotation, n))`. With rotation 1 (the pod's `POD_ROTATION`) the model's +x is the frame's +z (forward), as `model_transform` draws it.
- A round volume's section edges and their outward normals, for the cone from radius 1000 at height 0 to 500 at height 1000 (solid): edge 1 runs (1000, 0) to (500, 1000), `d = (-500, 1000)`, outward normal `(1000, 500) / 1118`; the point at radius 1000, height 500 has `cross(d, p - c) = -500 * 500 - 1000 * 0 = -250000 < 0` (outside), the segment's closest point `(800, 400)`, offset `(200, 100)`, distance `vector_length` 223 (floor of 223.6), normal `(0.894, 0.447)` times `UNIT_VECTOR_ONE` in (radial, axis).
- The shell's inside: a cylinder shell of radius 1000 and shell 200 about y from 0 to 1000; at radius 500, height 500 the point lies outside the section (edge 3, the inner surface from (800, 1000) to (800, 0), gives `cross = (0)(...) - (-1000)(500 - 800) < 0`), closest `(800, 500)`, distance 300, normal minus the radial: the inner surface holds the point inside the hollow.
- A sector's face: sector 30 to 330 degrees, a point at angle 25 degrees (outside the sector), radius 900, mid height: the nearest face is the start (30 degrees), `s = 900 cos 5 = 896.6`, inside the section's radius band, so the distance is the perpendicular `900 sin 5 = 78.4` and the normal the start face's outward `(sin 30, -cos 30)` across the axis.
