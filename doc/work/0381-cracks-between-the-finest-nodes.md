# 0381: Cracks between the finest nodes

Status: verified (2026-11-13)

Found in 0327's landing capture and traced by 0380's design (2026-11-12): single pixels of the globe's colour show through the ground along the border between two level 0 nodes, where no skirt hangs. Each node is drawn with its own translation (`render_field.odin`, `rl.MatrixTranslate(origin)`; `field.vs`, `mvp * vertexPosition`), so at 8 km from the planet's centre a vertex two nodes share comes out a fraction of a millimetre apart in each, and a few pixel centres fall between the two edges. The cracks are on every such border and show only against a dark floor, as in a cave. The mechanism is read from the matrix path; no capture of the camera matrices confirmed it.

## Goal

A vertex two nodes share lands on the same pixel from both, so no border shows through.

## Controls

None.

## Change

- Draw the nodes so a shared vertex has the same eye relative bits in both (one eye relative origin for the pass, or positions snapped to a grid both nodes share). At a spacing of 333 mm a position unit is not a binary fraction of a metre, which the design has to handle.
- Docs: `doc/presentation.md` (The field session).

## Verify

- The checks and the suite, with a test that two nodes' shared vertices transform to equal clip positions.
- The cave floor capture of 0380 before and after, sent to the user.

## Specification (design, 2026-11-12)

Designed against `main` at `082bd44` and 0349's specification as approved on 2026-11-12, read together with its implementer's tree in `.claude/worktrees/0349` (`field_node_draw_box`, the terrain box, `draw_field` returning the visible list, `draw_field_passes` with a `switch`). Implemented on 0349's tree.

### The mechanism

The item's mechanism holds, with one correction of where it rounds and a second source at 333 mm.

- Same-level neighbours compute a shared vertex from the same inputs in the same order. With A on B's negative side, B's cell −1 is A's cell 31: the same eight samples (each grid's shell holds the neighbour's samples, copied from the same chunks or generated by the same function), the same integer code (`field_cell_offset`), so the two integer positions differ by exactly 32 × 256 units, the node's size. The meshes meet along the layer of those cells, each quad owned by one node, so with equal coordinates their union is watertight.
- A border towards a coarser node is different: the grids differ and the flaps and curtains, a cell wide, cover the seam, so a sub-millimetre offset never opens there. Hence the cracks only between nodes of one level, where no skirt hangs.
- The float path gives the two copies different coordinates. `field_mesh_from_surface` writes metres from the node's origin, the origin is f32 metres, and `draw_field` passes `rl.MatrixTranslate(origin)` to `DrawMesh`, which multiplies it with the camera's view on the CPU in f32 (`MatrixMultiply(transform, view)`). The model view's translation is o·R + t, terms of about 8,000 m whose f32 step is 0.49 mm, rounded five times and differently for each node's origin. The shader's `mvp * vertexPosition` then adds only small local terms: the rounding is in the CPU product, not in the shader.
- Size: an f32 emulation of raylib's `MatrixLookAt` and that product at the capture's place (79.007 N, 151.054 E, 8 km, 1 m spacing, the border x = −1344 m between level 0 nodes x −43 and −42; 4,000 border vertices within 6 m of eyes looking at them) puts the two copies 0.22 mm apart in eye space at the median, 0.41 mm at the 90th percentile and 0.98 mm at most, none bit equal; on screen 0.029 px at the median at 1080 lines, 0.063 px at the 90th. Where the copies separate rather than overlap, about one pixel centre in 30 along the border falls between the edges: the capture's sparse row of the globe's dots.
- At 333 mm a second source: 0.333 m is not a binary fraction, so a node's origin in metres is rounded by up to 0.24 mm at 8 km and its positions (n × f32(step × 333 / 256000)) by up to a micrometre. The copies differ before any matrix. At 1000 and 500 mm both are exact.

### The fix

Each viewport's terrain and water passes draw from an anchor, the sample nearest the eye. A node's positions go up in samples from its first sample; `field.vs` adds the node's offset from the anchor in whole samples, then applies one matrix for the pass: the viewport's projection times the camera's own view re-expressed about the anchor, computed in f64 from the view's f32 entries and rounded once.

- Exact: a position is n × 2^(L−8) samples with |n| under 2^14 (cells −1 to 32 and the skirts' reach), an offset whole samples, so their sum is a multiple of 2^(L−8) and exact in f32 within 2^(16+L) samples of the anchor (level 0: 65,536 samples, 21.8 km at 333 mm, all of an 8 km planet). Any IEEE binary32 adder returns a representable sum exactly, so both copies of a shared vertex are the same bits and, under the one matrix uniform, so is `gl_Position`. GL's rasterization rules make an edge with identical window coordinates watertight.
- `invariant gl_Position` forbids the compiler from regrouping `vertexPosition + node_offset` into per term products, which it may do otherwise.
- Residual: past 2^16 samples at level 0 (only on a 16 km planet at 333 mm, for nodes another viewport selected over 21.8 km away, behind the globe) a sum rounds by at most 1/256 of a sample, under a ten thousandth of a pixel there.
- The picture: the anchored view is the camera's view matrix, the one every other pass uses, evaluated about the anchor. The terrain's eye space positions become that matrix applied to the exact world position, within 3e-7 of the distance plus a micrometre (emulated worst 1.7e-7). They move against the old path by its per node rounding, at most 1.13 mm in the emulation at all three spacings.
- The shading's inputs stay: the world position is the node's origin in metres plus its positions times the metres of a sample, which at 1000 and 500 mm is the old `matModel * vertexPosition` bit for bit (one rounding of v + o) and at 333 mm within one f32 step of the world coordinate (0.49 mm); the normal is the vertex's (the old `mat3(matModel)` was the identity).
- 333 mm: the positions are samples, so the spacing never enters the sum, only the anchored view's rotation columns (× f32(0.333)), one value for the pass. At 1000 mm the arrays hold the same bits as today (step / 256 is what `metres_per_unit` was).

Rejected:

- One eye relative origin with positions in metres: exact at 1000 and 500 mm only; at 333 mm the nodes' sums already differ.
- Snapping in the shader to the 256th of a cell both nodes share, or converting metres back to samples at upload: exact, but recovers per vertex what the mesher's float conversion threw away; samples are the same numbers without the round trip.
- A skirt on same-level borders: removed on purpose (a straight leg leaving a curved surface stood in the air, Seams), costs triangles and hides a gap only from some angles.
- The anchored translation from the exact eye, −R·(eye − anchor) in f64: more precise, but moves the terrain against every pass still on the camera's matrix by that matrix's own rounding (up to about 1 mm), where things stand on the ground.
- Double precision matrices: GLSL 3.30 and ES 3.00 have none.
- raylib's `mvp` with rlgl's modelview set to the anchored view: global state the cover and the globe, drawn between the terrain and the water, would have to restore; an own uniform leaves rlgl alone.
- The sun's shadow pass anchored too: its cracks are sub-millimetre under texels of 6.4 to 9.8 cm read 16 at a time, and anchoring it means `sun_shadow.vs`, which the trees, the models and the foundations share.

### The passes

- Terrain and water: anchored. They share `field.vs`, so the water keeps its relation to the terrain.
- The sun's shadow pass (`draw_sun_shadow_terrain`): the terrain's arrays are now samples, so its model matrix is the origin with a scale by the metres of a sample (`field_node_transform`), at 1000 mm bit for bit `rl.MatrixTranslate(origin)` as today; at 333 and 500 mm its depth moves by micrometres, under the receivers' half texel bias. The kept map's key: `field_mesh_depth_hash` hashes the positions, now samples. At 1000 mm the same bits; at 333 and 500 mm other values, but the hash lives in memory and is compared only with itself, so a map is kept and redrawn exactly as before.
- On the camera's matrix, unchanged: the cover (instances in world metres rising from the ground), the globe (8 m under the lowest relief), the trees and their tiles, the foundations, the machines, 0360's flat batch (runs, poles, torch boxes), the players, the flames and the ghosts. The terrain moves against them by at most the old per node rounding (about 1 mm, 0.2 mm typical), the size of their own rounding today. Nothing is drawn coplanar with the ground (the source holds no polygon offset, decal or depth bias outside the shadow receivers), so nothing starts to z-fight or float.
- Culling: in world metres as before; 0349's boxes scale the meshes' bounds, now samples, by the metres of a sample.
