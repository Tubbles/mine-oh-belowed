# Presentation

What the game draws and plays. It reads the world, the tick, the render time and the camera; the simulation never reads it back, and nothing here is saved (render state lives in `Frame_State` and resets with the session). Texture and sound values: [content.md](content.md).

- Every pass follows the no perceivable repetition principle ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md)).
- Values (sizes, periods, rates, colours) are the constants atop each file named below.

## Frame order

Inside the 3D pass, in order: the sky, the solid chunks, entities and models, the bottleneck markers, fluid and power entities, belts, loose items, torch and furnace flames, fish shadows, the water pass, birds, insect motes, particles, weather, the player overlay. Then the hands, the underwater tint, the HUD and the UI (`loop.odin`).

A field session's viewport (0179, The field session, below) draws its own 3D pass instead; the HUD and the UI follow as in the block world.

## The field session

A field session (0179, [architecture.md](architecture.md), The field session) draws through `loop_field_session.odin`; the block world's renderer is not called, and the sounds and cues, which read the block world, are off; the field's only sounds are the arrival's and the hatches' (The arrival).

- Before the viewports (`prepare_field_frame`): the sun's shadow map is released when the setting turned it off or its size changed (`serve_sun_shadows`, Light on the field, 0328); the haze's end is set to the frame's view distance (0343, Field meshes); the finished field meshes go up; each player's crouch progress (`Field_Renderer.crouch_progress`, 0218) moves towards its crouch flag as predicted (`advance_field_crouch`).
- Per viewport (`draw_field_viewport_world`): the field camera of the viewport's player (interpolated as the block camera is; the eye dips to the crouch's eye over `CROUCH_EASE_SECONDS`, 0.15 s of frame time smoothstepped, `field_player_view`), the frame's open sky light and haze for that camera (`field_frame_lights`, Light on the field), the sky dome turned about the player's up (`field_sky_camera`) with the haze's glow (`Sky_Glow`), then the clip planes `FIELD_NEAR_METRES` to `FIELD_FAR_RADII` planet radii (the block world's are restored after), then the sun's shadow map for that camera (`draw_field_sun_shadows`, Light on the field, 0328) before the viewport's 3D pass, with the split screen viewport's target bound again after it (`resume_viewport_target`).
- The scene (`draw_field_scene`), in order: the working arms', machines' and torches' lights gathered round the camera (`gather_field_scene_point_lights`, none from the machines during the fall, the torches' from those within `FLAME_DRAW_DISTANCE_METRES`, at their flames' middle in `FIELD_TORCH_LIGHT_COLOR`, reaching `FIELD_TORCH_LIGHT_RADIUS_METRES`, flickering with their flames and not moved by the pod's transform, `field_torch_point_light`, 0284), the open sky on the model shader (`set_model_open_sky`, 0313), the globe (`draw_field_globe`), the field (terrain, its ground cover, then water), the foundations (`draw_frames`, each cell a flat slab in its record's `color`, `foundation_slab_color`, 0196), the machines on frames (`draw_entities`, lit by the open sky: `Model_Frame.open_sky` gives every model the full sky light, since the field's light is not in the block light grid, except inside a pod: the pod's model, its fixtures' and the bodies of players standing in its footprint take the pod's `interior_light_share` of that light, `gather_interior_lights` once per scene, `posed_model_light`, `field_player_interior_light_share`, 0225), the trees (`draw_field_trees`, below), the runs and poles, the torches' small glowing boxes (`FIELD_TORCH_SIZE_METRES`), every player's body (the player model's limbs, seated in the chair as The player draws it, or a capsule when it did not load; a crouched body is the model squashed along the up to the crouch height, since the standing legs do not fold, or the capsule at that height, eased as the eye, 0218) but the viewer's in first person or with its camera pulled in to the body (`Field_Scene.viewer_body_shown`, `field_player_body_drawn`, 0261), then, with the pod's transform popped, the open sky off on the model shader (`clear_model_open_sky`), so the ghosts keep their own colour, the flames in one list (`draw_field_flames`, Machine models, Flames): the working machines', moved by the pod's transform on the CPU as their models are, and those of the torches within `FLAME_DRAW_DISTANCE_METRES`, above their boxes, the block torch's scaled as the box is to its post (`FIELD_TORCH_FLAME_SCALE`), drawn before the ghosts so a ghost's depth never hides them; and last the viewer's ghosts (the run tool's curve, the frame placement's footprint, in the block ghost's refused colour when `field_placement_refusal` would refuse it, `frame_ghost_color`). With the placement editor (0215, `Field_Scene.placement_editor`, `draw_placement_editor_ghost`) the footprint gives way to the editor's ghost: before the anchor low walls round the footprint's lowest row (`draw_footprint_outline`, `footprint_outline_boxes`, 0219: four walls a cell tall and a tenth of a cell thick just inside its edges, lifted 0.02 cells off the cells' floor, see through and drawn without writing depth, under an opaque rim 0.04 cells high, so a slope does not hide them) with a chevron along the machine's front at the rim's top (`machine_front_direction`, as long as 0.6 of the shorter side; none for a foundation block), white, red where the placement would be refused (`placement_outline_color`), static; anchored, the machine's model on its frame in the ghost colour drawn without writing depth, so its far side shows through its near side (`draw_frame_ghost_model`), and a box per cell for a foundation block, an arm machine or a machine without a model.
- The trees (0197, 0310, `render_field_trees.odin`): the standing trees round the eyes come in tiles of `field_tree_tile_cells` tree cubes an edge (about `FIELD_TREE_TILE_METRES`, 128 m), generated on the field's workers when no chunk job waits (Threads and chunk streaming in [architecture.md](architecture.md)) and kept by the main thread (`update_field_tree_cache`, in `prepare_field_frame` and the preview's stream step, before any viewport draws): the tiles within `far_metres` of an eye, nearest first, at most `FIELD_TREE_TILES_PENDING_LIMIT` (8) on the workers and `FIELD_TREE_UPLOADS_PER_FRAME` (4) going up a frame (`upload_field_tree_tiles`), evicted two tiles past `far_metres`. The far distance is `field_trees_view_row`'s (0343): the row's `far_metres` cut to the view distance (Field meshes) and its `far_fade_metres` to a quarter of that, so a shorter view evicts the tiles past it and scans fewer. A tile holds its keys and an instance a tree (`tree_instance`: the tree's transform at its yaw and scale with its species and shade in the bottom row) on the CPU and in a vertex buffer; a felled tree drops out of its tile, which goes up again. Per viewport `draw_field_trees` draws the near trees, the standing ones within `near_metres` in the frustum, at most `near_limit` nearest first with ties by key (`update_near_trees`), instanced with their species' model (a machine of kind `tree`, Machine models). Each viewport keeps a view of the cache (`Tree_View`, four, 0346): its near candidates, the standing trees within `near_metres` and an eighth of it more round where the camera stood, are held while the camera stays within that eighth and no change of the tiles (a tile arriving or leaving, a tree felled, kept as boxes in a ring of `FIELD_TREE_CHANGE_RING`) reaches them, and each frame the cut runs exactly over them (`cut_near_trees`), so the drawn set is always what a fresh scan would select. The selection goes into one instance buffer a view and pass, written only when it changed, packed by species and drawn a species at a time through the model's own vertex array (`draw_tree_pass`). The rest draw as cards: the tiles within `far_metres` in the frustum, nearest first, while their trees stay within `far_limit` (`select_far_tiles`), a draw a tile of three crossed quads a tree (along forward, along right, from above). The cards are baked from each tree model when the machine models load (`render_tree_cards.odin`): orthographic, 128 texel cells with a 2 texel margin in a 512 texel wide atlas of a row a model, two passes of colour bled into the empty texels, mips down to 1 by 1 whose alpha is scaled so each cell keeps level 0's share at or above 128, and up through `load_rgba_texture`, trilinear. Both through the tree shader (`data/shaders/tree.vs`, `tree.fs`): a dither crosses a tree from its model to its card over `fade_metres` before the near distance (the nearer of `near_metres` and the first tree the cap left out) so none doubles or vanishes, the cards fade out the same way over the last `far_fade_metres` before the far distance, the card's normal runs from the crown, and both are lit as the models (the open sky, the haze, the point lights and the torchlight, the same text as `model.fs`) times the species' tint and the tree's shade. The distances and the caps are `field_trees` of `data/game.sjson`, a row for the desktop and one for Android (`far_metres` 512 and `far_limit` 40000 there, against 1024 and 160000). A species whose model did not load draws no model and no card. The planet preview's screenshot waits for the tiles to settle as for the meshes.
- The ground cover (0311, `world_field_cover.odin`, `render_field_cover.odin`): grass tufts, flowers, ferns, pebbles, dry stones, boulders and ore specks, presentation only (nothing collides; nothing reaches the simulation, the hash or the save). The finest level's mesh job places them on the triangles it just made (`place_field_cover`, Field meshes), so they arrive with the terrain under its revision and an edit clears them through the remesh it already causes. Per triangle and kind the count is the area times the kind's density plus a hashed share, the hashes keyed on the triangle's grid edge in global samples, so a remesh gives the same instances and nothing lines up on a lattice. The density (`cover_kind_density`) is the kind's rate times its tents over the temperature, the moisture and the slope (full inside the range, falling linearly over the fade outside it), the shares of the surfaces it grows on (grass, sand, snow, rock, stone and the three ores, split as `field.fs` splits the topsoil, `cover_ground_shares`), a boundary term that gathers a kind where its surfaces meet others (pebbles along the rock's edge) and a patch noise of its own, so grass and flowers come in drifts. An instance of a kind growing on less than the full snow is buried where `field.fs` draws snow (`cover_snow_buries`, 0335): the shader blends the snow over the whole ground by its weight, so 0.5 already reads as snow; at the instance's own point (`cover_point_snow`, the shader's term) such a kind thins from `COVER_SNOW_THIN_WEIGHT` (0.05) to none at `COVER_SNOW_GONE_WEIGHT` (0.25), times its share not growing on snow, against a hash of the instance. A triangle carries none with a corner under water, in air under half the full sky light (`COVER_LEAST_SKY_LIGHT`, a cave's or a carved overhang's) or cut off the generated surface (the look's offset: thinning from `COVER_CUT_MILLIMETRES`, 200 mm, none past twice it); an instance whose base, middle or top lies in an occupied frame cell is dropped, the frames reaching the job through the streaming ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Threads and chunk streaming), so a foundation clears the grass under it while the terrain under it stays. An instance is a transform with its rank, its light and shade and its colour in the bottom row; per node each kind's instances are sorted by rank and cut to the row's `node_limit`. An instance is drawn out to its kind's far less the span to its near times its rank and shrinks into its base over the last fifth of that (`cover.vs`), so the density falls from the near to the far and nothing pops; every kind ends `COVER_LEVEL_MARGIN_METRES` (8 m) inside the first level's distance (`cover_reach`), so no cover is lost at a level change, and a node draws only the prefix of the rank step its nearest point can show (`cover_draw_count`). `draw_field_cover` draws after the terrain and before the water, kind by kind, an instanced draw (`render_instancing.odin`, shared with the trees) per visible node, foliage without back face culling. The shapes are stand-ins built in code per kind (`cover_shape_vertices`): blades, a flower (a stem keeping its own green under a head of five triangles taking the instance's colour), fern fronds, a pebble (a jittered icosahedron) and a crystal (an octahedron); no textures and no discard. `cover.vs` leans each foliage part by a hash of the rank and the part, so no two tufts share a silhouette. The kinds are the planet's `cover` ([content.md](content.md), Planets); the share of the distance, the share of the ranks and the node limit are `field_cover` of `data/game.sjson`, a row for the desktop and one for Android (60 % of the distance, 45 % of the density, 4096 a node). The planet preview's line counts the instances drawn (`cover`).
- One node selection serves every viewport: `Field_View.more_cameras` holds the other eyes and a node's distance is its nearest eye's (`field_viewport_selection`). The view distance is the process's setting, so every viewport on a machine draws to one view (0343).

### Light on the field

- The open sky's light (0313, `outdoor_light`, the same text in `field.fs` and `model.fs`): the sky's light from above and the ground's bounce from below, mixed by the normal's facing of the local up (`0.5 + 0.5 * dot(normal, up)`, the up the position's direction, the planet's centre being the origin), plus the sun's light times `max(0, dot(normal, sun_direction))`. The colours follow the clock (`render_day.odin`): the sun's runs from `sun_high_color` to `sun_low_color` as its height (`sun_direction`'s y) falls below `SUN_HIGH_HEIGHT` (0.5) and fades out between `SUN_LIGHT_RISEN_HEIGHT` and `SUN_LIGHT_SET_HEIGHT` (0.05 and -0.05), times `sun_strength_percent` (`sun_light_color`); the sky's is the dome's horizon and zenith mixed two thirds towards the zenith (`SKY_LIGHT_ZENITH_SHARE`, the cosine weighted mean of a dome linear in the sine of the elevation) times `sky_light_percent` (`sky_light_color`), so it matches the sky the player sees and at night is the night dome's, about 5 percent and blue, with no moon term, so the torchlight stays needed; the bounce is the globe's colour lit by the sun above the horizon and by the sky, times `ground_bounce_percent` (`ground_bounce_color`). Flat ground at noon takes about 1.35 of its albedo, a slope away from the sun the sky's blue and the bounce, never black; at dawn and dusk the sun is half `sun_low_color` and grazes.
- The terrain (`field.fs`, the terrain and the water pass alike): the open sky's light times the vertex's sky light, against the block light per channel, the brighter taken and never added, so a torch-lit wall keeps its light whichever way it faces; then the point lights and the torchlight add on top. The sky light gates the open sky: a cave's samples take neither sky nor sun, the cheap shadow. The ground cover (`cover.fs`, 0311) is lit and hazed as the terrain, the same text as `field.fs`, from its triangle's mean sky and block light times its hashed shade; a foliage instance takes its up as its normal, so it reads as the ground it stands on. On the water a glint adds in the sun's colour where the half vector of the sun and the eye meets the normal to the power of 48 times 0.6 (the shader's water glint power and strength, constants for the water's look items to revisit), so the sheet and the waves catch the sun.
- The models (`model.fs`, `outdoor_on`): the CPU's light path stays (the face shade shared with the block world, `model_light_tint`, an idle emissive layer following the light). On the field the open sky's light on an upward face, clamped to 1 (`Field_Frame_Lights.flat_light`), is `Model_Frame.sky_tint` with a `day_factor` of 1, and the shader divides that flat light out (`daylight_tint`, `model_frame_daylight_tint`) and multiplies in the open sky's light by the face's normal, so trees and machines have a lit and a shade side; the face shade stays, so a side face reads about 0.8 of the terrain under the same light, and `MINIMUM_MODEL_BRIGHTNESS` keeps them a little brighter than the ground at night. `set_model_open_sky` turns it on for the scene up to the players, `clear_model_open_sky` off before the flames and the ghosts and in the block world (`draw_session_world`). The shader selects the open sky by `outdoor_on` rather than mixing by it: the uniforms are zero until a field session sets them, and the haze of zero uniforms is not a number. A model in a cave takes the open sky as it took the full sky light before.
- The globe (`draw_field_globe`): through the model shader's lit material, its colour times the flat light as an open sky model's, so it is lit and hazed as the terrain is.
- The haze (`apply_haze`, the same text in both shaders, `Field_Haze`): an aerial haze from the eye, `1 - exp(-reach * density * mean)`, with the density `1 / haze.metres` of air at the planet's radius and the mean the exact mean of `exp(-h / H)` along the ray, the height running linearly from the eye's to the fragment's (`H` the atmosphere's `scale_height_metres`, the sky's air), so a valley holds more haze than a crest and the view from above less. Its colour is the horizon of the sky at the eye's altitude (`altitude_day_sky`), what the dome and the clear show behind the terrain, plus a warm lobe towards the sun (`haze_sun_glow_color`: `sun_low_color` at `sun_glow_percent` while the sun is within 0.2 of the horizon, `twilight_share`, to the power `HAZE_SUN_EXPONENT`, 6, of the facing). Over it the end fade (a smoothstep over 0.6, `FOG_START_SHARE`, to 1 of the last level of detail's distance, the view distance of Field meshes, 0343) goes to that sky colour in the air and, above the atmosphere's top, to the globe's colour lit by the open sky (`globe_color` times `outdoor_light` on an upward face), mixed by the sky's share at the eye (`atmosphere_sky_share`), so the far nodes, the globe and the horizon read as one from the ground and the drawn terrain blends into the globe during the arrival's first seconds. The terrain's aerial haze is scaled by its sky light (a cave's air scatters no sky colour), the models' is full; the end fade is full on every surface (0354), since past the view lies only the end colour, so a carved landmark's unlit faces fade at the view's end as the lit ground does. The foundations' slabs, the runs and poles, the torch boxes, the flames, the debris and the emissive layers stay unhazed (about 8 percent at the trees' 128 m at the radius's height).
- The dome's glow (`Sky_Glow`, `draw_field_sky`): the field's dome adds the same lobe round the sun to each vertex and reaches below the horizon (Sky and day), so the glow runs on across the horizon line into the hazed far terrain and the globe without a band; the block world passes none.
- The sun's frame: the sky is drawn about the viewer's up (`field_sky_camera`, `up_to_sky_rotation` in `render_day.odin`), so the sun the field lights by is `sun_direction(fraction)` turned back by the inverse of that rotation, per viewport (`field_sun_world_direction`); noon is overhead for every longitude at once. `Field_Frame_Lights` (`render_point_lights.odin`, `field_frame_lights` from `Field_Light_Inputs`, which `field_light_inputs` fills from the renderer, the clock's sky at the ground and the data) carries the sun's unit direction and light, the sky's light, the bounce, the flat light, the haze and the torchlight; `draw_field` sets them on the field shader each frame (`set_field_frame_lights`, `set_open_sky_uniforms` with the `Open_Sky_Uniform` locations), before the terrain so the water pass shares them, and `set_model_open_sky` the same on the model shader, from `Field_Scene.lights`, which the viewport fills for the camera it draws with. The planet preview's sun stands at noon over its camera, the sun's and the sky's light scaled by `--planet-preview-daylight` (`Field_Light_Inputs.daylight`), and its frame is cleared to the noon horizon.
- The sun's shadow (0328, `render_sun_shadows.odin`): one depth map a viewport over a cascade round the eye, not fitted to the frustum, so turning the camera redraws nothing. The receivers are the sphere of `radius_metres` round the camera, the shadow fading out over its last quarter (`SUN_SHADOW_FADE_SHARE`), and the map's half size is the radius plus `SUN_SHADOW_MARGIN_TEXELS` (4) texels: a texel is 6.3 cm on the desktop row. The light's axes are the sun and the sun path's axis turned into the world as the sun is (`field_sun_path_world_axis`, always square to the sun, so the basis never degenerates); the centre is the eye moved in the light's plane to the nearest whole texel (in f64; not along the sun), so a walk inside a texel draws the same map and the shadow's edges do not crawl (`sun_shadow_cascade`). The ortho box runs from the sphere's sun side to its far side, so the depth's bits cover only the receivers; a caster up to `caster_reach_metres` beyond that near plane towards the sun is drawn and pressed onto it (`sun_shadow.vs` clamps its depth), so a ridge or a tree towards a low sun still shadows the sphere. The casters (`draw_field_sun_shadows`, depth only, both faces, since models may be open shells): the selected nodes' terrain whose box meets the casters' box (`sun_shadow_box_casts`), the standing trees in it instanced, nearest the eye first up to `tree_limit` whatever their distance band, so a tree drawn as a card casts its model (`update_shadow_trees`: the view's casting candidates are gathered in the casters' box grown by the near trees' margin, held while the cascade's move plus the box's span times the turn of the light's axes stays within that margin, about 8 s of the sun's turn with the eye still, and no change of the tiles reaches the grown box, and cut exactly each frame like the near trees), the foundations, the machines whose box (the footprint up to the model's top, a cell wider each way, `sun_shadow_entity_box`) meets it (`Model_Frame.casters`, so a large factory costs only what stands near the eye) and every player's body, the viewer's in first person too, under the pod's transform as the scene draws them; the batch's boxes and capsules go through the depth shader too, so they are pressed as well. Not drawn: the cards, the water, the ground cover (it only receives), the runs and poles, the torches, the flames, the ghosts, the debris, the arrival's wake and flakes and the globe. A receiver (`sun_shadow_light`, the same text in `field.fs`, `model.fs`, `tree.fs` and `cover.fs`) moves `SUN_SHADOW_NORMAL_OFFSET_TEXELS` (2) along its shading normal and `SUN_SHADOW_DEPTH_BIAS_TEXELS` (0.5) towards the sun in depth and compares the 4 by 4 texels round it, weighted as the mean of the 3 by 3 bilinear lookups, an edge three texels soft: 16 reads on a plain `highp sampler2D` (rlgl offers no compare mode, and `sampler2DShadow` would be a new driver path on Gladio and the phone). The shadow scales the sun's term of `outdoor_light` only (its `sunlit`) and the water's glint, never the sky's or the bounce: a full shadow takes `strength_percent` of the sun, so flat ground at noon keeps about half its light at 70; a face turned from the sun reads nothing. The trees' cards cast through their models but receive none (`tree.fs`): their planes run through the casting model's crown, which would shade their sun side. The setting (`settings.sun_shadows`, [ui.md](ui.md), Display) takes the build's row's `on` for default: on for the desktop builds, the Steam Deck's included, off on Android. Off, and while the sun gives no light, there is no pass and every receiving shader pays one compare; off releases the map between frames, as a changed `map_texels` does, the receivers' material slot (`SUN_SHADOW_MAP_SLOT`, texture unit 2) cleared first. A map that does not load (its shaders, an incomplete framebuffer) logs one line and the field draws without shadows until the setting or the size changes. Split screen draws the map again before each viewport, twice the cost; the planet preview draws it with its row's default before its frame.
- The torchlight (`Spot_Light`, `torchlight_spot`): a cone from the viewer's eye along its view, full inside the inner half angle and gone past the outer (a smoothstep between their cosines), falling off by the inverse square from a 4 m reference (half at 4 m, a seventh at 10 m, a hundredth at 40 m) and ended smoothly at the range, half its facing wrapped as the point lights do, in `TORCHLIGHT_COLOR` (a warm white) times the strength. Off, every field is zero and the shader pays one compare. The field shader and the model shader take the same six `spot_` uniforms and the same `spot_light` text (`shader_source_test.odin` compares them), set on the models' lit layer with the scene's point lights (`set_model_spot_light`), so a dug-out machine at night shows in the beam, and the block world clears the spot with the point lights. The toggle is the viewport's render state (`Viewport_Presentation.torchlight`, [input.md](input.md), Gamepad) and never reaches the simulation.
- The keys, `sun_and_torchlight`, `haze` and `sun_shadows` in `data/game.sjson` ([content.md](content.md), World): `sun_strength_percent` 100, `sun_high_color` [255, 243, 222], `sun_low_color` [255, 150, 72], `sky_light_percent` 60, `ground_bounce_percent` 60, `torchlight_strength_percent` 100, `torchlight_inner_cone_degrees` 28, `torchlight_outer_cone_degrees` 36, `torchlight_range_metres` 40; `metres` 1500, `sun_glow_percent` 50; `strength_percent` 70, the desktop row on with `map_texels` 2048, `radius_metres` 64, `caster_reach_metres` 96 and `tree_limit` 2048, the Android row off with 1024, 48, 64 and 512.

### The arrival

A new world's fall (0200, `render_arrival.odin`, [architecture.md](architecture.md), The field session, The arrival) is drawn from `Field_Arrival`, the tick and the interpolation alpha alone (`arrival_view`); nothing here writes the simulation, and the hash is the same with and without it (`test_the_arrivals_presentation_leaves_the_hash`). Offline the pause menu stops the tick, so the fall stands while it is open.

- Phases: Descent from the start to `arrival_settle_ticks` before the planned landing (the hit), Settled from the hit for `arrival_settled_seconds` (until the dust has settled and the last clod has sunk, 166 s shipped), then None. A fall skipped before its planned end (`landed_tick` before it) is None at once: no crash, no dust and no debris, and no doors' sound, since the landing leaves the doors closed (0222). It keeps the soot its fall had reached on the portholes (0273).
- Descent: the pod is drawn base first along the curve's tangent, its up against the travel and the chair's porthole on the travel's side of the motion's plane, easing by slerp into the pose it rests in over the last `arrival_real_seconds` (`Arrival_View.rest_share`, smoothed), about its base centre moved by the path's offset (`arrival_pod_transform`, 0270); the frames, the machines, the players and the windows are drawn under that transform (`Field_Scene.pod_transform`, pushed on the `rlgl` stack), so the cabin travels and turns with the eye and its lamps light it. The viewport's camera is the seated player's first person camera, its position and target moved by the same transform and its up the player's (the planet's), so a sideways look keeps the horizon level and nothing snaps at the hit, where the simulation's rested frame takes over (architecture.md, The arrival). The path is the entry's curve (0269, `arrival_descent_offset` from `arrival_curve_at`, content.md, The arrival): the offset is the curve's altitude along the pod frame's up and the range left behind its travel heading (the chair's facing, `pod_travel_heading`), so the pod meets the floor exactly at the hit; the progress (linear in the descent) reaches the curve through `arrival_curve_progress`, so the space and the entry are stretched and the last `arrival_real_seconds` play 1:1. The travel is the curve's tangent (`arrival_travel_direction`). raylib 6.2's `DrawMesh` sends the model shader the mesh transform times the `rlgl` matrix stack, so the lamps are moved with the models they light (`moved_point_light`, their clip boxes with them) and the nearest are picked round the moved camera. The stored camera stays the resting one, so the HUD's projections stay on the cabin. The node selection, the streaming and the tree cache stay round the resting eye (`field_viewport_eyes`), so the crater is meshed finest from the first frame and nothing remeshes along the path. The ground is whole through the descent (0271). The dug chunks remesh in the frames after the hit, under the settle's dust.
- The wake (0304, `render_arrival_wake.odin`, `data/shaders/arrival_wake.vs`, `arrival_wake.fs`): the entry's plasma sits where it burns (DESIGN.md, Fire), outside the pod, the same for every viewer. From the pod's `heat_shield` (content.md, Models) it is a tube of 32 flat quads about the pod's axis in the model frame (`arrival_wake_shape`, `arrival_wake_quad`), its flats 0.05 cells outside the rim, running straight aft from the rim at the base's width for two shield diameters (21.8 cells), and a fan of 32 triangles of the rim's radius 0.03 cells under the base, the shield's glow (`arrival_wake_shield_triangle`). The pod's +y is aft through the whole descent, so the wake turns with the pod through the rest easing as the flakes do; it stands 0.05 cells off the rim, 0.5 off the skirt and 3.1 off the cone's top, so no flame licks the cone's slanted walls. ## Chunk meshes

- Greedy meshing per chunk over the block atlas, remeshed on edit, culled per chunk. Only cubes join the greedy pass; slabs, stairs, posts and crosses mesh cell by cell, a border quad hidden by an opaque neighbour dropped.
- Texcoords run in blocks and repeat with `fract()` (`face_texcoord`); on side faces y runs down from the face's top, so a tile's top row is up on every side and a slab side shows the lower half.
- Water faces go to separate parts (`Chunk_Mesh_Data.water_parts`); lit posts become flame cells (`render_flames.odin`).
- Fog in the horizon colour ends one chunk inside `LOAD_RADIUS_HORIZONTAL` (`fog_distances`), so chunks fade into the sky.

|---|---|
| Colour red | Sky light |
| Colour green | The face's `Tile_Variation` (`orientation_flag_green`) |
| Colour blue | Ambient occlusion |
| Colour alpha | `SWAY_VERTEX_ALPHA` for vertices the wind sways (`sway_vertex_light`) |

- Sky light is scaled by the day factor and tinted by the sky tint; block light keeps its colour and takes each torch's fire flicker by nearness (0284): the 16 torches nearest the camera, from every chunk mesh, each with its flame's flicker at `BLOCK_LIGHT_FLICKER_SHARE` of its swing (`block_torch_flicker_uniform`, `block_torch_light_factor`, 0.93 to 1.10), blended by `torch_light_flicker` in `chunk.fs` and `water.fs` and falling to 1 past a torch's 15 blocks. It runs on the tick clock and stands at 1 under reduced motion, as do the portholes' flicker (`arrival_window_flicker`, through `fire_flicker`, whose flow runs calm, 0286) and the flames' flicker (0274), whose flow runs at its slow layer only.

### Field meshes

The terrain field (M13) is drawn beside the blocks, by the field session (0179, The field session, above) and the planet preview (`--planet-preview`, `loop_planet_preview.odin`, [build.md](build.md)).

- Mesher (`world_field_mesh.odin`): naive surface nets over a `Field_Grid`, the samples of a node and one more on every side. A cell the surface crosses gets one vertex at the mean of its edge crossings (linear in the `i8` densities, in 1/`FIELD_MESH_POSITION_UNITS` of a cell, integer throughout); each grid edge the surface crosses that starts inside the node becomes a quad between the four cells around it, wound against the density gradient. The quads reach the cells one step below the node, which the neighbour computes from the same samples, so neighbours of one level meet without a gap. Floats appear only in `field_mesh_from_surface`, the GPU's arrays.
- Per vertex: the normal against the cell's gradient (the density's change across the cell); the material weights, the share of the cell's ground corners per material (`Field_Surface_Vertex.weights`, one byte per material but air in the enum's order, `field_material_slot`, `FIELD_TEXTURED_MATERIAL_COUNT`: topsoil, stone, deep stone and bedrock as the tangent attribute, the three ores' bytes packed into one float as the texture coordinate's second component, `field_pack_bytes`, the free attributes raylib binds without extra code; every float uploaded is read); the colour, the strata's tint: without a climate the mean palette colour of the ground corners, the tint taken modulo the palette's length since a saved tint is not checked, with one the ground look's smooth tint (below); the ground's look (0307, `Field_Surface_Vertex.look`, zero without a climate), the grass colour packed into the second texture coordinate's first component and the snow, the sand and the natural share into its second (`Field_Mesh_Data.looks`), which `field.vs` unpacks (`unpack_bytes`; a packed float stays under 2^24, exact); the vertex light (0173, `field_vertex_light`): the mean of each channel of the field light over the cell's air corners (ground holds no light, so counting it would halve every surface's light), the sky light in the colour's alpha and the block light in the texture coordinate's first component (`Field_Mesh_Data.lights`). The water's mesh takes the terrain's light at the same corners. A coarse grid takes the light of the loaded samples it reads and gives a generated sample full sky light in air and none in ground and none in carved air under a roof (0324), as the generation does ([architecture.md](architecture.md), The field light).
- The ground's look (0307, `world_field_ground_look.odin`): with a climate ([content.md](content.md), Planets) the mesh job gives every vertex, after the surface and before the skirts (which copy their border vertex, so no skirt reads as cut ground), its look from its world position (`write_field_ground_look`): the climate there at its own height (`planet_climate`, [architecture.md](architecture.md), World generation), moved by a variation noise of `GROUND_VARIATION_WAVELENGTH_METRES` (48 m, two octaves) by up to `GROUND_VARIATION_CELSIUS` and `GROUND_VARIATION_MOISTURE`; the grass colour, bilinear over the climate's 3 by 3 anchors (`field_grass_color`), its brightness moved by `GROUND_VARIATION_BRIGHTNESS`; the snow's cover from the temperature against the snow line, the sand's from the height over the sea against the sand line moved by `GROUND_VARIATION_SAND_METRES`, both fading over their fade (`field_snow_cover`, `field_sand_cover`); the strata's tint, a noise of `GROUND_TINT_WAVELENGTH_METRES` (256 m) read as a continuous index along the palette, linear between its entries, in place of the palette's 256 m cubes (`field_ground_tint`, also the arrival debris' tint); and the natural share (`field_natural_share`): every vertex of a grid holding loaded samples (`measure_cuts`, a job's own grid) and of every finest node's grid (0311), the stone ones too, so a cut topsoil vertex beside a mined stone floor does not interpolate grass onto it, is natural within the larger of `FIELD_CUT_TOLERANCE_MILLIMETRES` (750 mm) and the grid's cell of the generated surface along its radial, cut past twice that, linear between, so a dug pit's floor and a mound show bare topsoil while a scrape under the tolerance stays green; the surface compared is the relief without the crater, so the arrival's crater reads as cut ground. A coarser grid generated whole is natural throughout; a finest one is measured, since the generation carves arches and grottos below the height map, so its cover is the same from a generated grid as from the loaded chunks. Each vertex keeps its varied temperature and moisture and its measured offset from the generated surface (`Field_Vertex_Look.celsius`, `moisture`, `offset_millimetres`, 0 without `measure_cuts`), which the ground cover reads: a finest level job places the cover on its triangles after the look and before the skirts (`place_field_cover`, `Field_Surface.quad_edges` naming each quad's grid edge, The field session, 0311). The look is a pure function of the position, so neighbouring nodes and levels agree at a shared place. It costs seven value noises a vertex and the relief at each measured vertex: in the test build (`GROUND_LOOK_COST`, one thread) a mesh took about 1.5 times as long, 1.9 times at the home and 2.4 at the wild spring start with the cuts measured at every vertex, and a job that generates its grid about 1.02 times.
- Level of detail (`world_field_lod.odin`): an octree over field chunks, a node of level L covering 2^L chunks a side and sampling every 2^L-th sample, so every node meshes 32 cells a side. `select_field_nodes` walks down from the 27 nodes around the camera of the first level as wide as the last distance, splitting every node that crosses the surface shell and lies within the last distance (about 4,000 visits a frame at 1 m spacing and 40,000 at 333 mm with a 4096 m view), and from the coarsest level on splits wherever `field_level_for_distance` asks for a finer level, against `field_view.level_distances_metres` of `data/game.sjson` (64, 160, 384 and 1024 m: full, half, quarter, eighth resolution; rising, at most `MAXIMUM_FIELD_VIEW_DISTANCE_METRES`, `field_view_problem`); nodes outside the shell the relief can reach and beyond the last distance are skipped. The walk's nodes are then balanced (`balance_field_selection`, 0354): a selected node touching one two or more levels finer at a face, an edge or a corner is split, finest contacts first, until no two nodes in contact are two levels apart. The level gaps alone leave such contacts, since a split node's child reaches up to its parent's diagonal past its level's distance, where a node two levels coarser needs only the next level's distance. Only a node whose distance plus its diagonal reaches the next level's distance is checked, and the pass is skipped when no node can be (`field_selection_needs_no_balance`): a split parent's child lies under its level's distance plus twice its diagonal, so where every level with one two coarser present lies more than three of its diagonals below the next, the walk stands, as the shipped levels do at 333 mm spacing at every view and at 500 mm at 768 m and 1024 m; a split child lies past its parent's distance, so it is kept whole, or dropped outside the shell or past the view as the walk drops it. The View distance setting (0343, `settings.view_distance_metres`, [ui.md](ui.md), Display) packs these levels inside a shorter view (`field_view_level_distances`): the view is the smaller of the setting and the last distance; from the coarsest level down, the first level that can end at the view while each finer level stays at least its gap below the next (`field_level_gap_metres`) and level 0 keeps its distance ends there, each finer level takes the smaller of its distance and the next level's less the gap, and the coarser levels are empty, their distance the view. The shipped data packs to 64, 145, 256 and 256 m at 256 m (no eighth level), 64, 160, 290 and 512 at 512, 64, 160, 384 and 768 at 768, and is unchanged at 1024. The distances are made with the frame's selection and kept beside it (`Frame_Presentation.field_level_distances`), so the selection, the haze's end and the trees of one frame read one view; a change shows from the next frame, the nodes that left the selection unloaded in `prepare_field_frame` and the new ones streaming in finest first. The finest level meshes from the loaded chunks; the coarser ones take the samples of the loaded chunks their grid reads and generate the rest from the planet on the worker (`generate_field_grid`), so an edit shows at every level ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Threads and chunk streaming). A loaded sample enters the coarse grid as its density over the step, except a saturated one on the generation's side of the surface, which keeps the generation's coarse density (`coarse_field_sample`); its material is the chunk's, except stone where the coarse generation holds topsoil, which reads topsoil. A coarse grid's generation takes topsoil down to at least two of its spacings (`generation_topsoil_depth`, 0322), so a surface cell, whose ground corners lie up to a cell below the surface, reads topsoil as the finest level's does; with the 2 m band half the corners at 4 m and over two thirds at 8 m were stone, which striped the far ground along its contours. A coarse grid holds its densities in units of its own spacing (`field_grid_generation`): the planet's density saturates one spacing from the surface, so in the fine spacing a coarse edge would read full density at both ends and the slopes would terrace.
- Seams: a node's surface runs from half a cell below its origin to half a cell before its far border, so where a coarser node lies on its negative side a strip up to half a coarse cell wide between the two meshes is open from above. A node's mesh hangs an L shaped flap from its open border on the faces that border a coarser node or none (`field_node_skirt_faces`, carried by the mesh job; 0173's fix round), never between nodes of one level, which meet without a gap and where a straight leg leaving a curved surface (a dug pit's rim across a chunk border) stood in the air as a flat flap (`append_field_skirts`): from each border vertex one coarse cell (two cells) out of the node along the surface's slope and half a coarse cell below it, then a coarse cell further into the ground along the gradient. The outward leg covers the strip whichever side the coarser node is on; following the slope a little below the surface, it neither stands above the neighbour's ground nor fights it in depth. Its reach of one coarser cell holds at edges and corners too, since the selection is balanced there (Level of detail). Towards finer nodes (0321) the coarser node keeps its mesh in its box where the ground meets the face steeply: the vertices of its cells outside a negative face, where the surface stands steeper than 35.26 degrees to the face plane (`field_surface_steep_to_face`, `FIELD_CUT_COTANGENT_SQUARED`), slide along the surface onto the face (`cut_field_surface`), and a curtain one cell long hangs from its border into its ground in the face's plane. A ray from the finer side meeting the ground in the strip at a shallow angle passes over the finer flap and under the coarser surface, whose back faces are culled, and showed the sky or the globe; the curtain meets it. A flap there instead stood over the finer ground (a dug pit 3 m, a placed wall 7 m, a cliff top 8.5 samples), and the coarser cells beyond a negative face had lain over the finer node as a lid. A shallower cell keeps that lid: at an angle θ between the ground and the face plane the finer flap reaches only 2 sin θ - cos θ of its cells along the face's axis, nothing below about 27 degrees, and the curtain shows the coarser side its back, so a cut there opened rays from the coarser side's air that the lid closes (up to 31.5 degrees, measured). The cut also leaves the corner columns and the last row beside a node of its level, which that node meshes too; a node of its level diagonally across the cut face and a face towards another level can share a cut cell, a selection only two viewports make, where no ray opened. The coarser surface's edge still steps over a finer pit narrower than its cells. Each band of a flap or a curtain continues the surface's winding, so it faces out of the node. The streaming keeps each node's flap and curtain faces with its revision and meshes a node again when either changes (a neighbour split or merged), so no seam opens when a neighbour's level changes. A node whose skirts would pass the u16 indices is drawn without them and without the cut, and logged, which `FIELD_MESH_VERTEX_BOUND` (three more vertices per border cell) rules out for today's grid.
- Water (0172): the same job meshes a node's water as a second surface from the same grid (`mesh_field_water_grid`), from the water's values the gather writes (The water's look, below). The water has no skirts: its open border also runs along its faces against the ground, where a skirt would stand up out of the water as a fin, so the strip between two levels of detail stays open on water. One job, not a second one, so the water never lags the terrain it lies on and one revision covers both.
- The water pass (`draw_field_water`): after every node's terrain, the water meshes of the visible nodes through the field shader with `water_color` set (`FIELD_WATER_COLOR`: colour and opacity), alpha blended, without depth writes, and with the back faces culled, so the water's faces against the ground, which face into it, are not drawn and the water is not seen from below yet.
- The globe: drawn every frame before the nodes, a sphere in the ground's colour (`field_globe_color`: with a climate the temperate moderate grass anchor times the grass tile's mean times two, as the shader shows it, else the palette's first colour) `FIELD_GLOBE_MARGIN_METRES` below the lowest relief (`field_globe_radius_metres`), so the nodes cover it where they exist and it fills the planet's silhouette beyond the last distance; it draws through the model shader, lit and hazed as the terrain (`draw_field_globe`, The field session, Light on the field).
- The field shader (`data/shaders/field.vs`, `field.fs`, `render_field.odin`): every tile read from the ground atlas (Textures, below) by `textureLod` at a level computed from the position's derivatives outside any branch, one level per projection plane, so the reads may sit in branches and loops; each tile projected along the three axes and blended by the absolute normal to the fourth power, a projection whose share is under 0.05 not read and the rest renormalised, read at two scales 2 m and e times that apart (the second's level lower by log2 of e) so the tile's period never shows. With a climate (the `ground_look_on` uniform, 0307) the topsoil takes the looks: rock (the stone's tile) by the steepness against the local up (the position's direction, the planet's centre being the origin) from 40 to 50 degrees, cut or not, so a pit's walls read as rock as a cliff does; on the natural share of the rest snow, then sand on slopes under 30 to 40 degrees, then grass. The ten tiles' weights (the seven materials, the stone's raised by the rock, the topsoil's lowered by the rock and the cover, then grass, sand and snow), each less 0.02, are read largest first, at most four, and blended by their weights over the picked weights' sum: 2 reads a tile and projection (the two scales), so 2 on ground flatter than about 25 degrees with one tile, 4 on a steeper slope, at most 24, and none in the water pass, whose vertices have no weights (42 at every fragment before 0307). The strata take the vertex's tint, the grass its grass colour, the sand and the snow the `sand_color` and `snow_color` uniforms (`set_field_ground_look_uniforms`), multiplied in times two (the tiles being mid tones); then the light (per channel the brighter of the block light and the open sky's light times the vertex's sky light, 0179, 0313), the point lights and the torchlight added (0297), and the aerial haze, its end fade from `FOG_START_SHARE` of the last distance (The field session, Light on the field). With the `water_color` uniform's alpha above zero (the water pass) the surface takes its colour instead of the materials and its alpha as its opacity; the terrain pass sets it to zero, and the water is lit and hazed as the terrain is. Point lights of working parts (0175, `render_point_lights.odin`) come on top of the field's light and never in its place: the renderer keeps the `MAXIMUM_POINT_LIGHTS` (8) nearest the camera (`nearest_point_lights`) and uploads their positions, radii and colours before `draw_field` (`set_field_point_lights`); the shader adds the albedo times each light's colour, falling off smoothly to nothing at its radius and half of it regardless of the facing, so the light is soft. A machine's lamp is clipped to its machine's box (0229): its colour goes up with alpha 0, and the terrain takes nothing from it (the machine's floor covers the ground inside its box, and the ground round the machine stays unlit), so `field.fs` takes no boxes; the model shader tests the box (Machine models). The arm's lamp has no box and lights the ground as before. Unused slots have radius 0 and come last, so the loop stops at the first one. The water pass, drawn through the same shader, takes the point lights too. The field session gathers them every frame (`set_field_scene_point_lights`: the arms' lamps and the lamps of the working machines, `gather_machine_lights`, 0224), the planet preview through the same scene, and the same eight go to the model shader (`set_model_point_lights`, Machine models).

### The water's look

What the water grid holds (0294, `world_field_water_look.odin`, after the browser study's rules for the bodies, the waves and the surface mesher): the water drawn where it is and only where it is, at every level of detail.

- The base (`field_water_look_base`): ground and a missing chunk -127; a sample a body or the sea owns as still water (its owner set and not awake front water, `field_water_look_level`; a body's dry air above its level is owned too) its owner's level as a plane, `(level - middle) * 128 / (254 * step)`, a signed distance in the grid's spacing, so every crossing edge has both ends inside the clamp band and no level of detail draws the staircase (`Field_Grid.water_exact` marks it, and `generate_field_grid` keeps it as it is); the front its slab level (`field_water_density`: the water's level over the sample's own distance, full water +64 on an axis, empty -64), which a coarse grid scales through `coarse_field_water_density` as before, and a sample not loaded the sea level rule (`planet_sea_density`), which crosses at the same radius as the fine sea (`test_the_fine_and_coarse_sea_cross_at_one_radius`). At full resolution a dry sample no one owns beside still water takes the lower of its slab and the owner's plane (`field_look_base`), so the air just over the sea's surface, which the sea does not own, is a distance too (`test_the_seas_surface_residual_is_small_at_every_latitude_and_level`: under 0.05 sample at latitude 0, 45 and 90 and steps 1, 2 and 4). Under the teleport model only the sea's samples (the generation's mark) take a plane.
- The full resolution water (`write_field_water_look`, after the terrain's copy) reads the samples from three beyond the node to three beyond its far side (`FIELD_LOOK_REACH`), copied once from the 27 chunks a row run at a time into flat arrays (`make_field_look_samples`: terrain, fill, the still owner, loaded; an owner with no live body is no still owner, so its water draws as the front, `test_still_water_of_a_dead_owner_draws_as_the_front`), with each sample's span taken once and shared by the base and the sheet; the still water whose 26 neighbours are its owner's still water is found by a separable test on a bit a sample per row and stamps nothing (`mark_field_look_interior`). The gather costs about 2.8 ms for a lake's chunk and 5.5 ms for a sea chunk in the test build (from 6 and 15 ms, 0294's fix round).
- The sheet (`stamp_field_water_sheet`, full resolution only, after the base): every wet sample from two beyond the grid to two beyond its far side stamps onto itself and its 26 neighbours `min(terrain density + thickness, clip)`, each value the maximum of what it held and the stamps. The mode is derived from the simulation (`field_water_sheet_mode`): resting when the sample has not moved for `FIELD_WATER_MOVED_TICKS` (6) awake ticks or a body owns it; falling when the water under it along the radial lies 0.2 sample (`FIELD_WATER_FALL_GAP`) below its bottom; resting when it stands on water or touches no ground (a settled staircase on the sphere is no fall, the top of free water no sheet); else moving. The thickness is the fill's share at rest, at least 0.2 sample (`FIELD_WATER_SHEET_FLOOR_STEPS`) moving or falling; the clip the sample's level (its body's when owned) at rest, the level plus the floor moving, the cell's top plus the floor and half a sample (`FIELD_WATER_SHEET_FALL_LIFT_STEPS`) falling, so thin or fast water hugs the ground with a visible thickness, a resting film draws at its true share and a drained basin leaves no film on its walls. Ground targets are always stamped (the water closes inside the ground, which the terrain covers); an air target only through face-connected open space (an edge neighbour through either intermediate, a corner through one of its six two-step paths). Still water skips the samples its body owns, so a lake's interior costs nothing.
- The wall guard (`field_water_wall_limit`): a stamp onto a ground sample is limited, over its face neighbours that are not ground, lie outside the wet sample's 3×3×3 and hold no water (base value at or below zero), so the water's crossing on the edge to them stays at 60 percent of the way to the terrain's (0.3 of the edge against the wall's face at 0.5): no water shows through a one-sample wall. When the guard lowers a moving or falling sample's stamp, the sample's own value rises so the crossing on the edge to the wall stays where the unguarded sheet put it. The guard reads the base values as they stood before the first stamp (beyond the grid, taken from the arrays on the fly), so the order never matters and two neighbouring nodes compute their shared samples alike.
- `Field_Chunk.water_moved`: `set_field_water` sets it to 6 when a fill changes (a body's derived bytes are not movement); `expire_field_water_moved` counts it down once per awake tick (`step_field_water_sample`, `plan_field_water_front`) and marks the meshes once when it reaches zero, so the sheet returns to rest; sleeping, the absorption into a body and a teleport write the tick undid on a sample asleep (0367) clear it. Derived, never saved or hashed.
- The mark reach: since a node's water reads three samples beyond it, a change to a sample's fill, owner, moving stamp or terrain marks every chunk whose grid reads it (`mark_field_look_dirty`; inside the water step at its end, and only where the look's inputs differ then, 0367, [architecture.md](architecture.md), The water field): its own, and the neighbour on every axis where it lies within three samples of that border (local 0 to 2 or 29 to 31), the edges and corners between included (`field_look_reach_bits`, `test_a_change_within_three_samples_of_a_border_marks_the_neighbour`). The light keeps the one sample reach of the terrain's mesh.
- The remesh rule: a body's chunks mesh again when its level moved a thirty-second of a sample (`FIELD_WATER_LOOK_LEVEL_STEP`, 8 fill units) since they last did (`Water_Body.meshed_level`, in `write_water_body_fills`, whatever the bands do), with the neighbours its samples reach (`field_look_owned_reach`), and the meshes round a sample whose owner changes (`note_water_owner_change`: the flood's claim, its release sweep, the absorption, the sea's claim; a merge's chunks and a flood's release, `release_water_body_samples`, with the neighbours their samples reach, `test_a_body_marks_the_neighbours_its_samples_reach`); a front chunk on every change of a fill and once more at the stamp's expiry; a chunk whose waves' baked offsets changed (below); a settled chunk never (`test_a_settled_lakes_water_mesh_repeats_byte_for_byte`).
- The surface waves (`render_field_waves.odin`, `Session.field_waves`, made with the field streaming from `water_look`): a height offset and a velocity per surface sample (`Field_Wave_Chunk`, `f32`, frame side, never in the simulation, the save or the hash; a surface sample holds water with its radial up neighbour not full), stepped at the top of `stream_field_session` before the streaming (`advance_field_waves`), once per tick since the last call and at most `MAXIMUM_FIELD_WAVE_STEPS_PER_FRAME` (4) a frame, nothing on a frame without a tick, so the cadence follows the world and never the frame or the cheat speed. One step is the height-field wave equation on the active samples, order independent: each velocity from its four tangential neighbours' heights as the step began (a neighbour off the surface reflects; one on the surface joins the set when the height is not zero), `v = (v + c²Σ(h_j - h)) · damping`, then `h = (h + v) · damping`, the height clamped to `clamp` and the velocity to four times it; a sample under `FIELD_WAVE_REST` (0.003 sample) in both is set to exactly zero and leaves the set. The damping takes a kick's mean to zero too.
- The events (`Field_World.water_events`, cleared at the top of the field's tick before the edits, so a dig, a bucket's pour and a pump's intake (0295) count beside the step's own, at most `MAXIMUM_FIELD_WATER_EVENTS` a tick): water arriving onto standing water from a sample farther from the centre (an impact) or from the side (a stream's mouth), under bodies a move into a body's or the sea's water, under teleport a move into more than a film or onto full water; a dig (a wet sample turned ground, or ground turned air beside water); a pump's intake (`displace_water_body_volume`). The weir, the levelling and a body's bytes push none, so a settled lake stays still. An event excites the surface sample along its radial (itself, then three up, then three down): an impact pushes the velocity down by `kick` times the fill over a full sample and its tangential neighbours up by a quarter each, a stream's mouth half as hard, a dig lowers the height at once, a pump pulls an eighth. A frame that ran several ticks sees the last tick's events only.
- The gather (`write_field_water_look`) adds the offset in density steps, the height rounded (`field_wave_baked`): the sample's own when not zero, else its radial up neighbour's, else its radial down neighbour's, so the band shifts together (`field_wave_offset`), to every sample that is not ground, before the sheet; the job's grid carries it baked in, so the worker reads no table. Coarse grids take no sheet and no waves: both lie under a coarse cell.
- The waves' marks, by changed sample (`mark_field_wave_changes`, after the frame's steps): each chunk entry keeps every sample's baked offset as last marked (`Field_Wave_Chunk.baked`, zero off the active set); an active sample whose baked offset differs, and a sample that came to rest with one (`step_field_waves`), add their reach bits, and only the chunks those changed samples reach are marked (`Field_Chunk.wave_dirty`). So a ripple deep inside a chunk marks that chunk alone, a wave at a border remeshes the neighbour too, and a wave whose offsets round alike marks nothing however its heights move (`test_waves_mark_a_chunk_only_when_its_baked_offsets_change`, `test_a_wave_at_a_border_remeshes_both_nodes`, where no mark falls on an unchanged grid). The per sample compare replaced the first round's hash sum over a chunk, whose reach was every non-zero sample's. An entry come to rest is freed after its last mark; one whose chunk left the world marks the reach of its non-zero samples. The wave chunks are capped at `MAXIMUM_FIELD_WAVE_CHUNKS` (64, 288 KiB each) and an event or a spread beyond is dropped. At rest every height is exactly zero, nothing is marked and the mesh is the water's bytes' again (`test_waves_change_no_fill_byte_and_no_hash`).
- The waves' budget: a chunk only the waves marked meshes in its own pass of `schedule_field_jobs`, after every unmeshed or edited node and coarse node, at most `MAXIMUM_FIELD_WAVE_REMESHES_PER_FRAME` (2) a frame within the frame's cap of 8 (`schedule_field_wave_remeshes`, `test_the_waves_remesh_after_unmeshed_nodes_two_a_frame`), the oldest mark first (`Field_Chunk.wave_dirty_tick`, the tick of the mark a chunk waits on, kept while it waits) and the nearest first among marks of one tick, so nodes a stream's mouth marks every frame never hold the budget from one marked once: with nothing else taking the cap a mark waits at most the marked nodes over two frames (`test_the_waves_remesh_the_oldest_mark_first`: six marked, three frames); a chunk dirty for any other reason takes the waves along. The planet preview and a server (`start_server`, which meshes nothing) have the waves off, and the streaming then gathers none (`field_waves_drawn`).
- The keys (`data/game.sjson`, `water_look`, [content.md](content.md)): `waves` true, `wave_speed_percent` 25 (c²dt²/dx², CFL 0.5), `wave_damping_percent` 98, `wave_clamp_percent` 15, `wave_kick_percent` 20. The sheet's floor, the moving hold, the fall gap and the level step are constants. The shader does nothing of its own for the look: the water's glint is the lighting's (0297).

## Textures

- The block atlas has a 16 by 16 tile per block and face group: generated for the blocks in `data/textures/procedural.sjson` with the texture editor's edits over them (`texture_generate.odin`), else from `data/textures/blocks/`, else the block's colour (`render_atlas.odin`). Item icons have their own atlas (`render_icons.odin`).
- The field's materials (topsoil, stone, deep stone, bedrock and the ores hematite, chalcopyrite and coal of 0179) and its looks (grass, sand and snow of 0307, `Field_Look`, near neutral tiles the planet's colours multiply) each get one tile generated by `generate_ore_tile` from `data/textures/field_materials.sjson` (`texture_field_materials.odin`, `FIELD_GROUND_TILE_COUNT`): flecks over a ground colour, crystal size 1, so the tile is isotropic and the triplanar projection shows no stripes. They go up in one atlas of 256 by 256 texels (`field_ground_atlas_pixels`, `upload_field_ground_atlas`, the material's map 0, the shader's `ground_atlas`), the materials in their order and then the looks, which is the shader's tile index: cells of 64 texels in 4 columns, each holding its tile repeated so the copy the shader reads lies 24 texels in, every 16 texel block of a cell a whole period, so the cell's level 4 mipmap is exactly the tile's mean and no read up to that level (`largest_lod`) reaches a neighbour cell. Mipmaps, trilinear filtering, clamped; the shader wraps a tile inside its cell itself.
- Every texture goes up through `load_rgba_texture`, which converts a copy of any non RGBA image first: raylib uploads gray and gray alpha images with a texture swizzle that Winlator's Gladio drops, and they sample red on the phone (0106). Fonts are built as raylib's `LoadFontEx` does, with the glyph atlas through the same procedure (`ui_font.odin`).

### Per block variation

Rule: a tile never repeats identically from block to block (0088). `texture_variation.odin` mirrors the shader's choice for the tests.

- A hash of the block's integer position (the cell a hundredth of a block behind the face, so a face on a block boundary reads one cell) picks one of eight orientations of the tile (a mirror of x, then zero to three quarter turns), a whole texel slide wrapped inside the tile, and a brightness jitter (`TEXTURE_BRIGHTNESS_JITTER`).
- The slide keeps mirrored neighbours from forming a symmetric motif; a sliding tile must be periodic (`texture_periodicity_test.odin`).
- `face_tile_variation`: side faces of `keep_orientation` blocks stay upright and unshifted; faces of `framed` blocks (the log ends) turn and mirror but never slide.
- Water takes the jitter only: its tile keeps its orientation for the flow and needs no offset, being isotropic.

## Shaders

- `data/shaders/` holds `chunk.vs`, `chunk.fs`, `water.vs`, `water.fs`, `field.vs`, `field.fs`, `arrival.vs`, `arrival.fs` (the soot on the portholes, 0200, 0223, 0273, The field session, The arrival), `arrival_wake.vs`, `arrival_wake.fs` (the entry's plasma outside the pod, 0304, The arrival), `model.vs` and `model.fs` (0224, the machine models' lit layer, Machine models), `flame.vs` and `flame.fs` (the flames of the furnace and the torch, 0274, Machine models, Flames), `sun_shadow.vs` and `sun_shadow.fs` (the sun's shadow map's casters, depth only, 0328, Light on the field), GLSL 330. The chunk shader applies per vertex light and occlusion and discards texels with alpha below 0.5 (ground cover, the torch).
- Every integer literal carries the `u` suffix: Gladio appends `.0` to every bare integer on a line with a float variable (`hash >> 8` becomes `hash >> 8.0`, which does not compile). `shader_source_test.odin` fails on a bare integer literal (0105).
- No identifier is a word GLSL 3.30 or GLSL ES 3.00 reserves for future use (`GLSL_RESERVED_WORDS`, both lists) or holds two consecutive underscores: Mesa refused `packed` as a parameter name that glslangValidator took (0307). `shader_source_test.odin` fails on one outside a comment.
- `load_shader_pair` loads from memory and tries up to `SHADER_LOAD_ATTEMPTS` times, logging `shader: <name> failed to load, attempt <n> of 3`, since Gladio fails a compile now and then and succeeds on the next try. Nothing is released between attempts: a failed load returns raylib's default shader, which must never be unloaded.
- On Android `shader_source_for_gles` turns `#version 330` into `#version 300 es` with `precision highp float;` and `precision highp int;` for the 32 bit integer hashes. The chunk and water shaders, rewritten so, pass `glslangValidator` (`sudo dnf install glslang` in the Android container).

## Sky and day

- `render_day.odin` derives the frame's `Day_Sky` from the tick and the day length. The clear colour is the horizon colour; the dome reaches `SKY_DOME_RINGS_BELOW` (2) rings, 22.5 degrees, below the horizon in the horizon's colour, and the clear shows below that.
- The sky pass (`render_sky.odin`) draws first without depth test or writes: a dome of `SKY_DOME_SEGMENTS` (48) segments recoloured each frame, on the field with the haze's glow round a low sun added (`Sky_Glow`, `fill_sky_dome_colors`, 0313, The field session, Light on the field), hashed stars, the sun (its disc in the sun's tint with the glow added and per channel at least the dome's colour beside it, `sun_disc_color`), and the moon with its phase. Discs face the camera along their own direction (`disc_axes`), since raylib's billboards squash a disc high in the sky into an ellipse.
- A field camera's sky follows its altitude above the planet's radius (0269, `atmosphere_sky_share`, `altitude_day_sky`): black (`SPACE_SKY_COLOR`) with the stars above `atmosphere.top_metres`, the day's from two scale heights under it; the frame is cleared to that horizon; the scene stays lit by the day. Any field camera above that height sees it (the developer's fly camera too); the block world never calls it.

## Weather

- `weather_at(seed, tick, day_length_ticks)` (`weather.odin`) is a pure schedule, not state: each game hour gets a kind (`weather_kind_weights`) and a peak intensity from a hash of the seed and the hour, ramped at the hour's ends. Rain over a column colder than `SNOW_TEMPERATURE` falls as snow.
- `render_weather.odin` blends each look value from clear to the kind's by the intensity: fog distance, sky gloom, wind and cloud shadow (`weather_fog_scales`, `weather_glooms`, `weather_wind_strengths`, `weather_cloud_shadow_strengths`). Rain darkens the sky tint (wet ground).
- The renderer reads the weather once per frame (`session_weather`): the `weather` command's forced kind (`Session.weather_override`) wins, and with the Weather setting off it is clear.
- The fog distance, rain, snow, sway and cloud shadows need the Weather setting on and reduced motion off (`weather_motion_enabled`); under reduced motion the sky still greys and the rain is still heard.
- Rain and snow wrap into a cylinder around the camera, counted by the intensity and the sky light at the camera, so none fall under a roof. The chunk shaders sway plants by the wind and dim sky light by a drifting noise texture, without shadows at night.

## Water pass

`render_water.odin` (0065).

- Water parts carry per vertex the cell's flow direction (downhill, zero for a source) and a shore value; both are part of the greedy face key, so water merges only where they match.
- `draw_water_chunks` uses its own material (`water.vs`, `water.fs`, sharing the atlas, hot reloaded with the chunk pair): alpha blending, depth test on, depth writes and backface culling off. The shader scrolls ripples over world x and z, runs the texture along the flow and lays a foam band along the shore.
- With the camera in water both materials take an underwater fog and a tint covers the view.

## Ambient life

`ambient_life.odin` (pure) and `render_life.odin` (drawing), 0075: a function of the world seed, the tick, the render time and the camera. Depth tested without depth writes. Biome densities are each biome's `bird_density` in `data/biomes.sjson`.

- Birds: a world aligned grid of flock cells holds a flock where a hash lies below the `bird_density` of the biome at the loop's centre. A flock flies a rounded rectangle above the surface (`sample_column`) on tick time (`loop_seconds`), so it is at the same place at the same tick on every run. Every period and phase comes from a hash of the flock and the bird.
- Birds draw only by day and not in heavy rain (`life_presence`).
- Insects: one in `INSECT_FLOWER_SHARE` cells of a block a ground cover marks with `insects` gets motes on Lissajous paths (`Chunk_Render.covers`), by day.
- Fish: source water cells under an open cell with water two cells deep (`water_surface_cell`; the mesh shell reaches past the chunk, so the sea surface on a chunk's bottom layer counts); `apply_chunk_mesh` keeps one in `FISH_CELL_SHARE` (`Chunk_Render.fish`). A shadow glides a lemniscate below the surface, drawn before the water pass so the surface tints it.

## Cues

`cues.odin` (0162).

- Once a frame per viewport (split screen, 0178), before the player's animation, `observe_cue_counters` reads the counters of the viewport's player and the records, and `detect_cues` compares them with `Cue_Memory` (the last frame's): the walked distance, the placed counters (`placed_total`), `blocks_mined`, the dig (`Mining_State`) and the block now at its cell, the blocks at and under the feet, the launching pads, the shipments and the quest messages.
- It returns `Frame_Cues`: a bit set of `Cue` (footstep, place, block break, dig break with its cell and block, dig quarter, launch, shipment, discovery, survey) and the walk (cadence distance, moving) with the blocks at the feet.
- The first frame of a session only learns the counters, so a loaded world neither steps, swings, chimes nor drops a capsule.
- The walk is followed per tick, not per frame, so a display faster than the tick rate neither stops the walk every other frame nor steps twice: a footstep fires once per half walk cycle of the cadence distance, which counts each tick's walk divided by the cheat speed factor, so the cheat never quickens steps or bob (`WALK_CYCLE_MILLIMETRES`).
- The landing is not a counter: the particles add `Landing` to the frame's cues when the capsule descent ends, before the sounds read them.
- The player's animation, the particles and the sounds read the cues and keep only their own state between frames.

## Particles

`particles.odin`, `render_particles.odin` (0067).

- A pool of `PARTICLE_CAPACITY` whose next index wraps, so a new particle replaces the oldest. Each `Particle_Kind` has its gravity, drag, launch speed, lifetime and growth; the step is the frame time capped at `PARTICLE_MAXIMUM_FRAME_SECONDS`.
- `emitters_for_frame` reads the working machines and the digging player; a fraction of a spawn rounds by a hash of the frame and the cell, so no emitter keeps state.
- Read from the cues (Cues): a dig break bursts, a shipment drops a capsule under a parachute (`Particle_Memory` keeps the descent and the survey satellite's pass), a footstep kicks up dust.

## The player

`player_animation.odin`, `render_player_model.odin`, `render_player.odin` (0066).

- Six limb models (`data/models/player_*.vox`), each the whole frame with its limb filled, swing about pivots read from the voxel bounds; without them a capsule stands in.
- The walk phase is the cues' cadence distance (Cues).
- The mine chop runs on the render time while `Mining_State.active`; a place swing starts on the place cue.
- First person draws the right arm and the held stack in a second 3D pass with the depth test off; third person draws the body at the interpolated pose, except while its camera is pulled in within `VIEWER_BODY_HIDDEN_WITHIN_METRES` (0.4 m, the body's 0.3 m radius plus the field's 0.1 m near plane) of the body's axis from the feet to the eye (looking steeply up the ground pulls it in to the feet, 0267), where the viewport draws neither its own body nor the arm, as if in first person (`viewer_body_shown`, 0261, 0267); other players' bodies are drawn either way.
- A player in the pod's chair (Strapped or Seated, 0268, `render_player_seated.odin`) is drawn seated in the chair's basis (its facing, the pod frame's up, so it leans with the rested pod): the eye on the seat's, the torso leaning back 8 degrees, the thighs level, the shins down (each leg cut at the knee at load, `PLAYER_KNEE_SHARE`), the hands on the knees, the head turned to the look within 75 degrees of yaw and 60 of pitch, lit at the pod's interior share. The chair shows one body: the viewer's own when it sits, else the first seated player's (`field_player_body_pose`). A seated viewer in first person sees none. Strapped in for the descent the camera is first person whatever the stored mode (0223). Seated by choice it is the stored mode, which the camera toggle switches in the chair as on foot (0285, `field_view_camera_mode`, `tick_seated_field_player`), so a player in third person sees itself in the chair. A player waiting in the charging cradle (0358) is not drawn, the viewer or another (the model has no lying pose and the bed stands for it), the chair's body is the first player in the chair (`first_seated_player`, `field_seat_in_chair`), and its camera is first person whatever the stored mode.
- In a field session a body whose feet cell lies in a pod's footprint is lit at the pod's interior share (`field_player_interior_light_share`, 0225), so a second player in the cabin is as dark as its walls.

## Machine models

- A machine's `model` key names a file in `data/models` (`model_mesh.odin`, `load_machine_model_mesh`): `<model>.obj` with its `.mtl` when that exists (0204), else `<model>.vox`. An OBJ (`model_obj.odin`: `v`, `vn`, `f` fan triangulated, `o`/`g`, `usemtl`, `mtllib`; MTL `newmtl`, `Kd`, `Ke`) is in cells of the unrotated footprint, x and z centred, y from the bottom, +x the front, y up, not scaled. `model_triangle_mesh.odin` refuses one whose x or z leave the footprint, or whose bottom goes below it, by more than `MODEL_FOOTPRINT_TOLERANCE_CELLS` (0.02), and reads its top off the mesh, so a chimney may rise above the footprint's height (the markers, the ghost chevron and the particle source follow the top). Every triangle is flat shaded: three vertices of its own, `Kd` times `clamp(MODEL_SHADE_BASE + dot(normal, MODEL_SHADE_GRADIENT), 0, 1)` (0.775 and (0.03, 0.225, 0.085), within 0.1 of the voxel shades on a box), the normal from `vn` or else the winding. The group named `part` is the moving part; a material with a non-zero `Ke` is emissive, drawn in its `Kd` unshaded. A voxel model (`model_vox.odin`: the first model's `SIZE`, `XYZI` and `RGBA`, z up mapped to y up) is meshed with a shade per face direction (`model_face_shades`, until 0206 removes the voxel reader) and scaled to the footprint. Both go up through `render_models.odin` as the same layers. The triangle mesher writes no indices, so its layers go up unindexed (`indices` nil, raylib draws every vertex in order) with no vertex limit, only the model check's triangle caps; the voxel mesher shares a quad's corners and goes up indexed, at most `MESH_PART_VERTEX_LIMIT` vertices a layer (u16 indices). Readers of a layer's triangles go through `model_mesh_triangle` (`model_mesh.odin`), which serves both (0226). A machine without a model keeps the box `draw_entity_cells` draws; every machine but belts, pipes, foundations and belt poles has one. The stone cutting table (0196, `tools/models/machines/stone_cutting_table.py`) is a timber workbench with a stone block, a chisel and a mallet on its top; the stone cutter (`stone_cutter.py`) a stone saw bench with a glowing fire door at the front, its vertical blade the part spinning about z through a slot in the bench top (`motion` spin, 0.6 s a turn). The foundations' pool is drawn by `draw_entities` for every entity whose machine is not a foundation (the pod and the crafting stations); `draw_frames` draws the foundations. A machine of kind `tree` (0197) holds a tree's model and is never an entity: `draw_field_trees` draws it per tree.
- A pod's hatch (0198) has a `slide`, (0221) a `spin` or (0231) an `iris` motion. A slide moves its shutter along the axis by `amplitude` cells, a spin turns it by `amplitude` turns about the pivot, times the open fraction. An iris draws the part `blades` times about the axis through the pivot (the aperture's centre), the k-th turned by k / `blades` of a turn, each opened about its own pin (`hinge`, the first blade's, turning with its blade) by `amplitude` turns times the open fraction, counterclockwise seen from the front for a positive turn (`iris_blade_transform`, `motion_part_transforms`, through which every part draw goes: the world, the ghosts, the workbench preview and the model check). The open fraction follows the hatch's state, not the clock (`hatch_open_fraction`: eased by `motion_stroke` over `period_seconds` from the tick of its last toggle, `hatch_pose`, `draw_hatch`), and its strips light while it is open. The shipped `pod_hatch` is an iris of `<n>` blades opening in 0.15 s. The model check sweeps each fixture's part, every blade, over open fractions 0 to 1 at its fixture box against the pod's body and its open cells boxes (`pod_fixture_part_problems`, 0221, 0231). The charge controller's strip lights always (0358, the cradle's supply is unlimited).
- Point lights on models (0224): both meshers upload a normal per vertex (a voxel face's direction, an OBJ triangle's normal). The lit layer draws with the model shader (`data/shaders/model.vs`, `model.fs`, `use_model_shader`): the vertex colour times the material's diffuse colour as before, plus the vertex colour times the point lights' sum and the torchlight's cone (0297, The field session, Light on the field), the `field.fs` terms (its point light sum and spot light copied, kept in step with it); on the field the open sky's light by the normal in place of the CPU's flat light, and the haze (0313, The field session, Light on the field). The emissive layer draws with raylib's default material (`Model_Renderer.emissive_material`, `model_layer_material`) and never takes point lights. A model shader that fails to load logs one line and the models draw as before. A machine's record may name lamps (`lights`, [content.md](content.md), Models): positions in the model's frame, a colour and a radius in cells, put in the world by the body matrix (`entity_body_matrix`, where the model is drawn, so a lamp turns with its machine) with the radius scaled by the frame's pitch (`machine_point_light`). They shine while the model works, the flag that lights its emissive materials (`foundation_model_working` for the foundations' pool: a pod always, a hatch while open, the charge controller always (0358); `furnace_model_working` and the others for the other pools), and share the eight slots with the arms' lamps. The block world clears the model shader's lights before it draws (`draw_session_world`). The lights have no shadows; a lamp lights only inside its machine's box, the footprint padded by `MACHINE_LIGHT_CLIP_PADDING_CELLS` up to the model's top when it rises higher (`machine_light_clip_box`, `machine_model_top`), and a face turned away from it takes nothing, so a pod's hull seen from outside, the ground round it and the machines beside it take no light from the cabin, while the cabin seen through a porthole or the open doors is lit. The renderer sends each light's matrix from the world to its box to the model shader (`point_light_boxes`, three rows a light, zero rows for a light with no box, which pass); a clipped light (colour alpha 0) fades out over a quarter of the cosine past grazing. A record lamp with `clip = false` shines out of its machine (0229). Inside a pod's box the lit tint is scaled by the pod's interior share before the point lights add (`interior_light_tint`, never below `MINIMUM_MODEL_BRIGHTNESS`, 0225); a working model's emissive materials keep their brightness, an idle one's follow the darkened tint.
- A model takes the world light of one cell above its footprint's centre (in front of a 1 by 1 machine). Palette indices from `EMISSIVE_PALETTE_START` and emissive materials glow; the OBJ group `part`, or for a voxel model an optional `<model>_part.vox`, moves per the machine's `motion` from the tick and entity state (`model_motion.odin`).

- Flames (0274, `render_flames.odin`, DESIGN.md, Fire): a furnace's record lists its flames (`flames`, [content.md](content.md), Models), quads standing on their base in the model's frame, each turned about the up by its yaw, put in the world by the body matrix as the lamps are (`machine_flame_draws`); the stone furnace has four fanned in its firebox (a broad sheet before the back wall, two tongues and a near side on one), so the fire reads from the front and the three quarter views. They draw only while the model works (`gather_machine_flames`, `furnace_model_working`). A frame's flames, the machines' and the torches', are one list on the tick clock (`model_frame_seconds`, so a paused game stills them) after everything opaque (`draw_session_flames`, `draw_field_flames`), sorted nearest the camera first and cut to `MAXIMUM_FLAME_DRAWS` (`nearest_flame_draws`), and drawn in one batch: each quad's flicker, seed and height over width travel in its vertex colour (`flame_vertex_color`, decoded in `flame.vs`), and the uniform locations are looked up at the load. The flame shader (`flame.fs`) draws, in order: fractal value noise rising up the quad (four octaves, their scales and speeds in no small integer ratio, an integer cell hash, the clock split into whole seconds and the fraction so the flow never wraps), a bend that grows with the height, a teardrop mask, a density eaten from the top, a continuous ramp from the dark red tips through the orange body to the yellow white core (`FLAME_TIP_COLOR`, `FLAME_BODY_COLOR`, `FLAME_CORE_COLOR`, coal and wood), and a soft halo. It blends premultiplied (one, one minus source alpha), depth tested without depth writes, so the fanned quads need no sorting by depth. The flicker (`fire_flicker`, 0.4 to 1.5, salted per machine's cell and frame, 0284) travels over `FIRE_FLICKER_HIGHEST` in the vertex colour's red byte and moves the reach (`0.82 + 0.12 * flicker` of the quad, the highest flare filling it), the brightness and the place on the ramp, hotter whiter; the firebox lamp (`flicker = true`, unclipped) and a field torch's light take it at `FIRE_POINT_LIGHT_SHARE` of its swing (`fire_point_light_flicker`) and the block light at `BLOCK_LIGHT_FLICKER_SHARE` (`block_torch_light_factor`), and the working furnace's whole emissive layer pulses slowly with the embers (`flame_ember_glow`, in `posed_model_light`) in place of the glow motion's fixed cosine, the console's lights with it. Under reduced motion the flicker, the lamps and the embers hold at `FIRE_FLICKER_MEAN`, the block light at 1 and the flow runs at its slow layer only: the slowest octave rises, the others and the bend stand still (the shader's `calm`). A torch's flame is the same shader on one quad on its post (`torch_flame_draw`, `FLAME_SIZE` wide, `FLAME_TORCH_HEIGHT` tall), turned square to the camera about the up (`flame_facing_across`): the block world's from the chunks in the camera's frustum (`append_torch_flames`), the field's within `FLAME_DRAW_DISTANCE_METRES` (`append_field_torch_flames`). Without the shader (a failed load, logged) nothing burns. The workbench preview draws a working machine's flames at the mean flicker, their flow at the phase.
- The seed rule: a detail row draws from `model_random(machine, salt)`, a `random.Random` seeded with the CRC-32 of `<model>:<salt>` (never Python's `hash`, which is salted per process), one salt per row, so the same script writes the same model and editing one row leaves the others' draws unchanged.

### The arm

The inserter's arm (0175, `model_arm.odin`, `render_arm.odin`), the first model at real scale; it replaced the inserter's miniature crane.

- Files: `tools/make_placeholder_models.py` (`arm()`) writes six, `arm.vox` (the base) and `arm_turret.vox`, `arm_upper_arm.vox`, `arm_forearm.vox`, `arm_gripper.vox`, `arm_finger.vox` (`arm_part_suffixes` after the model's name), each the whole `ARM_MODEL_FRAME` (24 by 128 by 24 voxels) at `ARM_VOXEL_MILLIMETRES` (25) with only its part filled, standing in the authored pose: straight up, fingers up, the joints on the frame's vertical centre line, the shoulder 22 voxels up (0.55 m), the elbow 50 above it (1.25 m), the wrist 42 above that (1.05 m) and the hand between the fingertips 12 above the wrist (0.3 m), as `model_arm.odin` has them in metres. A machine with `motion = {kind = "arm"}` loads them all with the machine models (`load_arm_part_meshes`, into `Machine_Model_Mesh.arm`, one mesh per part and layer) instead of a body and a part; a missing or misshaped file refuses the models as any model does. Grimy steel and yellow warning paint with black hazard stripes; a work lamp (emissive) on the wrist block. Nothing of the upper arm lies below the shoulder: its plates and hub start at the joint (0207), since the segment stretches about the joint and anything below it would sink into the base at a long reach.
- Joints: the turret turns about the vertical (yaw), the upper arm about the shoulder, the forearm about the elbow and the gripper about the wrist, all about the arm's sideways axis; the finger is drawn twice, offset either side of the gripper by the grip (open `ARM_FINGER_OPEN_METRES`, closed `ARM_FINGER_CLOSED_METRES`). `arm_part_transforms` chains them from the joint angles; `arm_reach_angles` solves the two segments for a wrist position, the elbow up.
- Scale (`arm_dimensions`): the parts keep their thickness in metres on every frame. The segments together are 2.3 m against the 2 m reach, so the elbow stays bent at full reach (over 40 degrees, `test_the_elbow_stays_bent_at_full_reach`). They scale with the span from the shoulder to the wrist over a cell at the reach, so they are their authored length at 2 m over 500 mm and bend alike on every frame: shorter on the block frame's one metre, longer for the long inserter's 4 m. The hand stops `ARM_WORK_CLEARANCE_METRES` over the top of the target cell, a cell of the frame's pitch. The base plate's width follows the pitch, so the base fills one cell; the inserter's footprint in cells stays one by one by one until the slice's real sizes (0179). `arm_entity_transform` puts the arm on its footprint's bottom centre, turned to the facing, in the frame's cells.
- Animation (`arm_pose_at`): the pose is a pure function of the inserter's cycle fraction (`inserter_cycle_fraction`), so the cadence is the work: rest folded over the base at 0, reach to the pickup cell, grab, swing over the right hand side lifted and drawn in, lower onto the drop cell at one half, release, swing back and fold. An idle arm (nothing to pick) rests folded, an arm waiting for room holds over the drop cell, and an arm out of fuel or power stops mid move. The rest pose (`arm_rest_pose`) stands the upper arm with its elbow just behind the turret's axis and hangs the forearm forward to a wrist within the base for any length of the segments. The held item shows between the grab and the release (`arm_shows_held_item`), since the simulation takes it at the cycle's start. Tests: at rest the elbow, the wrist, the hand and the lamp lie within the base's footprint, and at full reach the hand is over the top of the drop cell and at the grab over the pickup cell, for every facing, on the 500, 333 and 1000 mm frames, the long arm and the block frame.
- Light: the lamp lights only while the arm moves (`Inserter_State.Moving`): at rest its emissive voxels are dark (`ARM_LAMP_DARK_SHARE` of the light round it), while the other machines' emissive voxels keep their old look at rest, lit like the rest of the model. While it moves a point light shines from the lamp (`arm_point_light`, `ARM_LIGHT_COLOR`, `ARM_LIGHT_RADIUS_METRES`) for the field shader (The field shader, above) and the model shader, so it lights the models round it too, the arm's own lit layer included (Machine models); it has no clip box (0229). The block world's renderer takes no point lights, and the models' lights are cleared there.
- Ghosts: an arm's placement ghost is the arm at rest, and its model top (`machine_model_top`) is the folded arm's on the block frame, so the ghost's chevron clears it.
- Without its models an inserter is drawn as the post and bar it had before, in its frame's cells. The frame renderer draws foundations only (0179); a machine on a frame, the pod included, takes `draw_entities`' path under its frame's matrix.
- The planet preview's screenshot holds one arm at full reach, working (`Model_Frame.reaching_arm`, `model_frame_arm_placement`), so its lamp's point light shows; in a session every arm follows its cycle.

## Runs

Belt and pipe runs between poles (0176, `render_belt_runs.odin`; the curve: [logistics.md](logistics.md), Runs), drawn after the frames by the field session (0179) and the planet preview.

- Swept meshes: one cross section per polyline point (`belt_run_sections`), its up blended from the start frame's up to the end frame's along the run, its right across the tangent (the neighbouring points' difference). A belt run is one quad per subdivision between two sections, a cell of the start frame's pitch wide at `BELT_SURFACE_HEIGHT` of a cell over the polyline, textured with the belt texture, its v counting cells along the run so the stripes keep the block belt's spacing, and scrolled at the run's belt speed as the belt meshes are (`belt_scroll_offset`); its sides hang `BELT_RUN_SIDE_DEPTH_SHARE` of the pitch below the surface, so a run seen edge on reads as a belt. Both windings, like the belt quad. A pipe run is a cylinder of `PIPE_COLOR` per subdivision, its radius `PIPE_RUN_RADIUS_SHARE` of the pitch, lying on the polyline.
- Items on a run stand at their distance through `belt_run_point_at`, offset across the section to their lane by `BELT_LANE_OFFSET` of the pitch, drawn as cubes of their category's colour.
- A belt pole is a post from its cell's bottom to the run end (`draw_belt_poles`).
- The ghost (`draw_belt_run_ghost`): once the run tool's start is picked, the curve to the reticle's candidate as an untextured see-through surface, white, red when the shape would be refused.

## Sound

`audio.odin` (mixer), `sound_events.odin` (triggers), 0068.

- Without an audio device every call is a no op. The mixer loads `data/sounds/sounds.sjson`: effects as `Sound`, never twice within `EFFECT_MINIMUM_GAP_SECONDS` (raylib replays a `Sound` on one voice), loops as `Music` fading to the frame's target over `LOOP_FADE_SECONDS`, so a loop nobody asks for fades out.
- Loops and ambience calls follow the ambience volume, other effects the effects volume, all the master volume.
- The effects follow the cues (Cues): footsteps by the material underfoot, mining hits per dig quarter by tool tier (at most `MINING_HIT_MAXIMUM_PER_SECOND`, paced by `Sound_Memory`), break, place, launch, landing, the discovery chime; the UI sounds follow `ui_sound_ids`. A field session plays the arrival's roar, crash, bang and patter and the hatches' slide instead (The field session, The arrival).
- Hum: the nearest working machine (as the bottleneck markers define working) within `HUM_RANGE_BLOCKS` picks its family (fluid, electric, burner), with a drifting level.
- Ambience: the biome under the player picks a loop, or variants `ambience_<name>_1`, `_2` played in clusters of calls with long hashed pauses; a `day_only` ambience calls only by day.
- Rain plays at the intensity times the open sky at the eye; snow is silent.
