# Presentation

What the game draws and plays. It reads the world, the tick, the render time and the camera; the simulation never reads it back, and nothing here is saved (render state lives in `Frame_State` and resets with the session). Texture and sound values: [content.md](content.md).

- Every pass follows the no perceivable repetition principle ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md)).
- Values (sizes, periods, rates, colours) are the constants atop each file named below.

## Frame order

Inside the 3D pass, in order: the sky, the solid chunks, entities and models, the bottleneck markers, fluid and power entities, belts, loose items, torch flames, fish shadows, the water pass, birds, insect motes, particles, weather, the player overlay. Then the hands, the underwater tint, the HUD and the UI (`loop.odin`).

A field session's viewport (0179, The field session, below) draws its own 3D pass instead; the HUD and the UI follow as in the block world.

## The field session

A field session (0179, [architecture.md](architecture.md), The field session) draws through `loop_field_session.odin`; the block world's renderer is not called, and the sounds and cues, which read the block world, are off; the field's only sounds are the arrival's and the hatches' (The arrival).

- Before the viewports (`prepare_field_frame`): the finished field meshes go up and the field shader's daylight is the shared clock's (`daylight_blend`), the same at every longitude; each player's crouch progress (`Field_Renderer.crouch_progress`, 0218) moves towards its crouch flag as predicted (`advance_field_crouch`).
- Per viewport (`draw_field_viewport_world`): the field camera of the viewport's player (interpolated as the block camera is; the eye dips to the crouch's eye over `CROUCH_EASE_SECONDS`, 0.15 s of frame time smoothstepped, `field_player_view`), the sky dome turned about the player's up (`field_sky_camera`), then the clip planes `FIELD_NEAR_METRES` to `FIELD_FAR_RADII` planet radii (the block world's are restored after).
- The scene (`draw_field_scene`), in order: the working arms' and machines' lights gathered round the camera (none from the machines during the fall), the field (terrain, then water), the foundations (`draw_frames`, each cell a flat slab in its record's `color`, `foundation_slab_color`, 0196), the machines on frames (`draw_entities`, lit by the open sky: `Model_Frame.open_sky` gives every model the full sky light, since the field's light is not in the block light grid, except inside a pod: the pod's model, its fixtures' and the bodies of players standing in its footprint take the pod's `interior_light_share` of that light, `gather_interior_lights` once per scene, `posed_model_light`, `field_player_interior_light_share`, 0225), the trees (`draw_field_trees`, below), the runs and poles, the torches as small glowing boxes (`FIELD_TORCH_SIZE_METRES`), every player's body (the player model's limbs, or a capsule when it did not load; a crouched body is the model squashed along the up to the crouch height, since the legs have no knee, or the capsule at that height, eased as the eye, 0218) but the viewer's in first person or with its camera pulled in to the eye (`Field_Scene.viewer_body_shown`, `field_player_body_drawn`, 0261), and the viewer's ghosts (the run tool's curve, the frame placement's footprint, in the block ghost's refused colour when `field_placement_refusal` would refuse it, `frame_ghost_color`). With the placement editor (0215, `Field_Scene.placement_editor`, `draw_placement_editor_ghost`) the footprint gives way to the editor's ghost: before the anchor low walls round the footprint's lowest row (`draw_footprint_outline`, `footprint_outline_boxes`, 0219: four walls a cell tall and a tenth of a cell thick just inside its edges, lifted 0.02 cells off the cells' floor, see through and drawn without writing depth, under an opaque rim 0.04 cells high, so a slope does not hide them) with a chevron along the machine's front at the rim's top (`machine_front_direction`, as long as 0.6 of the shorter side; none for a foundation block), white, red where the placement would be refused (`placement_outline_color`), static; anchored, the machine's model on its frame in the ghost colour drawn without writing depth, so its far side shows through its near side (`draw_frame_ghost_model`), and a box per cell for a foundation block, an arm machine or a machine without a model.
- The trees (0197, `render_field_trees.odin`): the standing trees round the eyes are generated into a cache of regions of `FIELD_TREE_REGION_CELLS` (8) tree cubes an edge, `FIELD_TREE_REGIONS_PER_FRAME` (3) missing ones a frame nearest first, evicted once no eye is within the draw distance (`update_field_tree_cache`, in `prepare_field_frame` and the preview's stream step, before any viewport draws). Per viewport `draw_field_trees` draws the trees within `FIELD_TREE_DRAW_METRES` (128 m) whose box is in the frustum, at most `FIELD_TREE_DRAW_LIMIT` (256) nearest first, a felled one never; each is its species' model (a machine of kind `tree`, Machine models) at its yaw and scale, grown in from nothing over the last `FIELD_TREE_GROW_IN_METRES` (16 m), lit by the open sky times the species' tint and the tree's shade. A species whose model did not load is not drawn. The planet preview's screenshot waits for the cache to settle as for the meshes.
- One node selection serves every viewport: `Field_View.more_cameras` holds the other eyes and a node's distance is its nearest eye's (`field_viewport_selection`).

### The arrival

A new world's fall (0200, `render_arrival.odin`, [architecture.md](architecture.md), The field session, The arrival) is drawn from `Field_Arrival`, the tick and the interpolation alpha alone (`arrival_view`); nothing here writes the simulation, and the hash is the same with and without it (`test_the_arrivals_presentation_leaves_the_hash`). Offline the pause menu stops the tick, so the fall stands while it is open.

- Phases: Descent from the start to `arrival_settle_ticks` before the planned landing (the hit), Settled from the hit for `arrival_settled_seconds` (until the dust has settled and the last clod has sunk, 166 s shipped), then None. A fall skipped before its planned end (`landed_tick` before it) is None at once: no crash, no dust and no debris, and no doors' sound, since the landing leaves the doors closed (0222).
- Descent: the pod is drawn base first along the curve's tangent, its up against the travel and the chair's porthole on the travel's side of the motion's plane, easing by slerp into the pose it rests in over the last `arrival_real_seconds` (`Arrival_View.rest_share`, smoothed), about its base centre moved by the path's offset (`arrival_pod_transform`, 0270); the frames, the machines, the players and the windows are drawn under that transform (`Field_Scene.pod_transform`, pushed on the `rlgl` stack), so the cabin travels and turns with the eye and its lamps light it. The viewport's camera is the seated player's first person camera, its position and target moved by the same transform and its up the player's (the planet's), so a sideways look keeps the horizon level and nothing snaps at the hit, where the simulation's rested frame takes over (architecture.md, The arrival). The path is the entry's curve (0269, `arrival_descent_offset` from `arrival_curve_at`, content.md, The arrival): the offset is the curve's altitude along the pod frame's up and the range left behind its travel heading (the chair's facing, `pod_travel_heading`), so the pod meets the floor exactly at the hit; the progress (linear in the descent) reaches the curve through `arrival_curve_progress`, so the space and the entry are stretched and the last `arrival_real_seconds` play 1:1. The travel is the curve's tangent (`arrival_travel_direction`), which lays the windows' flames, turned back through the transform's rotation since the windows draw in the frame's unmoved space. raylib 6.0's `DrawMesh` sends the model shader the mesh transform times the `rlgl` matrix stack, so the lamps are moved with the models they light (`moved_point_light`, their clip boxes with them) and the nearest are picked round the moved camera. The stored camera stays the resting one, so the HUD's projections stay on the cabin. The node selection, the streaming and the tree cache stay round the resting eye (`field_viewport_eyes`), so the crater is meshed finest from the first frame and nothing remeshes along the path. The ground is whole through the descent (0271). The dug chunks remesh in the frames after the hit, under the settle's dust.
- The windows (`data/shaders/arrival.vs`, `arrival.fs`): each of the pod's `windows` gets a disc of the arrival shader on its glass at the sleeve's cabin mouth, its rim the hole's edge seen from the cabin (`draw_arrival_windows`, a quad laid by `arrival_window_corners`), its flames leading on the edge the pod travels towards; the planet shows through the portholes' holes. The flames of the drag follow the curve's heat (`heat`, 0269), at heat 1 (the drag's peak) reaching the glass's centre from the leading edge and a third as far from the trailing one, shaped by four octaves of value noise whose scales and speeds are in no small integer ratio and shifted by the world's seed, so they never repeat, each window's seed its own; a glow over the glass follows them (up to 0.35). Their tint follows the heat: pink as they rise, then orange, white hot at the peak, after it (`cooling`) orange to a dull red, out; at heat 0 nothing is drawn on the windows. Without the shader (a failed load, logged) the fall plays without its flames.
- The buffeting (`arrival_buffet_offset`, 0269): during the descent the camera alone (not the cabin) moves by hashed value noise at `ARRIVAL_BUFFET_HERTZ` (7.3), up to `ARRIVAL_BUFFET_METRES` (0.05) times the heat cubed, so it shakes round the drag's peak only, the look moved half as much; off under reduced motion.
- The hit: the pod meets the floor at the hit, the offset reaching zero there and the attitude the resting one; Settled draws the rested frame unmoved, and the camera shakes (`arrival_shake_offset`: hashed value noise at `ARRIVAL_SHAKE_HERTZ`, up to `ARRIVAL_SHAKE_METRES` (0.3), fading over `ARRIVAL_SHAKE_SECONDS` (1.5), the look moved more than the eye; off under reduced motion), and the dust curtain of `ARRIVAL_DUST_PUFFS` (64) translucent puffs rises along the crater's rim on the ground there, drifting out and up about the planet's up at the crater's home in the debris' topsoil colour brightened, each hashed per index so no ring or rhythm shows (`arrival_dust_puff`, `draw_arrival_dust`).
- The debris (0272, `render_arrival_debris.odin`): `arrival_debris_pieces` boxes leave the bowl at the hit, the inner ones first, on parabolas at about `arrival_debris_angle_degrees`, landing between the rim and five crater radii with the thickness falling as the cube of the distance, the inner ones farthest; they rest on the baked generation's surface, tumbled and sunk a quarter, for up to `arrival_debris_rest_seconds` and sink away over 8 s. Each is a pure function of its index, the seed, the ground and the pod's frame (its clearance from the crater's home), so a joiner or a load inside those seconds sees the same clods. Nothing on a planet without a crater or after a Skip.
- The sounds (`play_field_session_sounds`, once a frame at the first viewport): the roar loop at the heat during the descent (`arrival_roar`, faded out under the pause menu and after the hit), the crash and the bang layered on it on the cut from the descent to the settled cabin (`arrival_crash`, `arrival_bang`), all pitched by the world's hash (`play_arrival_sounds`), then the patter of half the clods as they land (`play_arrival_patter`), three variants pitched per piece, never on a fixed period, from the last frame's seconds since the hit, so a joiner hears no backlog; then a second of silence until the simulation lands, the doors staying closed until the player comes to the inner one (0222). The hatch cue (`play_hatch_sounds`) plays `hatch_slide` once for every hatch whose toggle tick lies after the last frame's tick, opening and closing alike, pitched by a hash of the hatch and its toggle tick; its memory starts at the session's tick, so a loaded or joined world never replays old toggles.
- The start height: the start's distance from the crater is bounded by the coarsest level (content.md, The arrival); a view from orbit would need a far level drawn for the fall.

## Chunk meshes

- Greedy meshing per chunk over the block atlas, remeshed on edit, culled per chunk. Only cubes join the greedy pass; slabs, stairs, posts and crosses mesh cell by cell, a border quad hidden by an opaque neighbour dropped.
- Texcoords run in blocks and repeat with `fract()` (`face_texcoord`); on side faces y runs down from the face's top, so a tile's top row is up on every side and a slab side shows the lower half.
- Water faces go to separate parts (`Chunk_Mesh_Data.water_parts`); lit posts become flame cells (`render_flames.odin`).
- Fog in the horizon colour ends one chunk inside `LOAD_RADIUS_HORIZONTAL` (`fog_distances`), so chunks fade into the sky.

| Vertex data | Carries (`world_mesh_light.odin`) |
|---|---|
| Colour red | Sky light |
| Colour green | The face's `Tile_Variation` (`orientation_flag_green`) |
| Colour blue | Ambient occlusion |
| Colour alpha | `SWAY_VERTEX_ALPHA` for vertices the wind sways (`sway_vertex_light`) |
| Normal | Red, green and blue block light (`block_light_normal`) |

- Sky light is scaled by the day factor and tinted by the sky tint; block light keeps its colour and takes one flicker for all lights (`light_flicker`), which stands still under reduced motion (`flicker_seconds`), as do the torch flames.
- Models light the same way without the flicker (`model_light_tint`).

### Field meshes

The terrain field (M13) is drawn beside the blocks, by the field session (0179, The field session, above) and the planet preview (`--planet-preview`, `loop_planet_preview.odin`, [build.md](build.md)).

- Mesher (`world_field_mesh.odin`): naive surface nets over a `Field_Grid`, the samples of a node and one more on every side. A cell the surface crosses gets one vertex at the mean of its edge crossings (linear in the `i8` densities, in 1/`FIELD_MESH_POSITION_UNITS` of a cell, integer throughout); each grid edge the surface crosses that starts inside the node becomes a quad between the four cells around it, wound against the density gradient. The quads reach the cells one step below the node, which the neighbour computes from the same samples, so neighbours of one level meet without a gap. Floats appear only in `field_mesh_from_surface`, the GPU's arrays.
- Per vertex: the normal against the cell's gradient (the density's change across the cell); the material weights, the share of the cell's ground corners per material (`Field_Surface_Vertex.weights`, one byte per material but air in the enum's order, `field_material_slot`, `FIELD_TEXTURED_MATERIAL_COUNT`: topsoil, stone, deep stone and bedrock as the tangent attribute, hematite ore as the texture coordinate's second component, chalcopyrite and coal ore as the second texture coordinate, `Field_Mesh_Data.ore_weights`, the free attributes raylib binds without extra code; every float uploaded is read); the colour, the mean palette colour of the ground corners, the tint taken modulo the palette's length since a saved tint is not checked; the vertex light (0173, `field_vertex_light`): the mean of each channel of the field light over the cell's air corners (ground holds no light, so counting it would halve every surface's light), the sky light in the colour's alpha and the block light in the texture coordinate's first component (`Field_Mesh_Data.lights`). The water's mesh takes the terrain's light at the same corners. A coarse grid takes the light of the loaded samples it reads and gives a generated sample full sky light in air and none in ground, as the generation does ([architecture.md](architecture.md), The field light).
- Level of detail (`world_field_lod.odin`): an octree over field chunks, a node of level L covering 2^L chunks a side and sampling every 2^L-th sample, so every node meshes 32 cells a side. `select_field_nodes` walks down from the 27 nodes around the camera of the first level as wide as the last distance, splitting every node that crosses the surface shell and lies within the last distance (about 4,000 visits a frame at 1 m spacing and 40,000 at 333 mm with a 4096 m view), and from the coarsest level on splits wherever `field_level_for_distance` asks for a finer level, against `field_view.level_distances_metres` of `data/game.sjson` (64, 160, 384 and 1024 m: full, half, quarter, eighth resolution; rising, at most `MAXIMUM_FIELD_VIEW_DISTANCE_METRES`, `field_view_problem`); nodes outside the shell the relief can reach and beyond the last distance are skipped. The finest level meshes from the loaded chunks; the coarser ones take the samples of the loaded chunks their grid reads and generate the rest from the planet on the worker (`generate_field_grid`), so an edit shows at every level ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Threads and chunk streaming). A loaded sample enters the coarse grid as its density over the step, except a saturated one on the generation's side of the surface, which keeps the generation's coarse density (`coarse_field_sample`). A coarse grid holds its densities in units of its own spacing (`field_grid_generation`): the planet's density saturates one spacing from the surface, so in the fine spacing a coarse edge would read full density at both ends and the slopes would terrace.
- Seams: a node's surface runs from half a cell below its origin to half a cell before its far border, so where a coarser node lies on its negative side a strip up to half a coarse cell wide between the two meshes is open from above. A node's mesh hangs an L shaped flap from its open border on the faces that border a coarser node or none (`field_node_skirt_faces`, carried by the mesh job; 0173's fix round), never between nodes of one level, which meet without a gap and where a straight leg leaving a curved surface (a dug pit's rim across a chunk border) stood in the air as a flat flap (`append_field_skirts`): from each border vertex one coarse cell (two cells) out of the node along the surface's slope and half a coarse cell below it, then a coarse cell further into the ground along the gradient. The outward leg covers the strip whichever side the coarser node is on; following the slope a little below the surface, it neither stands above the neighbour's ground nor fights it in depth. Each band continues the surface's winding, so it faces out of the node. The streaming keeps each node's skirt faces with its revision and meshes a node again when they change (a neighbour split or merged), so no seam opens when a neighbour's level changes. A node whose skirts would pass the u16 indices is drawn without them and logged, which `FIELD_MESH_VERTEX_BOUND` rules out for today's grid.
- Water (0172): the same job meshes a node's water as a second surface from the same grid (`mesh_field_water_grid`), the mesher over the water as a density (`field_water_density`: the water's level over the sample's own distance in the terrain's scale, a signed distance to the surface wherever the sample sits in its cell, so a level surface crosses every grid edge at its level, on the axes and off them; full water on an axis is +64, empty -64, and ground -127, so the water's surface closes against the terrain, which the terrain mesh covers). A coarse grid takes the water of the loaded chunks it reads through `coarse_field_water_density` (full water or empty air on the generated sea's side keeps the generated density, anything else is its density over the step) and the sea level rule elsewhere (`planet_sea_density`, the depth below the sea level in the grid's spacing, -127 in ground), which crosses at the same radius as the fine sea (`test_the_fine_and_coarse_sea_cross_at_one_radius`), so the sea shows at every level. The water has no skirts: its open border also runs along its faces against the ground, where a skirt would stand up out of the water as a fin, so the strip between two levels of detail stays open on water. Fill below half a sample has no surface of its own. One job, not a second one, so the water never lags the terrain it lies on and one revision covers both.
- The water pass (`draw_field_water`): after every node's terrain, the water meshes of the visible nodes through the field shader with `water_color` set (`FIELD_WATER_COLOR`: colour and opacity), alpha blended, without depth writes, and with the back faces culled, so the water's faces against the ground, which face into it, are not drawn and the water is not seen from below yet.
- The globe: drawn every frame before the nodes, a sphere in the palette's first colour `FIELD_GLOBE_MARGIN_METRES` below the lowest relief (`field_globe_radius_metres`), so the nodes cover it where they exist and it fills the planet's silhouette beyond the last distance (`draw_field`).
- The field shader (`data/shaders/field.vs`, `field.fs`, `render_field.odin`): each material's tile projected along the three axes and blended by the absolute normal to the fourth power, read at two scales 2 m and e times that apart so the tile's period never shows, the seven materials blended by the vertex weights (every material sampled at every fragment, 42 tile reads a fragment, since a mipmapped sample inside a branch has no defined derivatives where a pixel quad straddles a zero weight), the tint multiplied in (times two, the tiles being mid tones), the vertex light (the brighter of the block light and the sky light times the `daylight` uniform, `Field_Renderer.daylight`, the shared clock's `daylight_blend` in a session, 0179; the planet preview's `--planet-preview-daylight` overrides it) and a gentle sun term (`FIELD_SUN_DIRECTION`) on top, fog to `FIELD_FOG_COLOR` from `FOG_START_SHARE` of the last distance. With the `water_color` uniform's alpha above zero (the water pass) the surface takes its colour instead of the materials and its alpha as its opacity; the terrain pass sets it to zero, and the water is lit by the same vertex light and daylight. Point lights of working parts (0175, `render_point_lights.odin`) come on top of the field's light and never in its place: the renderer keeps the `MAXIMUM_POINT_LIGHTS` (8) nearest the camera (`nearest_point_lights`) and uploads their positions, radii and colours before `draw_field` (`set_field_point_lights`); the shader adds the albedo times each light's colour, falling off smoothly to nothing at its radius and half of it regardless of the facing, so the light is soft. A machine's lamp is clipped to its machine's box (0229): its colour goes up with alpha 0, and the terrain takes nothing from it (the machine's floor covers the ground inside its box, and the ground round the machine stays unlit), so `field.fs` takes no boxes; the model shader tests the box (Machine models). The arm's lamp has no box and lights the ground as before. Unused slots have radius 0 and come last, so the loop stops at the first one. The water pass, drawn through the same shader, takes the point lights too. The field session gathers them every frame (`set_field_scene_point_lights`: the arms' lamps and the lamps of the working machines, `gather_machine_lights`, 0224), the planet preview through the same scene, and the same eight go to the model shader (`set_model_point_lights`, Machine models).

## Textures

- The block atlas has a 16 by 16 tile per block and face group: generated for the blocks in `data/textures/procedural.sjson` with the texture editor's edits over them (`texture_generate.odin`), else from `data/textures/blocks/`, else the block's colour (`render_atlas.odin`). Item icons have their own atlas (`render_icons.odin`).
- The field's materials (topsoil, stone, deep stone, bedrock and the ores hematite, chalcopyrite and coal of 0179) each get one tile generated by `generate_ore_tile` from `data/textures/field_materials.sjson` (`texture_field_materials.odin`, `FIELD_MATERIAL_TILE_COUNT`): flecks over a ground colour, crystal size 1, so the tile is isotropic and the triplanar projection shows no stripes. They go up with mipmaps, trilinear filtering and repeat wrapping, one texture per material on the material's maps 0 to 6 in the materials' order (the two dimensional maps; the cube maps start at 7), and the shader samples every one, so an outcrop shows its ore.
- Every texture goes up through `load_rgba_texture`, which converts a copy of any non RGBA image first: raylib uploads gray and gray alpha images with a texture swizzle that Winlator's Gladio drops, and they sample red on the phone (0106). Fonts are built as raylib's `LoadFontEx` does, with the glyph atlas through the same procedure (`ui_font.odin`).

### Per block variation

Rule: a tile never repeats identically from block to block (0088). `texture_variation.odin` mirrors the shader's choice for the tests.

- A hash of the block's integer position (the cell a hundredth of a block behind the face, so a face on a block boundary reads one cell) picks one of eight orientations of the tile (a mirror of x, then zero to three quarter turns), a whole texel slide wrapped inside the tile, and a brightness jitter (`TEXTURE_BRIGHTNESS_JITTER`).
- The slide keeps mirrored neighbours from forming a symmetric motif; a sliding tile must be periodic (`texture_periodicity_test.odin`).
- `face_tile_variation`: side faces of `keep_orientation` blocks stay upright and unshifted; faces of `framed` blocks (the log ends) turn and mirror but never slide.
- Water takes the jitter only: its tile keeps its orientation for the flow and needs no offset, being isotropic.

## Shaders

- `data/shaders/` holds `chunk.vs`, `chunk.fs`, `water.vs`, `water.fs`, `field.vs`, `field.fs`, `arrival.vs`, `arrival.fs` (the arrival's flames on the portholes, 0200, 0223, The field session, The arrival), `model.vs` and `model.fs` (0224, the machine models' lit layer, Machine models), GLSL 330. The chunk shader applies per vertex light and occlusion and discards texels with alpha below 0.5 (ground cover, the torch).
- Every integer literal carries the `u` suffix: Gladio appends `.0` to every bare integer on a line with a float variable (`hash >> 8` becomes `hash >> 8.0`, which does not compile). `shader_source_test.odin` fails on a bare integer literal (0105).
- `load_shader_pair` loads from memory and tries up to `SHADER_LOAD_ATTEMPTS` times, logging `shader: <name> failed to load, attempt <n> of 3`, since Gladio fails a compile now and then and succeeds on the next try. Nothing is released between attempts: a failed load returns raylib's default shader, which must never be unloaded.
- On Android `shader_source_for_gles` turns `#version 330` into `#version 300 es` with `precision highp float;` and `precision highp int;` for the 32 bit integer hashes. The chunk and water shaders, rewritten so, pass `glslangValidator` (`sudo dnf install glslang` in the Android container).

## Sky and day

- `render_day.odin` derives the frame's `Day_Sky` from the tick and the day length. The clear colour is the horizon colour, which shows below the dome.
- The sky pass (`render_sky.odin`) draws first without depth test or writes: a dome recoloured each frame, hashed stars, the sun, and the moon with its phase. Discs face the camera along their own direction (`disc_axes`), since raylib's billboards squash a disc high in the sky into an ellipse.
- A field camera's sky follows its altitude above the planet's radius (0269, `atmosphere_sky_share`, `altitude_day_sky`): black (`SPACE_SKY_COLOR`) with the stars above `atmosphere.top_metres`, the day's from two scale heights under it; the frame is cleared to that horizon; the scene stays lit by the day. Any field camera above that height sees it (the developer's fly camera too); the block world never calls it.

## Weather

- `weather_at(seed, tick, day_length_ticks)` (`weather.odin`) is a pure schedule, not state: each game hour gets a kind (`weather_kind_weights`) and a peak intensity from a hash of the seed and the hour, ramped at the hour's ends. Rain over a column colder than `SNOW_TEMPERATURE` falls as snow.
- `render_weather.odin` blends each look value from clear to the kind's by the intensity: fog distance, sky gloom, wind and cloud shadow (`weather_fog_scales`, `weather_glooms`, `weather_wind_strengths`, `weather_cloud_shadow_strengths`). Rain darkens the sky tint (wet ground).
- The renderer reads the weather once per frame (`session_weather`): the `weather` command's forced kind (`Session.weather_override`) wins, and with the Weather setting off it is clear.
- The fog distance, rain, snow, sway and cloud shadows need the Weather setting on and reduced motion off (`weather_motion_enabled`); under reduced motion the sky still greys and the rain is still heard.
- Rain and snow wrap into a cylinder around the camera, counted by the intensity and the sky light at the camera, so none fall under a roof. The chunk shaders sway plants by the wind and dim sky light by a drifting noise texture, without shadows at night.

## Water pass

`render_water.odin` (0065).

- Water parts carry per vertex the cell's flow direction (downhill, zero for a source) and a shore value; both are part of the greedy face key, so water merges only where they match.
- `draw_water_chunks` uses its own material (`water.vs`, `water.fs`, sharing the atlas, hot reloaded with the chunk pair): alpha blending, depth test on, depth writes and backface culling off. The shader scrolls ripples over world x and z, runs the texture along the flow and lays a foam band along the shore.
- With the camera in water both materials take an underwater fog and a tint covers the view.

## Ambient life

`ambient_life.odin` (pure) and `render_life.odin` (drawing), 0075: a function of the world seed, the tick, the render time and the camera. Depth tested without depth writes. Biome densities are each biome's `bird_density` in `data/biomes.sjson`.

- Birds: a world aligned grid of flock cells holds a flock where a hash lies below the `bird_density` of the biome at the loop's centre. A flock flies a rounded rectangle above the surface (`sample_column`) on tick time (`loop_seconds`), so it is at the same place at the same tick on every run. Every period and phase comes from a hash of the flock and the bird.
- Birds draw only by day and not in heavy rain (`life_presence`).
- Insects: one in `INSECT_FLOWER_SHARE` cells of a block a ground cover marks with `insects` gets motes on Lissajous paths (`Chunk_Render.covers`), by day.
- Fish: source water cells under an open cell with water two cells deep (`water_surface_cell`; the mesh shell reaches past the chunk, so the sea surface on a chunk's bottom layer counts); `apply_chunk_mesh` keeps one in `FISH_CELL_SHARE` (`Chunk_Render.fish`). A shadow glides a lemniscate below the surface, drawn before the water pass so the surface tints it.

## Cues

`cues.odin` (0162).

- Once a frame per viewport (split screen, 0178), before the player's animation, `observe_cue_counters` reads the counters of the viewport's player and the records, and `detect_cues` compares them with `Cue_Memory` (the last frame's): the walked distance, the placed counters (`placed_total`), `blocks_mined`, the dig (`Mining_State`) and the block now at its cell, the blocks at and under the feet, the launching pads, the shipments and the quest messages.
- It returns `Frame_Cues`: a bit set of `Cue` (footstep, place, block break, dig break with its cell and block, dig quarter, launch, shipment, discovery, survey) and the walk (cadence distance, moving) with the blocks at the feet.
- The first frame of a session only learns the counters, so a loaded world neither steps, swings, chimes nor drops a capsule.
- The walk is followed per tick, not per frame, so a display faster than the tick rate neither stops the walk every other frame nor steps twice: a footstep fires once per half walk cycle of the cadence distance, which counts each tick's walk divided by the cheat speed factor, so the cheat never quickens steps or bob (`WALK_CYCLE_MILLIMETRES`).
- The landing is not a counter: the particles add `Landing` to the frame's cues when the capsule descent ends, before the sounds read them.
- The player's animation, the particles and the sounds read the cues and keep only their own state between frames.

## Particles

`particles.odin`, `render_particles.odin` (0067).

- A pool of `PARTICLE_CAPACITY` whose next index wraps, so a new particle replaces the oldest. Each `Particle_Kind` has its gravity, drag, launch speed, lifetime and growth; the step is the frame time capped at `PARTICLE_MAXIMUM_FRAME_SECONDS`.
- `emitters_for_frame` reads the working machines and the digging player; a fraction of a spawn rounds by a hash of the frame and the cell, so no emitter keeps state.
- Read from the cues (Cues): a dig break bursts, a shipment drops a capsule under a parachute (`Particle_Memory` keeps the descent and the survey satellite's pass), a footstep kicks up dust.

## The player

`player_animation.odin`, `render_player_model.odin`, `render_player.odin` (0066).

- Six limb models (`data/models/player_*.vox`), each the whole frame with its limb filled, swing about pivots read from the voxel bounds; without them a capsule stands in.
- The walk phase is the cues' cadence distance (Cues).
- The mine chop runs on the render time while `Mining_State.active`; a place swing starts on the place cue.
- First person draws the right arm and the held stack in a second 3D pass with the depth test off; third person draws the body at the interpolated pose, except while its camera is pulled in within `VIEWER_BODY_HIDDEN_WITHIN_METRES` (0.4 m, the body's 0.3 m radius plus the field's 0.1 m near plane) of the eye, where the viewport draws neither its own body nor the arm, as if in first person (`viewer_body_shown`, 0261); other players' bodies are drawn either way.
- In a field session a body whose feet cell lies in a pod's footprint is lit at the pod's interior share (`field_player_interior_light_share`, 0225), so a second player in the cabin is as dark as its walls.

## Machine models

- A machine's `model` key names a file in `data/models` (`model_mesh.odin`, `load_machine_model_mesh`): `<model>.obj` with its `.mtl` when that exists (0204), else `<model>.vox`. An OBJ (`model_obj.odin`: `v`, `vn`, `f` fan triangulated, `o`/`g`, `usemtl`, `mtllib`; MTL `newmtl`, `Kd`, `Ke`) is in cells of the unrotated footprint, x and z centred, y from the bottom, +x the front, y up, not scaled. `model_triangle_mesh.odin` refuses one whose x or z leave the footprint, or whose bottom goes below it, by more than `MODEL_FOOTPRINT_TOLERANCE_CELLS` (0.02), and reads its top off the mesh, so a chimney may rise above the footprint's height (the markers, the ghost chevron and the particle source follow the top). Every triangle is flat shaded: three vertices of its own, `Kd` times `clamp(MODEL_SHADE_BASE + dot(normal, MODEL_SHADE_GRADIENT), 0, 1)` (0.775 and (0.03, 0.225, 0.085), within 0.1 of the voxel shades on a box), the normal from `vn` or else the winding. The group named `part` is the moving part; a material with a non-zero `Ke` is emissive, drawn in its `Kd` unshaded. A voxel model (`model_vox.odin`: the first model's `SIZE`, `XYZI` and `RGBA`, z up mapped to y up) is meshed with a shade per face direction (`model_face_shades`, until 0206 removes the voxel reader) and scaled to the footprint. Both go up through `render_models.odin` as the same layers. The triangle mesher writes no indices, so its layers go up unindexed (`indices` nil, raylib draws every vertex in order) with no vertex limit, only the model check's triangle budget; the voxel mesher shares a quad's corners and goes up indexed, at most `MESH_PART_VERTEX_LIMIT` vertices a layer (u16 indices). Readers of a layer's triangles go through `model_mesh_triangle` (`model_mesh.odin`), which serves both (0226). A machine without a model keeps the box `draw_entity_cells` draws; every machine but belts, pipes, foundations and belt poles has one. The stone cutting table (0196, `tools/models/machines/stone_cutting_table.py`) is a timber workbench with a stone block, a chisel and a mallet on its top; the stone cutter (`stone_cutter.py`) a stone saw bench with a glowing fire door at the front, its vertical blade the part spinning about z through a slot in the bench top (`motion` spin, 0.6 s a turn). The foundations' pool is drawn by `draw_entities` for every entity whose machine is not a foundation (the pod and the crafting stations); `draw_frames` draws the foundations. A machine of kind `tree` (0197) holds a tree's model and is never an entity: `draw_field_trees` draws it per tree.
- A pod's hatch (0198) has a `slide`, (0221) a `spin` or (0231) an `iris` motion. A slide moves its shutter along the axis by `amplitude` cells, a spin turns it by `amplitude` turns about the pivot, times the open fraction. An iris draws the part `blades` times about the axis through the pivot (the aperture's centre), the k-th turned by k / `blades` of a turn, each opened about its own pin (`hinge`, the first blade's, turning with its blade) by `amplitude` turns times the open fraction, counterclockwise seen from the front for a positive turn (`iris_blade_transform`, `motion_part_transforms`, through which every part draw goes: the world, the ghosts, the workbench preview and the model check). The open fraction follows the hatch's state, not the clock (`hatch_open_fraction`: eased by `motion_stroke` over `period_seconds` from the tick of its last toggle, `hatch_pose`, `draw_hatch`), and its strips light while it is open. The shipped `pod_hatch` is an iris of `<n>` blades opening in 0.15 s. The model check sweeps each fixture's part, every blade, over open fractions 0 to 1 at its fixture box against the pod's body and its open cells boxes (`pod_fixture_part_problems`, 0221, 0231). The oxygen generator's strip lights while it supplies a sealed room (`oxygen_generator_supplies_a_room`).
- Point lights on models (0224): both meshers upload a normal per vertex (a voxel face's direction, an OBJ triangle's normal). The lit layer draws with the model shader (`data/shaders/model.vs`, `model.fs`, `use_model_shader`): the vertex colour times the material's diffuse colour as before, plus the vertex colour times the point lights' sum, the `field.fs` term (its point light sum copied, kept in step with it). The emissive layer draws with raylib's default material (`Model_Renderer.emissive_material`, `model_layer_material`) and never takes point lights. A model shader that fails to load logs one line and the models draw as before. A machine's record may name lamps (`lights`, [content.md](content.md), Models): positions in the model's frame, a colour and a radius in cells, put in the world by the body matrix (`entity_body_matrix`, where the model is drawn, so a lamp turns with its machine) with the radius scaled by the frame's pitch (`machine_point_light`). They shine while the model works, the flag that lights its emissive materials (`foundation_model_working` for the foundations' pool: a pod always, a hatch while open, the oxygen generator while it supplies a room; `furnace_model_working` and the others for the other pools), and share the eight slots with the arms' lamps. The block world clears the model shader's lights before it draws (`draw_session_world`). The lights have no shadows; a lamp lights only inside its machine's box, the footprint padded by `MACHINE_LIGHT_CLIP_PADDING_CELLS` up to the model's top when it rises higher (`machine_light_clip_box`, `machine_model_top`), and a face turned away from it takes nothing, so a pod's hull seen from outside, the ground round it and the machines beside it take no light from the cabin, while the cabin seen through a porthole or the open doors is lit. The renderer sends each light's matrix from the world to its box to the model shader (`point_light_boxes`, three rows a light, zero rows for a light with no box, which pass); a clipped light (colour alpha 0) fades out over a quarter of the cosine past grazing. A record lamp with `clip = false` shines out of its machine (0229). Inside a pod's box the lit tint is scaled by the pod's interior share before the point lights add (`interior_light_tint`, never below `MINIMUM_MODEL_BRIGHTNESS`, 0225); a working model's emissive materials keep their brightness, an idle one's follow the darkened tint.
- A model takes the world light of one cell above its footprint's centre (in front of a 1 by 1 machine). Palette indices from `EMISSIVE_PALETTE_START` and emissive materials glow; the OBJ group `part`, or for a voxel model an optional `<model>_part.vox`, moves per the machine's `motion` from the tick and entity state (`model_motion.odin`).
- A model is checked by the game's own mesher and motion (`model_check.odin`, `--model-check`: the budget, the part's sweep against the body, the arm's parts against its base, the open cells, a pod's fixture boxes and its fixtures' parts) and previewed by its own renderer (`--model-preview`), [build.md](build.md), The workbench.
- `tools/make_models.py` writes the OBJ machines in Blender ([build.md](build.md), Models), and `tools/make_placeholder_models.py` writes the voxel ones; both are deterministic, so rerun the one changed and commit the files.
- The OBJ machines (0204, 0205) are the stone furnace, the burner mining drill and the machines before oil: the chests, the schematic crate, the poles, the lamp, the power switch, the splitter, the electric mining drill, the boiler, the steam engine, the offshore pump, the pump, the storage tank, the wood gasifier, assembler 1, the lab, the stone cutting table and the stone cutter; since 0198 also the pod, its hatch, its locker, its crafting bench and its oxygen generator, so the pod is no longer a voxel model. The rest stay voxel models until 0206. Their look follows `DESIGN.md`, Art direction. The kit's primitives beyond the box, cylinder and cone (`tools/models/kit.py`, all in the Blender frame, a face named `+X`, `-X`, `+Y`, `-Y` or `+Z`):
  - `wedge`: a right triangular prism in a box, full height on one side.
  - `ring`: a torus of few sides with a flat of its tube facing outward, for flanges and valve wheels.
  - `pipe`: a round pipe along a path of points, every corner bent by three arc points the kit computes (a radius of twice the pipe's by default), its ends capped; 8 or 12 sides.
  - `hatch`: a bevelled plate on a face with a handle bar standing off it.
  - `rib_row`: ribs along an axis, each nudged off its even spot by a seeded draw.
  - `strip`: a thin plate set into a face, for emissive indicators.
  - `opening`: a box cut out of a volume; its walls keep the volume's material, so a dark inside is a volume of its own.
  - `expect_footprint` stops the build when the record's footprint differs from the one the script was designed for.
- The stone furnace (0212, `tools/models/machines/stone_furnace.py`) is the first machine made from nothing in the art direction, from the reference sheet of 0211, in a sealed lab from the reference images alone: 10 by 10 by 12 cells (5 by 5 by 6 m), solid over its whole footprint (no open cells). A tapering rubble stone stack of about 150 raised stones in courses with slanted joints on a riveted iron frame with diagonal braces, corner blocks and a top band; a rust hood with a riveted collar, plate seams and a stone lined throat; the firebox mouth in a chamfered iron frame with a glowing coal bed and a flame sheet against a dark back; a sloped console on the left face with two screens, light bars, lights, buttons, toggles, sliders and a lever, three hoses round the front corner; pipes from the console into the wall and down the back corner, a tall roof pipe with a downturned top; an iron box with a hatch and a small slanted panel on the right face. Eight materials (`furnace_stone`, `furnace_mortar`, `furnace_iron`, `furnace_rust`, `console_white`, `signal_glow`, `heat_glow`, `electric_glow`), 3173 triangles.
- The seed rule: a detail row draws from `model_random(machine, salt)`, a `random.Random` seeded with the CRC-32 of `<model>:<salt>` (never Python's `hash`, which is salted per process), one salt per row, so the same script writes the same model and editing one row leaves the others' draws unchanged.
- The pod (0179, 0198, 0221, `tools/models/machines/pod.py`): an OBJ of its 12 by 12 by 8 cells, the cone and the cabin of the sealed lab ([content.md](content.md), Models), its outer door at the model's front (+x, turned to the frame's forward by `place_pod`). Its body has the budget of 25600 triangles (`MODEL_POD_BODY_TRIANGLES_MAXIMUM`, `model_body_triangles_maximum`, the user's of 2026-10-04 for the model every player sees first, from inside, at arm's length; every other body keeps 3200), and uses 25488. Its emissive materials (screens, lights) are always lit (`foundation_model_working`), and it lists two lamps: the overhead strip and the desk light. It draws as a machine on a frame does: `draw_entities` gives it `draw_entity_cells` (it rides in the foundations' pool, which the other loops skip), so its model goes under its frame's matrix (`entity_frame_matrix`, the pitch scaling the cells to metres) with the door towards the frame's forward; `draw_frames` skips its cells. Its record's `interior_light_share` darkens its hull, cabin and fixtures to that share of the light round them, inside and out, so the lamps' pools show and the portholes' and screens' emissive patches stand out (0225); the shipped pod has 0.25 (0221). ### The arm

The inserter's arm (0175, `model_arm.odin`, `render_arm.odin`), the first model at real scale; it replaced the inserter's miniature crane.

- Files: `tools/make_placeholder_models.py` (`arm()`) writes six, `arm.vox` (the base) and `arm_turret.vox`, `arm_upper_arm.vox`, `arm_forearm.vox`, `arm_gripper.vox`, `arm_finger.vox` (`arm_part_suffixes` after the model's name), each the whole `ARM_MODEL_FRAME` (24 by 128 by 24 voxels) at `ARM_VOXEL_MILLIMETRES` (25) with only its part filled, standing in the authored pose: straight up, fingers up, the joints on the frame's vertical centre line, the shoulder 22 voxels up (0.55 m), the elbow 50 above it (1.25 m), the wrist 42 above that (1.05 m) and the hand between the fingertips 12 above the wrist (0.3 m), as `model_arm.odin` has them in metres. A machine with `motion = {kind = "arm"}` loads them all with the machine models (`load_arm_part_meshes`, into `Machine_Model_Mesh.arm`, one mesh per part and layer) instead of a body and a part; a missing or misshaped file refuses the models as any model does. Grimy steel and yellow warning paint with black hazard stripes; a work lamp (emissive) on the wrist block. Nothing of the upper arm lies below the shoulder: its plates and hub start at the joint (0207), since the segment stretches about the joint and anything below it would sink into the base at a long reach.
- Joints: the turret turns about the vertical (yaw), the upper arm about the shoulder, the forearm about the elbow and the gripper about the wrist, all about the arm's sideways axis; the finger is drawn twice, offset either side of the gripper by the grip (open `ARM_FINGER_OPEN_METRES`, closed `ARM_FINGER_CLOSED_METRES`). `arm_part_transforms` chains them from the joint angles; `arm_reach_angles` solves the two segments for a wrist position, the elbow up.
- Scale (`arm_dimensions`): the parts keep their thickness in metres on every frame. The segments together are 2.3 m against the 2 m reach, so the elbow stays bent at full reach (over 40 degrees, `test_the_elbow_stays_bent_at_full_reach`). They scale with the span from the shoulder to the wrist over a cell at the reach, so they are their authored length at 2 m over 500 mm and bend alike on every frame: shorter on the block frame's one metre, longer for the long inserter's 4 m. The hand stops `ARM_WORK_CLEARANCE_METRES` over the top of the target cell, a cell of the frame's pitch. The base plate's width follows the pitch, so the base fills one cell; the inserter's footprint in cells stays one by one by one until the slice's real sizes (0179). `arm_entity_transform` puts the arm on its footprint's bottom centre, turned to the facing, in the frame's cells.
- Animation (`arm_pose_at`): the pose is a pure function of the inserter's cycle fraction (`inserter_cycle_fraction`), so the cadence is the work: rest folded over the base at 0, reach to the pickup cell, grab, swing over the right hand side lifted and drawn in, lower onto the drop cell at one half, release, swing back and fold. An idle arm (nothing to pick) rests folded, an arm waiting for room holds over the drop cell, and an arm out of fuel or power stops mid move. The rest pose (`arm_rest_pose`) stands the upper arm with its elbow just behind the turret's axis and hangs the forearm forward to a wrist within the base for any length of the segments. The held item shows between the grab and the release (`arm_shows_held_item`), since the simulation takes it at the cycle's start. Tests: at rest the elbow, the wrist, the hand and the lamp lie within the base's footprint, and at full reach the hand is over the top of the drop cell and at the grab over the pickup cell, for every facing, on the 500, 333 and 1000 mm frames, the long arm and the block frame.
- Light: the lamp lights only while the arm moves (`Inserter_State.Moving`): at rest its emissive voxels are dark (`ARM_LAMP_DARK_SHARE` of the light round it), while the other machines' emissive voxels keep their old look at rest, lit like the rest of the model. While it moves a point light shines from the lamp (`arm_point_light`, `ARM_LIGHT_COLOR`, `ARM_LIGHT_RADIUS_METRES`) for the field shader (The field shader, above) and the model shader, so it lights the models round it too, the arm's own lit layer included (Machine models); it has no clip box (0229). The block world's renderer takes no point lights, and the models' lights are cleared there.
- Ghosts: an arm's placement ghost is the arm at rest, and its model top (`machine_model_top`) is the folded arm's on the block frame, so the ghost's chevron clears it.
- Without its models an inserter is drawn as the post and bar it had before, in its frame's cells. The frame renderer draws foundations only (0179); a machine on a frame, the pod included, takes `draw_entities`' path under its frame's matrix.
- The planet preview's screenshot holds one arm at full reach, working (`Model_Frame.reaching_arm`, `model_frame_arm_placement`), so its lamp's point light shows; in a session every arm follows its cycle.

## Runs

Belt and pipe runs between poles (0176, `render_belt_runs.odin`; the curve: [logistics.md](logistics.md), Runs), drawn after the frames by the field session (0179) and the planet preview.

- Swept meshes: one cross section per polyline point (`belt_run_sections`), its up blended from the start frame's up to the end frame's along the run, its right across the tangent (the neighbouring points' difference). A belt run is one quad per subdivision between two sections, a cell of the start frame's pitch wide at `BELT_SURFACE_HEIGHT` of a cell over the polyline, textured with the belt texture, its v counting cells along the run so the stripes keep the block belt's spacing, and scrolled at the run's belt speed as the belt meshes are (`belt_scroll_offset`); its sides hang `BELT_RUN_SIDE_DEPTH_SHARE` of the pitch below the surface, so a run seen edge on reads as a belt. Both windings, like the belt quad. A pipe run is a cylinder of `PIPE_COLOR` per subdivision, its radius `PIPE_RUN_RADIUS_SHARE` of the pitch, lying on the polyline.
- Items on a run stand at their distance through `belt_run_point_at`, offset across the section to their lane by `BELT_LANE_OFFSET` of the pitch, drawn as cubes of their category's colour.
- A belt pole is a post from its cell's bottom to the run end (`draw_belt_poles`).
- The ghost (`draw_belt_run_ghost`): once the run tool's start is picked, the curve to the reticle's candidate as an untextured see-through surface, white, red when the shape would be refused.

## Sound

`audio.odin` (mixer), `sound_events.odin` (triggers), 0068.

- Without an audio device every call is a no op. The mixer loads `data/sounds/sounds.sjson`: effects as `Sound`, never twice within `EFFECT_MINIMUM_GAP_SECONDS` (raylib replays a `Sound` on one voice), loops as `Music` fading to the frame's target over `LOOP_FADE_SECONDS`, so a loop nobody asks for fades out.
- Loops and ambience calls follow the ambience volume, other effects the effects volume, all the master volume.
- The effects follow the cues (Cues): footsteps by the material underfoot, mining hits per dig quarter by tool tier (at most `MINING_HIT_MAXIMUM_PER_SECOND`, paced by `Sound_Memory`), break, place, launch, landing, the discovery chime; the UI sounds follow `ui_sound_ids`. A field session plays the arrival's roar, crash, bang and patter and the hatches' slide instead (The field session, The arrival).
- Hum: the nearest working machine (as the bottleneck markers define working) within `HUM_RANGE_BLOCKS` picks its family (fluid, electric, burner), with a drifting level.
- Ambience: the biome under the player picks a loop, or variants `ambience_<name>_1`, `_2` played in clusters of calls with long hashed pauses; a `day_only` ambience calls only by day.
- Rain plays at the intensity times the open sky at the eye; snow is silent.
