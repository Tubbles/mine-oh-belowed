# Architecture

How the game is put together. The agent rules (stack, imports, determinism, naming) are in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md), what is drawn and heard in [presentation.md](presentation.md), building and shipping in [build.md](build.md).

- One `game` package, data oriented: procedures over arrays of structs, no entity component system.
- The simulation ticks at a fixed rate, owns the `World` and the game's records (`Game_Records`) and never reads the wall clock, the frame time, the settings or the socket.
- Worker threads only generate and mesh chunks; everything else runs on the main thread.

## Stack

- raylib's GLFW has the Wayland and the X11 backend and takes Wayland when the session offers one ([build.md](build.md), Display). The window asks for high DPI (`WINDOW_HIGHDPI`), so on a scaled Wayland desktop the framebuffer has the panel's pixels.
- raylib also gives rlgl rendering, textures, fonts and audio; SDL3 gives the controller ([input.md](input.md)).

## Frame and tick

Rule: frames render as fast as allowed, the simulation advances in fixed ticks, and only rendering interpolates.

- The frame renders at vsync or at the frame rate cap from the settings (both optional). An accumulator runs 60 ticks per second (`tick_rate` in `data/game.sjson`).
- The camera and the player's drawn pose interpolate from the previous tick's player state to the current one.
- Frame input reaches ticks through `Tick_Input_Accumulator` ([input.md](input.md), The input frame).
- Players are an array in the simulation state, each with its own camera and input frame; each machine of a session drives one, or one per viewport in split screen, and a tick takes every player's input (Multiplayer).
- Viewports (`viewport.odin`, work item 0178): up to four local players on one machine, one simulation. `Frame_State` holds `viewports` (`viewport_count` in use, the first always there) beside its groups; a viewport holds its player's index, its device (a pad's SDL id, the keyboard and mouse for the first), its rectangle of the window, its tick input accumulator, its request set and the per player halves of the interaction and presentation groups (`Viewport_Interaction`: input, `Ui_State`, session views, biome banner, rumble, world action guard; `Viewport_Presentation`: camera, sprint kick, cue memory, particles, body animation, render target). One viewport covers the window, two split it stacked or side by side (`split_screen`), three and four take quarters (`viewport_rectangles`).
- A frame with viewports: the input is read per viewport, the ticks run once (`update_session` stamps one record per viewport's player), and the render pass runs per viewport. Offline only the first viewport's pausing screen holds the ticks; a guest's pause menu and settings run with the world going on, as they do online, so one couch player in the settings does not freeze the others. A viewport whose player has no entry yet opens only the pause menu and the settings (`WAITING_PLAYER_SCREENS`), since every other screen reads the player. One viewport draws straight to the window as single player always did; in split screen each viewport's world and UI draw into its render texture at its size (`draw_split_viewports`), since raylib's `BeginMode3D` takes its aspect from the current framebuffer, so the projection, the frustum culling and the hands' pass follow the viewport without a hand built projection; the textures then go onto their rectangles with blending off (a target's alpha is below 1 under its translucent draws) and the diagnostics draw over the whole window. A UI clip takes its scissor in the target's own height (`ui_target_height`), and each viewport keeps its own map image cache. The cue memory, the particles and the body animation are per viewport; the mixer and the sound memory are global and play once a frame from the first viewport's cues and player, so the other players' steps and digs are silent in split screen. A viewport whose player has no entry yet (its join tick has not run) draws nothing but its notice.
- Random generators are seeded by the world seed, a chunk coordinate or the tick. Belt positions, fluid volumes, energy buffers, crafting progress and vein reservoirs are fixed point integers.
- Player physics of the block world is the one place plain `f32` accumulates (the field player is integer, The player on the field): axis separated swept collision against block collision boxes (`block_shape.odin`: a cube's cell, a slab's half, the two boxes of stairs, none for torches) and the whole cells of entities other than belts and splitters. It stays deterministic for one binary and one input stream.
- The raycast hits a shaped block only where it meets its boxes, or a post's bounds.
- Requests between frames: a screen never frees or replaces memory the frame draws from. It adds a `Frame_Request` to its viewport's `Frame_Requests` (`Screen_Context.requests`). After the UI pass the game's requests (`GLOBAL_FRAME_REQUESTS`: the reload and the quit) move into `Frame_State.requests` (`collect_global_requests`), so they serve once however many viewports asked; the rest serve the viewport that asked, its screen and its toasts. The loop serves them in one order (`serve_frame_requests_before_draw` in `loop.odin`, viewport by viewport): before the draw the texture editor's refresh and save, the data browser's close (the last frame's draw list pointed into the file), discard, save, export, tree refresh and open, then the categories a discard or save changed, then the touch layout editor's request and the layouts' write; in the draw, after the UI pass and before the frame is shown, the screenshot (of the whole window, toasted in the asking viewport); after the draw and the audio a guest viewport's leaving (`Remove_Viewport`, which frees its UI), the title's session request, the data watch and the reload, which frees memory the frame drew from; the quit ends the loop before the next frame. The pause menu's save stays on the session, since the frame's ticks take it (`save_when_due`).

## Sessions

- The window, renderer, UI and input backend exist once per process. A `Session` holds one world: the simulation, a copy of the generator with the world's seed and richness, the streaming state, the save location, the tick accumulator, the forced weather, the lockstep driver (`Lockstep`) and the network (`Session_Network`, offline in single player). The per session UI state (the recipe and technology browsers, the statistics and the map, `Session_Views`) lives beside the session in each viewport, not in it.
- The process state is `Frame_State` (`loop.odin`): the loop's own fields (configuration, content, session, frame timing, directories, settings, the viewports) at the top, the rest in four groups, `interaction` (the input devices, the fonts, the touch overlay, the title), `presentation` (the renderers, atlases, display state and sound), `developer` (diagnostics, the command socket, the texture editor and data browser) and `reload` (hot reload's watcher, arenas and request). Each viewport holds the per player halves of the first two (Frame and tick). `run_game` keeps it on the heap, as four viewports make it too large for a stack frame.
- The title state has no session. Starting or loading a world creates one; quit to title saves it, destroys it and drops the chunk meshes.
- Menus request session changes and the frame loop applies them after the frame, so a failed start toasts and leaves the menus in place.
- Biomes and vein tables load once and are copied per session; the research cost setting makes a per session scaled copy of the technology registry.
- The world settings of the field (0179, [content.md](content.md), Planets): `start_session` resolves the planet first (`resolve_world_planet`): the settings' planet from `data/planets.sjson` (or, with a log line, the default one), and its generation values from the world file's record when it has one, else from the data at the settings' radius preset (a radius that is not a preset falls back to the default with a log line). The session owns the result, the data's record with those values (`Session.planet`, `make_recorded_planet`); `World.planet` and the settings' planet id borrow it. The mode and keep inventory are stored settings only.

### The field session

New World and Load start a field session (0179, `session_plays_field`, `start_field_world`): the players walk the terrain field of the world's planet. A world made without planet data and the debug terrain stay block sessions; the tests reach the block world that way, the title never does.

- The state: `Simulation_State.field` (`Field_Simulation`, `field_mining.odin`) holds the field world, the tick's queues (brush edits, placements; empty between ticks), the torches and the simulated chunk set. Each player's body, credit and refusals are on `Player` (`Player.field`, `field_credit`, `field_refusal`), its inventory is `Player.inventory`, and the frames, foundations, machines on frames, poles and runs live in `World.entities`. The field's tables are `Simulation_Content.field` (`Field_Content`, made per session by `make_field_content` from the planet, the spacing and `data/game.sjson`).
- The tick (`simulation_field.odin`): after the commands apply, each player in order turns its input frame into the field's integers (`field_tick_input`), the hotbar's selected stack decides the tool (`field_tool_for_item`: a material's item places it, a foundation item its foundation (0196: the held item's tier), a belt or pole item the belt run tool, a pipe item the pipe run tool, another machine item the frame placement with Next_Brush turning it, the torch item the torch; anything else is the hand), the player moves and queues its edit and placements, `Open_Aimed` (the inventory binding the presentation routed, 0194) on a frame's machine opens its panel and Interact turns a switch (`interact_on_field`; a pad launches from its panel, 0233); then the queues drain, the water steps and the light spreads (`finish_field_tick`), and the entities tick as in the block world. Dig is always the hand tool; Next_Brush cycles the brushes. `drain_field_dig` fills the edit's dig rate from the material table and its tick.
- The trees (0197, `field_trees.odin`): the standing trees are the generation's (World generation); the state holds only the felled keys (`Field_Simulation.felled_trees`, hashed and saved). Each tick a player is pushed out of the trunks within its reach (the trunk a capsule of the species' radius and height, scaled with the tree; skipped in fly with no clip) and aims at the nearest trunk the view ray meets before the ground and the frames (`move_and_aim_field_player`, run by the tick and the lockstep's prediction alike, so `Field_Player.tree_target` agrees on every machine). Mine held on the aimed trunk advances `Player.mining` (`mining.tree`) to the species' felling time (`advance_field_felling`); at its end a `.Fell` placement drains (`drain_field_felling`), which checks the tree still stands and the yield fits, adds the species' items and the key to the felled set. The edit's drain fells without yield every standing tree the brush reaches whose ground on the trunk's axis half the trunk's height below its base is hollow (`fell_trees_over_dug_ground`; a probe in a chunk not loaded leaves the tree). The species come from the planet's data cut to the recorded count (`make_field_content`, `Field_Content.tree_species`).
- The torch: Place with the torch held queues a torch at the air sample in front of the targeted ground (`field_torch_sample`), which takes one torch item at the drain and adds the emitter of `data/game.sjson`'s `field_simulation.torch_emitter` to the light; a Dig press aimed at a torch within the reach and nearer along the ray than the terrain or frame hit (`field_aimed_torch`, `field_aim_limit`) takes it back with its item, or reports `Inventory_Full` when there is no room; a held Dig digs. `Field_Simulation.torches` lists them; the frame draws each as a small glowing box.
- The simulated set (`simulation_field_chunk_set.odin`) mirrors the block world's (Threads and chunk streaming): at the start of each tick the set becomes every field chunk within `field_simulation.chunk_radius` of a player's chunk plus the chunks it held within the margin more, and the field world holds exactly these. The frame asks the field streaming's workers for the set and one ring more (`field_chunk_requests`); a generated chunk comes back as a `Field_Chunk_Ready_Command` without a player, which stays on this machine (`command_is_relayed`) and is never sent: the next tick stages it among the arrived chunks, and it enters the world inside a tick in coordinate order with its saved bytes when a tick changed it before. A tick whose set holds a chunk this machine has not generated yet does not run (`field_chunks_ready`), so every machine inserts the same chunks at the same tick from its own generation, which is the same on every machine. A chunk a tick changed that leaves the set is compared with a fresh generation of it: one whose terrain or water still differs keeps its bytes and its state hash, computed once, in `Field_Simulation.saved_chunks`; one that went back to its generation (water the sea woke and that slept again) leaves nothing (`unload_left_field_chunks`). Brush edits skip chunks outside the set, and the water's sea edge and missing chunk rule read it, so both agree on every machine. A loaded world's set enters the same way (0185): the load leaves the world empty and the set restoring (`Field_Chunk_Set.restoring`), the frame asks the workers for the saved set too (`needed_field_chunks`), and the first tick waits until every chunk of it has arrived, then inserts them in coordinate order as they were saved (`restore_arrived_field_set`). Until then the frames run (the network's pings, the UI) and the viewport shows the loading notice from the first frame; a world alone takes no part of the set early, since a partial restore would differ from the saved world.
- The start: a new world lays the pod at the planet's home (`home` of `data/planets.sjson`, `planet_home_direction`) on the floor of the crater the generation digs there (0199, World generation): `field_home_site` reads the generated surface at the home, which is the crater floor, and `place_pod` in `enable_new_field_world` stands the pod on a free frame there, its frame's forward the yaw step of the heading towards the first spring (`field_home_heading`: the home to spring chord normalised and projected onto the home's tangent plane, so the spring's distance does not matter; the shipped door lies 6 degrees off the spring at every preset, 0260), with no foundations, its hatches closed (the airlock opens them one at a time as a player comes, 0222) and its fixtures placed (0198, [content.md](content.md), The pod); a loaded world has it in its save. Every new player spawns inside the pod's cabin, at the centre of the floor of its record's first `open_cells` box, the clearance above it and facing the door (`field_pod_spawn`, `pod_cabin_floor_centre`), with the starter kit (`field_spawn_player`, `make_field_session_player`); it reads the saved pod and frame, so it holds for a joiner and a loaded world. Without a pod in the world the player stands on the generated surface at the home facing the spring (`field_home_player`). The first player of a new world and every joining one (`add_player_entry`, which the local add and the network join reach through `Add_Player_Command`) come through it.
- The arrival (0200, `simulation_arrival.odin`): a new world's first `arrival_ticks` ([content.md](content.md), The arrival) are the pod's fall. `start_field_world` begins it for a new world only (`begin_field_arrival`; the benchmark's world never begins one, and the planet preview clears it after its session starts, so neither falls) in `Field_Simulation.arrival` (`Field_Arrival`: the start tick, the length, the landed tick). While it falls every player's input frame is empty (`arrival_input`), in the tick (`tick_field_session_players`) and in the prediction (`rebuild_prediction`); the screens' commands still apply. At the end of its last tick, or at the start of the tick a `Skip_Arrival_Command` applies (the pause menu's Skip arrival, relayed like every player command, idempotent, any player), it lands (`land_field_arrival`): `landed_tick` is set; the hatches stay closed (0222, until 0221 the landing opened both). It is saved in its table (Save format), since a Skip before the planned end and a save inside the fall must survive a load and a join snapshot; a world loaded or joined past the fall never falls again. The presentation reads it and never writes it ([presentation.md](presentation.md), The arrival).
- The airlock (0222, 0231, `entity_pod_airlock.odin`, [content.md](content.md), Hatches): `tick_field_session_players` steps every hatch (`tick_pod_airlocks`) after `finish_field_tick` and before the refusal events and the landing, skipped while the world falls (so on the landing tick too, the landing coming after). A hatch is open exactly while some player's capsule is within the reach of its cells (0231), toggled through `toggle_hatch`, so it is lockstep state, hashed and saved; the prediction never runs it, as it never runs `toggle_hatch`.
- Refusals: after the drain, a player's `field_refusal` raises a `Field_Refused` simulation event carrying it, which the HUD toasts through `field_refusal_keys` (`data/strings/en.sjson`), when it differs from the last tick's or Dig or Place was pressed this tick (`field_refusal_is_news`), so a held Dig refused tick after tick toasts once.
- The veins: `start_field_world` registers the planet's starter veins (`register_planet_veins`) after the field is enabled or restored, for a new world (their reservoirs) and a loaded one (which keeps its reservoirs and gets the discs back, which the save leaves out). Generation and the registry plan the discs round the same home, the planet record's (`make_planet_generation` reads `planet.home`; a planet without one takes the north pole).
- The day: the shared clock's daylight (`daylight_blend`) lights the field shader, the same at every longitude; the sky dome turns about the viewer's up (`field_sky_camera`).
- The state hash covers the players' field state and credit (reflected with `Player`) and the set's chunks: density, material, water fill, still ticks, the awake and source bits, not the tint, the flow or the light planes (`field_chunk_state_hash`, cached per chunk and recomputed after a change), plus the hash each kept chunk outside the set took when it was kept. The light's emitters and its pending queues are hashed with the entity tables (`write_field_tables`), so the light is covered through its inputs.
- The frame side (`loop_field_session.odin`): the field renderer is made when a field session starts; one node selection serves every viewport (`Field_View.more_cameras`, a node's distance is its nearest eye's), and the streaming meshes the finest nodes from the simulated chunks where a node's chunk and its shell are in the set, from a generated grid elsewhere. A server takes the edited chunks every frame and meshes nothing. The block renderer is not called for a field session ([presentation.md](presentation.md), The field session), and the sounds and cues, which read the block world, are off.

## Multiplayer

Rule: every machine runs the whole simulation from the same inputs (Factorio style lockstep, work item 0177); nothing but input records and the join snapshot crosses the network.

- Input records (`lockstep.odin`): one per player per tick, holding the tick's `Input_Frame` without the raw device state (`Record_Input`, an `#assert` keeps the two in step), the player's queued commands (`Player_Command`, Simulation) and the command socket's write lines. A tick runs when every connected member's record for it is there (`lockstep_records_ready`); a member has a join tick and a leave tick, so a player that joined later or left is not waited for. The socket lines run inside the tick through its hook (`Tick_Hook`), after the simulated chunk set is derived and before the commands apply, so a teleport moves the set alike on every machine.
- The driver is the same for single player: one member, no network, a window of zero, the local records delivered straight back, one tick per accumulator tick as before.
- Every game hosts (0188, `session_discovery.odin`): a windowed game's own world (new, loaded or from the command line, not a joined one) hosts from the frame that takes it (`host_windowed_session` in `enter_session`), its listener, relay and join snapshot running in the frame loop on the main thread as the server's do, the local player the host's own. The listener takes the first free port of `DEFAULT_NETWORK_PORT` and the nine after it (`HOST_PORT_COUNT`, `listen_on_free_port`) and logs `network: hosting on port N` (the server `server: hosting on port N`), so a second game on the machine hosts too; a game that cannot host plays offline with a toast. A host no machine joined yet is alone (`session_alone`) and plays as single player: a pausing screen and the socket's `pause` hold its ticks, the socket's `tick` and the content reload run, and a tick waiting for chunks takes the arrived ones. A host whose joiner still restores is not alone (0190), so its pause and the socket's `tick` wait like in any online session. Alone, its records still go through the relay (`deliver_records_alone`), so its frontier, the next join's tick, stays true; a join that lands during the frame's exchange stops the socket's tick command for that frame. The host toasts the players on the game when a joiner's player arrives or a machine leaves (`session_player_count`: members that joined and have not left).
- LAN discovery (`platform/network_discovery.odin`): UDP on `DISCOVERY_PORT` (47316). The title's Multiplayer screen queries once a second while it shows (`serve_lan_query`, between frames) to the limited broadcast address and each interface's subnet broadcast, read once when the screen opens (`discovery_broadcast_endpoints`; on Linux and Android through the `SIOCGIFCONF` requests, on Windows through `GetAdaptersAddresses`); every hosting game, the server included, answers the query's sender (`answer_discovery_queries`, in `update_host_network`). Several games on one machine share the discovery port through the address reuse, and a broadcast reaches each. A datagram is text: the line `mine-oh-belowed-discovery 1`, then `key=value` lines each ending in a newline (a query `kind=query` and `pad`, filled to the 512 byte maximum; an answer `kind=answer`, `world`, `host`, `players`, `port`, `build`), at most 512 bytes, the answer's texts cut to 128 bytes of valid UTF-8 without control characters. A host answers only a query at least as long as its answer, so a query with a forged sender cannot make it send more than it received. The parse is strict: another tag, an empty line, a last line without its newline, an unknown, repeated or missing key, a value with a control character or invalid UTF-8, a number out of range or a longer datagram is dropped. The screen's list is keyed by the answer's address and TCP port, each answer refreshes its game, and a game unanswered for three seconds drops out (`LAN_GAME_LIFETIME`). There is no player cap, so an answer carries none.
- Several local members (split screen, 0178): a machine drives a list of local players (`Lockstep.locals`, `Local_Member`), the first its own (the command socket's lines ride in its records), each with its own records stamped per tick, its held commands and its prediction. They go out over the machine's one connection; the host keeps the players of each client (`Network_Peer.players`) and checks each one's records apart. A local player joins through the add player path of a join (`request_local_player`, `next_join`): offline and on the host at once, from the tick after the newest record relayed; a client sends `Add_Local_Player`, the host announces the member and answers `Local_Player_Added` with the player and the join tick. A guest leaves from the tick after its last stamped record (`leave_local_player`, `Remove_Local_Player` from a client); its entry stays for a later join. A client guest that leaves before the host's answer is counted (`cancelled_local_players`), and the answer's member leaves at once when it lands, so a local member never exists without a viewport and no machine waits for its records. How many local members feed a machine changes nothing a tick reads (`test_two_local_members_keep_the_hash_of_one_member_machines`).
- Window and prediction: a machine stamps its local records up to its window ahead of the simulation; the window is the measured round trip in ticks, rounded up, plus one (`latency_window_ticks`). Inside the window each local player's movement and camera are predicted: after the frame's ticks the confirmed player is copied and moved by its stamped inputs not yet run (`rebuild_prediction`); the rebuild is the reconciliation: a confirmed state that differs from the prediction (another player's edit, a frame placed in the way) replaces it on the next rebuild, a snap without a blend. In a field session the copy runs the field tick's movement alone (`predict_field_player_motion`, 0182): `tick_field_player` against the loaded set and the frame table, and the aim; no walk counter, edit, placement, pick up progress (0195) or event comes from it, so the mining progress the HUD draws is the confirmed tick's. The field camera, the eye the field streams round, the third person body, the placement ghost, the placement editor's outline, anchor and ghost (0215) and the HUD's tool line read the predicted field player (`lockstep_view_player`, `Field_Scene.lockstep`, `Hud_Context.field_view`). The prediction keeps the confirmed tool, held machine and rotation, so a hotbar change or a rotate inside the window shows on the ghost a window late. During a new world's fall the prediction holds the input as the tick does (`arrival_input`, 0200). Its cost is up to the window's field steps per local player per frame, each with the tick's probes over the field and the frame table; it is not measured. Only the presentation reads the prediction, and the hash is the same with and without it (`test_the_field_prediction_matches_the_confirmed_walk`).
- Transport (`platform/network.odin`): TCP through `core:net` on every target, non blocking sockets, messages with a little endian `u32` length prefix and at most 512 MB (`MAXIMUM_NETWORK_MESSAGE_SIZE`); a larger length closes the connection. The default port is 47317 (`DEFAULT_NETWORK_PORT`). A client connects on a thread of its own (`start_dial`), since `net.dial_tcp` blocks up to the system's connect timeout; the frame polls it and gives up after `NETWORK_TIMEOUT`.
- Roles (`session_network.odin`): the host relays every record to every machine, itself included, and has no authority over the state. The messages and their encoding (the save codec's `write_value_of` and `read_value_of`) are listed at the top of that file.
- Keepalive: every machine pings each peer every second (`NETWORK_KEEPALIVE_INTERVAL`) and drops a peer from which no byte arrived for ten seconds (`NETWORK_TIMEOUT`), so a silent client leaves like a closed one and a silent host ends the session.
- The host trusts no client's numbers: a record must be for the tick after the client's last one and no further ahead of the host than the client's window plus `RECORD_TICK_MARGIN`, else the client is dropped; a client whose unsent bytes pass `MAXIMUM_CLIENT_BACKLOG_BYTES` (beyond its join snapshot) is dropped; a machine has at most `MAXIMUM_PENDING_HASH_REPORTS` reports waiting, and reports older than the host's kept hashes go. Every machine refuses a command whose indices its content lacks (`player_command_valid`) instead of indexing past a table.
- State hash: every `STATE_HASH_INTERVAL_TICKS` (300) ticks each machine hashes its state (`lockstep_state_hash`: the loaded columns' surfaces refreshed, then `simulation_state_hash`, cheat speed, free crafting and the saved blocks of the chunks not loaded), the clients report theirs, and the host compares. A mismatch is logged with the tick and both hashes and toasted on every machine naming the machine that differs; play continues.
- Join: a client measures the round trip with a ping and asks to join with its window, its build stamp and a hash of its content tables (`content_tables_hash`); the host refuses another build or other tables with the reason (`Join_Refused`). Otherwise it sends a snapshot: the save's files in memory (`encode_save_files`), the simulated chunk set with its radii, the members and the records after its tick, but no player (0190). From the snapshot on the joiner gets every record and member change like a client (`Network_Peer.receives_records`) and no machine waits for it, so the host plays on while it restores; a host with a joiner restoring is no longer alone, and a server with nobody playing holds its clock's ticks while one restores, since no record would tell the joiner they ran. Records and member changes that arrive with the snapshot are held until the session exists (`take_held_messages`). The joiner loads it (`start_joined_session`, `load_world_from_files`) and, still on the title with a loading notice, runs the held and arriving records (`catch_up_joined_session`). Once its world is restored and no relayed tick is left to run (`joined_world_caught_up`), it asks for its player through the add player path (`request_own_player`, `Add_Local_Player`): the host takes the first save entry no member holds, else a new one an `Add_Player_Command` adds on every machine, from the tick after the newest record relayed (`next_join`), announces the member and answers `Local_Player_Added`; from that tick every machine waits for the joiner's records, which follow within its window, a short wait instead of the whole restore. The frame takes the world only once the local player's entry exists and no record is left to run (`joined_session_ready`). A joiner that drops before it asked leaves no member, entry or waited tick behind. A join starts from `--join` or the Multiplayer screen, both through the title's `Join` request (`start_title_join`), only while no world plays and no other join runs. A local world started while a join is pending cancels the join. The content reload (F8, the Developer screen, the socket's `reload`, watch all) is refused while other machines play or a join runs.
- Leaving: a client that disconnects leaves from the tick after its last relayed record, on every machine; a client whose host is gone returns to the title with a toast.
- Server (`session_server.odin`, `--server`): a host without a window, input backend or local player, on `--port` or the game's port range, answering the discovery like a game; with nobody connected the clock paces its ticks (not while a joiner restores, see Join). It empties the events and quest notices only a UI would, saves on the autosave interval (a failed save waits for the next one) and on SIGINT or SIGTERM before it exits (not on Windows, whose stop handler is a stub), and does not serve the command socket.
- Known limits: player physics is `f32` and may differ between processor architectures; a host name needs a resolver, so the phone joins by IP address.

## Source layout

- Files by concern: `world_*.odin`, `generation_*.odin`, `render_*.odin`, `input_*.odin`, `ui_*.odin`, `data_*.odin`, `save_*.odin`, one file per entity kind (`furnace.odin`, `inserter.odin`), tests beside their file as `*_test.odin`.
- The files of the game package form seven clusters by prefix with an allowed dependency table between them; [code_map.md](code_map.md) lists every file by cluster.
- Leaf utilities with no back references are packages under `src/` (Odin forbids import cycles), each a cluster of its own: `src/platform/`, `src/generation_seed/`, `src/model_vox/`, `src/model_obj/`, `src/render_frustum/`, `src/run_length/`, `src/sjson_text/` (the 0145 pilot split) and `src/android_libc/` ([code_map.md](code_map.md), Packages).

## Threads and chunk streaming

Rule: workers never touch the `World`; results reach the main thread through mutex protected queues.

- Workers: `core:thread`, processor cores minus one, 1 to 6 (`default_worker_count`). A generate job carries a coordinate; a mesh job carries private copies of the chunk and a one cell shell of blocks and light from all 26 neighbours.
- Per frame limits: 16 generated chunks inserted, 8 mesh jobs submitted, 6 non empty mesh uploads, 48 jobs pending (`world_streaming.odin`).
- Streaming loads what the simulated chunk set asks for (Simulation), its radius plus one ring ahead (`SIMULATED_CHUNK_PREFETCH`); the tick inserts and unloads the chunks, not the frame. The fog still reads `LOAD_RADIUS_HORIZONTAL` (6). The debug terrain, the tests and the benchmark turn the set off and load around the camera as before.
- A chunk is meshed once all its neighbours inside the load volume are loaded. Stale mesh results are dropped by revision.
- The terrain field streams beside the blocks on its own workers in the same pattern (`world_field_streaming.odin`): each frame the main thread takes the selected level of detail nodes (`select_field_nodes`) and the chunks the simulated set asks for (`field_chunk_requests`, The field session), has the workers generate the requested chunks and hands the generated ones back as arrivals, which only the tick inserts; the streaming never inserts or unloads a chunk. It submits a mesh job per node with a new revision, the finest nodes first, nearest first, and only then the coarser nodes, so the ground under the camera is not starved by the distant nodes' single jobs (`schedule_field_jobs`). A finest node whose chunk and 26 neighbours are simulated is meshed from them and again whenever its chunk is dirty, from a private `Field_Grid` copied on the main thread (`gather_field_grid`); a finest node beyond the set is meshed from a generated grid as a coarser one is, and keeps a mesh made from its chunks when they leave the set. A coarser node's job takes the samples of the loaded chunks its grid reads, copied on the main thread (`gather_coarse_field_grid`), and generates the rest on the worker (`generate_field_grid`, `coarse_field_sample`), so an edit shows at every level. A set marks its chunk in `Field_World.edited_chunks`, and each frame the coarser nodes over those chunks (and the chunks beside them, since a grid reads a step past its node) are marked to mesh again (`mark_edited_coarse_nodes`); the old mesh stays drawn until the new one arrives. A node is not submitted while its last job is still on the workers (`Field_Streaming.in_flight`): its dirty chunk or remesh mark waits for the result, so an edit made every tick (a held brush) still lands as the next result instead of dropping each one as stale. A changed chunk leaving the set keeps its bytes (The field session). A mesh result travels back through the mutex protected queue; `take_current_field_meshes` keeps those whose revision is still the node's latest, and the renderer uploads them (`upload_streamed_field_meshes`) and drops the meshes of nodes that left the selection. Per frame: 16 chunks inserted, 8 mesh jobs submitted, 8 results taken, 32 jobs pending.

## World generation

Rule: generation is a pure function of the world seed and the chunk coordinate, so load order and thread count never change the world.

- Each purpose (height, moisture, caves, trees, veins) has its own sub seed from the world seed. Features that cross chunk borders (trees, outcrops) come from per column and per region hashes any chunk can recompute.
- `terrain_height` is one pure function of the sub seeds and a column, in layers: continental swell into lowlands and raised masses, ridged ranges and hills on raised ground at a domain warped position, plateaus pulled up to multiples of 12 blocks above sea level with a cliff band, damped detail, then river valleys.
- A chunk generates terrain and caves, the schematic crate site, trees and boulders, outcrops, clears the column above every outcrop, sets the ground cover (at most one cross per column from a column hash, never in a vein footprint), stamps the landing pad, then computes its sky light (`generation_chunk.odin`). Vein footprints are open to the sky; ground cover is not opaque.
- `GENERATOR_VERSION` counts terrain changes (Save format).
- The terrain field (M13) generates beside the blocks: `generate_field_chunk` is a pure function of the seed, the planet record, the sample spacing and the chunk coordinate, in integers and fixed point only (`integer_square_root`, value noise on `generation_seed` hashes), so every machine generates the same bytes. A session holds its world's planet record (`Session.planet`, borrowed by `World.planet`, Sessions): the record of `data/planets.sjson` with the generation values the world file recorded (Save format). A field session generates from it (0179), so the data reshapes new worlds only. The local surface is the radius plus the record's three octaves of value noise (512, 128 and 32 m in the shipped `home`) read at the sample's position projected onto the sphere of the radius, so there is no latitude, longitude or pole, shaped by the record's relief terms (0189, `relief_shape`, each off at zero, so a record without them generates the plain sum as before; `surface_relief`): the basins (`basin_relief`) deepen the first octave where its noise lies below a threshold, by up to their depth with the square of the share below it, so the sea gathers in a few hollows; the terraces (`terrace_height`) break the first octave and the basins into steps of a rise under the mantle height, each a flat tread and a riser over a share of its span; the ledges (`ledge_relief`) are a fourth octave of value noise read through 1 - (1 - |noise|) raised to the sharpness with the noise's sign (`ledge_profile`, `fixed_power`), which steps the ground once along each zero line of the noise and stays flat either side, steeper than the walkable angle where the noise changes fast. The octaves' amplitudes, the ledges' amplitude and the basins' depth add up to at most `MAXIMUM_RELIEF_METRES`, and the first octave is clamped to its amplitude above and its amplitude and the basins' depth below, so the bound the level of detail, the light and the generation's shortcuts read is unchanged. The ledges cost one value noise more per sample in the relief band (four instead of three), the basins and the terraces a few integer operations on the first octave's noise. The crater (0199) is the relief's last term: `surface_relief` is the shaped relief (`uncratered_relief`) passed through `crater_relief` with the distance from the home on the sphere (`crater_distance`), so every reader of the generated ground (`planet_sample` at every level of detail and in every chunk, `field_surface_under`, the springs, the sea fill) sees one bowl: a flat floor out to the record's floor radius at its depth below the home's uncratered relief, a smoothstep bowl out to its radius, where it meets the surrounding relief plus the rim, and the rim falling back over six times its height (`CRATER_RIM_FALL_PER_HEIGHT`). The distance early outs without a root beyond the reach, so only the samples within it pay one `integer_square_root`; the floor is clamped to at least `-MAXIMUM_RELIEF_METRES` and the result to at most `+MAXIMUM_RELIEF_METRES`, so the relief's bound holds unchanged. A planet with the zero crater (the code built test planets) has none. Strata go by depth below the local surface: topsoil for `TOPSOIL_DEPTH_METRES`, stone to `DEEP_STONE_DEPTH_METRES`, deep stone below; bedrock from the planet's bedrock depth below the radius. The tint is one palette entry per `TINT_REGION_METRES` cube. Samples beyond the relief band skip the noise. The water (0172): every sample that is not ground holds the sea's fill and sleeps (`fill_field_chunk_sea`, skipped for a chunk wholly above the sea level), and each spring of the planet record makes the first air sample above the surface under its latitude and longitude full, awake and a source (`planet_spring_sample`, the direction from the fixed point sine, the search climbing from the local surface in quarter spacings up to the highest relief; a spring with no air there is skipped with a log line, never a source inside ground).
- The trees (0197, `generation_planet_trees.odin`, [content.md](content.md), Trees): a term of the planet's generation (`Planet_Tree_Term` in `Planet_Generation`), a pure function of the seed, the recorded planet and the tree key, never of the sample spacing. Two jittered lattices on the sphere of the radius: grove centres on the coarse one (`Planet_Groves` of `generation_seed`), trees on the fine one (`Planet_Trees`). A candidate is a hashed point in its lattice cube projected onto the sphere, kept only when the projection stays in the same cube, the same rule for both, so every point of the sphere belongs to one cube and no key (`Tree_Key`, the tree cube) is generated twice. Keys sit on the sphere of the radius, not on the relief, so a relief term never moves a key; a kept tree stands on the relief under its key (`surface_relief`, the crater's term included), 300 mm sunk. A tree cube keeps its candidate with a chance falling with the square of the distance from the nearest grove's centre, none within the clearing of the home, below the sea level plus a metre or on ground steeper than the planet's slope (`planet_tree_slope_ok`, the relief a metre either way along two tangents). `planet_trees_in_box` returns the standing trees of a box in key order; `planet_tree_at_key` regenerates one. Only the felled keys are state (The field session).

## World storage

- A chunk is 32 by 32 by 32 blocks in flat arrays: block id (`u16`) and light (`u16`, four nibbles from the highest: sky, red, green, blue). Chunks live in a map keyed by chunk coordinate, each on the heap.
- Water levels are block ids: the source and seven flowing ids marked by `water_level` in `data/blocks.sjson` (8 the source, 7 to 1 flowing), so the chunk layout and the save hold them for free.
- Every `world_set_block` records a block change; the next tick turns the changes into light and water updates.
- Entities (machines, belts, inserters, chests) are not blocks: each occupied cell stays air in the chunk and has an entry in the frames' occupant index (Frames, below), so a raycast hits the entity and placement checks are cell lookups. What the world stores of an entity is that entry: an opaque handle (`Occupant_Handle`, the simulation packs its `Entity_Handle` into it and the world never unpacks it) and the flags it reads (solid, blocks light, blocks water). The raycast (`raycast_cells`, unpacked to `Raycast_Hit` by the simulation's `raycast_blocks`) and the water read the index of the block frame, never the entity pools.
- `World` holds the chunks, the saved chunks, the block changes, the settings, the veins and their outcrops, light, water and, for now, the entities. The game's records are not on it (Simulation).
- The terrain field (M13, `world_field.odin`) lives beside the blocks: a `Field_Chunk` is 32 by 32 by 32 samples in parallel arrays, a density (`i8`, positive inside the ground, the surface where it crosses zero, `DENSITY_STEPS_PER_SAMPLE` steps per spacing, saturating one spacing away), a material (`Field_Material`, the generator's fixed roles until the material table replaces them) and a tint (an index into the planet's palette), and the water field's arrays beside them (The water field) and the light's two bytes per sample (The field light). `Field_World` keys the chunks by `Field_Chunk_Coordinate`; a set marks the chunk and the neighbours beside a border sample dirty, and a missing chunk reads as air. The grid is aligned with the planet's axes with the centre at the origin; a `World_Position` is in 1/`POSITION_UNITS_PER_METRE` m (`i64`), and a sample's position is its index times the world setting `sample_spacing_millimetres`, rounded up to the unit, so `world_position_to_sample` gives the index back.
- A field chunk encodes whole as its coordinate and its arrays run length encoded each on its own, the terrain's three, the water's fill, still ticks, awake bits and source bits, and the block light and the sky light (`encode_field_chunk_delta`, format `FIELD_CHUNK_FORMAT_VERSION` 4; the flow is measured again each tick and not written; the light is saved, not rebuilt, The field light), and the decoder refuses another version by number and a fill above full and drops light in a ground sample. `field.bin` (0179) holds every chunk of the set a tick changed and every chunk kept outside it, that is one whose terrain or water differed from its generation when it left the set; `encode_field_chunk_delta` (nothing for a chunk equal to its generation) is the codec's form for the tests.

### Frames

Foundation frames (0174, `world_frame.odin`): the local grids machines stand on, beside the chunks.

- A `Frame` is an id (`Frame_Id`, a `u32` from the counter `Frame_Table.last_id`, saved), an origin (`World_Position`), three orthonormal axes right, up and forward (unit vectors in `UNIT_VECTOR_ONE`) and a pitch in millimetres. Cell (x, y, z) spans x to x + 1 pitches along the right, y to y + 1 along the up and z to z + 1 along the forward from the origin; `frame_cell_centre` is the origin plus the axes times the cell plus a half times the pitch, divided down by 2^24 once (a few thousand cells out the sums stay near 2^50), and `world_to_frame_cell` takes the offset's dot product with each axis over the pitch, rounded down, the cell whose centre is nearest. Integer only, so every machine agrees on every cell.
- The block world is frame 0 (`BLOCK_FRAME`): identity axes, origin zero, 1000 mm, present in every world without a record, so a block coordinate is a cell of frame 0 and the block placement, belts, splitters, inserters and drills run on it unchanged.
- A new frame's orientation (`frame_axes`): the up along the radial (the hit's position normalised), the forward the planet's north (+y) projected onto the tangent plane, or +z at the poles where that projection vanishes, turned round the up by a yaw step of a twenty fourth of a turn (`FRAME_YAW_STEPS`; the step's angle is rounded to a whole angle unit, since 65536 does not divide by 24), and the right the up cross the forward. A frame never re-tangents.
- The frame table (`Frame_Table`) holds the frames other than frame 0, the occupant index (a map from frame and cell to the occupant) and per frame the occupied cell count and the box round its cells. The index is maintained by `occupy_frame_cell` and `vacate_frame_cell`, which `add_entity`, `remove_entity` and the rebuild after loading (`rebuild_entity_cells`) call, and is never saved. A machine's open cells (0186, `open_cells` of its record, [content.md](content.md), The pod) are occupied without the solid flag (`occupy_entity_cells`, `machine_open_cells`): placement refuses them as any occupied cell, and everything that reads `Solid` (the field player's probe and ground ray, `frame_cell_is_solid`, `field_solid_raycast`, and the water) treats them as not solid. Only the aiming ray accepts every occupant, so the open cells also carry the `Open` flag, which it excludes (`aim_field_player_at_frames`, the `excluded` flags of `raycast_frames`): the player aims out of the pod's door. A machine holds its footprint less its fixtures' boxes (`machine_held_cells`, 0198): a pod's hatches and fixtures are entities of their own in those cells, so every cell has one occupant whatever order the pools are rebuilt in, and removing the pod vacates only its held cells. An open hatch occupies its cells without `Solid`, every row but the top with `Open` (`occupy_open_hatch_cells`), so the player walks under the top row and the aiming ray stops at it; `toggle_hatch` overwrites the same cells, the count unchanged. The table rides on `Entities` (`World.entities.frames`), so `entity_at` and `add_entity` keep their parameters; a field session's frames are in the same table (0179). A frame left with no cell loses its record unless a run's endpoint names it (`release_empty_frame` after `vacate_entity_cells` and after a run's removal, `remove_frame`).
- `raycast_frames` (`world_frame_raycast.odin`) takes the ray into each frame's axes and walks its cells in integers (the voxel walk), skipping a frame whose cells lie out of reach; the nearest occupied cell of any frame wins, with the face entered and the cell in front of it.
- Bodies (0230, `world_frame_body.odin`): a machine whose model has collision volumes ([content.md](content.md), Models) and stands on a frame (`entity_is_shaped`) marks its held cells `Shaped` beside `Solid` and registers a `Frame_Body` in `Frame_Table.bodies` (`occupy_entity_cells`, `register_entity_body`; `vacate_entity_cells` unregisters it). A body is the machine's occupant, a copy of its frame, the model frame's origin on the rotated footprint in the frame's axes, its quarter turns, a bound radius and its volumes scaled from 1/4096 cell to position units by the frame's pitch (exact at 500, 1000 and 2000 mm, floor division otherwise). The bodies are kept sorted by occupant handle, so a world loaded from a snapshot breaks ties as the host does; they are derived like the occupant index, rebuilt by `rebuild_entity_cells`, never saved or hashed (the volumes are content, loaded alike on every machine; the movement they cause is hashed as any collision is). The cells stay `Solid` for placement, the sealed room and the water; the player's cell probe (`frame_cell_collides`) and the frames' rays skip `Shaped` cells, and the body stands in for them: `frame_probe` is the nearer of the solid cells and the bodies' volumes (`frame_body_probe`: per volume an exact signed distance in integers, a box's from its clamped point, a round volume's from its cross section in the half plane of (radius, height) with its sector's faces, the depth to the nearest face inside), and `raycast_frames_and_bodies` the nearer of the cells' ray and a sphere traced ray against the bodies (at most `COLLISION_RAY_STEPS`, 64, each the exact distance, so it never passes through a volume; a grazing ray that needs more misses), whose hit is a cell hit of the body's occupant (the cell a quarter pitch inside the volume, the face of the normal's largest component and the cell in front), so Interact, the tool and placement against the hull work unchanged. Ties keep the first: the cells before the bodies, the bodies in handle order, a body's volumes in file order. A machine's hatches and fixtures are entities of their own and collide by their cells.

### Light

- Workers compute a new chunk's sky light from its own columns. Everything that crosses chunk borders or follows an edit runs on the main thread inside the tick through bounded queues, removals before additions, so the result does not depend on visiting order.
- Block light is coloured: the queues carry the channel, every channel spreads alike and never mixes, and two emitters mix by the larger level per channel. The budget (`MAXIMUM_LIGHT_STEPS_PER_TICK`, 4096) counts nodes over all channels, so a white emitter costs three times one channel.
- Emitters take their colour from `light_color` (`block_light_color`, the lamp's `Machine.light_color`); `World.entity_lights` maps a lit entity's cell to its colour.

### Water

- Minecraft style cellular flow on the main thread: a change next to water schedules the cell `WATER_FLOW_DELAY_TICKS` (10) later, updates run in scheduling order, at most 256 per tick.
- A machine's cells stay dry: the water does not flow into a cell of the block frame whose occupant blocks water, which every machine's does but a hydro turbine's (`machine_occupant_flags`). An entity picked up or removed by the developer menu schedules water around the cells it leaves (`schedule_water_around_freed_cells`), so a pool flows into them.

## Simulation

- Entity pools per type (`Entity_Pool` of furnaces, inserters, drills and so on) with generational handles (`Entity_Handle`: kind, index, generation). Each type has its own update procedure over its own array.
- An entity's coordinate is a frame cell: `Entity_Common.frame` and the integer triple `origin` (the footprint's minimum corner) in that frame, the rotation in quarter turns round the frame's up. Neighbour arithmetic (a cell plus a direction: an inserter's pickup and drop cells, a drill's drop cell, a belt's output and lift column, a splitter's input) stays inside the entity's frame, since frames never touch and belts between frames come with the poles (0176). An entity's world position is its frame's transform of its cell (`frame_cell_centre`); no code maps an entity to a chunk yet (every entity ticks and the save writes one `entities.bin`), and when one does, the chunk is that of the cell's world position, the block chunk for frame 0. Not yet per frame: the fluid networks (pipes connect by cell across frames), the loose items and the entity tick's block query, which read frame 0 and the block world.
- Placement on frames (0174, `entity_frames.odin`): a foundation (machine kind `foundation`, one cell thick, [content.md](content.md), Foundations) placed by snapping to a frame's cell joins that frame exactly; one placed free starts a new frame (`place_free_foundation`) standing on the hit, its up along the radial there and its yaw the player's heading rounded to the nearest step, at the data's `foundation_pitch_millimetres`. A free entity (a foundation, a belt pole (0176), a torch later) is a frame of its own with one occupant. Two frames never merge, and the terrain under a frame is not changed. A machine on a frame (`place_on_frame`) needs its footprint's cells free and a solid occupant (a foundation) under each of its bottom cells (`frame_placement_refusal`: occupied, unsupported); a foundation needs no support. Place with another machine aimed at bare ground (no frame targeted, `field_bare_ground_placement`, 0187) queues a free placement: a frame without foundations (0201, `place_on_bare_ground`), a new frame standing on the hit as a free foundation's with the machine's footprint centred on cell (0, 0, 0) (0215: its origin is `field_footprint_origin({}, ...)`, computed by the simulation from the machine and the rotation, never taken from a command), which the drain refuses with `Field_Edit_Refusal.Too_Steep` where the ground under the footprint is not flat, and with `Frame_Cell_Taken` where a cell's centre lies in another frame's occupied cell, as a free foundation's block is (`new_frame_cells_meet_a_frame`; `bare_ground_placement_refusal`, [content.md](content.md), Machines on bare ground); its ghost is the machine's footprint there, red when refused. Every entity's `founded` flag (`Entity_Common.founded`, the block frame or a foundation under each bottom cell, `machine_is_founded`) is set by `add_entity` and, for the machines above, by a foundation's `add_entity` and `remove_entity` (`refresh_founded_above`), never by a tick. The centre rule (0215, folding 0213, `field_footprint_origin`): every machine Place puts on the field has its footprint centred on the aimed cell, an odd side's middle cell on it and an even side's centre corner at the aimed cell's high corner (offset `(side - 1) / 2` below it, as `foundation_block_first_offset` centres a foundation block), the height never offset, so a 1 by 1 and a 2 by 2 footprint keep their origin; the block world keeps its corner placement. The placement editor's commit (0215, `ui_placement_editor.odin`) is a `Machine_Placement_Command` carrying the anchored and nudged placement (the machine, the rotation, the frame and origin cell or the new frame's ground point and heading, a foundation's normal) without the aim; applied at the tick's start it is queued into `Field_Simulation.placements` (`queue_machine_placement`, `field_placement_of_command`: a foundation takes the player's own block, a bare ground machine the simulation's own origin) ahead of the placements the players' ticks queue, and drained with them, so a refusal toasts through `Field_Refused` as Place's does. The editor itself is presentation state per viewport, never saved or hashed; the command list is not saved, and its network tag is appended last (`Player_Command_Tag.Machine_Placement`).
- Belt and pipe runs (0176, `belt_run.odin`, [logistics.md](logistics.md), Runs): a run joins two endpoints, each a belt pole (`Machine_Kind.Belt_Pole`, its own pool) or a frame cell's belt end, so it is how belts cross between frames. A run is a pool entry (`Entities.belt_runs`, handle kind `Belt_Run`) with no `Entity_Common`: it occupies no cell and the occupant index never sees it. It keeps its endpoints and the four control points of its curve; the polyline, the arc length and the length in line units are derived from them on add and on load (`belt_run_curve`), never saved. Removing a pole removes the runs on it.
- Run placement on the field (`belt_run_placement.odin`): the run tool's first Place stores the start candidate on the player (`Field_Player.run_start`), the second queues a `Field_Placement` of kind `Run` on `Field_Simulation.placements`; the drain (`drain_field_run_placement`) checks the plan against the world as the placements before it left it, places the new poles (a free pole through `place_free_belt_pole`, a snapped one through `place_on_frame`), adds the run and takes a pole item per new pole, and takes the poles away again when the run refuses after all. Refusals report as `Field_Edit_Refusal.Run_Refused` with the reason in `Player.field_run_refusal`. A laid belt run counts as one flat belt placed (`record_placed` of `Field_Content.run_belt`), so chapter 1's `line` completes on the field.
- A drill on a frame (0179, `place_drill_on_frame`, `entity_placement.odin`) taps the vein whose disc holds the centre of one of its bottom cells (`frame_drill_vein_under`, `vein_under_world_position`); off every disc it is refused with `Frame_Placement_Refusal.No_Vein`, which a queued placement reports as `Field_Edit_Refusal.No_Vein`. `place_on_frame` refuses every drill, so a drill never stands on a frame without its vein. The field's drain (`apply_field_placement`) routes a drill through it, and every machine it places counts for the quests (`record_placed`).
- The wear tick (0201, `machine_wear.odin`): each kind's tick that advanced its work (the drill's, the lab's and the splitter's tick return it, the furnace's state, an inserter's `Moving`, `assembler_operated`, `fluid_machine_operated` for the fluid machines that are not generators, a generator's delivered energy) calls `record_operation`, which counts `Entity_Common.wear_ticks` on an unfounded machine whose record does not stand on the ground and sets `broken` at its life, listing the machine in `Entities.breakdowns`; after the entity tick `log_machine_breakdowns` turns the list into toasts through the message log, as finished research is told, and empties it. A broken machine is skipped by its kind's tick and asks for and offers no power (`make_participant`); a broken lab's unit is no longer in progress (`units_in_progress`), so another lab takes it.
- Belts are transport lines ([logistics.md](logistics.md)); fluid and electric networks are rebuilt on topology change and evaluated per tick in fixed point ([fluids.md](fluids.md)).
- Recipes have any number of item or fluid inputs and outputs; crafting machines hold a recipe index and a progress counter. Recipes resolve to dense indices at load.
- Veins are entities, not block data: a reservoir with per ore amounts, centre, radius and size class, placed per region of 8 by 8 chunk columns (`REGION_SIZE_IN_CHUNKS`) so each footprint lies inside its region. Workers compute footprints; the main thread registers a vein once when the first chunk of an overlapping column loads. Drills hold a vein handle; there are no per block ore counters.
- `vein_at_column` finds the registered surface vein under a column for drill placement and the HUD; the geologist's hammer asks for an outcrop block. While the landing pad exists, a region's surface veins also include the starter veins centred in it, after the natural veins and without the natural veins they overlap.
- The game's records are one struct on `Simulation_State` beside the `World` (`Game_Records`, `simulation_state.odin`): statistics, research, shipments, contracts, venture credit, catalogue orders, the five prospecting lists, crate sites, the explored columns and the leaf decay queue. The entity tick's procedures reach the records through their context (next bullet); a tick still on `^World` takes the record it writes (`statistics: ^Statistics`) or the records beside it; the screens reach them through `Screen_Context.records`. Chunk arrival registers the crate sites and the explored column into them (`insert_generated_chunk`), and the world tick runs the leaf decay queue on the world's blocks (`tick_world`).
- The entity tick never takes `^World`: `tick_entities` and the kind ticks under it take an `Entity_Tick_Context` (`simulation_world.odin`) built once per tick by `world_tick_context`, holding the entities, the records, the content, the tick rate, the world's settings, the vein registry's fields (still on `World`) and the block interface: a block query (block and whether its chunk is loaded) and a block write, as procedure values with a data pointer (`Block_Query`, `Block_Write`), the two calls the plugin boundary will carry. `tick_entities_on_world` runs it, then syncs the lit lamps into the world's light (`sync_entity_lights`). The player tick, the magnetometer, the item uses (`apply_item_use`, through the simulation state), the launch requests and the world's own tick (`tick_world`) still take `^World`; the player tick is the next cut.
- Statistics live in the records (`statistics.odin`): produced, obtained, delivered, consumed, voided per item, placed per machine, stalls, fuel burned, blocks mined, distance walked, and per item rate rings of 60 buckets each at one second, ten seconds and one minute, the coarser fed from the finer, all saved. Furnaces, crafting machines and drills keep a 60 bucket ring of their own output for the panel rate. The quests, the statistics screen and the bottleneck overlay read them.
- Shipments (tick and cargo of every launch) live in the records and are saved. The UI's launch requests are served inside the tick, since a shipment needs the tick.
- The venture (`venture.odin`: open contracts, deliveries, offer counts, credit, catalogue orders) lives in the records and is saved. Shipments are served right after the launches: contracts oldest first, then free trade. Levels of infinite technologies live in the research state, since drills and labs read them every tick.
- `Simulation_Content.generator` points at the session's generator (nil in tests), so the orbital survey can chart veins in chunks never loaded.
- Quests are data evaluated against the statistics, entity counts and research state every tick ([quests.md](quests.md)).
- The one rule of writes: everything outside the tick that changes the simulation queues a `Player_Command` on `Simulation_State.player_commands` (`player_command.odin`), applied in order at the start of the next tick and never saved. The screens queue after the pure refusal check the tick repeats, so a refused action toasts and never queues; a command the tick refuses after all raises `Action_Refused`, which toasts. Developer requests (the Developer screen, `--chapter`, `--give`) are one kind of command (`Developer_Request`, served by `serve_developer_request`). The command socket's write lines run at the start of the tick they were stamped for ([commands.md](commands.md)). A closed machine panel forgets its machine through `Close_Machine_Command`. The slot transfers of the inventory screen and the machine panel are commands as well (0179, `player_command_slots.odin`): a slot's pick up, drop, split and sort, the end of a distribute gesture, the return of the hand (`Player.held`, saved with the player), the drop on the ground, each quick move step, the transfer buttons, the touch row's grid transfers and an inserter's hand. A sort carries its order, since the ranks follow the display language. The tick refuses a stale one (`slot_command_stale`: the machine is no longer the player's open machine, a slot is out of range, the hand or the target slot holds another item than the screen showed (`Held_Expectation`), a sort order no longer names the slots' stacks, none of a distribute's slots still takes the hand's item) with `Action_Refused`; a distribute leaves out the slots whose filter refuses the item. A return of the hand is queued only when the inventory has room for some of it (`return_changes_hand`). There is no frame side prediction: a transfer shows a frame later offline and the latency window later online. Veins added by command live in `World.veins` with `added` set.
- Writes inside the tick to the terrain field go the same way (0167, decided): a player's tick (and later a drill's) queues its brush edit on `Field_Simulation.edits`, and `drain_field_edits` applies them at the end of the tick in the order queued, so no system reads a half edited field and every machine applies them in one order. The queue is filled and emptied inside one tick, so it is empty between ticks where a save runs, and it is not saved. The drain marks the edited chunks dirty; they remesh on the next revision.
- The simulated chunk set (`simulation_chunk_set.odin`): each tick derives the chunks it may read from the players' chunks with the radius of `data/game.sjson` (`simulated_chunk_radius_horizontal` 6, `simulated_chunk_radius_vertical` 3), keeps a loaded chunk one chunk beyond (`SIMULATED_CHUNK_UNLOAD_MARGIN`), and `World.chunks` holds exactly that set. Generated chunks arrive as `Chunk_Ready_Command` and wait in `arrived_chunks` until the tick inserts them in coordinate order, so every machine inserts alike. A tick stalls while a chunk of its set is missing; offline, a stalled tick still inserts the chunks that arrived, so a new world fills in while it waits.
- Sealed rooms (0198, `entity_pod.odin`): `Entities.sealed_rooms` holds, per pod, the cells the outside does not reach (`Sealed_Room`: the pod, its frame, the cells in scan order, the supplying oxygen generator and the supply). `rebuild_sealed_rooms` derives them from the occupancy with a breadth first fill inside the pod's box through the cells with no solid occupant (`pod_sealed_cells`), seeded from the box's four side faces and its top face (the bottom face is the hull's floor on the ground); the cells the fill misses are the room. It runs when a pod is placed, a hatch toggles and a world loads (`rebuild_loaded_world`), so it is derived, never saved: the hatch states it comes from are saved and hashed, and two sessions or a loaded save agree. A room touching an oxygen generator's footprint cell face to face (`room_supplier`, the first in pool order) has an `Unlimited` supply until M15. `sealed_room_at_feet` tells whether a position's cell lies in a room, for the F3 World page now and the suit's drain in M15.
- Cheat speed and free crafting (0234) are simulation flags, not saved; the join snapshot carries both ([developer_tools.md](developer_tools.md)). The day cycle reads the tick plus `day_offset_ticks`, saved through the world file's day time field.

### The terrain field's brushes

Digging and placing (0171): `world_field_edit.odin` edits the field, `field_mining.odin` turns the edits into items.

- A brush (`Field_Brush`, from `field_brushes` of `data/game.sjson`, [content.md](content.md), Field materials and brushes) is a sphere or the level mode with a radius and a rate. `apply_field_edit` visits the samples of the loaded chunks within the radius of the hit in one order (z, then y, then x, rising) and moves each by at most the rate towards its target: air for a dig, full ground for a place, and in the level mode the density of the plane through the hit across the player's up (`field_plane_density`), a dig cutting what lies above it and a place filling what lies below. Integer only, so two machines editing alike get the same bytes.
- The volume of a sample's ground is the positive part of its density, `MAXIMUM_DENSITY` steps the whole sample; a sample at zero or below is air (material Air, tint 0) as the generation makes it. A dig counts the steps it takes off per material and leaves ground outside the edit's `diggable` set, reporting it as blocked; a place adds from one material up to its budget in steps and stops there, raises only air and ground of its own material (so it never turns one material into another), and gives the samples it turns to ground its material and the tint of the targeted ground (until items carry tints). A place followed by a dig of the same samples returns exactly what it took. The world never names an item: the result is steps per material (`Field_Edit_Result`).
- The yield: steps become volume (the spacing cubed per step, in `FIELD_ITEM_VOLUME` units, a cubic metre an item) and credit the material's item from `data/materials.sjson`; the volume short of a whole item, and items the inventory has no room for, stay in the player's credit per material (`Player.field_credit`, the credit pattern of `take_power_step`), so no volume is lost. A place's budget is the held items and the credit; it takes the credit first and whole items for the rest, the change back into the credit, and is refused with the reason when the player holds none (`Field_Edit_Refusal`).
- A dig moves a sample of ground by the brush's rate scaled by its material's `dig_rate_percent` (0179, `Field_Edit.dig_rate_percent`, `scaled_dig_rate`); the fraction of a step is carried by the edit's tick, so any 100 ticks in a row take exactly the rate times the percent. A zero percent leaves the rate; the hand tool's edits carry the material table's rate and the tick (`drain_field_dig`).
- The tool tier gates the dig as the pickaxe gates hand mining (0051): the best `tool_tier` carried (`field_tool_tier`) sets the diggable materials (`field_diggable_materials`); ground of a harder material is left and reported, and a material without an item (bedrock) is never dug.
- A place is refused when a sample it would raise (a dry run over the edit's samples, `field_place_meets_capsule`) lies within its trilinear support, a sphere of 7/4 spacing, of any field player's capsule (`field_place_buries_a_player`), so a player cannot bury anyone.
- Foundations on the field (0174): a foundation item selected in the hotbar holds the foundation tool (`Field_Player.tool`, `Field_Held_Tool`, 0179) with its machine in `Field_Player.held_machine`, so the held item's tier is placed (`field_placed_machine`, 0196); the benchmark's pad uses the content's `pad_foundation` instead (`field_pad_foundation`, [content.md](content.md), Foundations), a belt, pole or pipe item the run tools (0176, Belt and pipe runs below); Place then queues a place command (`Field_Placement`: the frame and cell of the targeted frame's face, or a new frame at the targeted ground with the player's heading) on `Field_Simulation.placements`, drained after the brush edits at the end of the tick (`drain_field_placements`), each taking one foundation item. It is refused (`Field_Edit_Refusal`) when no foundation is held, the cell is taken or its frame is gone, or the cell could reach a player's capsule (`frame_cell_meets_capsule`).
- Foundation blocks (0193): a foundation placement covers the player's block (`Field_Player.foundation_size_index` and `foundation_height_index` into the lists of [content.md](content.md), Foundations; `field_foundation_block`), which the inventory view's configure pop-up sets through a `Foundation_Block_Command` ([ui.md](ui.md), Configure pop-up; an index past either list is refused, no world control changes them), carried on the placement as `size`, `height` and the face's `normal` (the adjacent cell less the hit cell). `foundation_block_cells` lays it out: a square of size by size cells spans the face and the block runs height cells along the normal from the adjacent cell (a free block from cell (0, 0, 0) of its new frame along the frame's up). Across the face the square is centred on the anchor, an even side's extra cell on the high side; on a side face the square's vertical axis rises from the anchor's row instead, so a wall stands on the pad's level rather than reaching into the ground. The drain refuses the whole block (`foundation_block_refusal`) when fewer foundations are held than cells (`Too_Few_Foundations`, its event carrying the refused placement's cells and the foundations held for the toast "Needs 25 foundations, 16 held", `record_refused_foundation_counts`), when any cell is taken (`Frame_Cell_Taken`), or when any cell could reach a player; otherwise the cells enter the entity table as single foundations in the list's fixed order (layer, then the face's second axis, then its first), so the hash agrees on every machine, and each costs one item and counts as one placed foundation. A save from before 0193 loads the indices as 0, the first of each list; an index past a shrunk list reads the first entry.
- Trees in the way (0197): a free foundation, a snapped foundation or machine and a machine on bare ground whose cells meet a standing trunk are refused with `Tree_In_The_Way` (`placement_cells_meet_a_trunk`, in `field_placement_refusal`, `foundation_block_refusal` and `bare_ground_placement_refusal`). Runs and torches are not checked. `clear_trees_under_frames` fells, without yield, every tree whose trunk meets an occupied frame cell: once at the load of a save written before 0197, after the benchmark's pad and after the planet preview's pad.
- Picking up (0195, `entity_frames.odin`): Mine held on a frame cell in reach (the frame target, nearer than the ground) advances `Player.mining` towards `PICK_UP_SECONDS` as the block world's `mine_entity` does, whatever the hotbar holds (`advance_field_pick_up`, in the player's tick); a finished hold queues a `Field_Placement` of kind `Pick_Up` naming the frame and cell, and the drain (`drain_field_pick_up`) takes the entity there with its contents into the inventory (`take_entity_into_inventory`, shared with the block world's `pick_up_entity`). One entity per hold, so a foundation block goes cell by cell; the frame goes with its last cell (`release_empty_frame`). The pod (`field_entity_is_placed_by_world`: a machine without an item) gets no progress, no hint and no refusal; since 0199 a new world lays no pad, and an old save's pad foundations are ordinary foundations, those under the pod refused with `Something_Stands_On_It`. An entity that holds something up gets no progress and is refused with `Something_Stands_On_It` (`field_entity_is_held_up`): in a cell right above one of its cells stands a machine other than a foundation (a foundation needs no support), a run's end (a pole's or a belt end's cell) or a torch's sample; a pole with a run on it refuses too (`pole_holds_run`), so no run or item on one goes silently and the player takes the runs down first. The field has no loose items yet, so where the block world spills what does not fit, the field refuses the whole pick up with `Inventory_Full` and the entity stays (`inventory_fits_all_picked_up`). The drain checks again against the world the commands before it left, so two players finishing on one entity in one tick take it once.
- `Field_Simulation` (`Simulation_State.field`, 0179) holds the field's share of the simulation: the field world, the edit queue (above, Simulation), the place queue, the torches and the simulated set; the players' credits are on `Player` and the frames in `World.entities` (The field session).

### The player on the field

The field player (0170, `player_field.odin`) is each player's body in a field session (`Player.field`, 0179, The field session); the planet preview's walk mode drives it through the same session ([build.md](build.md), Command line).

- Integer and fixed point only, since lockstep (0177) needs every machine to agree: the feet's `World_Position`, a velocity in 1/`VELOCITY_FRACTION_ONE` of a position unit per tick with the fraction short of a whole unit carried into the next tick (each speed at most `MAXIMUM_FIELD_PLAYER_MILLIMETRES_PER_TICK` a tick, so velocities stay below 2^31 and their products inside an i64; `vector_length` shifts large components down before squaring), directions as unit vectors of length `UNIT_VECTOR_ONE` (2^24), yaw and pitch in 1/`ANGLE_UNITS_PER_TURN` of a turn with an integer Taylor series for the sine (`world_field_vector.odin`). The tick reads a `Field_Player_Input` of integers, quantised from the input frame before the tick.
- The walk counter (0187): each tick the feet's move less its part along the up counts in millimetres for `Statistics.distance_walked_millimetres` (`record_field_walked`, called round `tick_field_player` in `queue_field_player_edit`), except while flying, the block world's rule (`record_walked`); a metre is a block for chapter 1's walk objective.
- The up is the normalised position, every tick. The forward (the heading at yaw 0) is re-projected onto the up's tangent plane every tick, so a walk round the planet carries the heading along and returns to its start (the test walks round a 64 m planet).
- Collision reads the field as a signed distance (`world_field_distance.odin`). The density between samples is trilinear and the gradient a central difference half a sample to each side, but the density is no distance once one side saturates (a spacing from the surface) or after a brush edit, so `field_surface_probe` finds the surface: it marches from the point towards the surface (inwards from the air, outwards from the ground) in half sample steps until the density changes sign and bisects to the crossing, along the gradient, along the radial and along the gradient's part across the radial, keeping the nearest crossing (the gradient alone points into a floor and wall corner and overreads both). The distance is that crossing's, the normal the gradient there reversed (the radial up where the gradient is zero, `field_normal_at`). Beyond `FIELD_PROBE_REACH_SAMPLES` it reports no surface and the reach as the distance. The capsule (radius and height in `data/game.sjson`) is spheres along its axis at most a radius apart; an overlapping sphere is pushed out along the normal, deep in the ground along the up, and the velocity loses its part into the surface. A move runs in steps of half a radius.
- The ground is a ray straight down from the bottom sphere's centre (`field_ground_under`): on it when the hit faces the up, the feet do not move away, and they stand within a tolerance of their resting height over the hit (the bottom sphere on a slope leaves the feet r (1 / cos - 1) above it), a sixteenth of a spacing to land and a quarter to stay, so `on_ground` cannot toggle every tick. The slope compares the normal's cosine against the up with the walkable angle's cosine (`fixed_cosine` of the data's degrees, once at load), no trigonometry in the tick. Ground steep under the feet is judged again over the footprint (0203, `judge_ground_over_footprint`): four rays down at a capsule radius ahead, behind, right and left of the feet give the ground's heights, the least squares plane through them and the feet's ground is the footprint's (`footprint_plane_normal`), and the flatter of the two planes is the ground's, so a bump narrower than the capsule is walkable while a player at a drop's edge keeps the feet's plane; beside a face taller than the step (a ray starting inside the ground) the feet's plane stays. The ray under the feet alone decides `on_ground`.
- Walkable ground holds the player (no gravity) and the walk follows its plane at full speed; the feet follow the ground down up to the step height (a sphere trace of at most half a spacing a step), then settle to their resting height over the ground under them (unless the bottom sphere rests on a walkable frame contact, below), and whatever steeper than walkable they touch then pushes them across the up only, so a walk into a face never rides its rounded foot past the walkable angle and a player left there stands on the floor, while a walkable contact lifts them (a lip lower than the step, which the field blurs into a walkable ramp, is walked up at full speed). Steeper ground drops the walk's uphill part and scales it by the slope's cosine over the walkable one, and gravity (the planet record's) slides the player down at up to the slide speed. The walk's own collision pushes along the tangent plane on a surface steeper than walkable, so walking into a steep face never lifts the player; gravity's move takes the full normal and slides.
- An impeded walk (less than 15/16 of its move, `field_walk_impeded`) on walkable or steep ground also tries the move a step height higher (`step_field_walk`: raised where nothing overlaps, swept, dropped back) and keeps it when it went farther and lands on walkable ground judged over the footprint; that tick skips the settle. On steep ground the walk first tries the ledge a step up and a stride ahead (`find_field_ledge` with the step height), and the step tries the stick's walk rather than the walk without its uphill part, so a dug lip lower than the step is walked out of and only a steep face taller than the step slides. A walk still blocked on walkable ground tries the step, and Jump while walking tries the mantle: the capsule lifted by the step (or mantle) height, moved a radius and a sample ahead and dropped back must overlap nothing on the way and land on walkable ground, above the ground for a step and above the step height for a mantle. Heights count from the ground straight under the feet, not from the feet, so the data's step and mantle heights are the thresholds within a tenth of a sample at every spacing. The field blurs a ledge's face over a sample, so the move is that long and takes one tick. Otherwise Jump launches at the speed that rises the jump height.
- The foundation frames are solid ground too (`world_frame_collision.odin`): each solid occupied cell is a box in its frame's axes. Each capsule sphere is pushed out of the field and, separately, out of the nearest solid cell within a radius and half a sample (`field_solid_probe`), the drop and the probes take the nearer of the two, and the ground ray takes a cell's top face where it is nearer than the field (`field_solid_raycast`). With a solid cell in front of the bottom sphere the step height is at least the cell's pitch (`field_step_height`), so a one cell pad is a step at every spacing. A machine with collision volumes (0230, Frames, Bodies) collides by them: the frames' probe (`frame_probe`) is the deeper of a cell and a volume, which pushes as a cell does, the ground ray meets a volume's surface with its normal (`field_solid_raycast` through `raycast_frames_and_bodies`), and the normal decides walkable or slide as on the field, so a cone steeper than the walkable angle is slid off and a gentle one walked up; the step height against a volume is the data's, since `field_step_height` reads the cells only. The impeded walk's step refuses a landing whose bottom sphere rests on a contact steeper than walkable more than the land tolerance (spacing/16) above the ground under its centre (`field_landing_held_by_steep_contact`, volumes only: a steep field bump narrower than the footprint is walked over by design), so walking into a steep volume's foot does not perch the player on it; a landing on a lip's edge whose centre is still short of the top, steep at crouch speed, is taken when the point a capsule radius ahead along the walk is within a radius of a volume's walkable surface (`field_walkable_volume_ahead`, 0232), which a steep slope never is there; beside a wall that touches the bottom sphere the step is refused before this rule, since the drop stops at the wall (`drop_field_capsule` takes the nearest surface); the settle that follows a tick on walkable ground leaves a capsule whose bottom sphere rests on a walkable frame contact where the walk's drop rested it (`capsule_rests_on_walkable_frame_contact`, 0232), so a lip's edge is climbed by the sweep instead of rolled back over; a sharp topped volume about a metre across is not stepped onto at 1000 mm, since the step onto a sharp edge is then the ledge's, whose stride overshoots it (the pod's chair seat and desk are no steps). The ledge's move ahead is checked every radius on the way (`field_capsule_path_clear`), so the step and the mantle never pass through a column thinner than a sample.
- The crouch (0218): Sneak on foot sets `Field_Player.crouching`; a crouching player stands again only when the standing capsule fits at the feet (`update_field_crouch`), in every mode but flying with no clip, which stands at once. The flag is decided from the input and the world before the move, in the tick and in the prediction alike, and the tick's tuning is `field_posture_tuning` of it (the crouch heights in place of the standing ones), so the walk, the step, the mantle, the ledge and the ground probes read the crouched capsule. The eye (`field_player_eye`) and the capsule (`field_player_capsule`) follow the flag, so the aim, the place's bury check and the frame and hatch capsule checks use the crouched body. The walk is at the sneak speed while crouched, Sneak released or not (`field_walk_speed`). Fly mode keeps the standing capsule and Sneak's descent. Sneak's hold or toggle applies as in the block world (`update_sneaking`, [input.md](input.md), Hold or toggle). The pod's airlock is 1 m high, so the player crawls through it crouched (0221, [content.md](content.md), Hatches).
- The developer fly mode (Jump double tap, the fly toggle) moves along the heading, its right and the up at the fly speeds, swept against the field unless no clip is on. While the chunk under the feet is missing the player holds still.
- The tool's target is `raycast_field` from the eye along the look within the tool reach: steps of a quarter sample, the crossing refined by twelve bisections on the density, the hit's position, normal (the radial up for a ray starting deep in the ground) and nearest sample. The frames are cast beside it (`raycast_frames`, `aim_field_player_at_frames`) and the nearer hit stays (`target` or `frame_target`), so a brush never digs through a foundation and a foundation snaps to the frame in front of the ground.
- The hand tool (0171, `field_mining.odin`): Dig or Place held with the ground in reach queues a brush edit at the target's hit (The terrain field's brushes); the brush is the player's (`brush`, cycled by Next_Brush) and the placed material the hotbar's (`update_field_held_tool`, 0179).
- The cameras (`field_camera`, `render_field_camera.odin`) take the player's up as their up, so the horizon tilts as the player walks round the planet; the third person camera sits behind the eye and is not yet pulled in by the field.
- The player does not swim yet: water is no obstacle to the capsule and the field player walks through it until a later item gives it buoyancy.

### The water field

Water as a second field (0172, `world_field_water.odin`): a fill per sample beside the terrain, moved by a cellular rule towards the planet's centre.

- Storage: `Field_Chunk.water`, a byte per sample from 0 to `FIELD_WATER_FULL` (254), held only where the terrain density is at or below zero; `water_still`, the ticks the sample has been still (zero again once it sleeps, so a settled chunk equals its generation); `water_awake` and `water_source`, one bit per sample (`Field_Sample_Bits`); and `water_flow`, the fill that moved out of the sample in the last tick, for hydro later and not saved. The water is addressed exactly like the terrain and the codec writes it with the chunk (World storage).
- Levels: a sample's water fills its cell from the bottom along the radial (`Field_Water_Span`): the cell's middle is the sample's distance to the planet's centre, its height the radial extent of the sample's cube, |n.x| + |n.y| + |n.z| samples for the radial unit n (one on an axis, up to the square root of three on a diagonal), and the water's level is the cell's bottom plus the fill's share of the height (`field_water_level`), in fill units (254 a sample) from the centre. The height is what makes the water level off the axes: the cells a level surface crosses touch face to face only when they are that tall, and with a height of one sample (floored shells, the first version) water stayed in steps a third of a sample high on a slope of the sphere. The sea level is a level too (`field_sea_level`).
- The rule (`step_field_water`), integer only: each tick the samples awake when the tick began, chunks in coordinate order (z, y, x rising) and samples in sample order, run once each. A sample moves fill to its six neighbours in sample order, first to those whose middle is nearer the centre, then to the others: the fill that makes the two levels equal (compared exactly, `field_water_level_exact`), rounded to the nearest with a half rounded down, at most the neighbour's room, `fill_rate_per_tick` and the fill the sample held when the tick began. Down a column that is at least what fits, so water falls until the cell below is full; across, it levels, and what is left between two neighbours is under half a fill, so a move never turns back. A settled surface may tilt by up to half a fill a sample across a basin for that rounding. A sample moves only what it held when the tick began and only samples awake then run, so water arriving in a tick moves on in the next and spreads alike in every direction; the order is fixed by the data alone. Ground and a missing chunk take nothing.
- Sleeping: every change of a fill goes through `set_field_water`, which marks the meshes and wakes the sample and its six neighbours, those that have something to do (`field_water_can_wake`: water, a spring bit, or air below sea level); a sample whose fill did not change for `still_ticks_to_sleep` ticks clears its awake bit and its still ticks. A terrain set wakes the sample and its neighbours too, so a dug hole floods on the next tick and a dig in dry ground wakes nothing. A chunk that arrives wakes the wet samples of the loaded chunks' faces towards it whose sample across the face is air with room (`wake_field_water_facing`), so water a missing chunk held as a wall flows on while a settled sea against an arriving sea stays asleep; a chunk that leaves wakes the air samples facing it below sea level short of full (`wake_field_water_beside_missing`), which may now be sea edges.
- Films: fill at or below `minimum_fill` that can neither fall nor rests on full water stops moving and drops by one on every tick that is a multiple of `dry_ticks_per_step`; it stays awake until it has dried. This is the run limit: a trickle spreads until it is a film and then dries.
- Sources: a spring sample (its source bit) is refilled to full, and a sea edge sample (beside a missing sample that is sea) to the sea's fill at its level (`field_sea_fill`), each tick they are awake. A missing chunk's sample holds the sea's fill when the generation makes it air (`field_missing_sample_sea`, read through `Field_World.water_planet`, which the world's owner sets), so a tunnel dug below sea level up to the loaded boundary does not fill from the rock beyond it; any other missing sample is a wall. The generation gives every sample that is not ground the sea's fill (full below, partly where the sea level crosses its cell, so the generated sea is level and asleep) and marks the springs of the planet record full, awake and a source (World generation). Rain is a rate per planet in data applied by nothing until the weather (M15); per planet rather than per biome, since the field has no biomes yet.
- Volume is conserved but at the sources, the films' drying and the displacement: a terrain set that turns a wet sample into ground (a place onto water) pushes its fill to the neighbours with room in sample order and drops what fits nowhere, counted in `Field_World.water_dropped`.
- The step runs in `tick_field_simulation` after the edit drain (Simulation), so the edits of a tick and the water that follows them run in one order on every machine. Reading the water outside the loaded chunks from the generation is lockstep safe only while every machine loads the same chunks; 0179 ties the field to the simulated chunk set, as for the edits, and adds the field to the state hash.
- Known limits: a spring's water that reaches the sea spreads over the loaded part of the sea's surface and raises it slowly, since only the sea's edge is a source and nothing drains above it.

### The field light

Light on the terrain field (0173, `world_field_light.odin`): a flood fill over samples on the block light's pattern (Light above), with a falloff curve from data.

- Storage: `Field_Chunk.block_light` and `sky_light`, a byte per sample each, 0 to 255 (`FIELD_LIGHT_FULL`), 0 in ground. The codec writes both (World storage), so the light is saved rather than rebuilt on load as the block light is: the sky of an edited chunk depends on edits in the chunks above it along the radial, which need not be loaded when it loads, so it cannot be derived from the chunk and its neighbours, and a lockstep joiner holds the host's bytes without relighting. An emitter in a neighbour chunk still lights a chunk again when it arrives.
- Spread: light enters the six face neighbours whose terrain density is at or below zero (water does not block it) and loses per step the falloff of `data/lighting.sjson` for the level it leaves, scaled by the spacing (`Field_Light_Tuning.spread`, [content.md](content.md), Lighting). The losses never rise from a dark band to a brighter one, so a brighter level never spreads dimmer and the result is the brightest path from any source, whatever the visiting order.
- Queues (`Field_Lighting`): a removal and an addition queue per channel, every removal before any addition, the block channel before the sky's. A removal clears the neighbours dimmer than the removed level and queues them in turn; brighter ones have another source and spread back, and a cleared emitter gets its own level back. `tick_field_light` runs inside `tick_field_simulation` after the water: the borders of up to `chunk_seeds_per_tick` arrived chunks (`seed_field_chunk_light`: each face sample brighter than the sample across the face spreads, both ways, and the chunk's emitters shine in sample order), then up to `steps_per_tick` nodes. The arrived chunks are a set taken in coordinate order; a chunk inserted twice is seeded once and one that has left is dropped without spending the budget (`seed_arrived_field_chunks`). Every walk over a map that feeds a queue goes in sorted order, since a map's iteration order differs between machines and the budget makes the bytes between ticks depend on the queue's order. The budget only spreads the same result over more ticks (`test_a_light_spread_over_ticks_ends_as_one_unbounded_run`).
- Emitters: `add_field_light_source` and `remove_field_light_source` keep a map from sample to level on the world (`Field_Lighting.sources`), which stays while the sample's chunk is away; the session saves it with the world and connects the torch entity to it in the slice (0179). For now the planet preview's torch key calls them between ticks.
- Sky light: full in an air sample whose radial march outward meets no ground (`field_sky_is_open`: the samples nearest points a quarter spacing apart). Within the relief's shell a missing chunk reads the generation, so an unloaded overhang shades; above the shell the march goes on through the loaded chunks and ends at the first missing one (the generation is air there), so ground placed above the shell, a roof on a tall build, shades too. The generation gives every air sample full sky (`light_generated_field_chunk`): the generated terrain is a height field along the radial, and marching every air sample at generation measured 6 to 72 times the chunk's generation time (4 surface chunks generated in about 170 ms; marching their air samples took 1.0 s at 1000 mm, 3.9 s at 500 mm and 12.7 s at 333 mm, debug build). Under an edit, a terrain set that turns a sample to ground or to air (`follow_field_terrain_with_light`) clears or refills its light at once and records the sample; at the end of the edit drain `update_field_sky_after_edits` gathers the edit's shadow, the sample if air and the air samples along its radial inward to the first ground, and marches each on the final field of the tick in sample order: open gives full sky and spreads it, shut removes a full sky. The sky channel's fill then carries the sky inward with the curve, so a cave mouth is lit and its depths are not, and an overhang's underside gets some from its mouth.
- Properties, not defects: a light pool is a diamond along the planet's axes, as the block light's is, since the fill steps between face neighbours. A diagonal wall one sample thick stops the fill but lets the sky march through, since the march reads the sample nearest each point and can step past it diagonally. Generated air has full sky; once an edit marches a sample on a slope again its sky may drop to the fill's value where its radial meets the slope's own ground.
- Limits: removing an emitter whose chunk is not loaded leaves the light it cast in the loaded chunks until those relight (0179 removes torches in loaded chunks only). An edit's shadow stops at the loaded chunks, so air in a chunk that arrives later under an edit made above it keeps the generation's full sky until an edit beside it marches again; the queues and the sky edits are not saved, so the save of the slice runs where they are empty or saves them. Reading the generation beyond the loaded chunks is lockstep safe only while every machine loads the same chunks, as for the water (0179).

## Data driven content

- `data/*.sjson` holds blocks (`name_key`; `discoverable` for an ore that reads "Unknown ore" until its drop was obtained), items, recipes, machines, fluids, technologies, vein types and ore tables, biomes, tree species, quest chapters (`data/quests/`), contracts, developer kits, the planets (`planets.sjson`, for the terrain field) and the touch overlay's layout (`touch_overlay.sjson`, loaded with the tables though only the input layer reads it). The values live in `data/*.sjson`, the rules behind them in [content.md](content.md).
- `load_game_data` parses them with `core:encoding/json` (`Specification.SJSON`) into prototype tables in an arena the frame state owns, one per load. String ids resolve to dense indices once.
- `Game_Content` embeds the registries the simulation reads (`Simulation_Content`) and adds the presentation tables (notes, the touch layout, display names and orders) and the process flags. A session hands the simulation that embedded value with its scaled technologies and its generator (`frame_simulation_content`), and the tick, the command socket's developer requests and the screens see the recipes with the found schematics (`content_with_found_schematics`).

### Hot reload

- The operating system reports changes under the data directory through `shared:fsw` (inotify on Linux, ReadDirectoryChangesW on Windows); the main thread takes the events every frame without reading a file's status. A watcher that cannot open logs one `data: cannot watch` line and stays off for the run.
- What reloads is `--watch-data=<off|presentation|all>` or `settings.watch_data` (default: presentation in developer mode, else off).
- Presentation files reload in place (`data_file_category`): strings, bindings, developer kits, shaders, fonts, models, sounds, textures (both atlases rebuilt; the chunk meshes stay, since the layout depends on the block count alone) and the UI theme (`ui/theme.sjson` and `ui/icons/`). A file that fails keeps the old data and says so.
- Content tables reload only on request: the `reload` command, F8 in developer mode, the Developer screen, or with `watch_data` all after a quiet second. The collision files under `models/` (0230) are content, not models: they change the lockstep simulation, so they reload with the content, which rebuilds the entities and with them the bodies.
- A content reload encodes the world through the save codec in memory, the data loads and validates as at start, and the world is decoded back with the content remap; loaded chunks keep their blocks, remapped and remeshed.
- A generator change affects only chunks loaded afterwards; `game.sjson` needs a restart.

### Data edits overlay

Rule: the editors never write a data file; an edit is a copy under `<state>/mine-oh-belowed/data_edits/<relative path>` that wins over the data file (the screen: [developer_tools.md](developer_tools.md), Data files screen). A second source, `<edits_directory>/data_edits/<relative path>` (0228, the `edits_directory` setting), is read in between: the state copy first, then the edits directory's copy, then the data file (`data_edits_directories`).

- Every SJSON file and the shaders are read through `read_data_file(data_directory, relative_path, allocator)` (`data_load.odin`). It takes the overlay copy when one exists and logs `data: <relative path> from the data edits overlay <path>` once per read; a broken copy is reported like a broken data file, naming the overlay path.
- The quest chapter list comes from the data directory's listing. Binary files (png, wav, vox, ttf), the models' collision files (read with the models from the data directory, 0230) and the blueprints never read the overlay.
- `start_reachable_data_edits` (`data_load.odin`), called once by `main` before the start load, resolves the edits directory: read when the setting is an absolute path (after `~/`), All files access is granted and `<edits_directory>/data_edits` exists; otherwise one log line names the reason and it is not read for the run. A directory that goes away later sets `reachable_off` with one log line (`turn_reachable_data_edits_off`), found at the next read or by its watcher.
- The state overlay is not watched: the Data files screen's Save and Discard edit (`save_data_edit`, `discard_data_edit`, served by `serve_data_browser` between frames) return the changed file's category, and the frame loop passes them to `apply_data_edit_change(state, changed)` (`hot_reload.odin`), which does what the watcher would for those files, still before the frame's draw. The edits directory's overlay is watched with the data directory (`open_data_edits_watch`, `data_watch.odin`), its files mapped to the same categories by relative path.
- Save writes the parsed tree through `sjson_text` (`sjson_text.odin`) in the data files' style: the root without braces, bare keys with `=`, tabs, a container of leaves on one line, integers kept integers and floats given a point, so the text parses back to an equal tree. `json.marshal` is not used: it prints 0.5 as 0.5000000000000000.
- The copy has none of the data file's comments and its object keys sorted by name (`json.Object` keeps no order). Reconciling a copy into the checked in file happens outside the game, by hand or by an agent ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), Editors).
- A broken copy never stops the start: `main` loads the fonts, bindings, game config, strings and game data through `load_start_data` (`main.odin`); when that fails with the overlay read, `turn_data_edits_off` logs it, turns both overlays off for the run (`data_edits_reading.off`, consulted through `reading_data_edits_directories`) and `main` loads again from the data files. A second failure exits. The chunk shaders and the UI theme, loaded after the window opens, fall back the same way.
- `data_edits_reading` is thread local: the game reads data on the main thread only, and under `odin test` each test thread gets its own overlay directory ("" unless a test sets one), so tests see the shipped data whatever the machine holds. It also holds the edits directory's overlay (`reachable_directory`, set at start or by a test through `use_reachable_data_edits`).

## Save format

Worlds live under `$XDG_DATA_HOME/mine-oh-belowed/saves/<world>/` (`MINE_OH_BELOWED_SAVES` or `paths.saves` override it).

| File | Holds |
|---|---|
| `world.sjson` | Format version, generator version, name, seed, world settings, tick, day time, last played, the landing pad's centre, the planet's generation values |
| `entities.bin` | Magic `MOBE`, header, the content id tables, then every entity pool and the simulation state (players, research, quests, statistics, unlocks, shipments, venture, pending block and water updates, loose items, leaf decay) |
| `regions/<x>_<z>.bin` | Only modified chunks of a region, each as a palette plus run length encoded indices |
| `field.bin` | A field world's changed field chunks (0179): magic `MOBF`, the header, then each chunk's coordinate and its codec bytes in coordinate order |

- `entities.bin` is written by a codec driven by Odin type information (`save_binary.odin`), so a new field cannot be left out silently. Structs carry a schema of field names, shallow kinds and sizes: fields can be added, removed or reordered, and only a retyped field refuses the file. Enums and bit sets go by name; an unknown name reads as zero.
- A fixed array saved shorter than this build's fills its start; a longer one refuses. A slice of fixed length, such as the inventory, refuses on any length change.
- The body writes the world's lists and the records interleaved in the order of format version 2 (`write_world_state` takes both), so moving the records off `World` (0154) left the bytes unchanged.
- Tables added after format version 2 follow the players at the end in the order they were added, each read only while bytes are left (`write_later_tables`, `read_later_tables`): the loose items, then the leaf decay queue, then the frame tables (`write_frame_tables`: the foundation pool, the frame records with the id counter, a list of the entities off frame 0 with their frame but the belt poles, then the run tables (0176, `write_belt_run_tables`): the belt pole pool, the poles' frames and the run pool, written only when a pole or a run exists, so a world without them keeps its bytes and its state hash). The poles' frames ride with their pool because the frame list is read before it. An older file loads with them empty without a version step.
- A field world (0179, `simulation_field_save.odin`) always writes the frame and run tables and then the field tables: the spacing, the simulated set (radius, margin, chunks, centres), the torches, the light's emitters, its pending removal and addition queues per channel and its arrived chunks, and the water a place dropped. The queues are deterministic state and are saved as they stand, never drained at save time. The wear table follows (0201, `write_machine_wear_table`): every live entity with `wear_ticks` above zero or `broken`, by handle, in pool order, always written in a field world; a field save that ends before it was written before 0201 and loads with every machine unworn and one log line. The felled trees' table follows the wear table (0197, `write_felled_tree_table`): the count and each key in key order, always written in a field world; a field save that ends before it was written before 0197 and loads with no tree felled by a player, then every tree standing in a frame is felled once with one log line (`clear_trees_of_an_old_save`). The arrival's table follows the felled trees (0200, `write_field_arrival_table`): its start tick, length and landed tick, 24 bytes, always written in a field world; a field save that ends before it was written before 0200 and loads with no fall, its hatches as saved, and one log line. `founded` is never saved: it is derived again after loading (`refresh_all_founded`). `field.bin` holds every chunk a tick changed, the set's and the ones that left it, whole (`encode_field_chunk`). Loading leaves the saved set to the field streaming's workers (0185, The field, the simulated set): once every chunk of it has arrived, the first tick lays the saved bytes over them and inserts them in coordinate order without the arrival's water wake and light seeding (`restore_arrived_field_set`, `restore_field_chunk`), so a loaded world and a joining machine (the join snapshot carries `field.bin`) hold the state of the machine that saved. `world.sjson` says `field_world`; a world without it was saved by a block build and the title's load refuses it naming M14 (`saved_world_plan`); the block world's loaders stay for the tests.
- `Entity_Common.frame` (and since 0201 `founded`, `wear_ticks` and `broken`, which travel in the wear table) is tagged `save:"-"`, which the codec leaves out of a struct's schema and body (`field_is_saved`), and travels in the frame tables instead; a world that never had a frame, a foundation, a pole or a run writes no frame tables, so its bytes and its state hash are those of a build before frames, and a save without them loads with every entity on frame 0. That load writes no log line: no header field tells an old save from a block only world of this build, so a line would print for both. The occupant index is rebuilt from the pools after loading.
- Loading maps every saved id to this build's by name (`save_remap.odin`): the distinct id types inside the codec, every id indexed array and plain index explicitly, chunk palettes as regions load. A saved id missing from this build that a record lists in `former_ids` (0196, items, machines and recipes, `content_former_ids`) maps to that record (`make_content_remap`), and the load logs one line naming every rename (`renamed_content_line`: `save: loaded under renamed ids: items foundation as stone_brick_foundation, ...`). What vanished is dropped (a stack empties, a queued technology dequeues, a gone active quest gives way to the first quest not done); a placed machine, a registered vein's type or a chunk block that vanished refuses the file.
- Kept by index, not remapped: vein size classes, quest objective and hint arrays, contract deliveries.
- The planet's generation values (0179, `Planet_Generation_Record`, `generation_planet_record.odin`): the radius, the gravity, the bedrock depth, the sea level, the rotation period, the palette's length, the relief octaves, the springs, the home, the crater (0199, `crater` with `crater_recorded`) and the trees' placement keys and species count (0197, `trees` with `trees_recorded`) the world was made with, written from `World.planet`. A file without the crater or the trees takes the data's with one log line each (`resolve_world_planet`), as one without the home takes the data's home; its unedited chunks then regenerate with the crater. A loaded world generates from them, not from the data, so an edit of `data/planets.sjson` cannot reshape the unedited ground round the saved chunks; the colours stay the data's, repeated or cut to the recorded length. A record that would not pass the planet record's checks refuses the file. A file without it (a radius of zero, written before 0179) takes the data's values with one log line and records them at the next save; like the other fields added to `world.sjson` (the pad, the spacing), it needs no format version step. The mode is written by name and checked on the raw tree (`world_file_mode_problem`), since the unmarshal assigns a number to the enum unchecked and reads an unknown name as the first value. The join snapshot carries the record, as it carries `world.sjson` (`encode_join_snapshot` through `encode_save_files`).
- A hatch's state (0198) is `Foundation.hatch_open` and `hatch_toggle_tick`, read by name, so a save from before 0198 loads them false and 0; 0222's `hatch_close_tick` went with 0231's rule: the reader skips the field by name, so a 0222 save loads with no remap and no log line, and a door it saved open with nobody near shuts on the first tick. A pod whose saved size is not its record's (an older build's) is replaced at load before the occupancy is built (`upgrade_resized_pods`, then `finish_pod_upgrades` after `rebuild_loaded_world`): the record's pod and its fixtures on a new frame at the old floor's centre, the old pod's hatches, bench, generator and locker taken off the old frame once the new pod stands (0221, `take_old_pod_fixtures`) and the old locker's stacks moved into the new locker as far as its slots go (`fill_pod_locker`), the old frame released when empty, the players in the new pod's box moved to its cabin, and one log line, the sizes as width by depth by height, `save: the pod of an older build (12 by 8 by 8 cells) is replaced by the pod of 12 by 12 by 8 cells with its hatches and fixtures, on a frame of its own at the old floor; N players moved into its cabin, M stacks moved into its locker, K dropped`. No save layout changes; the old hatches' states are lost (the new ones closed, the arrival not run again).
- A file without a generator version reads as 1. An older generator version still loads, is marked "terrain changed" in the load list, and its unmodified chunks regenerate with the new terrain. A file without a pad centre falls back to the spawn search.
- The title's save list (`save_list.odin`) reads each save's versions and content tables, so a save of another format shows as not loadable instead of failing at load.
- Unmodified chunks regenerate from the seed. Light is not saved: a loaded chunk recomputes sky light with the generation procedure and re-seeds block and entity lights. Networks are rebuilt after load.
- Saving runs on the main thread between ticks, writes to `<world>.saving` and swaps it in through `<world>.previous`, so a crash leaves a complete save; with only `.previous` left, loading falls back to it. Autosave counts simulated ticks.
- Regions use palette plus run length, not zlib: `core:compress/zlib` only inflates.

## Configuration and directories

- SJSON layers, lowest first: `$XDG_CONFIG_DIRS/mine-oh-belowed/config.sjson` (last entry to first), `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/mine-oh-belowed/config.sjson`, `config.d/*.sjson` by file name, then `--set=<key>=<value>`. No project layer.
- Trees merge with provenance per key (objects merge, scalars and arrays replace), then map onto `Configuration` (settings, bindings, paths) strictly: an unknown key, a wrong type or an out of range value is an error naming the file. `settings.shadows` is a retired key, accepted and ignored with a log line.
- `mine-oh-belowed config` prints the files in precedence order and the effective values with their source.
- Every screen that changes a setting writes `config.d/90-settings.sjson`, strings quoted. The export settings `settings.export_directory` (default empty) and `settings.export_on_save` (default false) and the edits directory `settings.edits_directory` (default empty) are ordinary keys, so the phone's file can set them over USB.
- The touch layout editor writes `touch_overlay.sjson` beside `config.d/`, a file of its own, no configuration layer ([touch_overlay.md](touch_overlay.md)).
- The files the game writes and reads back (the settings, the touch layouts, the texture edits, the data edits, the export) go through `platform.write_file_replacing`: written beside the path as `<path>.tmp`, then renamed over it, so a crash or a full disk mid write leaves the old file whole. A save gets the same guarantee from its staging directory, renamed whole.
- A `config.d/90-settings.sjson` that stops the start (cut off, or a value the validation refuses, found by loading again without it) is renamed to `90-settings.sjson.broken`, one `configuration:` log line names it and the problem, the game starts on the other layers, and the title screen toasts it once (`load_configuration_at_start`, 0149). A problem in any other file or in `--set` still logs the error and exits: the user wrote those, and strictness surfaces their typos.
- `platform_directories` (`platform_paths.odin`) is the one place that reads the base directories from the environment; the pure path helpers apply the rules. Every directory is made through `make_directory_path`, never `os.make_directory_all` ([android.md](android.md) has why).

| Base | Linux | Windows | Android |
|---|---|---|---|
| Config home | `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME`, else `~/.config` | `%APPDATA%` | `<files>/config` |
| Config dirs | `$XDG_CONFIG_DIRS`, else `/etc/xdg` | none | none |
| Data home (saves) | `$XDG_DATA_HOME`, else `~/.local/share` | `%APPDATA%` | `<files>/share` |
| State home | `$XDG_STATE_HOME`, else `~/.local/state` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%` | `<files>/state` |
| Runtime directory | `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` | none | none |

- A relative XDG value is ignored. On Windows a missing variable means no directory and a leading `~/` stays as written. On Android `<files>` is the activity's external files folder, else the internal one.
- The state directory `<state home>/mine-oh-belowed/` holds `log.txt` (on Windows the log sits beside the executable), `screenshots/`, `texture_edits.sjson` and `data_edits/` (mirroring the data directory: `data_edits/blocks.sjson`, `data_edits/quests/chapter_01.sjson`).

## Logging

- `log_printf` writes every line to stderr and to `<state>/mine-oh-belowed/log.txt` (appended, one header line per start with the build stamp); on Android also to logcat.
- When stderr is not a terminal (Steam), the log file is put on the stderr descriptor so Odin's runtime reports (bounds checks, type assertions) land in the log; the game's own lines go once to the log and once to the original stderr.
- raylib's trace log takes the same path through a callback (`raylib_log.odin`), which drops GLFW's feature unavailable error (65548): on Wayland every window position query raises one, raylib makes them at start and on every monitor lookup, and nothing is wrong.
- After the display line the start logs one `gl:` line with the GL vendor, renderer, version and GLSL version (`log_gl_info`: `glGetString` through `glfwGetProcAddress`, so nothing links libGL or opengl32; on Android straight from `libGLESv3`), because a translation layer under Wine can reject a shader without naming itself.
- The SDL3 backend logs the joysticks SDL sees at start and one line per joystick added or removed (name, vendor, product, GUID, whether SDL has a gamepad mapping), so a controller SDL sees but does not map is told apart from one it never sees.
- Main thread assertions and panics log a back trace; SIGSEGV and SIGILL write a raw back trace from the signal handler before the process ends (not on Windows or Android).

## Performance

Target: 1080p at 60 frames per second on the couch machine (AMD Navi 10, Radeon RX 5700 class), 60 ticks per second with several thousand belts and hundreds of machines; chunk generation keeps up with a sprinting player.

- The headless factory benchmark (`benchmark_factory.odin`) runs through `test_factory_benchmark` (size 1, two minutes of warm up and one measured) and `--benchmark=<size>` (two and ten minutes; [build.md](build.md)). `./build.sh bench` runs the test optimised and fails when size 1 averages 8 ms per tick or more. The table ends with the state hash after the ticks.
- Since the slice (0179) the benchmark world is a field world: the player starts in the pod at the planet's home on the field, whose set is generated before the first tick (`stage_generated_field_set`), with a pad of foundations `BENCHMARK_PAD_DISTANCE_MILLIMETRES` (24 m) ahead on the generated surface beyond the crater (`start_benchmark_field`), so every tick runs the field's part. The modules still stand on the block floor below, since their drills mine the block world's veins; the factories on frames come with M14, and until then sizes above 1 are refused naming M14 (`benchmark_size_problem`).
- Sizes stop at 16 (`BENCHMARK_LARGEST_SIZE`): the cost per tick is close to linear in the size, so larger factories are read off size 16, and a size 64 build took minutes of network rebuilds.
- The profile: `simulation_tick` and `tick_entities` take an optional `^Tick_Profile` (`tick_profile.odin`) and add each step's wall time to its `Tick_Section`. Without a profile no clock is read. The runner also times each whole tick for the average and the worst.
- The flat world: no generation and no streaming. `build_benchmark_world` fills every chunk column under the layout plus one chunk of margin, chunk y -1 and 0 stone with grass on top (world y 31), y 1 and 2 air, all under full sky light. The generator serves only the vein tables; veins are added where the manifest puts them, checked against each other only.
- The pad and the player stand in opposite corners of the floor. Research costs 1000 times its packs, so the one technology the builder queues outlasts every run.
- The layout is content: `data/blueprints/benchmark.sjson` lists modules with a footprint, copies per size and veins. `benchmark_layout` gives each module a column along x and its copies along z, 4 blocks apart, centred on the origin. Each copy runs its blueprint from `data/blueprints/benchmark/` (origin `[0, 0, 0]`, overridden with the copy's cell) through `run_blueprint`, after its veins are added.
- Modules: `smelting.sjson` (iron), `assembly.sjson` (gears, circuits, science pack 1 and a lab), `power.sjson` (steam), `oil.sjson` (refinery, cracking, plastic, flare); each file's header comment describes it. Every module has its own veins and power, so the cost grows linearly and no module starves another.
- A machine without progress over the last minute of the warm up is idle and fails the test and the command: drills and furnaces by their output rate, inserters by an idle streak of a whole minute, crafting machines, labs, fluid machines and launch pads by their working state on some tick. A generator in the Idle state is a reserve behind a lower dispatch order, not idle (0140).
