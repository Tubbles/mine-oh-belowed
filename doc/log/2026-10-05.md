## The home spawn on dry ground (0180)

Tags: field, home, spawn, sea, crater, pod, lockstep, save, 0180, m14

A new world searches for its home once, in `resolve_session_planet` (`new_world_home`, `find_dry_planet_home`), in whole degrees because the world file's record already holds `home` as whole degrees: no layout change and no remap, at the cost of coarse moves (a degree of latitude is 140 m at 8 km, a degree of longitude at latitude 83 about 17 m). Dry is measured on the crater's floor, not the uncratered surface, since the pod and its cabin stand on it, with a margin of 1 m (`HOME_DRY_MARGIN_MILLIMETRES`, one sample at the coarsest spacing) and a reach of 5 degrees (`HOME_SEARCH_DEGREES`), both constants until a planet needs them as data. Measured with the shipped record over the seeds 1 to 400: 24, 15 and 50 seeds put the floor less than 1 m above the sea at 4, 8 and 16 km, every one found dry ground within the reach, the farthest move 177, 144 and 280 m; the first wet seeds are 9, 3 and 7 (83/132 to 83/137, 83/130 and 83/133, each about 34 to 42 m, the moved floors 2.2, 2.8 and 5.1 m above the sea), and the default seed's floor stands 6.0, 5.9 and 2.9 m above the sea, so the shipped world does not move. A loaded world never searches: its pod already stands where its record says, and a file recording a wet home keeps it. The zero home's special case is gone (the data requires `home`, so only test planets had it): latitude 0, longitude 0 is +x, and `make_test_planet` names the pole. Since the cabin spawn (0221) reads the saved pod and frame, the pad pitch part of the item was already met, a foundation cannot reach the cabin (its cells are the pod's, Frame_Cell_Taken) and a pit dug under the cabin's floor drops a joiner to its bottom (2.695 m down for a 1.5 m sphere centred 1.5 m under the floor); the one gap left was ground raised in an empty cabin burying the next joiner, closed by keeping the pod spawn's capsule clear in the place's bury check with a refusal of its own, `Would_Bury_Spawn` ("That would bury where players arrive"), since "That would bury a player" with nobody in the cabin would read as a mistake. This reaches old saves: a place into a loaded world's empty cabin is now refused; a cabin an older build filled stays filled. Not done: `field_home_player` (content without a pod, test fields and a mod's) still stands the player on the generated surface, ignoring a foundation or raised ground there, since making it current needs a frame ray and a planet the flat test fields lack.

## The field's third person camera pulled in (0220)

Tags: field, camera, third-person, presentation, tunnel, crouch, frames, trees, 0220, m14

The field's third person camera is pulled in by the first surface on its line from the eye, as the block world's is by the blocks. The margin is measured along the surface's normal, not along the ray as in the block world: along the ray the camera ended 4 to 6 cm under the roof of a 1 m tunnel (a crouched player, the default distance and shoulder, the ray meeting the roof at a cosine of about 0.18), inside the reach of the 0.1 m near plane's top edge, while along the normal it sits 0.2 m under it, about 0.55 m behind the eye; a wall faced square on gives the same 2 m minus 0.2 m either way. The frames are cast with the walk's solid filter rather than the aim's, since belts and splitters fill whole cells without being solid and would pull the camera in over a thin belt; the price is that the top row of an open hatch does not stop it. Trunks stop it, canopies do not, since nothing in the simulation is solid there. Left as they are: a ray, not a sphere, so a side wall off the line can still meet the near plane's half width at a high field of view; the camera snaps in and out with no easing, as the block world's; the viewer's body is still drawn when the camera reaches the eye. The block world's camera keeps its margin along the ray.

## The command socket on the field (0183)

Tags: commands, socket, field, teleport, look, camera, crouch, frames, screenshots, lockstep, 0183, m14

The socket's commands read the block player while the default world became the field, so a field session's answers came from a body that never moved (`query player` printed the block spawn search's 352.50 35.00 64.50) and `fly`, `teleport` and `place` wrote where nothing looked. On a field world they now read and move the field player: positions in metres, latitude and longitude, a height above the sea, the look as a bearing from north and a pitch. The stored yaw counts from a heading carried along since the spawn, which a caller cannot know, so `look` sets the heading to north and the yaw to the bearing, and every answer prints the bearing. The new `look`, `camera` and `crouch` go through the input record and `serve_developer_request` as the other writes do, integer only, so a lockstep session stays equal; `crouch` is a hold kept in the field body and saved (a hold left out of the save would be lost on a join's snapshot and part the machines), since the Sneak hold setting's default would release a one tick crouch at once. `place` takes a frame and a cell and the frame's rules, with no free placement (a foundation on the pod's frame below the floor row starts one). `vein` is refused on the field: the sphere's veins are a function of the seed and the home and shape the generated ground, so an added one would need the generation to read saved veins. The commands that only mean something on the block world answer `no block world`, and the field's new ones `no field world` on an old block save. The Developer screen's fly and no clip toggles, dead on the field for the same reason, act on the field body too, and its Teleport goes to the cabin. The point of it all is framing headless screenshots without xdotool: with the ticks held (`pause`) the lines run at once and only `tick` moves the world, so the couch's gamepad cannot turn the view between a `look` and a `screenshot`. The factory benchmark builds its modules on the block floor beside its field, so its command context keeps the block forms (`Command_Context.block_forms`).

## No drop capsule in a field world (0262)

Tags: field, capsule, landing-pad, spawn, start, quests, save, lockstep, 0262, m14

A new field world now takes `field_world_start`: no search, no pad, no capsule, the block body (kept for the shared inventory) on the origin. Old field saves load as they are, capsule and pad included, rather than dropping the capsule with a log line: before 0210 a field world's rewards landed in it, so it can hold items, and a joiner reads the host's tables either way. The reverse direction, a field world saved by this build opened on an older one: that build finds no saved pad, runs the spawn search and places a pad, the saved empty capsule pool keeps the capsule away, and the next save records the pad, which this build then takes as a saved start; nothing breaks, and a join between builds is refused by the build stamp anyway. ## Digging needs a tool (0265)

Tags: field, tools, shovel, pickaxe, axe, trees, felling, touch, starter-kit, quests, 0265, m14

Which tool digs what is data on both sides (an item's `tool_role`, a material's `dug_with` and `tool_tier`); tiers gate and do not speed, so the stone and iron shovels dig the shipped topsoil like the wooden one. ## The fall as an atmospheric entry (0269)

Tags: field, arrival, entry, sky, atmosphere, heat, shader, presentation, 0269, m14

The curve is a drag model integrated at load in its own seconds, so the landing tick stays integer data and no float reaches the tick; the user tunes the seconds and the keys the shape. The stretch over the 30 s is not uniform: a uniform one halved every speed and the hit came at 10 m/s, so the curve's last `arrival_real_seconds` (6) play 1:1 at the end and the space and entry phases, where nothing near the eye moves, carry the stretch. Even so the hit is 22 m/s, not the 30 asked for: a faster terminal speed with the shipped atmosphere leaves the hit glowing above the threshold, and the entry speed that cures it starts the fall beyond the render distance; a thicker atmosphere makes the heat pop on at the top with no pink onset. The onset was kept over the last 8 m/s. At game scale the heating at terminal speed is 14 percent of the peak (a real entry's is near none), so a threshold key models the glow's onset and makes the hit dark. The start bound moved from the fog's start to 0.9 of the coarsest level: within 614 m a 30 s fall hits at 7 m/s; at 905 m the crater starts hazed in the fog, read as the atmosphere seen from above. The sky's altitude rule is general: any field camera above 360 m darkens. The user asked whether 30 s matches a real entry on a planet this size: no, an 8 km body with Earth gravity has an orbital speed of 280 m/s and would fall through its air in under ten seconds without any glow, while an Earth-sized planet glows for six minutes; 30 s is the storytelling middle and stays. Timeline from the built curve: the top crossed at 7.7 s, the heat on at 10 s, the peak at 21.2 s, out at 28.1 s, the hit at 30 s.
