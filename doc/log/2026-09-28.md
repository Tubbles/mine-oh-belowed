## Work item 0059 implemented

- Six tree species in the new content file `data/trees.sjson` (oak, birch, pine, acacia, palm, dead tree), picked per tree by weight from each biome's `trees` list; a biome with trees and no list is refused. Crowns are round, conical, flat or none, trunks 4 to 16, roots on tall oaks and pines. Forest and plains get clearings from a new appended `Clearings` sub seed (wavelength 48), plains a higher density so its trees stand in groves.
- `LEAF_REACH` is 4 and `FEATURE_MAXIMUM_HEIGHT` 20, from the species limits (`MAXIMUM_CROWN_RADIUS`, `MAXIMUM_TRUNK_HEIGHT`) the trees file is validated against. `GENERATOR_VERSION` is 4, so saves from 0058 show "terrain changed".
- Felling: mining a log drops the logs above it as loose items; the leaves around the column are queued and decay after 1 to 4 seconds unless a log lies within 4 blocks (Manhattan). The queue is the second later table of `entities.bin`.
- Decision: a conical crown over roots starts at least 6 blocks above the root, not at a third of the trunk, so no needle lies within the support distance of a root and a felled pine leaves no crown hanging on its roots.
- Decision: trees keep 4 blocks off the landing pad, since the pad clears only 10 blocks above itself and trees now grow to 20.
- Decision: the sapling item is named in `data/trees.sjson` (`sapling_item`) and checked against the items at load, rather than named in code.
- Decision: the simulation reads the tree blocks from the session's generator (`simulation_tree_felling`), so `tick_world` takes them as a parameter (empty in tests without trees) and `src/loop.odin` passes them.

## Work item 0082 implemented: ground cover per biome

Tags: #generation #biomes #blocks #placement #textures #decision

- Six cross blocks (`grass_tuft`, `tall_grass`, `flower_red`, `flower_yellow`, `dead_bush`, `reeds`), each with an item that places it back. `Biome_Definition.ground_cover` lists them per biome with a chance and the top blocks they stand on; `apply_ground_cover` runs after `clear_above_outcrops` and before the pad stamp. `GENERATOR_VERSION` is 5.
- Decision: the running sum runs over every entry in order, and the picked entry must stand on the column's top block, so an entry's chance is its share of the columns whatever the other entries stand on. Validation also refuses chances that add up to more than 1, which the running sum would silently cut.
- Decision: "dry land" is the cell above the surface being air after the features. Water fills that cell on flooded columns, so no separate height test is needed, and wetland columns at sea level get reeds.
- Decision: the top block is computed from the column (the biome's top block, or its pit block in a low spot), not read from the chunk, so the chunk that holds the cover cell decides alone when the surface is the top layer of the chunk below.
- Decision: the ray stops at cover (its bounds fill the cell), so a block or machine aimed at cover takes the cover's cell (`placement_target`), otherwise a machine aimed at covered ground would float one cell up. The replaced cover drops nothing.
- Decision: `commit_placement` has no block registry and the developer `place` command calls it too, so `placement_at` records `clears_cover` and `commit_placement` clears the non air cells of the footprint; a valid placement's footprint holds only air and cover.
- Follow up in the same item: the chunk shader discards texels with alpha below 0.5 (only the cover tiles have any, so every other tile draws as before); belts (placement, drag, the developer command) and pipes use `cell_takes_machine` and `placement_target` and clear the cover they replace; `mine_block` calls `drop_unsupported_cover` after the felling hook, which turns cover above the mined block to air and spills its item.
- Decision: `drag_target_column` gets the placement target, so a drag aimed at the side of a cover cell follows the cover's column.
- Not done: the bore drill ghost's vein line (`bore_drill_ghost_vein`) still reads the raw target, since passing the block registry changes its test in `deep_mining_test.odin` too. Only the column counts there, so it differs only when aiming at the side of a cover cell.

## Work item 0065 implemented: water rendering

Tags: #rendering #water #meshing #shaders #fog #decision

- The mesher routes water faces into `Chunk_Mesh_Data.water_parts` with a tangent per vertex (flow x, flow z, shore, 0); `render_water.odin` draws them in a transparent pass with the `water.vs` and `water.fs` pair, and closes the fog in under water.
- Decision: the flow sum and the four shore values are part of `Face_Key`, and the shore values count in `corners_uniform`, so a merged water quad carries one flow and one shore value. Open water still merges; cells at a shore stay single quads.
- Decision: the shore value reads the three cells around the vertex's vertical edge in the water cell's own layer (the fourth is the water cell), not the layer in front of the face that `vertex_light` reads: for the top face that layer lies above the water, where a bank level with the surface has no block. Side and bottom faces use the same rule, so their top vertices match the surface.
- Decision: the fragment shader turns the flow (world x and z) into the face's texcoords by the face normal from the derivatives of the world position, since the texcoords run along each face's own axes. The time uniform wraps every 600 seconds; an axis aligned flow and both ripples continue seamlessly, a diagonal flow jumps once per wrap.
- Decision: the water pass draws after the torch flames too (the item named the loose items and the weather particles as its neighbours), so flames behind water show through it. Foam lightens the colour but keeps the water's alpha; the water has no cloud shadows.
- Decision: under water is decided at the camera position of the frame, which in third person is the camera behind the player, so the view is fogged when the camera is in water.
- Decision: the game does not start when the water shader does not load, like the chunk shader; a hot reload of a broken pair keeps the old shader and the other pair still reloads.
- Not done: `take_mesh_results` (`world_streaming.odin`, outside this item's files) counts a mesh against the per frame upload limit only when it has opaque parts, so a chunk with water faces alone uploads without counting.

## Work item 0099 implemented: procedural ore textures

Tags: #textures #procedural #ores #atlas #repetition #hot-reload #decision

- Cause of the grid, read in `tools/make_placeholder_textures.py`: not the blur's edges. `gaussian_blur` already wraps (`values[(y + offset_y) % SIZE][(x + offset_x) % SIZE]`), and the old tiles had 186 of their 364 blob texels within two texels of the edge against 44 percent of the texels lying there, no real bias. The cause is the blob size: a width of 1.6 texels and the top fifth of the field give 2 to 3 blobs of up to 30 texels per tile (hematite: a band two texels wide down the left edge, wrapping onto the right; coal: one blob across a corner). A blob that crosses the wrapped edge is cut there, and since neighbouring blocks show different orientations (0088) the pieces never meet again: straight cut ore strips lie along the block boundaries and a corner blob shows as quarter blobs around a block vertex. With two or three blobs a tile, each block reads as one unit, which is the grid. The fix is many small blobs, 10 to 14 per tile with the largest 7 to 16 texels in the shipped defaults, whose cut pieces are small.
- Decision: seven parameters plus the seed (`share`, `blob_width`, `crystal_size`, `stone_grain`, `stone_mottle`, `ore_grain`, `rim_strength`), ranges and steps in `ore_texture_parameter_ranges` as the schema, every value required in the data. `crystal_size` gives galena its blocky look (the noise is constant over 2 by 2 cells); it allows 3 although 3 does not divide 16 (a narrower last row and column of cells), so the slider has no gaps. The stone's mottle width (1.5 texels) is a constant, not a parameter.
- Decision: the blobs are the texels of highest rank (value, then index), not the texels above a threshold, so the count is exactly the share times 256, rounded, even with the ties crystal cells can make.
- Defaults: `blob_width` 0.6 to 0.65 for most ores. A sweep over 100 seeds showed below about 0.55 the blur is close to white noise and single texels join diagonally into a checkerboard (visible in a 0.5 preview of pentlandite), and at 0.8 and above a tile holds 8 or fewer blobs, the largest 20 texels and more, which reads per block again. Coal 0.75 (coarser), galena `crystal_size` 2 at 0.4, cassiterite's grain and rim lower since its brown is close to the stone's grey. `stone_grain` 8 and `stone_mottle` 16 match the stone tile's noise.
- Decision: `read_block_textures` takes the loaded entries as a parameter and `upload_atlas` loads them (data file, then the edits file from `texture_edits_path_from_environment`), so the tests pass entries or none and never read the user's state directory. The edits file is read at every atlas build, not watched: the editor (0100) rebuilds the atlas itself.
- Decision: the preview (`test_write_ore_texture_previews`) runs only with `-define:TEXTURE_PREVIEW=true`, since `rl.ExportImage` pulls raylib into the test link, which needs the linker shims `build.sh` makes for builds; the command is in the test file's comment.

## Work item 0101 implemented: per block offset for periodic tiles

Tags: #textures #repetition #shader #water #mesher #blocks #test #decision

- The chunk shader slides a varying tile by a whole texel offset after the mirror and the turns: `varied_tile_texcoord` in `chunk.fs`, mirrored by `varied_tile_texcoord` and `tile_offset` in `src/texture_variation.odin`. The oriented texcoord is clamped to the last texel before the shift as well as after it, so a fragment exactly on the block edge reads the wrapped neighbour of the last texel and not texel 0 moved by the offset.
- Found: offset y takes bits 7 to 10 as the item says, and bits 8 to 10 are also the top of the brightness jitter's bits 8 to 15, so a block's offset y and its brightness are loosely coupled. Both are uniform per block, so no pattern follows from it.
- Measured symmetric motifs in a 32 by 32 field (2048 neighbour pairs, the four texels on each side of the shared edge): with the orientations alone 241 pairs mirror across their edge and 256 are point symmetric across it; with the offset none mirror and one is point symmetric, the rate a coincidence of offsets gives.
- Decision (coordinator): water takes no offset, only the jitter as before. Its tile is periodic and isotropic, so the same crop in every block joins seamlessly into one continuous surface, and that passed the couch after 0088. A per block offset would put a crop seam at every water block edge, a one block grid over a lake, which is the kind of pattern the user notices.
- Decision (coordinator): a block flag `framed` for the log ends. `rings_pattern` draws a picture, rings around the centre inside a bark rim, which the offset would push off centre. A framed block's varying faces turn and mirror but never slide. The mesher carries three values in the vertex colour's green channel (`Tile_Variation`: Full 0, Turn 128, Keep 255, `orientation_flag_green`) and the shader splits them at 0.25 and 0.75; `face_tile_variation(keep_orientation, framed, group)` replaces the boolean `face_keeps_orientation`. Set on the five logs; the periodicity check leaves framed blocks out, since a picture is not periodic by design.
- Decision: the periodicity check compares the wrap edge's mean texel difference with the interior edges' mean at a factor of 2.5. A periodic tile shifted cyclically, so that every interior line of edges takes a turn as the wrap, reaches up to 2.3 (the tar pit, whose rare glossy texels weigh heavily on a line of 16 edges; gold quartz 2.2, concrete slab 2.1), and the brick's own wrap lies on a mortar line (2.0). The unshifted shipped tiles range from 0.49 to 2.02 without the logs. A seam shows where the interior is smooth and the wrap jumps, and there the ratio runs far higher (a ramp across the tile gives 15, a test holds that).
- Brick: its pattern shaded the brick cut by the tile's edge in a shifted row with two different shades, a non periodic brick. `brick_pattern` now takes the brick index modulo two. The metric did not flag it (2.05 before, 2.02 after), since the mortar lines dominate the brick's roughness.
- Rock strata (coordinator): `rock_pattern` drew a line every 5 rows stepping every 6 columns, neither dividing 16, so a slid top showed a doubled line at the wrap; it passed the metric (0.80 to 1.14), a strata line being only 12 levels dark among noise. Now a line every 4 rows stepping every 4 columns, same colours and line weight (ratios now 1.15 to 1.47, the denser lines touching the wrap more often).
