## The placement editor and the tools radial (0215)

Tags: placement, editor, input, bindings, touch, hud, lockstep, m14

- The centre rule (folding 0213): every footprint Place puts on the field is centred on the aimed cell, offset `(side - 1) / 2` cells below it on each horizontal axis, as `foundation_block_first_offset` already centres a foundation block, so an even side's centre corner is the aimed cell's high corner and a 1 by 1 or 2 by 2 footprint keeps its origin. The aim is a cell, not a point: choosing the corner nearest the hit point would make the outline jump between four positions while the reticle moves inside one cell. The height is never offset. The block world keeps its corner placement.
- A machine on bare ground is centred on cell (0, 0, 0) of its new frame, and its origin is the simulation's own (`field_footprint_origin({}, ...)`), never a cell taken from a command; the frame keeps standing on its hit, so a nudge on bare ground re-stands the frame on the ground under the moved centre.
- The commit is a `Machine_Placement_Command` (the machine, the rotation, the frame and origin, or the new frame's ground point and heading) rather than input in the record: the editor's state is presentation per viewport, never saved, hashed or sent, so the commit carries its result and the simulation sees one command as Place's queued placement. It is queued into the field's placements at the tick's start and drained with them, so the drain checks it like any placement. No save layout changes (the command list is not saved); its network tag is appended last, so no older tag moves.
- Pipette has no effect anywhere today; the tools radial routes one Pipette press to the world on the release of a tap, so the pipette item (0216) finds it there. Once the radial has shown, its dead centre selects nothing.
- Touch: the editor's controls are HUD touch buttons pressing the gamepad controls of their actions, not a placement layout file, so they follow rebinding and every user layout. While the editor is anchored the free screen's taps and holds press nothing, since a stray tap would commit and a resting thumb cancel a 5 m machine. On narrow screens, where the touch row wraps into a stack, the grid stands left of that stack above the hotbar.
- The threshold is data: `direct_placement_limit` in `data/game.sjson`, 2 by 2 by 3 shipped.

## Point lights on machine models (0224)

Tags: models, light, shaders, pod, presentation, m15

- The models take the field's point lights through a shader pair of their own (`data/shaders/model.vs`, `model.fs`) on the lit layer only. The emissive layer draws with a second material on raylib's default shader instead of a per layer uniform in one shader: `DrawMesh` binds the material's program on every call anyway, so the second material costs no extra state change, while a uniform would add a program bind and an upload per layer draw, and the default shader keeps the emissive look exactly as before.
- The normal is `mat3(matModel)` times the vertex normal, normalized in the fragment, as `field.vs`. The model matrices scale uniformly (the pitch, a tree's scale) or per axis on axis aligned voxel faces, where the normalized result keeps the right direction; `matNormal` would cost raylib an inverse per draw for no visible gain under the half wrap term.
- A machine lists at most 8 lamps (`lights`), the shaders' slot count, since more could never all show. The pod's cabin uses at most six so the arms' lamps outside still find slots.
- A pod counts as working for its model, its emissive materials and its lamps (`foundation_model_working`), so its cabin is lit from the arrival on; a hatch works while open, the oxygen generator while it supplies a room.
- No machine lights are gathered during the fall (`hide_frames`), when the machines are not drawn; 0223, which draws the pod during the descent, revisits it.
- The block world takes no point lights; since the model renderer lives for the process, `draw_session_world` clears the model shader's lights before it draws, so a field session's lights never linger there.
- The lights have no shadows and shine through hulls: a cabin lamp lights the ground round the pod within its radius, read as light leaking from the portholes, accepted at the approval.
- The pod's record ships `lights = []`; the six lamps measured off the lab model's emissive patches go into the record with 0221.

## The pod's cabin dark until its lamps light it (0225)

Tags: models, light, pod, presentation, m15

- A pod's record may carry `interior_light_share` (0 to 1, pods only): the lit tint of the pod's model, its fixtures' and the bodies of players in its box is scaled by it before the point lights add, so the lamps' pools can show against a dark cabin instead of brightening walls already at full daylight.
- The share is found by place, not by handle: once per scene the alive pods' boxes on their frames are gathered (`gather_interior_lights`), and a model whose origin cell lies in one takes its share (`posed_model_light`). The pod and every fixture fall under one rule, the draw loops are unchanged, and no entity gains a field, so the save layout and the hash are untouched. A machine a player places inside the cabin takes the share too, which suits where it stands.
- Players' bodies take it by their feet cell (`field_player_interior_light_share`), so a second player in the cabin does not stand at full daylight against dark walls. A field session draws no loose items and no first person hands, so nothing else needs it.
- A working model's emissive layers keep their full brightness (the pod's screens, an open hatch's strips, a supplying generator's strip); an idle emissive layer is drawn lit like the rest and so follows the darkened tint, as a switched off strip should (confirmed at the approval).
- The darkened tint keeps the models' floor (`MINIMUM_MODEL_BRIGHTNESS`, 0.06), so at night the cabin sits where every other model does and the lamps carry it.
- The value meant for the remade pod is 0.25: at full day the far wall draws at a quarter of its colour, four times the floor, so the shaded faces still read, and an amber lamp's pool within half its radius is 2 to 5 times that. 0.5 leaves the pools at 1.5 times the base, the flatness the user saw; 0.1 sends the far side to one flat dark.
- The shipped pod record gets no key in this item: the old pod has no lamps and no emissive material, so 0.25 would make it a uniformly dim shell. The key defaults to 1, and 0221 writes 0.25 beside its two lamps with the remade model; the screenshot pass uses a private data copy with the key.
- The workbench previews keep full light (no point lights there, and the previews must stay readable). An arm placed in the cabin and the box fallback of a machine without a model are not covered.

## Machine model layers go up unindexed (0226)

Tags: models, presentation, raylib, m15

- The triangle mesher gave every triangle three vertices of its own and wrote the identity as its u16 index buffer, which carried nothing and capped a layer at 21845 triangles (65535 vertices): the round three pod, 25488 triangles, failed content validation. The triangle layers now carry no indices and go up unindexed (raylib's `DrawMesh` draws `vertexCount` vertices in order when `indices` is nil); the voxel mesher shares quad corners and keeps its indices.
- The model check's triangle budget is now the only bound on a triangle layer; the loader accepts any size, as it accepted any size under the vertex limit before.
- Readers of a layer's triangles go through `model_mesh_triangle`, which serves both shapes; the triangle count read off `indices` alone would have counted every OBJ model as 0 triangles and passed the budget silently.
- No save, record or network change: presentation only.

## Machine lamps clipped to their machine's box (0229)

Tags: models, light, shaders, pod, presentation, m15

- The user saw the pod's cabin lamps light the hull's outside and asked why solids do not block them. This renderer has no shadows (a point light is a soft falloff round a point), so a machine's lamp is clipped to its machine's footprint box instead. This reverses 0224's decision 1, which accepted the spill.
- The box alone does not keep the hull's skin dark: the skin lies inside its own machine's box, and the lamp term gives a face turned away from the light half its value (`point_light_wrap`). So a clipped light gives nothing to a face turned away from it, fading out over a quarter of the cosine past grazing. Backs of cabin fixtures turned away from a lamp go dark too; the cabin's base light (0225) still shows them.
- The terrain takes nothing from a clipped light (`field.fs` skips colour alpha 0): the pod's floor disc covers the ground inside the hull, and the band of ground inside the box but outside the round hull is exactly what should stay unlit. A future machine with an open floor would light none of its ground.
- The box's top is the model's top when it rises above the footprint (`machine_model_top`): the round three pod's cone reaches 9.9 cells over a footprint height of 8, and its inside is the cabin's ceiling.
- Three vec4 rows per light in one `vec4 point_light_boxes[24u]`, not a `mat4` array: `rlSetUniformMatrices` is not bound and transposes on desktop but not on GLES in raylib 6.0, and consecutive locations for array elements are not promised. A light with no box has zero rows, which map every point to the box's centre and pass, so the shader needs no flag; clipped or not travels in the colour's alpha.
- The padding (0.05 cells on every side) lives on the CPU in cells, so it pads every side alike whatever the box's shape.
- The arm's lamp is unclipped: it lights the ground it works over.
- A clipped lamp still takes one of the eight slots by its distance to the camera, also when the camera is outside its box; the arms' lamps outside find the other slots, as in 0224.

## The airlock's doors on their own (0222)

Tags: pod, airlock, hatch, lockstep, save, m15

- The user, on the round three preview: "the doors stay open? I want them to only open individually when the player is really close and then close behind the player". The pod's two hatches now open and close on their own (`entity_pod_airlock.odin`, `tick_pod_airlocks`), a lockstep step after the players move, skipped while the world falls and never run by the prediction.
- Near is a distance, not cells: a capsule within `pod_airlock.reach_millimetres` (150) of a door's cells. In the 2 cell bore every feet cell is a cell before one of the doors, so a cell rule could not leave the middle near neither door, nor tell 50 mm from 450 mm before a door. The 150 mm opens late (7 ticks before contact at the crouch's speed, or at contact, since a closed door is solid) and stays under two limits of the 1 m bore and the crouched 0.6 m capsule: the middle is 200 mm from both doors, so both close round a resting player, and against the far door the near one is 400 mm away, so it closes behind them. Bounds 50 to 300 mm.
- Facing within 60 degrees is needed to open a door on every side, so a player at the locker, passing the door or backing out does not open one; it is not needed to keep a door open.
- The interlock: a door opens only while the other is closed and both have finished their slide (48 ticks, the hatch's `period_seconds` rounded at load), closes come before opens and at most one door opens per pod and tick (the outer first), so the two doors never stand open at once and both are closed for at least one slide. Interact obeys the same interlock (`pod_airlock_refuses_opening`, no event, no toast) and the HUD hides the Open hint while it refuses; Interact stays an override of the moment otherwise.
- The hold: an open door no player is near closes after a hold drawn from a hash of the hatch's handle and the tick (`hash_combine`, deterministic and not seeded by the world), 36 to 72 ticks (`close_hold_minimum_ticks`, `close_hold_maximum_ticks`, each 1 to 600), so the hatch slide never sounds on a beat. A player in a doorway keeps it open; `toggle_hatch`'s capsule check stays the backstop.
- The arrival no longer opens the doors (decision at the approval): both stay closed at the landing and the inner one opens as the player comes to it, since both opening and closing on their own two seconds after the landing is what the user asked not to see. `open_closed_hatches` went with it.
- The save: `Foundation.hatch_close_tick`, read by name, so a save from before 0222 loads it 0, none due, which is what the step sets on the next tick; no remap, no format version, no log line. A save with both doors open (0198 to 0221) closes each after its hold unless a player is near it. The field rides the foundations' pool, so it is hashed and travels in the join snapshot; a save taken in a door's hold closes it on the same tick after the load.
