## 0360: the factory's flat draws take the haze

Tags: presentation, haze, shaders, rlgl

0347 left the slabs, the runs and the belt items ending on a step at the haze's end. Two ways were open: a second shader for raylib's immediate batch, or the model shader's flat mode taking the haze and the batch routed through it. The second was chosen, since the flat mode already drew the instanced slabs and one `apply_haze` then serves every factory draw.

The seam that decides whether it is right: rlgl transforms a batch vertex on the CPU, so the shader takes it as its world position and never multiplies by `matModel`, while the instanced flat path sets `matModel` to rlgl's transform. A draw through the model shader inside a flat region would flush the batch with the wrong switches, so the regions hold none: the foundations' pool, whose pod draws by `DrawMesh`, stays outside.

The review read raylib 6.2's `rlSetShader` and `rlDrawRenderBatch` for the order of flush and reset and found nothing wrong. The cull and the fade read the same `fog_end` from the eye, so nothing pops at any view distance.

Captures at a 256 m view, the profile's factory from 120 to 250 m and from beside it: the pad's dark edge under the faded machines is gone from 200 m out, and the near view differs from main's by 4 pixels of 921,600. The change on screen is small because the pad is a few pixels tall at that range.

Left as it is: the torch boxes are still drawn at every distance, now in the end colour, and the pod's emissive layer stays unhazed (decision 2).

## 0382: the state hash on a thread, alone or not

Tags: performance, sixty-fps, lockstep, multiplayer, threads, review, 0382, 0345

The user, on reading that 0345 spared only the lone host: "I don't want there to be a difference on single player and multi player except network effects", and of the two ways open, the thread. A fork of the process was weighed and left: Windows has none, so a session of mixed machines would pace differently, which is the difference the item removes.

The design corrected the item's premise. The 30 ms is the cold hash, the first after a load, a join, a teleport or a walk into new ground, nearly all of it FNV over about 31 MB of field chunks never hashed. At a view already hashed the night table has 1.1 to 2.3 ms, mostly the encode of the simulation state, which stays on the frame. So the estimate is a cold frame of 4 to 5 ms instead of 30 and a warm one unchanged, now paid by the lone host too (decision 3). No night table has measured it yet.

What keeps the copy small is the write-back: a stale chunk is copied once and flagged, and the thread's hash becomes its cached hash unless a write cleared the flag meanwhile. It rests on the rule the cache already had, that every tick write into a loaded chunk goes through `note_field_chunk_change`. The review listed every writer of the eight hashed planes against that rule and ran random writes, unloads and reloads around jobs in flight: every reported, cached and kept hash equalled its bytes' hash, and an early and a late write-back gave the same hash sequence. With the flag's clearing removed the tests failed.

The thread sanitizer's clean run shows little. The review planted a race between the hash thread and the ticks five ways and none was reported; this toolchain and test runner report only accesses that fall close together in time. Thread safety here rests on reading: the thread touches only the job's snapshot and result, the main thread fills the snapshot before the thread starts and reads it after the join.
