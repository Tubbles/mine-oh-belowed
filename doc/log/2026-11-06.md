## The water's look on the field (0294)

Tags: field, water, mesher, waves, streaming, presentation, 0294

The browser demos' look carried into the field's mesher: a body's radial plane, the thin sheet with its moving, resting and falling modes, the wall guard and a frame-side wave layer. The implementation left the specification in a few places, all weighed sound. Front water in a body's dry air counts as front for the look, following 0293's rule. At full resolution, an unowned dry sample beside still water takes the lower of its slab value and the owner's plane, or the sea's surface misses its own residual test by a factor of two. Waves spread only from a nonzero height, or two zero cells keep each other active forever. The first review found the look reading up to 3 samples past a chunk's border while every mark reached one, so a stream two samples from a border left a stale sheet in the neighbour; and wave remeshes taking the frame's whole submission cap ahead of unmeshed terrain, at 16 to 22 ms a gather in a debug build, forever under a steady stream mouth. The fix round made the look's marks reach 3 samples (terrain edits too), gave wave-only remeshes a flag of their own and 2 of the 8 submissions a frame after every unmeshed and edited node, and rewrote the gather over flat arrays with a per-row interior skip: a sea chunk went from 16.2 ms to 5.4 ms, a lake chunk from 7.1 to 2.8. `integer_square_root`, which planet generation and the simulation share, became an integer Newton's method seeded from the bit length; the second review compared it with the old bit-by-bit version on 6.2 billion inputs with no mismatch. The second review found the nearest wave nodes holding the 2 slots for good (a sixth node marked once waited over 200 frames) and an entry's reach taken over all its samples, marking about 36 times the nodes that changed: the pass now takes the oldest mark first (a mark waits at most the marked nodes over 2 frames), and the reach comes from the samples whose baked offset changed, with the previous baked byte kept per sample (288 KiB an entry, 64 entries at most). The dig events 0294 pushes were cleared by the water step before the waves read them; the fix is in 0295, which lands next, so between the two commits a dig makes no ripple.

## Water tools and pumps on the field (0295)

Tags: field, water, tools, pump, machine, tech-demo, 0295

The demo's last player verbs on the water: the two buckets and a pump that sinks or sources. Under bodies a write goes to the owner of the sample, the front, a body or the sea, so a pour into a lake raises its level at once and a drain lowers it; front water in a body's dry air counts as front, matching 0293's rule. Deviations weighed sound: the sink's test asserts 3000 L for 10 seconds, the honest figure of 300 litres a second, where the specification wrote 762 (the fill count); under teleport a single drain spot cannot empty a basin, since films at the minimum fill stop flowing; a drain or a sink takes nothing from an owned sample whose byte is 0, or aiming at a basin's dry air would drain the lake; the intake walk stops at the first ground sample, so a cave under the bed is never the intake; the three new items took placeholder icons because the atlas test wants one per item. The implementer found 0294's dig events cleared by the water step before the waves read them: the field's tick now clears them first and steps with `step_field_water_keeping_events`. It also found a shallow pour never settling under bodies, which became 0298. The review's fix round walked the intake up out of the ground on a slope (a pump on a slope steeper than 45 degrees read dry for good), fitted the pump's HUD line at 960 by 540 and the largest text, tested the sea's infinite give and take and a credit during a pending flood, and made the water bucket aimed into deep water credit the body or the sea a press, since the room-only rule poured nothing into a full lake. The pump over the spawn is refused by the pod's cabin cells (`Frame_Cell_Taken`), as for every bare-ground machine, now tested. The pump also places in the block world, reachable by cheats only, where it toggles and does nothing.

## A thin lake settles under bodies (0298)

Tags: field, water, bodies, front, 0298

Found by 0295's implementer: a pour of 20 samples on its test basin's floor never settled under bodies, about 50 samples awake for good and a body of volume 5. The cause was in 0293's front: its plan compared the tick-start fills on both sides, while the browser demo lowered the sender's level as it sent and the teleport model reads live bytes, either of which cannot form the cycle; 0293's specification dropped the demo's detail. The design prototyped three rules. The demo's sender-only remainder settled the pour in 164 ticks (184 at worst over 144 scattered pours); dividing the shares by the neighbour count settled it in 114 but slowed the streams and broke 0294's moving-stream mesh test; the projected fills on both sides settled it in 46 (53 at worst, teleport 71) and passed everything. The review measured further: a 4-sample pour settles in 25 ticks, a single 28-sample column (all front, never settled before) in 64; a falling cube and a dam break settle a few ticks slower than before (57 to 73, 61 to 70); 0293's trough stream runs 168 ticks against 157 and ends at the same volume. A front spring now pours about 8 percent less, its projected fill falling as it plans. A save holding front water loads unchanged and runs under the new rule. A one- or two-sample pour spreads into films that stay awake until they dry, under both models, by 0292's rule; that was decided not to change. The review's scenario of a drained lake joined to a second cave left empty bodies alive and a residue seeding every tick, both from 0293 and now 0299.
