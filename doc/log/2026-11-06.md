## The water's look on the field (0294)

Tags: field, water, mesher, waves, streaming, presentation, 0294

The browser demos' look carried into the field's mesher: a body's radial plane, the thin sheet with its moving, resting and falling modes, the wall guard and a frame-side wave layer. The implementation left the specification in a few places, all weighed sound. Front water in a body's dry air counts as front for the look, following 0293's rule. At full resolution, an unowned dry sample beside still water takes the lower of its slab value and the owner's plane, or the sea's surface misses its own residual test by a factor of two. Waves spread only from a nonzero height, or two zero cells keep each other active forever. The first review found the look reading up to 3 samples past a chunk's border while every mark reached one, so a stream two samples from a border left a stale sheet in the neighbour; and wave remeshes taking the frame's whole submission cap ahead of unmeshed terrain, at 16 to 22 ms a gather in a debug build, forever under a steady stream mouth. The fix round made the look's marks reach 3 samples (terrain edits too), gave wave-only remeshes a flag of their own and 2 of the 8 submissions a frame after every unmeshed and edited node, and rewrote the gather over flat arrays with a per-row interior skip: a sea chunk went from 16.2 ms to 5.4 ms, a lake chunk from 7.1 to 2.8. `integer_square_root`, which planet generation and the simulation share, became an integer Newton's method seeded from the bit length; the second review compared it with the old bit-by-bit version on 6.2 billion inputs with no mismatch. The second review found the nearest wave nodes holding the 2 slots for good (a sixth node marked once waited over 200 frames) and an entry's reach taken over all its samples, marking about 36 times the nodes that changed: the pass now takes the oldest mark first (a mark waits at most the marked nodes over 2 frames), and the reach comes from the samples whose baked offset changed, with the previous baked byte kept per sample (288 KiB an entry, 64 entries at most). The dig events 0294 pushes were cleared by the water step before the waves read them; the fix is in 0295, which lands next, so between the two commits a dig makes no ripple.

## Water tools and pumps on the field (0295)

Tags: field, water, tools, pump, machine, tech-demo, 0295

The demo's last player verbs on the water: the two buckets and a pump that sinks or sources. Under bodies a write goes to the owner of the sample, the front, a body or the sea, so a pour into a lake raises its level at once and a drain lowers it; front water in a body's dry air counts as front, matching 0293's rule. Deviations weighed sound: the sink's test asserts 3000 L for 10 seconds, the honest figure of 300 litres a second, where the specification wrote 762 (the fill count); under teleport a single drain spot cannot empty a basin, since films at the minimum fill stop flowing; a drain or a sink takes nothing from an owned sample whose byte is 0, or aiming at a basin's dry air would drain the lake; the intake walk stops at the first ground sample, so a cave under the bed is never the intake; the three new items took placeholder icons because the atlas test wants one per item. The implementer found 0294's dig events cleared by the water step before the waves read them: the field's tick now clears them first and steps with `step_field_water_keeping_events`. It also found a shallow pour never settling under bodies, which became 0298. The review's fix round walked the intake up out of the ground on a slope (a pump on a slope steeper than 45 degrees read dry for good), fitted the pump's HUD line at 960 by 540 and the largest text, tested the sea's infinite give and take and a credit during a pending flood, and made the water bucket aimed into deep water credit the body or the sea a press, since the room-only rule poured nothing into a full lake. ## A thin lake settles under bodies (0298)

Tags: field, water, bodies, front, 0298

Found by 0295's implementer: a pour of 20 samples on its test basin's floor never settled under bodies, about 50 samples awake for good and a body of volume 5. The cause was in 0293's front: its plan compared the tick-start fills on both sides, while the browser demo lowered the sender's level as it sent and the teleport model reads live bytes, either of which cannot form the cycle; 0293's specification dropped the demo's detail. The design prototyped three rules. The demo's sender-only remainder settled the pour in 164 ticks (184 at worst over 144 scattered pours); dividing the shares by the neighbour count settled it in 114 but slowed the streams and broke 0294's moving-stream mesh test; the projected fills on both sides settled it in 46 (53 at worst, teleport 71) and passed everything. The review measured further: a 4-sample pour settles in 25 ticks, a single 28-sample column (all front, never settled before) in 64; a falling cube and a dam break settle a few ticks slower than before (57 to 73, 61 to 70); 0293's trough stream runs 168 ticks against 157 and ends at the same volume. A front spring now pours about 8 percent less, its projected fill falling as it plans. A save holding front water loads unchanged and runs under the new rule. A one- or two-sample pour spreads into films that stay awake until they dry, under both models, by 0292's rule; that was decided not to change. The review's scenario of a drained lake joined to a second cave left empty bodies alive and a residue seeding every tick, both from 0293 and now 0299.

## Empty bodies die and a residue above a lake rests (0299)

Tags: field, water, bodies, flood, save, 0299

Found by 0298's review in one scenario: a drained lake joined to a second cave. Seven bodies stayed alive with no cells and volume 0, and a residue above the cave lake seeded a body every tick that merged at once into the lake (21 seeds and 21 merges a tick, forever). The design traced both. A seed walks away from its still sample, and its flood can stop at a saddle before reaching it, so the new body owns a few dry samples; the next ticks' absorption hands the front water in them to the lake below and leaves the body empty, while only `finish_water_body_flood` killed an empty body, and only its own. Four paths lose a last cell: another flood's claim, absorption, the sea's claim and a sample turned to ground; each now frees the body at its own site, its volume to the taker. The residue's seed merged the lake and stopped at the hole short of the sample, and the seeded front's absorption takes only owned water, so the sample stayed unowned and still and seeded again; the browser demo joins the drop to its new body, the rule the port left out. A body dies at its last cell, not at volume 0, so a basin pumped dry keeps its body and refills as it. The flood record's two new fields read as zero from an older record, with no remap: bodies saves are the tech demo's only. The scenario settles 1049 ticks after the carve. While the thin lake over the sill drains a seed comes about every 25 ticks, each taking its sample, which was accepted. The two caves end as two bodies joined under both their levels, which is 0300. The review found, as plausible and older than this item, that a frozen part can keep the owner tag of a body merged away, an id that can be reused; it went to 0300's design, since merges own it.

## Bodies joined under water merge (0300)

Tags: field, water, bodies, flood, merge, save, 0300

Found while designing 0299: two caves joined by a passage under both their levels ended as two bodies, so a pump in one would lower it alone. The design found two defects, each enough on its own. The weir merged only when the upper body's excess had drained into the dead band, a clause the browser demo's merge lacks; once the lower body also stood over the sill each body's spill lay in the other and the weirs traded both ways. And a flood treated its own released water beyond a saddle as its spill, so the next flood of a body spanning a saddle under its level cut the far side off to the front, undoing every merge across a saddle; that is where 0299's churn began, and in a variant of 0293's trough lakes it left 100 bodies and 93161 fill of front water after 3000 ticks. The demo hid the second only because its merge never gave the survivor the absorbed cells; its edit path has the rule followed here (internal saddles, a new flood when the level falls under one). A seed's flood that merges a lake keeps the flood's id: ids are internal and nothing keeps one across ticks but the frozen parts' tags, which follow the merge (the 0299 review's question, answered with a guard test). The review found the new pass able to merge or kill the pending flood's body, since a pending flood tags the samples it claimed with its own id and the guard went with the weir's clause; and a fed lake over its neighbour's sill draining through a lower outlet merging and splitting every few ticks (196 seeds and 29,448 flood visits in 500 ticks against main's 1 and 456): a body now merges only with a partner that spills no lower than it. A merge takes the absorbed body's lower seed only when its chunk is loaded. The three new record fields read as zero from an older record, by name, with no remap: bodies saves are the tech demo's only.

## Burning flakes shed by the heat shield (0290)

Tags: arrival, entry, plasma, flakes, portholes, presentation, 0290

The design followed the footage's dark tumbling plates, not its one frame sparks, which 0288 already draws. It assumed every flake is dead before the rest easing turns the pod, but in 0288's arrival the heat is still 0.85 when the easing starts, so flakes live into it and turn with the pod as the sheath does. The review found the porthole crossing test passing on one salt only (a median of 6 ticks at an air speed of 9 cells a second, 3 frames at 30 fps against the footage's 4 to 6); pooled over eight salts and with the air at 7 the median is 8 ticks over 52 runs. At 7 the slowest flakes reach only about 0.8 cells above the porthole when their life ends, so 1 crossing in 52 ends with the flake fading in view over its last 0.15 s; a longer shortest life would cure it but widens the slots a frame scans. A capture of the chair's porthole over the 120 ticks after the peak (1271), diffed against the same ticks with the rate at 0 (the sheath is deterministic, so the frames match pixel for pixel), showed no flake at the shipped 50 a second and three at 100, each a dark dot of about 3 pixels at 720p climbing the glass over about 10 frames, its streak lost against the sheath. The mechanism lands as specified; making the flakes read from the chair is 0303.
