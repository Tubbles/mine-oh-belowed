## Brushes and the item yield (0171)

Tags: field, brushes, yield, edit-queue, materials, tool-tier, level-of-detail, preview

Choices the work item left open, taken by the implementing agent:

- The volume of a sample's ground is the positive part of its density, 127 steps the whole sample. A dig credits what it takes off the positive part, a place debits what it adds, and a sample at zero or below is air (material Air, tint 0) as the generation makes it, so a place followed by a dig of the same samples returns exactly what it took. Counting the full range from -127 to 127 instead would let a place onto the generated surface's partly filled air samples cost less than the dig of the same samples yields. A place raises only air and ground of its own material, so placing stone into topsoil cannot turn topsoil into stone. The visible surface still moves without cost: a place raises air from -127 towards 0 for free (up to about a spacing outward over the brush) and a dig lowers near surface air for free; no items are created or lost, only the visible volume moves.
- The edit returns density steps per material (`Field_Edit_Result`), not cubic millimetres: the steps are exact integers at every spacing, and the simulation multiplies by the spacing cubed into the credit's unit (`FIELD_ITEM_VOLUME`, a cubic millimetre times 127).
- The edit queue lives on `Field_Simulation` (`field_mining.odin`), the field's share of the simulation, rather than on `Simulation_State`: the field world and its players are not on `Simulation_State` until the slice (0179), so a queue there would have no world to drain into. The planet preview drives `Field_Simulation`; 0179 moves it onto `Simulation_State`.
- The brushes are a `field_brushes` list in `data/game.sjson`, bounded like `field_view` and `field_player`, since they tune the hand tool like those tune the player. The material table is a new `data/materials.sjson` read with the configuration's strict keys; bedrock has an empty item and is never dug.
- A material's `id` is the generator's material name (`field_material_name`, moved from the presentation into the world so the simulation can read it).
- Placed samples take the tint of the targeted ground until items carry tints.
- The bury check (after the review) dry runs the place over the samples it would raise and refuses it when one lies within its trilinear support (a sphere of 7/4 spacing) of any field player's capsule; the first version's sphere of the brush plus half a sample was too small next to the feet and too wide elsewhere.
- The streaming does not submit a node again while its last mesh job is in flight (after the review): a held brush dirties its chunk every tick, and resubmitting each frame made every result stale, so the mesh only changed after the brush was released.
- Coarser levels of detail read the loaded chunks (`gather_coarse_field_grid` on the main thread, completed on the worker); a saturated loaded sample on the generation's side of the surface keeps the generation's coarse density so unedited slopes do not terrace, anything else is its density over the step. The coarser nodes over an edited chunk are marked to mesh again once their last job has landed.
- The preview's keys are the Mine and Place actions (mouse buttons and triggers), Rotate Building for the brush and Hotbar Next for the held material, so the gamepad works too. The preview's player carries an iron pickaxe.

## Viewports: the choices the item left open (0178)

Tags: split screen, viewports, input, rendering, lockstep, ui

Work item 0178 left these to the implementer, within the main agent's fixed choices:

- The per player halves of `Frame_Interaction` and `Frame_Presentation` moved into the viewport (`Viewport_Interaction`, `Viewport_Presentation`); the process wide halves (the SDL state, the fonts, the touch overlay, the title, the renderers, the atlases, the mixer) stay on `Frame_State` under the old names, since a GPU resource or the SDL subsystem exists once per process.
- Joining is Start, the Pause action, on a pad no viewport owns: Confirm is pressed constantly in play and would add players by accident, and Start is the convention of couch games. The first viewport takes a free pad at once, so one pad plays single player as before.
- Two players default to stacked: each keeps the full width of a 16:9 screen and so the horizontal field of view. Side by side is the setting's other value; its 8:9 halves scale the UI until the 16:9 layout fits their width (`viewport_ui_scale`), which the audit covers at 960 by 1080 and scale 0.5. At full scale 8172 audit problems showed that the screens do not fit an 8:9 layout.
- Split screen draws each viewport into a render texture of its size rather than a scissored part of the window: raylib's `BeginMode3D` takes its aspect from the current framebuffer, so the projection, the frustum culling and the first person hands follow the viewport with no hand built projection, and the UI lays out at the viewport's size unchanged. One viewport draws straight to the window as before, with no texture.
- Sounds play once a frame from the first viewport's cues and player (the listener): the world's cues fire alike in every viewport and would play several times. The other players' footsteps and digs are silent in split screen.
- The keyboard and the mouse belong to the first viewport, with no setting to move them; the touch overlay is off in split screen, as a phone is one remote player. The first viewport also takes the first free pad, so the keyboard and one pad are one player, not two; accepted in review. A free pad goes to a guest waiting for its lost pad first (on Start), so a replugged guest pad is never taken silently by a keyboard first viewport.
- Pausing (changed in review): offline, only the first viewport's pausing screen holds the ticks. A guest's pause menu and settings run with the world going on, as online, so one couch player in the settings does not freeze the others.
- A viewport whose player has no entry yet (its join tick has not run, or a joined machine waits for the host's answer) opens only the pause menu and the settings; a guest that leaves before the host's answer cancels it, and the answer's member leaves at once.
- A guest viewport leaves through its pause menu (Leave split screen, served after the draw since it frees the viewport's UI); its player's entry stays in the world and the next join takes it, as a remote player's does.

## The water field (0172)

Tags: field, water, sea, springs, rain, lockstep, level-of-detail, preview, shader

Choices the work item and the main agent's brief left open, taken by the implementing agent:

- Full is `FIELD_WATER_FULL` 254, a constant: levels and the mesher's density are counted in it. On an axis a sample's fill under 128 has no surface of its own; the shallowest water that shows there is half a sample.
- The water lives in parallel arrays on `Field_Chunk` (fill, still ticks, flow) and two bitsets (awake, source), addressed like the terrain, and the codec writes all but the flow (format 3). The still ticks are a byte per sample and saved, since whether a sample sleeps is state every machine and a joiner must agree on.
- A film is fill at or below the minimum that can neither fall nor rests on full water, so a lake's partial top layer over full water does not dry, but its shore samples at or below the minimum over ground are films and dry, which drains that layer slowly. A film stays awake until it has dried, since its drying is a change every dry ticks and would otherwise put it to sleep with fill left. The drying runs on the ticks that are multiples of `dry_ticks_per_step`, so no per sample timer is needed.
- A sea edge sample is any loaded sample with a face neighbour in a missing chunk that is sea, decided each tick it is awake rather than marked, since the loaded set moves with the players. After the review a missing sample is sea only where the generation makes it air (it then holds the sea's fill at its level); in ground it is a wall, so a tunnel dug below sea level to the loaded boundary does not fill from rock. No water leaves the simulated region.
- An arriving chunk wakes the wet face samples of the loaded chunks towards it whose sample across the face is air with room, so a river stopped by a missing chunk flows on and a settled sea meeting an arriving sea stays asleep (the first version woke every wet face sample, up to about 98,000 a frame). A leaving chunk wakes the air samples facing it below sea level short of full, which may now be sea edges. A dry sample wakes only where it can be a source (a spring, or air below sea level), and a sample that sleeps clears its still ticks, so a settled chunk equals its generation again.

### The fix round after the review

- The first rule's potential was the distance in samples floored, with the samples of one shell as equals. The review found the sea's mesh rippled by up to 0.9 samples off the pole. A settled lake at 30 degrees latitude also showed that a floored shell one sample thick is not connected face to face on a slope: water stayed in steps 0.3 samples high. The rule now compares levels: each sample's cell is as tall along the radial as the sample's cube reaches (|n.x| + |n.y| + |n.z| samples), the water fills it from the bottom, and neighbours move the fill that makes their levels equal. A digital plane that thick is connected face to face, so the lake settles level (its top surface meshes within 0.1 samples of one radius in the test). The mesher's density is the level over the sample's own distance, a signed distance, so the fine and coarse sea cross at one radius (`planet_sea_density` keeps the true sea radius) and the generated sea holds the sea's fill at its level, partly in the cells the sea level crosses.
- The levelling compares levels times 254 exactly and rounds to the nearest with a half rounded down. A floored level made a one fill move turn back and never settle; flooring the share instead made the surface tilt by over two fills a step. A settled surface may now tilt by up to half a fill per sample step across a basin; the 24 sample lake of the test settled within 19 fills, under a tenth of a sample.
- Water spread about seven samples a tick along +x, +y and +z and one along the negative axes, since a sample woken ahead of the one stepping ran in the same tick. Only the samples awake when the tick began run now, and each moves at most the fill it held then; copying the awake bits alone still let water received earlier in a tick move on in it.
- Rain per planet rather than per biome stays: the field has no biomes yet.
- The water mesh is meshed in the same job as the terrain from one grid, so the water never lags the terrain it lies on and one revision covers both. It has no skirts: the water's open border includes its faces against the ground, whose gradient points out of the ground, and the skirts stood up out of the water as fins in the screenshot; the strip between levels of detail stays open on water instead.
- The water pass keeps back face culling, so the water's faces against the ground (facing into the ground) are not drawn; the water is not seen from below yet. The colour and opacity are a presentation constant (`FIELD_WATER_COLOR`) through the field shader's `water_color` uniform, whose alpha of 0 is the terrain pass.
- `sea_level_metres` of `home` is -14 m rather than moving the start: with the default seed the pole lies about 9 m below the radius, so the start stays dry, and hollows below -14 m lie 70 to 500 m from the pole towards longitude -105. The preview's free and screenshot cameras now look that way (yaw 255), so the start and both screenshots show water.
- The spring of the slice is at latitude 88, longitude -120: about 280 m from the pole on the slope above those hollows, so its water runs into them. Latitude and longitude are whole degrees and turned into the fixed point sine at generation, so every machine finds the same sample. Rain is `rain_fill_per_minute` per planet, 2, read and bounded and applied by nothing until M15.
- A spring that reaches the sea raises the loaded part of the sea's surface slowly, since nothing drains above sea level; accepted until the slice ties the loaded set to the simulated chunks (0179).

## The field light (0173)

Tags: field, light, sky, lockstep, codec, level-of-detail, preview, shader, data

Choices the work item and the main agent's brief left open, taken by the implementing agent:

- The curve lives in `data/lighting.sjson`, not on the planet record: light is the same on every planet. Four bands of 64 levels with 24, 18, 12 and 6 per metre, the dark level 24, the torch at 192 and the lamp at 255. Multiples of 6 divide evenly at 333, 500 and 1000 mm, so the reach in metres is the same at every spacing: the torch reaches 10.7, 10.5 and 11 m along a corridor and lights every sample of an 8 m room but not a 20 m hall; the lamp reaches 21 m and lights a 16 m hall but not a 30 m one. The loader refuses losses that rise towards the bright bands: a brighter level spreading dimmer than a darker one would make the fill's result depend on its visiting order.
- The light is saved (codec format 4, two more run length planes), unlike the block light. The sky of an edited chunk depends on edits above it along the radial that need not be loaded when it loads, so it cannot be rebuilt from the chunk and its neighbours, and a lockstep joiner then holds the host's bytes without relighting over ticks the host already ran. A chunk where only the light differs from the generation is written whole.
- The march at generation was measured and dropped for the brief's fallback. Four surface chunks at the pole generate in about 170 ms (debug build); marching every air sample of them took 1.0 s at 1000 mm, 3.9 s at 500 mm and 12.7 s at 333 mm, 6 to 72 times the generation. The generation gives every air sample full sky (the generated terrain is a height field along the radial) and the march runs only under edits.
- An edit's shadow is gathered at the end of the drain (the edited sample if air, then the air samples along its radial inward to the first ground) and every sample in it marches on the final field of the tick, so the result does not depend on the order of the edits. The march and the shadow step a quarter spacing as the spring's climb does. Known limit: the shadow stops at the loaded chunks, so a chunk arriving later under an edit made above it keeps the generation's full sky until an edit beside it marches again.
- The block light and the sky light each have a removal and an addition queue; every removal runs before any addition. The budget is `steps_per_tick` 8192 nodes over both channels and `chunk_seeds_per_tick` 4 arrived chunks, from data.
- The vertex light is the mean of each channel over the cell's air corners, not all eight: ground holds no light, so the mean over all corners would halve every surface. The sky light rides in the vertex colour's alpha and the block light in the texture coordinate attribute, the one raylib attribute the field mesh left free. The water's mesh reads the terrain's light at the same corners. A coarse grid gives a generated air sample full sky and a generated ground sample none.
- The preview's torch key is L (unbound in `data/bindings.sjson`), toggling a torch at the air sample in front of the targeted ground. The screenshot pit is a sphere of radius 2.5 m, 3.3 m ahead and 1.5 m below the feet, with the camera 50 degrees down: farther away the rim hides the pit's floor. The screenshot now also waits for the light's queues and the remesh of the changed chunks; the first day shot was taken before the dug chunks had meshed again.
- The `Field_World.water_planet` generation also serves the sky march; a world without one (the tests' uniform worlds) skips the sky update.

### The fix round after the review (0173)

- The arrived chunks are a set taken in coordinate order, and a chunk that has left is dropped without spending the seed budget: the queue grew by every insert while the preview flew without ticking, and its duplicates and unloaded chunks spent the budget ahead of real arrivals.
- An arriving chunk's emitters and every other walk over a map that feeds the queues go in sorted order: Odin seeds a map's hash from its address, so the order differs between machines, and with the step budget the light bytes between ticks depend on the queue's order.
- The sky march goes on above the relief's shell through the loaded chunks and ends at the first missing one, so a roof placed above the shell shades the samples under it.
- The level of detail skirts hang only from the faces that border a coarser node or none. The flat brown flaps at the pit's rim in the first 0173 screenshots were skirts between two finest nodes: at a sharp convex rim across a chunk border the straight leg leaves the curved surface and stands in the dug air (a test digs the screenshot's pit across a node border and finds skirt vertices in the air with skirts on every face, none without the shared one). The mesh job carries the node's skirt faces, the streaming keeps them with the node's revision and meshes a node again when they change, so a neighbour that splits or merges opens no seam. The finer side of a level seam hangs the skirt, which covers the seam whichever side the coarser node is on (the 0169 seam test, now with only the fine node's skirt). The new screenshots show a few single pixel bright dots on the pit's walls that the first ones did not; they may be pinholes along the shared edges of nodes of one level, which the skirts covered before, but that is not verified.
- The lamp's level lives in `data/lighting.sjson` rather than its machine record until the slice (0179), and the loader requires the `torch` and `lamp` emitters. The decoder drops light in a ground sample.
- The screenshot's pit waits for the field round the player to stream: the dig skips chunks not loaded yet, and in some runs the chunks beyond the node border arrived undug after it, so part of the pit showed its old ground.

## Frames and placement (0174)

Tags: frames, foundations, occupant-index, placement, save, preview, m13

Choices the work item and the main agent's brief left to the implementer:

- The frame table and its occupant index ride on `Entities` (`World.entities.frames`) rather than as a field of `World` beside it: `entity_at`, `add_entity` and the neighbour lookups of the entity tick take `^Entities` only, and a pointer from the entities to a table on `World` would dangle when a `World` is returned by value, as every test world is. The world's own code (the raycast, the water) reads it through `world.entities.frames` with world procedures and never names an entity type, which lowers the world to simulation record by the raycast's and the water's references. The field simulation keeps an `Entities` of its own for its foundations until the slice (0179).
- The occupant handle packs the entity handle into a `u64` (kind in the top byte, index in the next 24 bits, generation in the low 32); an index at or past 2^24 asserts. The flags are set from the machine's kind when the entity is added: solid for all but belts and splitters (what the player walks into), blocks water for all but the hydro turbine, blocks light for foundations only (kept for the field light; the block light still treats occupied cells as air).
- The cell lattice: cell (x, y, z) spans x to x + 1 pitches from the origin, so the origin is the minimum corner of cell 0 and frame 0 with origin zero matches the block world's cells exactly. World to cell rounds down, which is the cell whose centre is nearest. A free foundation's frame is placed so cell 0 stands on the hit, centred over it.
- The planet's north is +y, the axis the preview starts over; at the poles the fallback is +z.
- `Entity_Common.frame` is tagged `save:"-"`, a tag the codec gained (`field_is_saved`), and the entities off frame 0 are saved in a later table with the foundation pool and the frame records. A world that never had a frame writes none of it, so a block world's bytes and state hash are unchanged and a save from before frames loads on frame 0. No log line marks that load: the header carries no version that tells an old save from a block only world of this build, so the line would print for every world that never had a frame (review, 0174). `Fluid_Machine.lets_water_through` stays in the struct for the same reason, though the water now reads the occupant flag.
- The foundation is machine kind `foundation` with its own pool, so foundations save, load and pick up as entities do; it has a 1 by 1 by 1 footprint, an item, a hand recipe of two stone bricks, and no model (the frame renderer draws it). The machines' footprints stay in cells, so they are half their block size on a 500 mm frame until 0179.
- Placement on the field is a place command queued on `Field_Simulation.placements` in the player's tick and drained after the brush edits at the end of the tick, the field's form of the queued writes, since `Field_Simulation` does not yet apply `Player_Command` (0179 merges them). Place with the foundation held reacts to the press, not the hold, so one press places one foundation.
- The field player collides with the frames' solid cells (review, 0174): each sphere is pushed out of the field and out of the nearest cell separately, so a sphere touching both the ground and a pad leaves both. A one cell pad is a step at every spacing because the step height in front of a solid cell is at least its pitch; at 1000 mm the field's step is one sample, so this changes nothing there, and at 333 and 500 mm it lifts the step to the pad. The step's and the mantle's move ahead (a radius and a sample, 1.3 m at 1000 mm) passed through a 0.5 m column in one jump, so the move is now checked for overlap every radius on the way (`field_capsule_path_clear`).
- A field placement into a frame that no longer exists is refused as `Unknown_Frame` rather than `Frame_Cell_Taken`.
- Not per frame yet: the fluid networks (pipes connect by cell across frames), the loose items and the entity tick's block query read frame 0 and the block world; the block placement and the belt drag place on frame 0 only. A frame emptied of cells keeps its record.
- Determinism: `simulation_state_hash` after the factory benchmark's ticks (2 minutes warm up and 1 measured, as `test_factory_benchmark`), by a throwaway test before and after the change: with the content tables of HEAD, size 1 `6712730d3d5fc666` and size 4 `d5bfd4fe996c48e4` before and after; with the new foundation item, recipe and machine in the data the hashes are size 1 `8aa957f6163b2e15` and size 4 `1fc7b5b949ef82f7`, since the statistics and research state are shaped by the content tables. The throwaway test is deleted.

## The slot transfers as player commands (0179, first part)

Tags: lockstep, player-command, inventory, slot-transfer, held-stack, drag, quick-move, multiplayer, m13

Choices the work item and the main agent's brief left to the implementer:

- The held stack was already simulation state: `Player.held` on the player in `Simulation_State.players`, written whole with the player by the save codec and covered by `simulation_state_hash`. Nothing moved; only its writes from the frame did.
- Ten commands, one per write kind, in `player_command_slots.odin`: `Slot_Primary_Command`, `Slot_Split_Command`, `Slot_Sort_Command`, `Distribute_Command`, `Return_Held_Command`, `Drop_Stack_Command`, `Quick_Move_Command`, `Transfer_Button_Command`, `Grid_Transfer_Command`, `Inserter_Hand_Command`. A slot is a `Slot_Command_Target`: `NO_ENTITY` and an inventory index, or the open machine's handle and its slot. The verbs the tick runs (quick moves, the transfer buttons, the grid transfers, the inserter hand, the machine slot filters) moved from `quick_transfer.odin` (ui cluster) to `inventory_transfer.odin` (simulation), so the simulation reaches no ui file for them.
- The check (`slot_command_stale`), the same pure procedure on both sides: the machine is the player's `open_machine` and still exists with its generation, a slot is in range, and the hand and the target slot hold the items the screen showed (`Held_Expectation`, item ids with `NO_ITEM` for empty and `ANY_ITEM` for what the screen cannot know: the hand of a drag whose pick up is on its way, which takes the pending pick up's item when it is known, and the target of a drag's drop). The review's first version compared empty against holding only, so a swap followed by Drop inside one window dropped the swapped stack; the item ids end that. The screen skips its own check while another slot command of the player is on its way (queued or unconfirmed), since its view is behind then; the tick decides.
- Sort carries its order (the non-empty slot indices in rank order, `sorted_slot_order`), because the ranks follow the display names and so the language; the tick refuses an order that no longer names exactly the non-empty slots (`sort_order_matches`), which would lose or double a stack.
- The pointer drag without prediction: a pick up on its way counts as held (`fills_hand`), so the drag keeps dragging until the tick shows the stack. The drop is a `Slot_Primary_Command` with `keeps_origin` and expecting a holding hand, followed by `Return_Held_Command`; the drag's origin is now the held stack's own (`Held_Stack.origin_slot`) taken by the tick, so `Slot_Drag.origin_slot` is gone. A closed slot screen queues one `Return_Held_Command` (`returns_hand`).
- One command per press: a frame whose A queues a primary command queues no split or sort with it, since those need the hand the A leaves.
- A quick move step, a transfer or a return that moves nothing is not refused (no toast), as before; only staleness is.
- Each command is relayed in the input record with its own tag (`Player_Command_Tag`, `session_network.odin`).
- Fix round after the review: a distribute leaves out the visited slots whose filter refuses the hand's item or that hold another item (`distribute_targets`, with `slot_takes_distribution` moved into the simulation), and is refused when none remain, so a malformed command or a recipe changed during the window cannot fill an output or fuel slot. `Distribute_Command` and `Slot_Split_Command` carry the expected items too. A return of the hand is queued only when `return_held_stack` would change it (`return_changes_hand`), so a full inventory does not put a command into every record. The bool fields of the commands need no extra check: the codec already refuses a byte other than 0 or 1 (`read_value`), which a test now covers.
- Known behaviour, accepted: a pointer drag whose pick up lifts nothing at the tick (the slot emptied in between) ends in a refused drop and its toast; online, a sort is refused when a slot of the grid fills during the latency window. Neither loses an item.

## The world settings and the planet presets (0179, second part)

Tags: world-settings, planet, radius-preset, world-file, generation-record, mode, keep-inventory, dig-rate, materials, new-world-screen, m13

Choices the work item and the main agent's brief left to the implementer:

- The relief octaves moved from a constant in `generation_planet.odin` into the planet record (`relief_octaves`, the shipped values unchanged), so a recorded world can generate from its own octaves: `make_planet_generation` copies them and `surface_relief` reads them off the generation. `MAXIMUM_RELIEF_METRES` (34) stays a constant as the bound the octaves' amplitudes add up to, since the generation's shortcuts, the level of detail and the light bound the relief by it.
- The record (`Planet_Generation_Record`) is what generation reads off a planet (the radius, the bedrock depth, the sea level, the springs, the octaves and the palette's length, since a tint is a hash modulo it) plus the gravity and the rotation. It is a top level field of `world.sjson`, not part of the settings, so the settings stay comparable values; a radius of zero marks a file written before it. No format version step: `world.sjson` has added fields without one before (the pad, the spacing), and a step would refuse every older save instead of loading it with the data's values.
- The session owns the world's planet (`Session.planet`, the data's record with the recorded values, its palette repeated or cut to the recorded length); `World.planet` and `World_Settings.planet_id` borrow it, so a data reload that copies the settings into a new simulation keeps valid pointers. A test that starts a session without planet data leaves the planet zero and writes no record.

## Runs on poles (0176)

- A pole endpoint's facing is the pole's facing or its reverse, whichever the chord is nearer, so a pole takes runs from either side; a run starts and ends on facings, and one run arrives at and one leaves each pole and belt end (no splits).
- The run tables (the pole pool, the poles' frames, the run pool) are written at the end of the frame tables by `write_frame_tables`, only when a pole or a run exists, so a world without them keeps its bytes and its state hash, and a save from 0174 loads with no poles and no runs without a version step. A run's control points are saved, its polyline and lengths derived again at load. A new pole faces the yaw step nearest the chord, so two new poles make a straight run.

The tools are `Field_Held_Tool` entries after the foundation in the held material cycle (which replaced `holding_foundation`), so the slice's hotbar (0179) can give them items.

- The preview's second pad lies 12 m from the first along the diagonal between its forward and its right (a yaw step), so the run crosses the view of a level shot instead of hiding behind the first pad, and the poles take that diagonal's yaw step exactly. ## Every game hosts, the LAN lists the games (0188)

Choices the item and the main agent's brief left to the implementer:

## Walkability over small steep ground (0203)

The ground used for the walk and the slide is the flatter of the feet's plane and the footprint's, not the footprint's alone, so a player standing at a drop's edge (the footprint tilting over the drop) keeps standing. ## The model pipeline (0204)

Tags: models, obj, blender, presentation, m14

Machine models may be Wavefront OBJ with an MTL file beside the voxel ones, read by the new leaf package `model_obj` and meshed flat shaded by `model_triangle_mesh.odin`; an .obj wins over a .vox of the same name. The export axes are Blender's default pair, forward `NEGATIVE_Z` and up `Y`: the machines are built front +X and Z up in Blender, and the exporter maps Blender +Y to the forward axis. ## The pod lies in an impact crater, no pad (0199)

Tags: field, pod, crater, generation, spawn, save-format, m14

- The crater is a term of the relief, not a list of edits at creation: `surface_relief` passes the shaped relief (`uncratered_relief`) through `crater_relief` with the distance from the home, so the bowl is smooth at every spacing and shows in every level's mesh with no creation cost. Edits on whole samples would step the bowl at the coarse spacing and never reach the distance meshes, which regenerate from the record.
- The profile, integer only: a flat floor out to `floor_radius_metres` at `depth_metres` below the home's uncratered relief, a smoothstep bowl to the crest at `radius_metres` meeting the relief plus `rim_metres`, the rim falling back over six times its height. The floor is clamped at `-MAXIMUM_RELIEF_METRES` and the result at `+MAXIMUM_RELIEF_METRES`, so the bound every shortcut reads holds. Shipped: 12, 3, 4 and 1 m (reach 18 m, steepest 37 degrees).
- The crater is recorded in the world record (`crater`, `crater_recorded`) like the home, so a data edit never reshapes a saved world; a record without it takes the data's with one log line. No format version step, no binary layout change.
- The pod stands on its own frame on the floor, no foundations. Players spawn in its cabin, the first `open_cells` box (`validate_pod_cabin`: on the floor row, 2 by 2 cells at least), facing the door. The pad's rules leave 0195 (the pick up exemption) and 0201.
- Behaviour change for old saves: their unedited chunks regenerate with the crater, so the old pad and the pod stand on their frame over the dug floor, about 3 m up; a chunk the old world changed keeps its saved terrain and meets the regenerated bowl at its border. The spawn stays in the cabin on the pad. The pad's foundations become ordinary foundations: those round the pod pick up and return a foundation each, those under it refuse with `Something_Stands_On_It` (main agent). `test_an_old_save_with_a_pad_still_loads` covers it.
- The benchmark's pad moves from 6 to 24 m ahead of the player, onto the generated surface beyond the crater's reach.

## The model kit and the machines before oil (0205)

Tags: models, obj, blender, kit, presentation, m14

The machines the player builds before oil are OBJ models now, nineteen scripts under `tools/models/machines/` on the kit of 0204: the seventeen of the item plus the stone cutting table and the stone cutter of 0196, which the main agent folded in. Their 25 voxel files are gone and `tools/make_placeholder_models.py` no longer writes them; the rest stay voxel models until 0206.

The palette gains `timber`, `timber_dark` and `stone` for the hand built pieces (the wooden chest, the crate, the small pole, the cutting table and the stone cutter), which the steel palette has no colour for, and `lamp_glow`, a warm white for a lamp's head, which is neither heat nor an electric indicator. `palette.MATERIALS` already is the palette table keyed by name, and a misspelt name is a `KeyError` at build time, so there is no second set of constants.

The seed rule: a detail row draws from `random.Random` seeded with the CRC-32 of `<model>:<salt>`, never Python's `hash`, which is salted per process. Each row has its own salt, so editing one row leaves every other row's draws unchanged.

An opening's walls keep the target's material, as the furnace's mouth does in brick, so `opening()` takes no material and a dark inside is a volume of its own. A boolean also lists the cutter's material on the result, which no face carries, so `join` now keeps only the materials a face uses: without it the cutter's `soot` reached the `.mtl` of every machine with an opening and counted against the budget of 8. 0204's two models are byte identical.

A pipe's corners are three arc points the kit computes (start of bend, midpoint, end of bend) on a POLY spline: a Bézier spline with automatic handles would bend the straight runs too. Blender 5.2's round bevel has 4 + 2 × resolution sides and its caps carry their own vertices, so the kit sets the resolution to (sides - 4) / 2 and checks the vertex count against sides × (points + 2).

The accents follow the role: chests, crate and splitter in logistics grey with yellow edges (the wooden chest and the crate in timber with steel straps); poles, lamp, switch, boiler and steam engine in power yellow with hazard black; the electric drill in mining ochre; the pumps, the tank and the wood gasifier in fluids teal; the lab in science white with cyan. The assemblers share the logistics colours and carry a cyan state strip (DESIGN.md names it). The wood gasifier and the stone cutter are crafting machines by kind but keep what they visibly are, a fluids maker and a stone saw bench with a fire. The stone cutter's record turns the blade about the game z axis, so it is a vertical table saw blade rising through a slot in the bench top; the record is the contract and no data changed.

The offshore pump's intake and strainer end at the footprint's bottom, since the loader refuses geometry below it, although the water may stand a cell lower: a known limit. The steam engine's pipe from the far steam port runs in 0.19 from the face before it rises, so the port is met and the rising run's radius stays inside the footprint.

## Trees on the planet (0197)

Tags: field, trees, generation, save, models, m14

- The trees are a list the generation makes, never stored: a standing tree is a pure function of the seed, the recorded planet and its key on two jittered lattices on the sphere (groves and trees); only the felled keys are state, 12 bytes a tree in the save and in the hash. A machine per tree was weighed against it: at about 4800 trees per km² a planet holds millions, and the frame loops (`raycast_frames`, the placement checks) try every frame, which thousands of tree frames would break.
- The model is a machine record of kind `tree` (`pine_tree`, no item, never an entity), so it loads, hot reloads, checks and previews through the 0204 and 0207 pipeline with no second loader. The kind is appended last, since kinds are saved by name.
- A standing trunk refuses a foundation, a snapped machine and a machine on bare ground (`Tree_In_The_Way`). Runs and torches are not checked against trunks in this pass; a later item if one is found in the way.
- Felling takes 6 s (`felling_milliseconds`): four logs at the block world's 1.5 s a log block, so wood comes at the block world's rate; the pick up's 0.6 s would make it nearly free.
- The clearing is at least the crater's reach (0199), so no tree stands on the crater floor, in the bowl or on the rim; the shipped 24 m against the 18 m reach.
- A tree whose ground is dug hollow on its axis half its trunk's height below its base is felled without yield in the edit's drain, as if it fell into the hole, so no tree floats and every machine agrees.
- The placement keys and the species count are recorded in `world.sjson` (`trees`, `trees_recorded`) as the relief is; a world file without them takes the data's with one log line. The felled table is appended to `entities.bin` after the wear table, no format version step.
- Behaviour change for old saves: trees now stand round an old world. Those whose trunk meets a frame of the save are felled once at load, without yield, with one log line ("save: written before trees (0197), ..."), so none grows through an old pad. The kit's planks are no longer the only wood.

## The landing pod: airlock, hatches, fixtures, sealed room (0198)

Tags: pod, hatch, sealed-room, fixtures, save, models, m14, m15

- The record's width is the front axis: "8 wide by 12 deep" is the player's sense, so the pod is `footprint = {width = 12, depth = 8, height = 8}`, 6 by 4 by 4 m at 500 mm, and `POD_ROTATION` puts the 12 along the frame's forward, the size 0199's crater floor was sized for. `MAXIMUM_FOOTPRINT_SIZE` rises from 9 to 12; only `validate_footprint` reads it.
- The hatches and fixtures are listed by the pod's record (`fixtures`: machine, cell, rotation), each its own entity on the pod's frame in cells the pod leaves to it (`machine_held_cells`), so every cell has one occupant whatever order the pools are rebuilt in. Data, not code, says where each stands.
- Four new kinds placed by the world: `hatch` (the foundations' pool, its state on `Foundation`), `locker` (the chests' pool, so every chest path works), `crafting_bench` (a crafting station with the `hand` maker) and `oxygen_generator` (a text panel). The bed stays solid pod cells.
- A closed hatch is solid; an open one has no solid cell, every row but the top an open cell and the top row neither, so the player walks under it and the aiming ray stops at it, which keeps it aimable to close. Interact toggles it through the power switch's path in `interact_on_field` (`toggle_hatch`), riding the lockstep input frame, with the hint Open or Close; no command or panel. Closing on a player's capsule is refused with no toast (a toast would need Interact counted in `field_refusal_is_news`, which would retell older refusals).
- The hatch's door is a part with a new motion kind `slide`, driven by the hatch's state and the tick of its last toggle (`hatch_open_fraction`), not the clock.
- The sealed room is derived, never saved: a flood fill from the pod box's side and top faces through non solid cells, rebuilt when a pod is placed, a hatch toggles and a world loads. The hatch states are saved and hashed, so every machine and every load agrees; saving the cells would be a second copy that could disagree. A room touching an oxygen generator has an unlimited supply until M15.
- Hatches start closed at a new world (0199's "open until 0200" is superseded): the room is sealed from tick 0 and the first player opens the inner hatch, then the outer. The quest rewards stay with the drop capsule; 0210 makes the locker the field's capsule.
- The cabin's first open box is the part past the bed's foot (x 5 to 7, z 1 to 6), so 0199's spawn rule (its floor's centre) puts new players on the hatches' centre line 0.75 m before the inner hatch, where a level eye meets it; the union of the open cells, and with it the fixtures, the sealed room and the model, is the same as with the cabin beside the bed first, whose centre lay on the doors' edge line. The tests that walk a script out of the pod open the hatches first.
- Behaviour change for old saves: a pod whose saved size is not its record's is replaced at load by the new pod and its fixtures on a frame of its own standing on the old floor's centre, facing the old frame's forward, with one log line; players whose feet lie in the new pod's box move to its cabin. Machines a player put round an old pod may now stand inside the larger hull. On a pre 0199 save with a pad, the new pod stands on the pad's top on its own frame and holds no pad foundation up, so every pad foundation picks up (0199's "one under the pod is refused" no longer holds). No format version step: `Foundation`'s two new fields are read by name and load zero from older saves.
- The pod is an OBJ model now (`tools/models/machines/pod.py`), with the hatch, the locker, the bench and the generator as models of their own; its cavities, door openings and pockets come from the record's open cells and fixture boxes. `pod.vox` and its builder in `tools/make_placeholder_models.py` are gone. The model check also looks for pod geometry inside a fixture box.

## The art direction: astro-industrial punk (0211)

Tags: art, models, booklet, design, m15

- The look of the machines is a juxtaposition the user named astro-industrial punk (user, 2026-10-03): "combining 1) clean spacey panels, LEDs, LCDs, bright blinking colors, intriguing buttons and levers. with 2) rough, hard, dirty materials and grime, pipes, bolts, glass, metal, stone, brown, dark muted colors and gray. Almost like a juxtaposition." Every machine carries both worlds.
- The ratio is the main theme of the technology progression: "the wood burning, coal and oil stages in the beginning slowly morphs into more cleanliness, futuristic droidy tech. But always a combination of the two, just it goes from 80/20 to 20/80 over the course of the game." The stone furnace sits at 80 rough to 20 clean, the electric mining drill near the middle, the fusion reactor at 20 to 80.
- How it was found: round one tried four directions on the stone furnace and the mining drill (A frontier foundry, B expedition kit, C monolith, D low-fi stylized; eight images, Nano Banana 2 through OpenRouter at about 10 cents each). The user chose B with punchier accents and A's dirt. Round two read that as soot and went too far ("the sootyness was taken too literally"); the dirt wanted is A's Victorian industrial grime, short of steampunk. Round three puts the spectrum on the furnace, the electric drill and the fusion reactor. The images stay under `work/art/`, untracked, until the booklet keeps the chosen ones.

## The pod's locker takes the quest rewards (0210)

Tags: quests, rewards, deliver, pod, locker, capsule, strings, save, field-session, m13

- The quests' reward target is derived, not saved: `Quest_State.capsule` stays the saved capsule handle, and `reward_target` is derived from the saved pools at a new field world and at every load (which also serves a joiner's snapshot and the content reload). Every machine derives it from the same pools, so the lockstep agrees, and the save layout, the format and `simulation_state_hash` stay as they were. When the target changes, the delivery baseline (`capsule_totals`) is taken from the new target, so what the locker already holds is not counted as delivered.
- "First" means index order: the first alive pod of the foundations' pool, the pod `field_pod_spawn` spawns players in, and the first alive locker of the chests' pool on its frame. The locker cannot change during play (pick up and the developer `remove` refuse it), so deriving at load is enough.
- The quest texts name the target through a `{target}` mark and a phrase key per target (`reward_target_capsule`, "the capsule on the landing pad", and `reward_target_locker`, "the locker in the pod"), not through the machines' name keys: "Drop capsule" and a title case locker label do not read mid sentence, and the capsule's phrase tells a new player where it stands. The toast has a key per target (`locker_stocked` beside `capsule_landed`), since "A capsule has landed" is a sentence of fiction, not a name. On a block world the three `mc_` lines that said "to the capsule" now say "to the capsule on the landing pad", one phrase per target.
- Behaviour change for an older field save (a 0198 build's): pending rewards now land in the locker, items already in the capsule stay there (no one time move, since no released save has the case and a move would need a saved flag), and a deliver objective in progress keeps its count, which the locker's contents now satisfy (the baseline is re-snapshotted from the locker, the delivered count is not); plates in the capsule no longer count toward it.
- Fallback: a field world whose pod has no locker (content without one, a data reload that drops it) keeps the capsule as the target and logs one line at that load or new world. Rewards that do not fit still wait and are never dropped.
- The locker's slots are bounded 8 (`CAPSULE_SLOT_COUNT`) to 48, so a content edit cannot ship a locker a delivery of eight full stacks never fits. The shipped locker keeps 16.
