## Brushes and the item yield (0171)

Tags: field, brushes, yield, edit-queue, materials, tool-tier, level-of-detail, preview

Choices the work item left open, taken by the implementing agent:

- The volume of a sample's ground is the positive part of its density, 127 steps the whole sample. A dig credits what it takes off the positive part, a place debits what it adds, and a sample at zero or below is air (material Air, tint 0) as the generation makes it, so a place followed by a dig of the same samples returns exactly what it took. Counting the full range from -127 to 127 instead would let a place onto the generated surface's partly filled air samples cost less than the dig of the same samples yields. A place raises only air and ground of its own material, so placing stone into topsoil cannot turn topsoil into stone. The visible surface still moves without cost: a place raises air from -127 towards 0 for free (up to about a spacing outward over the brush) and a dig lowers near surface air for free; no items are created or lost, only the visible volume moves.
- The edit returns density steps per material (`Field_Edit_Result`), not cubic millimetres: the steps are exact integers at every spacing, and the simulation multiplies by the spacing cubed into the credit's unit (`FIELD_ITEM_VOLUME`, a cubic millimetre times 127).
- The edit queue lives on `Field_Simulation` (`field_mining.odin`), the field's share of the simulation, rather than on `Simulation_State`: the field world and its players are not on `Simulation_State` until the slice (0179), so a queue there would have no world to drain into. The planet preview drives `Field_Simulation`; 0179 moves it onto `Simulation_State`.
- The brushes are a `field_brushes` list in `data/game.sjson`, bounded like `field_view` and `field_player`, since they tune the hand tool like those tune the player. The material table is a new `data/materials.sjson` read with the configuration's strict keys; bedrock has an empty item and is never dug.
- A material's `id` is the generator's material name (`field_material_name`, moved from the presentation into the world so the simulation can read it).
- Placed samples take the tint of the targeted ground until items carry tints.
- The bury check (after the review) dry runs the place over the samples it would raise and refuses it when one lies within its trilinear support (a sphere of 7/4 spacing) of any field player's capsule; the first version's sphere of the brush plus half a sample was too small next to the feet and too wide elsewhere.
- The streaming does not submit a node again while its last mesh job is in flight (after the review): a held brush dirties its chunk every tick, and resubmitting each frame made every result stale, so the mesh only changed after the brush was released.
- Coarser levels of detail read the loaded chunks (`gather_coarse_field_grid` on the main thread, completed on the worker); a saturated loaded sample on the generation's side of the surface keeps the generation's coarse density so unedited slopes do not terrace, anything else is its density over the step. The coarser nodes over an edited chunk are marked to mesh again once their last job has landed.
- The preview's keys are the Mine and Place actions (mouse buttons and triggers), Rotate Building for the brush and Hotbar Next for the held material, so the gamepad works too. The preview's player carries an iron pickaxe.

## Viewports: the choices the item left open (0178)

Tags: split screen, viewports, input, rendering, lockstep, ui

Work item 0178 left these to the implementer, within the main agent's fixed choices:

- The per player halves of `Frame_Interaction` and `Frame_Presentation` moved into the viewport (`Viewport_Interaction`, `Viewport_Presentation`); the process wide halves (the SDL state, the fonts, the touch overlay, the title, the renderers, the atlases, the mixer) stay on `Frame_State` under the old names, since a GPU resource or the SDL subsystem exists once per process.
- Joining is Start, the Pause action, on a pad no viewport owns: Confirm is pressed constantly in play and would add players by accident, and Start is the convention of couch games. The first viewport takes a free pad at once, so one pad plays single player as before.
- Two players default to stacked: each keeps the full width of a 16:9 screen and so the horizontal field of view. Side by side is the setting's other value; its 8:9 halves scale the UI until the 16:9 layout fits their width (`viewport_ui_scale`), which the audit covers at 960 by 1080 and scale 0.5. At full scale 8172 audit problems showed that the screens do not fit an 8:9 layout.
- Split screen draws each viewport into a render texture of its size rather than a scissored part of the window: raylib's `BeginMode3D` takes its aspect from the current framebuffer, so the projection, the frustum culling and the first person hands follow the viewport with no hand built projection, and the UI lays out at the viewport's size unchanged. One viewport draws straight to the window as before, with no texture.
- Sounds play once a frame from the first viewport's cues and player (the listener): the world's cues fire alike in every viewport and would play several times. The other players' footsteps and digs are silent in split screen.
- The keyboard and the mouse belong to the first viewport, with no setting to move them; the touch overlay is off in split screen, as a phone is one remote player. The first viewport also takes the first free pad, so the keyboard and one pad are one player, not two; accepted in review. A free pad goes to a guest waiting for its lost pad first (on Start), so a replugged guest pad is never taken silently by a keyboard first viewport.
- Pausing (changed in review): offline, only the first viewport's pausing screen holds the ticks. A guest's pause menu and settings run with the world going on, as online, so one couch player in the settings does not freeze the others.
- A viewport whose player has no entry yet (its join tick has not run, or a joined machine waits for the host's answer) opens only the pause menu and the settings; a guest that leaves before the host's answer cancels it, and the answer's member leaves at once.
- A guest viewport leaves through its pause menu (Leave split screen, served after the draw since it frees the viewport's UI); its player's entry stays in the world and the next join takes it, as a remote player's does.
