## 0349: the globe after the ground, a node culled by its meshes

Tags: performance, presentation, culling, shadows, review, 0349, 0348

The specification of 2026-11-11 was brought up to `main` before the implementation, since eight render items had landed over it. The one design change: two boxes a node, not one. The view's test reads a box round everything the node draws, the sun's casters a box round the terrain alone, because 0348's kept shadow map is keyed on the terrain's mesh: with one joined box a wave or a cover remesh could flip a caster at the edge of the casters' box and redraw the map for nothing, and the spring remeshes its water about every other frame.

## 0381: two nodes' shared vertex on the same pixel

Tags: presentation, terrain, precision, shaders, review, 0381, 0349, 0380

The item had the rounding in the shader's `mvp * vertexPosition`. The design placed it on the CPU: raylib's `DrawMesh` multiplies each node's translation by the camera's view in f32, and at 8 km from the planet's centre that product rounds differently per origin, 0.2 mm apart at the median between two nodes' copies of one vertex. About one pixel in thirty along a border then falls between the edges, the row of dots on a cave's dark floor.

Neither of the item's two ways works alone, so both went in. An eye relative origin alone is not exact at 333 mm, where a position in metres is already rounded; a shared grid alone does nothing while each node keeps its own matrix. Positions are stored in samples, exact in f32 at every spacing, each viewport's pass has an anchor sample near the eye, and the shader adds the node's whole sample offset before the one matrix of the pass.

What the exactness rests on: two copies of a shared vertex have equal exact sums, so IEEE rounding gives the same bits whatever the range. `invariant gl_Position` is there against a compiler that would regroup the sum, but GLSL promises invariance only for equal inputs, which two nodes' draws are not. Mesa keeps it. The phone's driver and Winlator's are not known to and are the user's to look at; a driver that regroups shows the cracks as before, not worse.

The review's probe: 12,396 shared vertices of 270 node pairs at the three spacings, at the poles and on a 16 km planet, none differing in clip position; the terrain moves against the other passes by at most 1.8 mm. The readers of positions that only a running game exercises at 333 mm (the terrain box, the shadow pass's scale, the uniforms) have no test there, so a build with the default spacing set to 333 mm was captured: ground, cover, trees, water and a cave sit together.

Captures in the cave by the spring: the dotted line across the floor on `main`, none after; the open ground beside it 3 pixels apart.

## 0362: the field session draws every machine

Tags: presentation, machines, field, rlgl, review, 0362, 0347, 0360

The item's list was partly wrong, which the design found. A pipe was never missing on a planet: field pipes are runs, drawn since 0176, and `place` refuses a cell pipe there. The splitter was missing and not named. A drill's bar is drawn only without a model, and every shipped drill has one, so on the field only its arrow was lost. The launch pad's tower and rocket are drawn beside its model, not as a fallback.

The seam: the missing kinds join `draw_entities`' pools, so the cull, the instancing, the light, the haze and the shadows of the machines already drawn take them with no second path, and every box on a frame goes under that frame's matrix through one pair of procedures. Ports and arrows left the body draws for a draw of their own, since they must never cast a shadow.

The launching rocket is the one translucent box. Inside `draw_entities`' flat region it would be flushed before the instanced machines and the trees and cut them out behind it, so the scene draws it after the players, without depth writes. On the block world it stays inline, and one thing changes there: a drill's arrow behind a launching rocket is now hidden by it instead of showing through.

A machine's animation phase now also hashes its frame. Machines on bare ground each have a frame of their own at the same cell, so engines and pumps would have turned in step. Every field machine's phase moves; nothing saves it.

What the tests cannot see: the bar's and the tower's tests check the boxes and the matrix, not the draw, since rlgl's matrix stack needs GL. The captures stand for it: the pad of 0362's layout, with the eleven fluid machines, the poles, the switch, the lamp, the splitter and the launch pad, shows each on its cell and the tower on the pad's corner, near and at 120 and 230 m. A drill was not captured.

The profile's `factory` view now draws its boiler, steam engine, poles and lamp, so its rows before and after this item are not comparable.

## 0368: cables, junction boxes and the poles' removal #power #controls #saves

Landed as step 1 of three: the cables as lines, the boxes as plain boxes. 0383 (batteries and the control unit on the netlist) and 0384 (the cable's look) follow, and the two boxes' models are sealed lab items (0387, 0388).

The netlist never sees a position: its topology is the cable table and the switches, and a cable stores only its whole metres. That is why a cable is a table of its own and not fields on the pools, and why the capacities are derived to watts when a cable is laid and at load.

It landed on 0355's solve. 0373 was meant to go first and is still in design after its first implementation stopped at its own measurement; until it lands, spare power can idle on one side of a limiting cable between two sources.

Old saves: the poles are deleted at load through `deleted_ids`, and the factory is unpowered until cables are run (the user's decision of 2026-11-11, nothing softens it). The unpowered statistic changes meaning with it: a consumer is unpowered when its tree has no generator and no store, and a consumer with no cable is a tree alone.

The review found the two control faults the design missed, both from a control that carries two actions. EAST is Sneak in the world and Back in menus and the contexts do not filter the action sets, so "Back ends a run" made a crouch end it: Back was dropped for cable runs (belt runs keep it, and have the same fault). On touch a tap on a thing with a panel presses the open control, and every powered thing has a panel, so no run could begin on the phone: with a cable held the tap presses Place. The price is that a touch player with a cable selected cannot open a panel or turn a switch by tap.

At load a cable longer than 100 m is cut to 100 m, and a cable on a machine a later build deletes is dropped with it instead of refusing the save, so the next deleted machine that carried cables does not strand its saves.

Not captured: a run carried in first person within and past its span (the captures are of laid cables, by the `cable` command, at noon and at night from 5, 20 and 60 m). The captures showed a fault older than this item: foundations draw at their noon brightness at night (0386).

## 0276: the screens' button layer #controls #ui

The item was split at its design: the input layer landed here, the window carousel with its title row is 0385. The split keeps `main` playable between the two: the triggers step the old inventory strip until the carousel replaces it.

The user's answers of 2026-11-13 corrected the item's first wording. B pops one level of the screen stack towards the world, it never closed every screen (that sentence was the main agent's, and `doc/inspiration.md` carried it as the user's note). Touch gets a third Craft max button, no held fan. Y stays the info panel. Power becomes a window of its own (0385).

Retired actions in an old configuration are skipped with a log line and not remapped: `Menu_Secondary` and `Menu_Quick_Move` were presses on the triggers, their successors are held modifiers on the bumpers, and a remap would bind a modifier to a control that now steps windows.

With a stack in hand the modifiers are ignored, so R1 or L1 held while placing never turns a place into a move. The hand is read from the simulation, so online a pick up still on its way to the tick reads as an empty hand for the modifiers while the drag code already counts it (`pending_hand_item`); the two agree again when the tick arrives.

The review's flick findings changed the specification's pure `right_stick_tab_step`: it carries an armed flag (`Viewport_Interaction.tab_flick_armed`) and re-arms inside 0.3, and on the map a flick's frame does not also zoom by the stick's vertical part.
