## 0381: two nodes' shared vertex on the same pixel

Tags: presentation, terrain, precision, shaders, review, 0381, 0349, 0380

The item had the rounding in the shader's `mvp * vertexPosition`. The design placed it on the CPU: raylib's `DrawMesh` multiplies each node's translation by the camera's view in f32, and at 8 km from the planet's centre that product rounds differently per origin, 0.2 mm apart at the median between two nodes' copies of one vertex. About one pixel in thirty along a border then falls between the edges, the row of dots on a cave's dark floor.

Neither of the item's two ways works alone, so both went in. An eye relative origin alone is not exact at 333 mm, where a position in metres is already rounded; a shared grid alone does nothing while each node keeps its own matrix. Positions are stored in samples, exact in f32 at every spacing, each viewport's pass has an anchor sample near the eye, and the shader adds the node's whole sample offset before the one matrix of the pass.

What the exactness rests on: two copies of a shared vertex have equal exact sums, so IEEE rounding gives the same bits whatever the range. `invariant gl_Position` is there against a compiler that would regroup the sum, but GLSL promises invariance only for equal inputs, which two nodes' draws are not. Mesa keeps it. The phone's driver and Winlator's are not known to and are the user's to look at; a driver that regroups shows the cracks as before, not worse.

The review's probe: 12,396 shared vertices of 270 node pairs at the three spacings, at the poles and on a 16 km planet, none differing in clip position; the terrain moves against the other passes by at most 1.8 mm. The readers of positions that only a running game exercises at 333 mm (the terrain box, the shadow pass's scale, the uniforms) have no test there, so a build with the default spacing set to 333 mm was captured: ground, cover, trees, water and a cave sit together.

Captures in the cave by the spring: the dotted line across the floor on `main`, none after; the open ground beside it 3 pixels apart.

## 0368: cables, junction boxes and the poles' removal #power #controls #saves

Landed as step 1 of three: the cables as lines, the boxes as plain boxes. 0383 (batteries and the control unit on the netlist) and 0384 (the cable's look) follow, and the two boxes' models are sealed lab items (0387, 0388).

The netlist never sees a position: its topology is the cable table and the switches, and a cable stores only its whole metres. That is why a cable is a table of its own and not fields on the pools, and why the capacities are derived to watts when a cable is laid and at load.

It landed on 0355's solve. 0373 was meant to go first and is still in design after its first implementation stopped at its own measurement; until it lands, spare power can idle on one side of a limiting cable between two sources.

Old saves: the poles are deleted at load through `deleted_ids`, and the factory is unpowered until cables are run (the user's decision of 2026-11-11, nothing softens it). The unpowered statistic changes meaning with it: a consumer is unpowered when its tree has no generator and no store, and a consumer with no cable is a tree alone.

The review found the two control faults the design missed, both from a control that carries two actions. EAST is Sneak in the world and Back in menus and the contexts do not filter the action sets, so "Back ends a run" made a crouch end it: Back was dropped for cable runs (belt runs keep it, and have the same fault). On touch a tap on a thing with a panel presses the open control, and every powered thing has a panel, so no run could begin on the phone: with a cable held the tap presses Place. The price is that a touch player with a cable selected cannot open a panel or turn a switch by tap.

At load a cable longer than 100 m is cut to 100 m, and a cable on a machine a later build deletes is dropped with it instead of refusing the save, so the next deleted machine that carried cables does not strand its saves.

Not captured: a run carried in first person within and past its span (the captures are of laid cables, by the `cable` command, at noon and at night from 5, 20 and 60 m). The captures showed a fault older than this item: foundations draw at their noon brightness at night (0386).

## 0384: the cable's look #power #presentation

Step 3 of 0368. The specification asked for boxes within 32 m and lines beyond, the cut blind to the thickness. The review's arithmetic showed what that does without multisampling: a light cable's box is under a pixel from about 9 m, a heavy one's from 18 m, so most near cables would have drawn as crawling dashes. The line now stays under every box and the 32 m cut only decides who gets boxes. A cut that scales with the thickness was the alternative and would have saved the instances of sub pixel boxes; the line was the smaller change and has no pop.

The instance matrices are in absolute metres, like the machines' and the belt items', not from 0381's camera anchor: the joints stay within a millimetre at 16 km from the centre.

Not measured: the frame cost of hundreds of near cables (a debug build put 760 heavy cables at about 7 ms of CPU a frame, which overstates it), and the factory view's row with its cables, both for the night window. Not captured: a carried run in first person, where a trunk is a bar tens of pixels wide at the hand.

## 0383: batteries and the control unit on the netlist #power #vessels #saves

Step 2 of 0368. A battery is a generator flagged as a store with the last dispatch order, so the solve needed no new kind of node: it offers what it holds up to its rate, takes what its room and rate allow, and a store that gave in a tick gets no charge room in it.

The specification's test could not be built as written: the docking connector had one power port and a port takes one cable. The implementer appended a second (`power_inner`), which the main agent accepted, since without it no ground power reaches a battery in play either. 0356 meets the same with the connector's one fluid port.

The review found the batteries' own arithmetic sound (no joule made or lost over the probes, a save in the middle of a run hashing equal to an unsaved run) and one fault in what they sit on: the solve's repair ignores the dispatch order when a generator is held back by its own cable, so a battery can drain beside a generator with spare. That is 0389, after 0373 in the same files, and `doc/fluids.md` says so until then.

Two decisions after the review. A network of vessel parts only raises neither the unpowered count nor the brownout warning (the overview still colours it), because every new vessel starts with empty batteries. An empty battery remains a source for the unpowered rule, 0368's wording, so on a docked vessel's network a starved lamp reads as brownout and not as unpowered; whether an empty store should stop counting is open. And the overview lists batteries on a line of their own.

## 0373: the netlist's spare power to its poorest region #power #netlist

The item went through design three times. The 2026-11-11 charge fixed the region fed from above and stopped at its own gate with 130 offenders left, every one a feeder whose poorer parent took less than it was charged. A feeders first return was specified and withdrawn. What landed is one pool: every spare joule goes a level at a time to the poorest region that can reach it, the fed regions' fills computed bottom up and the roots' slack handed out top down, capped. The designer's Python model of it (untracked, `tmp/0373_pool_model`) agreed with the Odin solve on the one seed the two were compared on.

What the tests can and cannot see decided two findings. The reference shares the solver's top down procedures, so it found nothing wrong with a feeder that sat at its parent's level and never took power in; the reviewer's independent check from the output alone did (5 of 175,000 netlists), and its shape, thin cable twins and chains, is now in the definition test. Twice the reference was the one corrected: a cable of capacity 0 acted as a feeder with a ceiling, and a feeding edge at equal levels was not open both ways.

Accepted and recorded in the item, not fixed: the fill charges a parent for a fed region's level the parent cannot fund (2 of 175,000), and on chains of cables of a few joules the last joules go to another region than the reference picks (about 1 in 100 of those). Real cables carry thousands of joules a tick.

The cost was decided on a wrong bound. The design said cubic, the review counted quartic in the depth of nested fed regions with cheap feeders. It landed all the same, since it removes idle power today and today's netlists are small, with 0390 (the replay) next in the same file and the nested chain cases in the night's cost test. 0389 (the repair's dispatch order) follows there too.

A region with no demand reports a level between its neighbours'. The HUD, the overview and the brownout flag never read it; an idle machine reads only whether it is 0.

## 0277: the crafting view #controls #ui

The control questions the item left open were settled by the main agent before the design: X on the schematics half clears the queue (the user's Techtonica note), "Unlocked only" is a toggle the focus reaches and not a tab, C opens the view on the schematics half. The design then proposed X clearing on the gamepad while the keyboard's F cancelled the last craft, both being `Context_Action`; that was refused, since a control's meaning never follows the device, so the action clears on every device and Cancel last keeps only its touch button in this view.

The tabs are our own categories through a `crafting_tab` key (Machines, Logistics, Power, Materials, Tools), not Techtonica's five. A base building tab would need a new recipe category.

The review's main finding was in the toolkit and older than the view: widgets inside a scroll region registered their whole rectangle though the draw clipped them, so anything interactive above or below a scrolled region could lose its press to a hidden row. At UI scale 1.5 the view is narrow enough that the specification's "the toggle and the summary fit their text" did not hold; the tabs go to icons alone and the header stacks. The audit now checks those texts whole in the view's cases, which before it did only for touch rows.

## 0390: the solve's fed regions replayed #power #netlist #performance

The design was made on a Python port of the pool that reproduced the 0373 review's visit counts exactly before anything was changed, and the Odin solve's counts after the change equal that model's to the visit. Two changes went in together because the model showed either alone leaves a cubic term on the nested chain: the replay of a fed region's recorded run, and the reach of a position computed once a pass.

The reviewer cut the replay one level early as a mutation and the output did not change, on 88,000 netlists: the enclosing greedy buys the cut step back. So the exact cut is not what the equality test guards; a step too many, a missing cap and a memo kept across passes each failed it by the hundred.

What is left is cubic and is 0373's own fill, not repetition: a chain whose every fed region has a feeder of a joule a level and a cable with spare room (16.8 million visits at 641 nodes). Accepted until the night's times or a save ask for more.

The solve reads below its level table on a negative demand. The game's power is unsigned and cannot produce one; the guard is a suggestion.

## 0369: cable loads and the limiting mark #power #netlist #ui

What "limiting" means was the whole item. The first rule (full, short behind, spare before) marked about one cable in five wrongly once 0373's pool decided the levels, and went back to design. The second measured what raising one cable alone would gain and was right on every factory shaped netlist, and silent in the case a player meets first: two full cables in series, where raising either alone gains nothing. So the meaning was widened by the main agent to a cable together with the other full cables on its power's path, and every cable of a starved series is marked.

The implementer's rule and its test reference then came from one hand, and an independent reference found what that test could not: a mark whose series partner delivers the whole gain. The reference now compares the path with and without the mark. What it finds is accepted and documented instead of fixed with a fourth rule: a full generator cable marked beside the truly limiting cable when a later generator with spare sits inside what it feeds, 1 mark in 942 on factory shapes, and never without the right mark beside it. The cause is known (spare is measured at the fed end, not where the shortage enters) and is in `SUGGESTIONS.md`.

The limits read a solve's outputs and never its inside, which is why the item could be designed while 0373 rewrote the solve, and why 0389 only moves its values.

## 0356: the pipes' netlist #fluids #netlist #saves

The user's 2026-11-11 decision (pipes as a netlist like power, no gravity in a pipe) carried out. What stops working as before is in the commit message and `doc/fluids.md`; the decisions the item did not foresee are here.

Whole litres. The solve works in per mille levels, which on a 20 L joint feeding 21 consumers of 1 L each move nothing. A top-up after the solve hands out what the levels leave, a dispatch order at a time, within the phase's bounds; it is a parameter of `solve` that power does not set.

The flare. As a consumer of the last order it shared the leftover with the tanks. So it is a relief valve by a rule on the fluid side: it takes nothing while a tank on its netlist has room. The review found the rule's edge (a tank whose joint the through flow saturates keeps the flare shut though it cannot take the leftover) and it was accepted: it needs 20 L a tick through one tank joint.

Two producers. With full output and a small demand the first in order serves and the second stands by, yellow, where the old model let both run at half. The factory benchmark read that as a stall, so its idle rule excuses producers at Output full.

Who names a netlist's fluid took three rules. The design let the first open port holding litres decide, inlets included, and an unpowered pump's inlet with 5 L of water stopped a gas line for good. Preferring open feeders fixed the case with a tank and not a tankless line, where the producer's outlet, once closed, ranked under the inlet for ever. What landed: any feeder holding litres outranks every inlet. `test_a_netlist_keeps_its_fluid_while_an_open_port_holds_it` changed with it: a closed steam outlet now takes a water line when no water feeder holds any.

The band. The specification drew a pipe's band while fluid passed in the last tick, which blinked at 10 Hz on a tar pit line (1 L every 6 ticks). It now shows the netlist's fluid, steady, so it no longer tells a running line from a stalled one; the panel's litres over the last second do. Machine ports' flow lines still show the last tick.

## 0389: the repair in dispatch order #power #netlist

The item's own Change line (the shortfall to the rooms of the lowest order first) could not be built as written: the design found a misorder inside a subtree whose root shows no difference at all, so a room per child cannot see it. Each subtree carries a range per order instead, which makes the repair the tree's min cost flow with the order as the cost. One order stays the old repair to the joule, which is why no game test's number moved.

The reviewer checked the rule against every feasible dispatch by enumeration, with a checker that found the old repair's misorders by the thousand, so its silence on the new one means something.

Within one order nothing is ranked: stores share a forced part in proportion and no store is drained first. A battery's net change can fall by a joule or two where several stores sit behind one cable, from the per mille levels, which is older than this item.

## 0376: the field's offshore pump needs water #fluids #field #saves

The item named two pumps and one landed. The field generates no tar, so the design's honest rule for the tar pit pump was a refusal on every planet, which would have ended the oil chapter on the field; the main agent kept that pump as it is and wrote 0391 for the pits.

## 0216: the pipette #controls #hud

Written on 2026-10-04 for a hotbar that held stacks: select the slot holding the aimed item, or move a stack in. After 0278 a slot names a kind, so the item was rewritten by the main agent before its design: select the slot naming the kind, else name it on the selected slot, whether or not the player holds any. Nothing moves, and a grey slot says what to craft.

The design made it a tick action and no command. A command built on the frame would read a selection that lags the lockstep window; the tick reads the aim the simulation holds for that player, which is also what the HUD's hint reads, so the hint and the tap agree.

The hint cost a fix round and a decision: what the held item does outranks the pipette. "Pipette" is wider than "Tools", so even in the Tools hint's own place it pushed Turn, Pause or Sprint out at some sizes. The bar is now laid out twice and the pipette's version kept only where both draw the same hints, so at the largest text the tap works unannounced.
