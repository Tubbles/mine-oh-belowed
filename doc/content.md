# Content catalogue

The rules behind the numbers in `data/*.sjson`. Every value lives in its data file, and each file's header comment states the keys, their ranges and the local rules. This document keeps what spans files: units, the stack and price classes, the phase budgets, the gates, the ratio checks, and the authoring rules for textures, models, sounds and text.

- A value is changed in its data file, never copied here. A ratio check below is recomputed when a value it names changes.
- Every number is a guess until the couch tests confirm it. When a phase runs long, lower the cost, not the step count.
- The fluid and power mechanisms are in [fluids.md](fluids.md), belts, inserters and drills in [logistics.md](logistics.md).

## Units

- A tick is a sixtieth of a second (`tick_rate` in `data/game.sjson`). A recipe's `seconds` is its time at speed 1 and a whole number of ticks. `recipe_ticks` truncates to whole ticks, at least one.
- Power in kilowatts and megawatts, energy in megajoules. An item's `fuel_megajoules` is what a machine extracts, with no efficiency factor, except a combustion generator's `fuel_efficiency_percent`.
- Fluids in litres. Fluids are never items and never stack.
- Terrain in cubic metres: one item of a field material is a cubic metre dug or placed whatever the sample spacing (Field materials and brushes).
- Factorio's proportions are the anchor, its absolute values are not: where a ratio matches Factorio it does so on purpose.

## Items

`data/items.sjson`.

- Nothing stacks below 50 (user, 2026-10-01), so a pocket holds a useful amount of anything. Classes: raw material, ore, spoils, blocks, plates, alloys, steel, crushed ore, alumina, sulfur and bitumen 50, slag 100, the other intermediates (planks, gears, wire, circuits, glass, silicon, plastic, science packs) 100, machines, tools, rocket parts (the stack also caps a launch pad slot, and a rocket takes at most 10), thumper charges and schematics 50, the machines placed by the dozen (inserters, belts with ramps and lifts, poles, pipes, lamps) 100. `item_test.odin` pins a plate, a gear and a machine.
- Mining a block yields exactly one item: the item whose `places_block` it is, or whose `mined_from` lists it. `also_mined_from` adds a second drop (gold quartz gives quartz and gold ore). `mined_from` serves the block world; on the field, `log` comes from the trees by their species' `item` (Trees).
- Prices in venture credit grow with recipe depth. The classes are in the header of `data/items.sjson`.
- `configurable` (optional, 0202) names what the inventory view's configure pop-up sets for every item of the kind ([ui.md](ui.md), Configure pop-up); the pop-up's content is chosen in Odin by the value (`Item_Configuration`). The one value is `foundation_block` (the three foundation items share one block size and height per player, Foundations); any other refuses the file, naming the item.
- `former_ids` (optional, 0196, on items, machines and recipes) lists the ids a record had in an older build: a save naming one loads it as the record, with one log line ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Save format). A former id that is also a current id of its table, or one listed twice in a table, refuses the content load (`content_former_id_problem`). The stone brick foundation lists `foundation`.

### Tools

Tools gate hand mining and nothing else (0051).

- Each block needs a tool tier that climbs with the hardness of the material, from bare hands for soil and wood to an iron pickaxe for deep rock. The tier list is in the header of `data/blocks.sjson`.
- The best `tool_tier` of any item in the inventory or on the cursor counts (`player_tool_tier`), so the geologist's hammer mines at tier 2. There is no speed bonus and no durability. The time is the block's `hardness_seconds`.
- Drills ignore tiers, and the developer cheat speed mines every tier (`effective_tool_tier`). `test_tool_tier_gates_hand_mining` guards the gate.
- On the terrain field a tool digs or fells only while selected on the hotbar (0265): an item's `tool_role` (`shovel`, `pickaxe` or `axe`, tools with `tool_tier` 1 or more) meets a material's `dug_with` and `tool_tier`, and the hand or any other item digs nothing. The block world still counts the best `tool_tier` carried, a shovel's and an axe's included.
- The water tech demo's buckets (0295): the roles `water_bucket` and `drain_bucket` (items `water_bucket` and `drain_bucket`, tier 1) are field tools of the water, not digging roles: no material's `dug_with` may name them (`field_material_record`), and Place with one held pours or takes water where it is aimed (a pour into water deeper than the brush raises the lake's volume by the press, or goes to the sea) ([input.md](input.md), the buckets; [architecture.md](architecture.md), The water field, Tools and pumps). The demo's kit carries one of each and ten water pumps before the torch (`tech_demo_items`).

## Recipes

`data/recipes.sjson`. Its header gives the channels (start, discovery, research, quest, schematic), the byproduct rule and the furnace limits.

- Craft times by kind: machines 2 s, inserters, belts, pipes, poles and chests 0.5 s, tools 1 s.
- A furnace makes one input recipes with at most a slag byproduct. Two input alloys (steel, bronze, brass) are alloy furnace recipes. Every ore smelt and alloy craft gives one slag.
- Low grade ore goes back to ore through the crusher and the washer (the chain is in the header of `data/recipes.sjson`). It smelts directly only through the low grade iron plate schematic.
- Crushers, washers, the alloy furnace, the stone cutter and the assembler, among them, are one kind: `crafting_machine` (`data/machines.sjson`). A crafting station (the stone cutting table, Stone cutting) is a maker too: its recipes are crafted by hand at it.
- The recycler reverses the first recipe that makes its input and returns 25 percent of each input, rounded down.
- The slice's chain (0179, 0196): the field yields dirt, stone, deep stone, hematite, chalcopyrite and coal (Field materials and brushes) and the trees give logs (0197, Trees); the starter kit adds wooden foundations and no planks (0265). Everything chapter 1 and the first line need is made from those by hand, in the stone furnace and at the stone cutting table: the wooden shovel, pickaxe and axe and the stone cutting table from the logs, the stone pickaxe and shovel, the stone furnace (5 stone), plates in it, the stone cutting table (4 planks, 4 stone), stone bricks cut at it, the three foundations, the belt pole, gears, the burner mining drill, the burner inserter, the belt, the iron chest and the stone cutter. The torch is made by hand from a coal and a stone (four torches). `test_the_slice_recipe_chain_is_reachable_from_the_fields_yield` walks the recipes from the materials' items, the trees' log and the kit, and asserts the logs make planks, wooden foundations, the stone cutting table and the wooden shovel, pickaxe and axe.

### Hand crafting

`crafting.odin` (0138). The hand crafts at speed 1, one run at a time. The HUD shows the queue ([hud.md](hud.md)).

- Queuing takes nothing. It plans the crafts against a virtual inventory: the inventory plus what the queued runs make minus what they use.
- An ingredient short by M gets ceil(M / output count) crafts of its hand recipe queued ahead: the first unlocked hand recipe in `data/recipes.sjson` order whose first product is the item. The resolution recurses up to `HAND_CRAFT_PLAN_DEPTH` levels.
- A crafting station (0196, Stone cutting) adds its `recipe_maker` to the hand's while its panel is open (`player_craft_makers`): a `Craft_Command` for a recipe of that maker is accepted, and its recipes plan as intermediates, only there. The station gates queuing only: a queued run keeps crafting after the panel closes, and shares the queue's `HAND_CRAFT_QUEUE_RUNS` with every other run.
- A raw shortage, a recipe cycle or a chain past the depth refuses the whole plan, and a toast names the reason ([ui.md](ui.md), Recipe browser).
- A plan never counts on covering a deficit of runs already queued: an item's planned count stops at zero.
- A craft takes its ingredients when it starts at the front of the queue. A front craft whose ingredient was spent or dropped waits. The next queue action first plans the makers of what the whole front run lacks against the inventory alone and queues them ahead (`plan_front_repair`), or leaves it waiting when the inventory cannot make the item.
- A finished craft whose outputs do not fit waits with its progress kept. Cancelling takes one craft off the newest run and gives back the ingredients of a craft in progress, refused when they no longer fit.
- Free crafting (0234, `Simulation_State.free_crafting`, the Developer screen): the plan charges no ingredient and queues no intermediate (`Craft_Plan.free_crafting`), so only the unlock, the maker and the queue's capacity refuse; a front craft starts without taking anything (`Craft_Queue.started_free`), finishes recording its products and no consumption, and cancelled gives nothing back. A craft finishes under the rule it started with: a front waiting for an ingredient starts on the first tick with the flag on, a craft that took its ingredients finishes as before, and crafts queued free that have not started when the flag turns off wait for their ingredients like any front. `started_free` is read by name, so a save from before 0234 loads it false; no remap, no log line.

## Phase budgets

Target playtime and what the player owns at the end of each phase. They drive recipe costs. Phases 1 to 4 add up to about two hours forty minutes over two or three sittings.

| Phase | Target | End state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Arrival | 10 min | Stone, hematite and coal dug, one stone furnace placed, ten plates, a stone cutting table placed and its first bricks cut (0196, quest `cutting`), a drill, an arm, a belt and a chest placed |
| 2 Workshop | 30 min | 3 stone furnaces, 2 burner drills, 200 plates, first gears, belts discovered |
| 3 Automation | 45 min | One self feeding coal drill, 4 drills feeding 3 furnaces, inserters, chests, ten unattended minutes |
| 4 Power | 75 min | 1 fuel generator, 1 offshore pump, 1 boiler, 2 steam engines, 10 poles, 4 electric drills, 4 assemblers, 2 labs, 5 technologies |

Budgets for phases 5 to 8 come with their couch tests. Planned machines not in the data yet: assembler 2, steel chest and medium pole (phase 5), assembler 3, express belt, stack inserter and science pack 4 (phase 7), science pack 5 (phase 8).

## Technologies and gates

`data/technologies.sjson`: packs times seconds per pack in a speed 1 lab, prerequisites listed earlier in the file.

- One main quest gate per phase, and nothing else waits on it ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), Research, quests and rockets). Phase 4: the steam engine is a quest channel recipe that chapter 3's main quest unlocks, so until then boilers make steam nothing can use. Phases 6 to 8: `oil_processing`, `deep_mining` and `rocket_program` carry `quest_gate`, labs refuse them, and the main quests of chapters 5 to 7 research them.
- `mining_productivity` and `research_speed` are infinite: each level costs `level_cost_growth_percent` of the one before and adds `effect_percent`.
- The venture's contracts and catalogue are in `data/contracts.sjson`. An orbital survey charts the surface veins within `ORBITAL_SURVEY_RADIUS` of the pad.

## Veins

`data/veins.sjson`: size classes (scattering, deposit, concentration), vein types with their output mix, deep veins at five times the surface class (`deep_units_factor`) for the phase 7 bore drills.

- An outcrop is the visible top of a vein, not its reservoir: hand mining takes outcrop blocks, drills draw from the reservoir under the whole footprint disc.
- Mining a vein's last outcrop block while its reservoir has units makes Mission Control say once per vein that the vein continues below (`mc_outcrop_spent`, 0096). The vein then counts as known: the map keeps its footprint drawn like an assayed one, while the drill panel does not call it assayed.
- A finite vein drained by drills turns its outcrop to `spent_block`.
- The low grade share of a draw rises linearly from `LOW_GRADE_START_PPM` at a full reservoir to `LOW_GRADE_END_PPM` with `LOW_GRADE_END_REMAINING_PERCENT` left (`drill.odin`). Infinite veins stay at the start.
- The starter veins by the landing pad: [quests.md](quests.md), Spawn requirements.

### Veins on the sphere

The terrain field's veins (0179, `generation_planet_veins.odin`): a vein is a disc on the planet, a unit direction and a radius along the sphere of the planet's radius, not voxels.

- The outcrop: within the disc, from the local surface down `OUTCROP_DEPTH_METRES` (2 m), generation makes the ground the vein's ore material (`planet_sample`), never bedrock, so hand mining the outcrop yields the ore through the material table. Below it the strata go on.
- The slice places the three starter veins only, one per ore of `PLANET_STARTER_VEIN_MATERIALS` (hematite, chalcopyrite, coal), round the planet's home (the pod's, `home` of the planet record, which `make_planet_generation` reads through `planet_home_direction`): each in its own third of the circle round the home, turned by up to a quarter of the third from a seeded rotation, at `PLANET_VEIN_MINIMUM_DISTANCE_METRES` to `PLANET_VEIN_MAXIMUM_DISTANCE_METRES` (30 to 80 m), with a radius of 3 to 5 m (the scattering class), all from the `Planet_Veins` sub seed. The rest of the sphere has none until M14 scatters veins.
- The discs come from the seed, the planet's radius and the home only, not from `data/veins.sjson`: they shape the ground, which a data edit must not reshape round saved chunks. The reservoir does come from it: `register_planet_veins` makes one `Vein` per disc, of the spawn vein type whose outcrop block has the ore's name (`hematite_ore` is the iron type's) and holding the starter size class's units by the type's mix. A file whose spawn types lack an ore refuses the registration.
- A `Vein` keeps its block footprint and gains the disc (`sphere_centre`, `sphere_radius`, zero for a block world vein), which the save leaves out: the session registers the veins again at start, which keeps a loaded reservoir and restores its disc. `vein_under_world_position` finds the vein whose disc holds a world position projected onto the sphere; a drill on a frame taps it ([logistics.md](logistics.md), Drills).

## Ratio checks

Recomputed from the data. A unit is one draw from a vein, ore or spoil.

- A burner drill draws 18.75 units a minute: 15 hematite on an iron vein. A stone furnace smelts 18.75 plates a minute. Five burner drills (75 hematite) feed four stone furnaces.
- Five electric drills (150 hematite) feed eight stone furnaces or four steel furnaces.
- A belt carries 900 items a minute: the hematite of sixty burner or thirty electric drills, fewer once spoil rides along.
- One boiler (60 L of steam a second) feeds two steam engines (30 L each). One offshore pump (1200 L a second) feeds twenty boilers, so forty engines and 36 MW.
- A boiler burns 1.8 MW, 27 coal a minute. A burner drill on a coal vein draws 16.9 coal a minute and burns 2.25, so the coal loop feeds itself with room to spare.
- The fuel generator lights a coal at 25 percent, 1 MJ, which lasts 13 seconds at its full 75 kW. For the same energy it burns four times the fuel of a boiler and engine: it starts the steam plant and is no substitute for it.
- A speed 0.5 assembler makes 6 science pack 1 a minute. A lab on a 15 second technology uses 4.
- A stone cutter (speed 0.5, 1.6 s a brick) cuts 18.75 bricks a minute from 37.5 stone, a stone furnace's pace. Its 90 kW burns a coal in 44 s, 14 bricks. A hand at the table cuts two bricks, one stone brick foundation, in 3.2 s.
- A 30,000 unit deposit lasts one burner drill about 27 hours and ten electric drills 80 minutes. Finite veins matter only at scale, which is the intent.
- A phase 4 base draws about 1.1 MW: 4 electric drills 360 kW, 4 assemblers 300 kW, 2 labs 120 kW, 20 inserters 260 kW, the offshore pump 60 kW, lamps. One boiler and two engines cover it.

## World

- Biomes (`data/biomes.sjson`): a column takes the first biome whose height, moisture and temperature ranges hold it. Temperature is a latitude band along z (`ORIGIN_TEMPERATURE` at the origin, colder towards -z, over `TEMPERATURE_PERIOD`), noise (`TEMPERATURE_NOISE_AMPLITUDE` at `TEMPERATURE_WAVELENGTH`) and a lapse of `TEMPERATURE_LAPSE` per block above sea level (`generation_terrain.odin`).
- Trees (`data/trees.sjson`, shapes in `generation_features.odin`): every log block drops `log` and every leaves block `leaves`, so recipes see one kind of wood. Trees have no roots: log blocks beside a trunk's foot read badly (0083).
- Trees and boulders keep off water and vein footprints. A clearing noise of `CLEARING_WAVELENGTH` keeps about a biome's `clearing_share` free of trees. Trees root at least `LEAF_REACH` off the landing pad, which clears only `LANDING_PAD_CLEARANCE` blocks above itself.
- Felling (`tree_felling.odin`): mining a log drops every log above it as loose items and queues the leaves near the column. Placed leaves decay only when a log near them is felled.
- A queued leaf decays after `LEAF_DECAY_MINIMUM_DELAY_TICKS` to `LEAF_DECAY_MAXIMUM_DELAY_TICKS` unless a log lies within `LEAF_SUPPORT_DISTANCE` (Manhattan), then queues its leaf neighbours. One decay in `LEAF_LITTER_CHANCE` drops leaves and one in `SAPLING_CHANCE` a sapling (no use yet).
- Ground cover (a biome's `ground_cover`, 0082): one hash of the column (the `Ground_Cover` sub seed) picks at most one entry, in order against the running sum of the chances. The cover goes into the cell above the surface when it is still air after trees and boulders and the column is outside every vein footprint. The landing pad clears its own cells, and mining under cover spills it ([hud.md](hud.md), Targeting).
- Ambient life (render only): a biome's `bird_density` and a ground cover entry's `insects` ([presentation.md](presentation.md), Ambient life).

## Trees

`trees` of a planet record in `data/planets.sjson` (0197): the trees on the field, in groves or, with a `forest` (0310), in forests read off the climate, generated and never stored ([architecture.md](architecture.md), World generation and The field session).

- Placement keys, all required (`trees_problem`; `grove_share_percent` 0 with no forest is no trees, and the other keys are then unchecked): `tree_spacing_metres` (2 to 16; the fine lattice, one candidate tree a cube), `density_percent` (1 to 100; the chance of a candidate at a grove's centre, falling with the square of the distance to none at its reach, or in a forest the chance at full cover), `clearing_metres` (the crater's reach to 64; no tree within it of the home, so none stands on the crater floor, in the bowl or on the rim), `maximum_slope_percent` (1 to 173, the steepest ground a tree stands on, 100 is 45 degrees), and the groves' `grove_spacing_metres` (twice the tree spacing to `MAXIMUM_GROVE_SPACING_METRES`, 256; the coarse lattice of grove centres), `grove_share_percent` (0 to 100, the share of grove cubes holding a grove) and `grove_radius_metres` (the tree spacing to the grove spacing; a grove's reach), checked only while the share is above 0. No tree stands below the sea level plus 1 m, under a lake's level plus 1 m or on carved ground (`planet_tree_ground`).
- `forest` (0310, optional; absent or a `cover_wavelength_metres` of 0 is off, and the planet keeps its groves; it needs the planet's climate): every key required when present (`forest_problem`). The cover is two gradient noises, `cover_wavelength_metres` (64 to 4096) and `clearing_wavelength_metres` (16 to 1024) mixed by `clearing_percent` (0 to 100), plus `moisture_cover_percent` (0 to 100) of the climate's moisture above or below its middle, ramped from `cover_threshold_percent` (-100 to 100) over `edge_percent` (1 to 100) to full cover. `meadow_metres` (0 to 512) takes the cover away round the home, fading in to twice it. `scatter_percent` (0 to 100) is the cover every place keeps for lone trees, the open land and the meadow alike. `minimum_gap_millimetres` (0 to the tree spacing) keeps two trees apart, the one of the lower priority hash yielding. Ground steeper than `full_slope_percent` (0 to `maximum_slope_percent`) thins to none at the maximum. `stand_wavelength_metres` (16 to 1024) and `stand_percent` (0 to 100) vary the species' weights in stands. `species_fade_celsius` (1 to 50), `species_fade_moisture_percent` (1 to 100), `species_fade_metres` (1 to 1000) and `species_fade_cover_percent` (1 to 100) fade a species out past its ranges. The cover and the climate's weight of the species there give a candidate's chance, so the forest thins where no species fits.
- `species` (1 to 8 records): `id` (1 to 32 bytes of a to z, 0 to 9 and `_`, once in the file), `machine` (a machine of kind `tree` holding the model, Models), `tint` (red, green and blue, 0 to 255, times the model's colours), `item` and `count` (1 to 16; the yield), `hand_felling_scale_percent` (0 to `MAXIMUM_TREE_SCALE_PERCENT`, 250; a tree of at most this scale is small and falls to the hand), `hand_felling_milliseconds` (100 to 60000; the hand's time on a small tree), `axe_felling_milliseconds` (three, each 100 to 60000; the wooden, stone and iron axe's time on any tree, a higher tier taking the last), `trunk_radius_millimetres` (50 to 1000) and `trunk_height_millimetres` (500 to 20000; the trunk's capsule the player collides with and aims at, scaled with the tree), and the forest's placement (0310, required, checked only while the forest is on, `tree_species_placement_problem`), each a range least first: `temperature_celsius` (-100 to 100), `moisture_percent` (0 to 100), `height_metres` (over the sea, -4000 to 4000), `cover_percent` (0 to 100), `scale_percent` (10 to 250), and `weight_percent` (0 to 100, some species above 0). The machine and the item are checked against their tables when the content loads (`planet_tree_species_problem`). In groves a tree takes a species by its hash; in a forest by the weights there.
- A world records the placement keys, the forest, the species count and each species' placement in its `world.sjson` (`trees`, `trees_recorded`), so a data edit of the placement never moves a saved world's trees; the species' other keys come from the data, the list repeated or cut to the recorded count. A `world.sjson` without them takes the data's with one log line. A world recorded before 0310 reads its forest as zero and keeps the groves of its record, with one log line (`recorded_forest_note`).
- Each tree has its own yaw and a shade of 92 to 108 percent from its hash, so no two neighbours look alike; its scale is 85 to 115 percent in groves, in a forest the species' `scale_percent`, triangular round its middle (`forest_scale_percent`).
- Felling: Mine held on a trunk fells it: an axe in `axe_felling_milliseconds` by its tier, the hand or any other item only a small tree (`scale_percent` at most `hand_felling_scale_percent`) in `hand_felling_milliseconds`; a larger one refuses `Needs_Axe` (0265, `field_felling_ticks`). In a forest world the time and the yield scale with the tree (0310): the time times the scale over 100, the `count` times it rounded and at least 1, so a giant broadleaf at 220 percent gives 11 logs; a world without a forest keeps the species' own. The time is divided by the cheat speed as hand mining is, and the yield goes into the inventory whole; a full inventory holds the progress, as a pick up does. The crown goes with the trunk: a felled tree is not drawn. A tree whose ground is dug away under its base (the field hollow on the trunk's axis half the trunk's height below its base) is felled without yield. A foundation, a snapped machine or a machine on bare ground whose cells meet a standing trunk is refused (`Tree_In_The_Way`); runs and torches are not checked against trunks.
- The shipped `home`: a forest on a 2 m lattice at 90 percent, a 24 m clearing (the crater reaches 18 m), slopes thinning from 40 percent to none at 70, a 56 m meadow, a 0.9 m gap. On the 4 km square round the home at 8 km, 42 percent of the land is interior (at least 60 percent of the full density), 19 percent edge and 39 percent open (`test_the_forest_share_of_the_land_holds`); the interior at 80.6 N 132 E holds about 1900 trees a hectare. Within 96 m of the home stand about 1000 trees, 170 of them a hand fells for logs. The groves' keys (40 m, half of them, 14 m) serve only a world recorded before the forest. Five species, every one the `pine_tree` model for now (each gets its own from a lab item), with a trunk of 180 by 2500 mm: `pine` (4 `log`, the hand to 100 percent in 10 s, the axes in 6, 4 and 2.5 s; -6 to 16 °C, 45 to 210 percent), `broadleaf` (5 logs; 6 to 30 °C, moist, below 70 m, 50 to 220 percent), `birch` (3 logs; -2 to 18 °C, 40 to 160 percent), `snag` (2 logs; dry and rare, 50 to 150 percent) and `bush` (a `count` of 5 `stick`, which its 20 to 45 percent makes 1 below 30 percent and 2 above in a forest world; the hand in 1.5 s up to 45 percent). Near the home pine and broadleaf make about 90 percent of the trees (`test_the_shipped_forest_mixes_species`).

## Lighting

The field light's numbers (0173; [architecture.md](architecture.md), The field light), in `data/lighting.sjson`, every key required and held to the configuration's strict keys (`parse_lighting_file`, `lighting_problem`).

- `falloff`: the light lost per metre by level band, the darkest band first. The bands split the levels 0 to 255 evenly, so their count is a power of two from 1 to 16; each loss is 1 to 255 and none rises above the one before it, so a brighter level never spreads dimmer than a darker one and the fill's result does not depend on its visiting order. A step between samples loses the falloff of the level it leaves times the spacing, rounded to the nearest and at least 1 (`make_field_light_tuning`), so a room is the same size in metres at every spacing; multiples of 6 divide evenly at 333, 500 and 1000 mm. The shipped curve is 24, 18, 12 and 6 per metre: gentle near the source, steep at the edge.
- `dark_level`: a level at or below it reads as dark (24; the tests' threshold).
- `steps_per_tick` (8192) and `chunk_seeds_per_tick` (4): the queue nodes the light runs per tick over both channels, and the arrived chunks whose borders are compared per tick.
- `emitters`: the light sources by `id` with their `level` at the source sample, 1 to 255, ids unique, `torch` and `lamp` required. The lamp's entry is required but nothing reads it yet: the lamps on frames do not light the field (0174), and the block world's lamps read `light_level` of their machine record. The torch (192) reaches about 11 m along a corridor and lights every sample of an 8 m room, the lamp (255) about 21 m and a 16 m hall (`test_the_light_radius_in_metres_is_the_same_at_every_spacing`). The field's torch places the emitter `field_simulation.torch_emitter` names (0179). The file loads with the tables and reloads with them.

## Machines on bare ground

Machines without foundations (0201, `machine_wear.odin`; [architecture.md](architecture.md), Frames and Simulation).

- Place with a machine other than a foundation aimed at bare ground (no frame targeted) stands it on a new frame of its own, free as a foundation's (`free_frame_at`: up along the radial at the hit, the player's heading's yaw step, `foundation_pitch_millimetres`), the machine's footprint centred on cell (0, 0, 0) (0215) and no foundation under it; the frame goes with its last entity. - Founded: a machine on the block frame, or one whose footprint's bottom row has a foundation in the cell right below each of its cells (`machine_is_founded`), set when it is placed and for the machines above a foundation placed or removed, and derived again after loading. A founded machine never wears.
- Wear: an unfounded machine counts its ticks of operation, the ticks its own tick advanced its work: a furnace burning, an assembler's or a lab's progress moving (a craft or a unit finishing counts), a drill boring or advancing its cycle, a boiler, pump, offshore pump, tar pit pump or flare stack producing, pumping or flaring, a generator delivering energy (once a tick), an inserter's arm moving, a splitter passing an item. Idle, starved, blocked by a full output or without power counts nothing. Chests, lamps, tanks, core sample drills and launch pads never count, so they never break. At its life (`bare_ground_life_minutes` of operation) it breaks down: its tick does nothing, it asks for and offers no power, a lab gives its unit up to another lab, its marker is red, its model is drawn dark, the toast names it once ("Stone furnace broke down"), and its panel and the pick up hint say "Broken down, tear it down".
- Tear down: picking up a worn (any operation counted) or broken machine returns `salvage_percent` of each direct input of the first hand recipe whose first product is its item (as the recipe book finds it, unlocked or not), per item the recipe makes, rounded down, plus its contents; a stone furnace (5 stone) returns 4 stone. An unworn machine, or one no hand recipe makes, returns itself. Placed again it starts at zero. Foundations and the stone cutting table never wear, so both always return themselves; a worn stone cutter returns 2 iron gears and 4 stone bricks.
- `stands_on_ground` (machine record, optional, default false) marks a machine that stands on the ground for good: never refused for slope, never worn. Set on the belts, the pipe, the small pole, the big pole, the substation, the belt pole, the pod, the stone cutting table (0196: it never operates, so the bare ground line and the slope refusal would promise a wear that never comes) and the water pump (0295: it stands on a lake bed, never wears in the demo).
- Water pumps (0295, `field_water_pump.odin`): the record `water_pump` (kind `water_pump`, the offshore pump's model and so its 2 by 1 by 1 footprint, `stands_on_ground`, its rate in `fluid_litres_per_second`, 1 to `MAXIMUM_WATER_PUMP_LITRES_PER_SECOND`, shipped 300; no slots, power or fluid ports, refused naming the pump otherwise) stands on the aimed ground or a lake bed on a frame of its own, still refused where it would bury a player. Its intake is the lowest sample that is not ground from the centre of its bottom row down to two samples below it (the water it stands in, or the bed's first air sample; on a slope that puts the centre in the ground, the first open sample up from it within 4 samples), its outlet the sample half a sample over its top. Interact cycles Off, Sink (takes the rate from the water at the intake), Source (adds the rate into the room over the outlet); the sea is infinite both ways. It has no panel; the crosshair's line names the mode, the state (Idle, Pumping, No water for a dry intake, Output full) and the litres moved ("Water pump  Sink  Pumping  3000 L moved"). The tick's rules are in [architecture.md](architecture.md), The water field, Tools and pumps.
- `data/game.sjson`, each required and bounded (`bare_ground_problem`): `bare_ground_flatness_millimetres` (0 to 2000, shipped 250), `bare_ground_life_minutes` (1 to 10080, a week; shipped 60) and `salvage_percent` (1 to 100, shipped 80). A machine record's own `bare_ground_life_minutes` (1 to 10080) overrides the life for that machine.

### Hatches

- A hatch (kind `hatch`, 0198, the record `pod_hatch`, 1 by 2 by 2 cells since 0221, its front facing out of the pod) has no item, is at least `MINIMUM_HATCH_HEIGHT` (2) cells high, lists no `open_cells` and has a `slide`, a `spin` or (0231) an `iris` motion or none (`validate_hatch_definition`; a spin of at most one turn, "hatch <id> has a spin amplitude outside 0 to 1 turn", since a spin's phase is the open fraction and it stands at its amplitude fully open; a `swing` goes and comes back over its phase, so it would close at fully open). It rides in the foundations' pool with its state on `Foundation` (`hatch_open`, `hatch_toggle_tick`).
- Closed its cells are solid. Open none is: every row but the top is an open cell, which the player walks and the aiming ray passes; the top row is neither, so the player passes it too (the player collides with solid cells only) and the ray stops at it, so an open hatch is still what the HUD's target names (nothing acts on a hatch since 0231; `occupy_open_hatch_cells`). The pod's 2 row hatches are passable over both rows; the ray passes row 0.
- The pod's airlock is crawled through (0221, the user: "The pod airlock doors shall need crouching to pass through"): the bore is 1 m high, so the standing capsule (1.8 m) is stopped at the drum's mouth by the solid cells over the door, and the crouched one (0.85 m, [architecture.md](architecture.md), The player on the field) passes with 150 mm over the head and 0.2 m each side; the crouch holds inside until the standing capsule fits, so the player stands up in the cabin's lane or outside. From inside the bore the crouched eye (0.7 m, row 1) meets a hatch level; from the lane or outside, standing, the eye is at 1.6 m and the door 0 to 1 m, so the player looks down at it or crouches. A crouched capsule centred in the bore is 0.5 m from both doors' cells, more than its 0.3 m radius, so both hatches close round it.
- Interact does nothing on a hatch and the HUD offers no Open or Close on one (0231): the airlock's rule would undo a press on the next tick, so X there opens the inventory as on any other cell without a panel ([input.md](input.md)). `toggle_hatch` still refuses to close a hatch while a player's capsule meets one of its cells (`capsule_meets_frame_cell`), the backstop of the rule below. The state is lockstep state, hashed and saved.
- The airlock (0222, 0231, `entity_pod_airlock.odin`, the user of 0231: "just open and close the doors when inside/outside 15 cm. Also it should react on any player, not just a crouching player"): once per tick after the players moved (`tick_pod_airlocks`), every hatch is open exactly while some player's capsule, standing or crouched, any heading, but never a cradled one's (0358, `field_player_capsules`: the cradled eye's capsule comes 5 cm from the inner hatch's cells), is within `pod_airlock.reach_millimetres` (150, 50 to 300, `data/game.sjson`) of its cells (`capsule_within_frame_cell`, sampled as `capsule_meets_frame_cell` samples), and `toggle_hatch` applies each change on the tick the condition flips: no hold, no interlock, no facing. The reach keeps both doors shut round a crouched player in the middle of the bore (200 mm from each), and no player is ever within reach of both (1 m less two times 300 plus 150 mm leaves 100 mm to spare), so a crawl has the near door open, both shut, then the far one open. Two players at the two doors open both; a player standing in a doorway keeps it open however long they stand there. The step skips the fall, and the prediction never runs it, so a predicted player meets a closed door until the confirmed tick opens it. A door saved open with nobody near shuts on the first tick, and a 0222 save's `hatch_close_tick` is skipped by name ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Save format).
- Its shutter is a camera iris (0231): the `iris` motion draws the model's one blade `<n>` times round the aperture and turns each about its own pin by the open fraction, in 0.15 s (9 ticks), and its strips light while it is open ([presentation.md](presentation.md), Machine models).

## The arrival

A new world opens with the pod's fall (0200, [architecture.md](architecture.md), The field session, The arrival; [presentation.md](presentation.md), The field session, The arrival). Its values are flat keys of `data/game.sjson`, checked at load (`arrival_problem`):

- `arrival_ticks` (1860, 30 s of descent and the settle): the fall's length, the players strapped into the chair, the look free (0223); at its last tick it lands with the hatches closed (0222: the airlock opens the inner one as the player comes to it, Hatches). 0 is no fall (the world starts with nothing held); otherwise more than `arrival_settle_ticks` and at most 3600 (`MAXIMUM_ARRIVAL_TICKS`, also the bound of a saved fall).
- `arrival_settle_ticks` (60, 0 to 300): the hit comes this many ticks before the landing, so the crash is followed by a second of silence before the landing.
- `arrival_entry_angle_degrees` (45, 5 to 85), `arrival_entry_speed_metres_per_second` (75, 1 to 1000) and `arrival_terminal_speed_metres_per_second` (20, 1 to 1000, at the planet's radius): the pod leaves the start at that speed and angle below the horizontal towards its travel, the chair's facing (0270, `pod_travel_heading`; the door's direction before), so the chair's porthole looks along it; gravity and a drag of the air's density times the speed squared bend the path towards vertical (`build_arrival_curve`, `data_arrival_curve.odin`), integrated once in its own seconds and stretched over the descent, so the seconds tune the tempo and the keys the shape. The drag per metre is the gravity over the terminal speed squared, at Earth's gravity: the stretch absorbs the planet's.
- `arrival_real_seconds` (6, 1 to 60): the curve's last natural seconds play 1:1 at the end of the descent, so the hit comes at the curve's own speed; the natural time before them is stretched over the rest of the descent (`arrival_curve_progress`, piecewise linear), where the space and the entry carry the stretch. The load fails when they are not below the curve's natural seconds and the descent's.
- `arrival_rest_tilt_degrees` (15, 0 to 25, 0270): the pod rests leaning this far from the planet's up towards its travel, about its door's axis, set at the hit ([architecture.md](architecture.md), The field session, The arrival).
- `arrival_debris_pieces` (160, 0 to 400), `arrival_debris_rest_seconds` (150, 0 to 900) and `arrival_debris_angle_degrees` (45, 30 to 60), 0272: the clods the hit throws out of the crater, how long each rests at most before it sinks away and the angle above the horizontal they leave at, presentation only; 0 pieces keeps the dust, the crash and the bang but throws no clods, so no patter plays ([presentation.md](presentation.md), The field session, The arrival).
- `arrival_flakes_per_second` (90, 0 to 160), 0290, 0304: the flakes the heat shield sheds a second at the heat's peak, fewer by the heat's square below it. At 0 none break off. Presentation only ([presentation.md](presentation.md), The field session, The arrival).
- `atmosphere` (`top_metres` 650, 64 to 4096; `scale_height_metres` 145, 1 to 4096): the air's density falls as exp(-altitude / scale height) and is 0 above the top; the sky is black above it, the day's from two scale heights under it. The top less two scale heights stays at least 64 m (`ATMOSPHERE_CLEAR_GROUND_METRES`), so the day's sky is untouched on the ground.
- `arrival_plasma` (0273, 0304, 0305, `haze_color` [255, 28, 6], `ablator_color` [255, 92, 30], `core_color` [255, 248, 150], `soot_percent` 35, `wake_opacity_percent` 60): the entry's plasma on the pod, its colours Kerbal Space Program's reentry. `haze_color` is the air's glow (red, the wake where its light is faint), `ablator_color` the heat shield's (orange, its rim and the wake's bright body), `core_color` the hottest (yellow white: the shield's face, the sparks, the flakes' rims), 0 to 255 per channel, never black (`arrival_plasma.<key> is black`, a missing key reads as black and is refused as the others); the heat drives the wake's opacity and its place between them, never their hue. `soot_percent` (0 to 60, `MAXIMUM_ARRIVAL_SOOT_PERCENT`, so a porthole stays a window) is the soot's opacity at the glass's rim once the heating is over, kept after the landing ([presentation.md](presentation.md), The field session, The arrival). `wake_opacity_percent` (5 to 95) is the wake's opacity face on where the chair's porthole looks at the peak; lower shows the pod through its flames from outside and the sky through the porthole. A missing key reads 0 and is refused, so a stale data edits copy falls back to the data files at the start. The colours sit beside `atmosphere` rather than on the planet, since the air is one for every planet; when `atmosphere` moves to the planet record, `haze_color` moves with it.
- `arrival_heat_threshold_percent` (20, 0 to 90): the heating is the density times the speed cubed, normalised to its peak; below this share nothing glows, and the load fails when the hit is not below it.
- `arrival_start_metres` (810, 20 to 4096): the start above the crater's floor. It stays at least the deepest relief any planet may have (`MAXIMUM_RELIEF_DEPTH_METRES`, 128 m, since the file is checked without the planets) above the atmosphere's top, so the start is in black sky wherever the crater lies, and the start lies inside 0.9 of the coarsest level's distance (921 m shipped) from the crater (`ARRIVAL_START_DISTANCE_SHARE`), so the crater shows hazed through the fog at the start; beyond the coarsest level only the globe is drawn.

## Blocks

`data/blocks.sjson` holds the shapes, orientation flags, light and sound materials.

- Slabs and stairs are solid and collide with their boxes. There is no step up: the player jumps onto them.
- A tile that varies per block is slid by whole texels and wrapped, so it must be periodic: `test_varying_block_tiles_are_periodic` fails when the mean texel difference across the wrap edge exceeds `TILE_WRAP_ROUGHNESS_FACTOR` times the mean across interior edges. `keep_orientation` and `framed` blocks are exempt ([presentation.md](presentation.md), Per block variation).
- Coloured light: the largest channel of `light_color` equals `light_level`, which stays the one level everything else reads. The furnace's and the boiler's glow is emissive materials or voxels in their models, not light; the stone furnace's fire is its flames and its flickering lamp (`flames`, `lights`, Models).

## Textures

Block tiles and item icons are 16 by 16 RGBA PNG files under `data/textures/`, or generated tiles, loaded into atlases at start, on a content reload and when a file changes (`render_atlas.odin`).

- `textures/blocks/<id>.png` serves all three face groups, and `<id>_top.png`, `<id>_side.png` and `<id>_bottom.png` override one group each. A block or group without a file takes its colour from `data/blocks.sjson` with a little noise. Shape variants use the listed block's tiles.
- `textures/items/<id>.png` is the item's icon ([ui.md](ui.md), Text and theme).
- A file that is not 16 by 16 with 8 bit channels, or does not decode, is logged and takes the fallback.
- `data/textures/procedural.sjson` (0099) lists the blocks whose tile the game generates (`texture_generate.odin`, pure): the seven ores. Its header gives the parameters and ranges. A generated tile serves every face group and wins over any file.
- `data/textures/field_materials.sjson` (0169) holds two arrays and nothing else: `materials`, one entry per field material but air (topsoil, stone, deep stone, bedrock and the three ores), and `looks` (0307), one entry per look (`grass`, `sand`, `snow`), keyed `look` instead of `material`. Each entry holds `ground` and `fleck` (red, green, blue) and the ore parameters of `procedural.sjson` with their ranges, every key required, except that `crystal_size` must be 1: round flecks are isotropic, while larger sizes make squares whose edges stripe along the axes of the triplanar projection, so the file is refused. An entry missing, repeated or unknown is refused. The tile is generated as an ore's, flecks over the ground; the ores (0179) take the stone's ground with rust red, brassy and black flecks. The looks' tiles are near neutral, since the planet's grass, sand and snow colours multiply them, each with its grain: the grass busy (blades), the sand smooth, the snow smooth with a sparkle. How they are drawn: [presentation.md](presentation.md), Textures. The planet palette's tint multiplies it ([presentation.md](presentation.md), Field meshes); it is read when the planet preview starts, not on a reload.
- `blob_width` below about 0.55 blurs little, and the blobs join diagonally into a checkerboard. Above about 0.8 a tile holds a few large blobs that read per block.
- The texture editor ([developer_tools.md](developer_tools.md)) saves `$XDG_STATE_HOME/mine-oh-belowed/texture_edits.sjson` in the data file's form. Its entries replace the data file's per block on every atlas build, so once the chosen values are copied into `procedural.sjson`, delete the edits file. A file that does not load is logged and ignored whole.
- `tools/make_placeholder_textures.py` writes the placeholder block tiles, item icons and UI icons from the ids and colours in the data, skipping the procedural blocks. Rerun it after adding a block or item. Hand made files replace its output one by one.
- Placeholder patterns follow the per block variation: stone, gold quartz, leaves and water use isotropic noise only (`isotropic_field`), with no rows or diagonals, and every varying pattern wraps at the tile edge. The log rings need not, being framed.

## Models

How models are loaded, lit and moved: [presentation.md](presentation.md), Machine models and The player.

- An OBJ machine model is authored by a script under `tools/models/machines/` in cells, with its +x side as the front, in the look of `DESIGN.md` (Art direction) ([build.md](build.md), Models); a voxel machine model at 8 or 16 voxels per block, z up, with its +x side as the front, until 0206. The keys are in the header of `data/machines.sjson`.
- An OBJ machine is a script that takes its record from `tools/models/records.py` (footprint, motion, pivot and hinge, ports by name, open cells, a pod's fixture boxes, light), so no number is typed twice; it is committed only when `tools/make_models.sh` passes, after its preview was read ([build.md](build.md), The workbench).
- A machine saved at an older size keeps its cells until it is picked up (0212, `entity_keeps_saved_size` in `entity.odin`): a saved entity keeps its rotated `size`, and when that differs from its record's rotated footprint (a pod aside, which `upgrade_resized_pods` replaces) it occupies the box it was saved with, all solid, instead of the record's cells and open cells, and every reader of its cells (`common_cells`: the founded flag, the pick up's held up test, the trees and the water) reads that box. After a load, one log line counts such machines ("N machines keep the footprint they were saved with; pick them up and place them again for the new size"). The renderer draws the record's model centred on the old box, so an old stone furnace overlaps its neighbours until it is placed again at the new size.
- A tree's model (0197) is a machine record of kind `tree` (no item, never an entity), so it loads, checks and previews as a machine's. `tools/models/machines/pine_tree.py` reads its trunk from its species in `data/planets.sjson` and the pitch from `data/game.sjson`, so the drawn trunk is the collided one; its crown rises past its footprint's height.
- The pod (0221) is an OBJ machine (`tools/models/machines/pod.py` with the shared mesh helpers of `pod_geometry.py`), made in a sealed lab (0221, the brief `tools/model_lab/pod/BRIEF.md`) from the art booklet's kept interior and the user's words: a squat riveted re-entry cone of 24 facets with ribs, scorch streaks, thruster blocks, portholes round the walls and a top can with its antennas; inside, the bolted impact chair with straps at the centre, the bed strapped flat on the lean wall, the desk with screens, keypads, gauges and toggles, cabinets and instrument boxes on every other surface, a riveted floor, the overhead lamp strip and the desk lamp, and the airlock drum low on +x with ribs round its bore and the pockets the shutters rise into. 25488 triangles in 8 materials (`pod_black`, `pod_dark`, `pod_metal`, `pod_padding`, `pod_orange` and the glows `pod_glow_amber`, `pod_glow_green`, `pod_glow_white`). Its hatches, locker, bench and charge controller are models of their own (`pod_hatch.py`, the drum's iris door, made in the same lab: a rim and frame in the door's slot with status strips, one iris blade its part (0231); `pod_locker.py`, `crafting_bench.py`, `oxygen_generator.py`: the charge controller's model is the oxygen generator's, `model = "oxygen_generator"`, until a sealed lab remakes it, 0358, 0366). Its collision file (0230) comes from `collision(b)` in `pod.py`, 35 volumes written from 0230's table of the script's own numbers: the hull's lower wall with the door's gap, its leaning cone, the plug and the top can, the heat shield's rim, the floor plate, the drum housing and the shutter's tower, the fairing round the outer door, the desk and the lower walls as wall sectors, the keypad console, the chair, the niches' posts and lintels, the manifold and the bed. A modeller's lab round on the accepted pod ([build.md](build.md), The workbench, `--rework`) may refine it.
- A machine's lamps (0224): the optional `lights` key lists at most `MAXIMUM_MACHINE_LIGHTS` (8) `{position = [x, y, z], color = [r, g, b], radius_cells}`, the position in cells of the model's frame (x and z centred on the unrotated footprint, y from its bottom, +x the front: the OBJ's own numbers) inside the footprint with `MODEL_FOOTPRINT_TOLERANCE_CELLS` (0.02) of slack, each colour channel 0 to 255 and the radius 1 to 64 cells (`validate_machine_lights`, which refuses the record naming the light's index). A lamp may add `clip = false` (0229): by default it lights only inside its machine's box, the footprint up to the model's top, and not the faces turned away from it, so a lamp inside a hull leaves the outside dark; `clip = false` lets it shine out of the machine. A lamp may add `flicker = true` (0274) on a machine with `flames`: its colour follows the fire's flicker (`validate_machine_lights` refuses it on a machine without flames). A value other than a bool fails the parse. Each lamp shines a point light in a field session while the machine's model works ([presentation.md](presentation.md), Machine models); a machine `draw_entities` does not draw (fluid and power machines, inserters, trees) may list lamps, which then do nothing. A lab's lamp list in the Blender frame (x the front, y the game's -z, z up) converts as a Blender point (bx, by, bz) to the record's (bx, bz, -by). The pod's record lists none yet: its cabin lamps come with its lab model (0221). `tools/models/records.py` ignores the key.
- A furnace's flames (0274): the optional `flames` key, furnaces only, lists at most `MAXIMUM_MACHINE_FLAMES` (8) `{position = [x, y, z], width, height, yaw_degrees}`: a quad of the flame shader standing on its base centre at the position (cells of the model's frame, as a lamp's, inside the footprint with the same slack), `width` and `height` 0.1 to 12 cells, its top (`y + height`) no higher than the footprint with the slack, turned `yaw_degrees` (-180 to 180) about the up from spanning z and facing the front (`validate_machine_flames`, which refuses the record naming the flame's index; NaN fails every bound). The flames burn while the model works ([presentation.md](presentation.md), Machine models, Flames); presentation only. `tools/models/records.py` ignores the key, and the model lab keeps it in a lab's records, so a rework's modeller sees where the flames stand.
- Collision volumes (0230, `machine_collision.odin`, `world_frame_body.odin`): a model may have `data/models/<model>.collision.sjson`, written by `tools/make_models.py` from its script's `collision(b)` through `tools/models/collision.py` ([build.md](build.md), Models), never by hand in practice. Numbers are cells of the model's frame, as the OBJ's and the `lights`' (x and z centred on the unrotated footprint, y from its bottom, +x the front). The file is `volumes = [...]`, each `{kind, axis, from, to, radius_from, radius_to, shell, sector}`: `kind` `box` (axis aligned, `from` and `to` its corners) or `round` (a cylinder, or a cone frustum when the radii differ, about `axis` `x`, `y` or `z` from the point `from` to the point `to` along it, with the outer radius at each); `shell` (optional) is the wall only, open at both ends: a round's thickness along the radius, a box's walls on the two axes across its `axis`, which a box shell then names; a round's `sector` (optional) is two angles in degrees about the axis by the right hand rule (about x from +y towards +z, about y from +z towards +x, about z from +x towards +y), the volume spanning from the first to the second and leaving the rest of the turn open, which is how the pod's hull leaves the door. At most `COLLISION_VOLUME_LIMIT` (64) volumes, a box shell counting 4. The reader refuses, naming the file and the volume's index: an unknown kind or axis, a box whose `from` is not below its `to`, a box with a sector, a box shell not under half the box across its axis, a round whose ends are not along its axis, a radius not above 0, a shell not under the smaller radius, a sector that is not two angles from -360 to 720 degrees spanning more than 0 and less than 360, too many volumes, and a volume (a round's full turn) outside the footprint plus `MODEL_FOOTPRINT_TOLERANCE_CELLS` on any side, the top included ("data/models/pod.collision.sjson: volume 3 is not inside the footprint of machine "pod" (12 by 12 by 8 cells with 0.02 of slack)"); the start-up stops with that line, as for a malformed model. The volumes load with the machines (`load_machine_collisions`) onto `Machine.collision` in 1/4096 cell (a box shell as its four walls), and several machines naming one model each read it against their own footprint. Such a machine on a frame collides by its volumes alone for the player and the aiming ray; its cells stay its own for placement, its open cells keep the spawn, the fixtures' boxes and the sealed room, and its hatches and fixtures collide by their own cells ([architecture.md](architecture.md), Frames). `--model-check` refuses a volume inside an open cells box or a fixture box ([build.md](build.md), The workbench). A collision file is lockstep content: it reloads with the content, not in place like the model.
- The arm (0175) is authored at real scale instead, six files at 25 mm per voxel, one per part; the files and the authored pose are in [presentation.md](presentation.md), The arm. Every inserter record names it with `model = "arm"` and `motion = {kind = "arm"}`.
- The player is six files (`player_torso.vox` and the limbs), each the whole `PLAYER_MODEL_FRAME` at 16 voxels per block with only its limb filled, +x the front and +z the right side. A replacement keeps the frame and puts the shoulders at the tops of the arms, the hips at the tops of the legs and the neck under the head.

## Sounds

`data/sounds/sounds.sjson` lists every sound and the ids the game asks for. The mixer and triggers, footsteps and mining hits included, are in [presentation.md](presentation.md), Sound.

- Every block's `sound_material` needs its `footstep_<material>` and every biome's `ambience` its loop or variants, or the table does not load.
- Ambience calls (`sound_events.odin`): a cluster plays two variants `AMBIENCE_CALL_GAP_MINIMUM_TICKS` to `AMBIENCE_CALL_GAP_MAXIMUM_TICKS` apart, a third in `AMBIENCE_THIRD_CALL_PERCENT` of clusters, then pauses `AMBIENCE_CLUSTER_PAUSE_MINIMUM_TICKS` to `AMBIENCE_CLUSTER_PAUSE_MAXIMUM_TICKS`. The variant never repeats the last one, and the pitch varies by `AMBIENCE_CALL_PITCH_SHARE`, all from a hash of the tick and the cluster count.
- A `day_only` ambience calls only while the daylight blend is above `AMBIENCE_DAYLIGHT_MINIMUM`. The first variant's flag speaks for all.
- The hum drifts by `HUM_DRIFT_SHARE` over `HUM_DRIFT_MINIMUM_TICKS` to `HUM_DRIFT_MAXIMUM_TICKS`.
- The field session's sounds (0200): `arrival_roar` (a loop) and `arrival_crash` for a new world's fall and `hatch_slide` for any hatch opening or closing.
- `tools/make_placeholder_sounds.py` (standard library only, deterministic) writes the placeholder files, mono at 22050 Hz, into `data/sounds/` or a directory given. The table is written by hand, and recorded sounds replace files one by one.

## Descriptions and notes

- Every item, machine and technology names a `description_key` (`describe_item_<id>` and so on, 0070). `test_shipped_descriptions_exist` keeps every shipped entry described.
- A description is one to three sentences in the venture's register ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), Lore and tone): what the thing is, what it does for the outpost, and one concrete fact, real (a formula, a melting point, an ore's metal content) or the game's own (a rate, a draw, a capacity).
- A game fact in a description copies a value in the data: changing the value means changing the text.
- A machine's item and the machine have two texts: what it is for (the recipe browser) and how it runs (the machine panel).
- Temperatures read "degrees Celsius": not every selectable font has a degree sign.
- Notes (`data/notes.sjson`, `notes.odin`) are the journal's world building, one unlock each, in the order their milestones come in play. Geology in a note is real geology (Mississippi Valley type lead and zinc, magmatic nickel with copper, lateritic bauxite), the rest is the venture. A note whose title or text names the block world's capsule or pad has a field variant, `<key>_field` ([quests.md](quests.md), Runtime).
